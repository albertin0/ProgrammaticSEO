## Saguenay's Outdoor Workout Report: June 20, 2026

Don’t let the mild temperatures fool you into thinking it's a completely clear shot out there. While the AQI is a pleasant 27, with "Ozone/Other" as the dominant pollutant, well below anything to fuss about, and no active severe weather alerts are screaming at us from Environment Canada or The Weather Network, there are still a few things to keep in mind.

First, let’s talk about that moderate pollen count. If you’re usually a watery-eyed, sneezing mess this time of year, consider popping an antihistamine before you head out. Nobody wants a snot-filled five-kilometre loop around Lac Kénogami, trust me.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Allergy Alert: Moderate Pollen
With a moderate pollen level today, sensitive individuals might experience symptoms. Plan your route to avoid overly green areas, or consider indoor alternatives if your allergies are really acting up.
</AlertBox>

Now, for the real Saguenay special: roadwork. It’s an unavoidable part of life here, and this week is no different. If your usual route takes you anywhere near Route 170 or Route 175, be prepared for some detours and lane closures. Specifically, you’re looking at lane disruptions on Route 170 at Rivière à Mars and along Chemin des Pics in La Baie. And if you're jogging or cycling through Chicoutimi, watch out for asphalting between Boulevard des Travailleurs and Rue du Vieux-Pont on Route 175, plus some cycle path development between Boulevard Barrette and Rue du Sauvignon. Your smooth ride might get a little bumpy.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Construction Chaos: Promenade Rivière-du-Moulin
Heads up! The Promenade Rivière-du-Moulin is undergoing repair work on its retaining wall and guardrail until July 17, 2026. This could mean restricted access or areas to avoid entirely. Check local signage before you head out for your scenic stroll or run.
</AlertBox>

The good news is that we haven't seen any recent, major flooding that would swamp our trails, unlike those severe events back in 2023 in Rivière-Éternité that wrecked parts of Route 170 and Fjord-du-Saguenay National Park. A small earthquake was felt on June 18 (two days ago), but there were no reports of damage, so your run won't be interrupted by any unexpected sinkholes from that.

However, with light rain expected throughout the day, trail conditions on some of our beloved paths, especially the less maintained ones, might lean towards "wet" or "variable." Check specific trail reports if you're hitting the dirt.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Wet Surfaces Expected
With light rain in the forecast, trails and sidewalks can become slick. Watch your footing, especially on any unpaved paths or wooden boardwalks. Trail shoes might be a good call if you're heading off-road.
</AlertBox>

Finally, if you were planning to use the waterfront areas, keep in mind the Festival bateaux dragons 2026 (Dragon Boat Festival) is wrapping up today, June 21. This might affect parking or general congestion around the docks.

All in all, it’s a perfectly acceptable day for an outdoor workout if you’re smart about it. Just be mindful of that pollen, navigate the construction, and don't expect perfectly dry footing everywhere.
