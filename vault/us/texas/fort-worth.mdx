## Today's Fort Worth Run Down: Don't Melt Out There

Alright, Fort Worth, today is Wednesday, June 24, 2026, and while the morning might start with some agreeable overcast skies, don't be fooled by Mother Nature's little head fake. We're looking at temperatures climbing into the mid to upper 90s, pushing that heat index well past the century mark. This isn't just "sweaty," this is "dangerously hot" territory.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Advisory Coming:** Expect heat index values to hit 100-104 degrees today. This is serious business. Hydrate aggressively, plan your workouts for very early or very late, and seriously consider an indoor option. Heat advisories may become necessary in portions of North Texas.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side for your respiratory system, our Air Quality Index (AQI) is sitting pretty at 38, which is considered "Good." The dominant pollutant is PM2.5, but at this level, it's not a major concern for most. However, if you're one of the many suffering from seasonal sniffles, you'll still be dealing with a moderate pollen count.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch for Residual Storm Effects:** While the severe thunderstorm warnings from yesterday have mostly expired for Tarrant County, some areas north of DFW still have a Level 1 risk for isolated storms that could bring gusty winds and brief heavy rain. Stay weather-aware, especially if you're venturing further out.
</AlertBox>

If you're hitting the pavement or the dirt, particularly on our beloved Trinity Trails, remember that construction is always a thing in this city. There are ongoing projects, like Phase 2 of the River Trails Park to River Legacy Park section, which are still under construction, even if some parts were expected to be completed earlier this year. Always be mindful of equipment, detours, and uneven surfaces. A new Bomber Spur Trail project also recently broke ground, so new activity zones are popping up.

My advice? If you absolutely *have* to get outside, be smart. Go at dawn. Seriously, like, before the sun even thinks about it. Carry more water than you think you need, wear light-colored, moisture-wicking gear, and listen to your body. That "overcast" can burn off fast, and then you're stuck baking on the concrete or exposed trails.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Key:** With heat index values soaring, start hydrating *before* your workout. Electrolyte drinks can be your best friend. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or excessively fatigued, stop immediately and get somewhere cool. There's no medal for heat stroke.
</AlertBox>
