## Today's Fort Worth Grind: Watch Your Step and the Sky

Let's be real, you woke up hoping for that crisp, clear North Texas air. And for your lungs, it's actually not bad. The Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at 29, which is firmly in the "Good" category. Your dominant pollutant, Ozone/Other, isn't doing much today to mess with your breathing rhythm. So, take a deep breath, but don't get too comfortable.

The pollen, on the other hand, is a different beast. While some sources might optimistically claim "none," the data I'm working with says it's a solid **Moderate**. If you're one of the many Fort Worth locals who sniffles their way through spring (and apparently early summer), you know the drill. That moderate level of ash, birch, and juniper pollen might make your run feel a little less breezy and a lot more congested.

Now, for your joints and general well-being: The temperature at a cool 18.1°C (about 64.6°F) sounds absolutely delightful for a run. Perfect, right? Not so fast, sunshine. While Fort Worth might be experiencing "Mainly Clear" conditions right now, the National Weather Service is calling for "isolated to scattered showers and storms across portions of North and Central Texas" throughout the day. We're talking gusty winds, frequent lightning, and pockets of locally heavy rainfall. So that "mainly clear" could turn into "suddenly drenched and dodging puddles" in a hot minute.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Mugginess and Storm Potential
While the temperature is decent, the chance of scattered thunderstorms and rain is real. Expect humidity, and keep an eye on the sky, especially if you're out on exposed sections of the Trinity Trails or any of our open parks. Don't be that person caught in a lightning storm.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the delightful world of Fort Worth infrastructure improvements. If your route takes you anywhere near the **Chisholm Trail Parkway**, be aware. The ongoing widening project means there are daytime road closures, including the southbound County Road 920 entrance ramp, which is closed from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. today. This might not directly impact foot traffic on dedicated trails, but it certainly complicates getting to and from certain workout spots.

And let's not forget the endless dance of downtown construction. E. Belknap Street, between Pecan and Nichols, has had two lanes closed. Good news, apparently that mess wraps up *today*, June 5th. Just in time for... more construction on the Trinity Trails themselves. The second phase of the Trinity Trails, connecting River Trails Park to River Legacy Park, is still in the construction phase and might throw a wrench in your long-distance plans. Detours are part of the Fort Worth runner's life.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route, Check for Updates
Before you head out, especially if you're hitting areas with known construction like the Trinity Trails or anywhere near the Chisholm Trail Parkway, do a quick check for local alerts. The City of Fort Worth's road closures page is your friend.
</AlertBox>

So, yeah, enjoy that lower AQI, but lace up with caution. You've got moderate pollen to contend with, and the weather's playing a bit of a game. Stay alert for slick spots, watch for construction detours, and for the love of all that is holy, if you hear thunder, get inside.
