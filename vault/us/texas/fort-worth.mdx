## What to Watch Out For Around Fort Worth

That beautiful, clear sky? It’s a bit of a siren song today, especially with the ozone brewing. While the immediate threat of widespread flash flooding has subsided from earlier in the week, remember Fort Worth's storm drains, particularly around West Seventh, are notorious for backing up. We've had enough rain recently that some low-lying areas, especially near the Trinity Trails, could still be a little squishy or muddy, so watch your step for ankle-twisting surprises.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ozone Alert for Sensitive Groups:** An "Ozone Action Day" means that while the air might be generally okay for most, if you have asthma, COPD, or other respiratory issues, your lungs are going to work harder, especially from late morning through early evening. Consider dialing back the intensity or moving your workout indoors during peak afternoon hours. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

Construction is still Fort Worth's middle name, and that hasn't changed. While I didn't see any immediate trail *closures* reported in the last 24 hours specifically for trails, the city is a constant construction zone. Major street closures were ongoing earlier this week, like sections of N. Commerce Street. If your route takes you through downtown or rapidly developing areas, keep an eye out for unexpected detours, closed sidewalks, or heavy equipment. A quick check of the City of Fort Worth's road closures page before you head out is always smarter than trusting your usual route.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Key:** With temperatures hitting the mid-90s, even though it's clear, you'll be sweating. Don't skimp on the water, people. For every hour you're out there, aim for at least 16-20 ounces of water or an electrolyte drink. Heat exhaustion isn't a badge of honor; it's a trip to the ER.
</AlertBox>

Finally, there's a very low chance of isolated thunderstorms later today, primarily west of US-281. These pop-up storms can be sneaky and sometimes pack a punch with large hail or damaging winds. If the sky starts looking even a little bit angry, don't play hero. Head indoors.
