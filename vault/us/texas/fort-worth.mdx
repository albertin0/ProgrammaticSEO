Look, March 12th in Fort Worth isn't the worst day to hit the pavement or the dirt, but it's definitely not a set-it-and-forget-it kind of morning. The air quality is actually looking pretty good, with our AQI hovering at a respectable 42. No smog warnings, no weird industrial haze—just your standard North Texas air.

However, if you're one of the many Fort Worth allergy sufferers, you're probably already feeling that "moderate" pollen level in your sinuses. It's not sky-high, but it's enough to make you sniffle through those last few miles. If you planned on pushing hard, maybe pop an antihistamine first, or stick to a treadmill where the air is filtered and free of whatever evil tree dust is floating around today.

The real kicker today is the Trinity River. There was a flood warning issued for the Trinity River at Dallas that included an advisory that minor lowland flooding would start and affect trails, specifically expecting it to crest and then fall below flood stage by late morning today, March 12th. While that's technically Dallas County, our stretch of the Trinity River in Fort Worth isn't exactly immune to its upstream neighbor's water levels. So, if your usual route takes you along the Trinity Trails, particularly around Panther Island, be ready for some detours or a damp surprise. Don't assume that just because the sun's out, the paths are dry. Flooded trails aren't just muddy; they can hide debris and create unstable footing, which is a twisted ankle waiting to happen.

The temperature's a bit on the cooler side at 8.5°C (47.3°F) this morning, but it's forecast to warm up nicely to a pleasant 67°F later. Yesterday had some significant wind gusts, up to 40 mph, but today should see much lighter winds at 5-10 mph. Still, a cool breeze can make that initial warm-up feel colder than the thermometer suggests, so layer up. You can always shed that jacket as you go, but you can't conjure one out of thin air when you're shivering by the river.
