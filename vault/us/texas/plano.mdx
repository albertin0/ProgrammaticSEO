## Today's Plano Pointers: Don't Be That Guy (or Gal)

Let's be real, you live in Plano, so you know construction is a way of life, like traffic on the DNT at 5 PM. What you might not know is how it impacts your run.

### Construction Chaos Continues

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures on Legacy Trail:** Expect intermittent closures due to the North Texas Municipal Water District's major sewer force main replacement project. This gig runs between Sam Rayburn Tollway (SH 121) and south of Rasor Boulevard/Ohio Drive, and it's set to last through Summer 2026. Don't be surprised if your usual loop is suddenly a detour-fest. Check the city's official site for current maps before you lace up.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, trying to navigate a "closed" trail because you "just wanna see if you can get through" is how you end up twisted around an orange cone or, worse, face-planting into a freshly dug trench. Plan your route, people.

### Wet Weather Woes

While today is looking quieter, we've had some significant rain lately, and more is on the horizon. Yesterday saw flash flood warnings for parts of Collin and Denton counties, and localized flooding can still be an issue. The forecast for Thursday and the weekend is calling for more widespread showers and thunderstorms with heavy rainfall and increased flash flooding concerns.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Keep an eye on creek levels and trail conditions.** Even if it's not actively raining, trails, especially soft-surface ones like those at Oak Point Park, can be muddy and slippery from previous downpours. Rowlett Creek runs through there, and you don't want to become an impromptu swimmer. Paved trails can also hold standing water, so watch for slick spots.
</AlertBox>

We’ve all seen people try to power through flooded areas, and it never ends well. "Turn Around, Don't Drown" isn't just a catchy phrase for idiots on the news; it's sound advice. Be smart. Your PR isn't worth a trip to the ER.

### Pollen Protocol

Okay, "moderate" pollen might not sound like much, but for some, it's enough to make a perfectly good run miserable. Grass, oak, and osage orange are the main culprits right now. If you start wheezing like an old accordion, don't blame the ozone; it's actually looking pretty good today. Blame the trees.
