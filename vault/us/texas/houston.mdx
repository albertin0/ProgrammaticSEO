<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Unhealthy Air Quality!**
An Air Quality Alert is in effect for Houston and surrounding counties. An AQI of 103 (with PM2.5 as the main culprit) means that sensitive groups – like children, older adults, pregnant women, and those with heart or lung conditions – should *definitely* limit time outdoors. Even healthy individuals should reduce prolonged or heavy exertion. Fine particulate matter can get deep into your lungs and cause problems. This isn't just about feeling winded; it's about your long-term health. Seriously, reconsider that outdoor long run today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Humidity is a Health Hazard**
Houston's summer humidity isn't just uncomfortable; it's dangerous. Your sweat won't evaporate effectively, preventing your body from cooling down. This significantly increases your risk of heat exhaustion or heat stroke. If you absolutely *must* be outside, keep it short, easy, and in the early morning or late evening when the "feels like" temperature might be slightly less oppressive. Carry water, wear light, moisture-wicking clothing, and know the signs of heat illness (dizziness, nausea, weakness, headache).
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction & Closures: Plan Your Route**
If you were hoping for a serene spin along the White Oak Bayou Greenway, think again. Various sections are under construction, like the I-10 elevation project near Studemont, which is causing lane reductions and trail disruptions "until further notice." Additionally, there are intermittent closures near Shepherd for flood control work. Expect to navigate detours and potentially less safe alternatives if you're insistent on using these paths. Check Houston TranStar before you leave the house if your route involves major roadways or bayou trails.
</AlertBox>
