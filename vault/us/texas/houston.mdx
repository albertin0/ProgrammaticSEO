Alright, Houston, let's talk about hitting the trails today. You looked at the "Mainly Clear" forecast and thought, "Great, a perfect day!" And while, yes, the sun is out, it's a classic Houston bait-and-switch.

First off, your lungs are going to feel it. Our Air Quality Index is clocking in at 103, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. That's squarely in the "Moderate" range, meaning if you're sensitive, you might experience some respiratory symptoms. Add to that, the tree pollen counts have been at their spring peak this week, and with no significant rain to wash it away, it's just hanging in the air, waiting to assault your sinuses.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### AQI and Pollen Double Whammy
With a Moderate AQI and high tree pollen, consider scaling back your intensity or moving your workout indoors, especially if you have asthma or allergies. Your lungs and nasal passages will thank you.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the heat. It's March 25th, and we're seeing temperatures in the low to mid-80s, with predictions of hitting 90 degrees later this week. This isn't just "warm," it's "could set daily heat records" warm. Coupled with our delightful humidity, that "feels like" temperature is going to be a lot higher than what the thermometer says. Hydrate *before*, *during*, and *after* your run, and seriously, get out there early if you can.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Early Season Heat Stress
Don't underestimate the early season heat. Your body isn't fully acclimated yet. Wear light, breathable clothing, carry water, and listen to your body. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are no joke.
</AlertBox>

Now, for those of you who planned a nice run along the **MKT Trail** near White Oak Bayou, prepare for more frustration. The Hogan Street pedestrian bridge, a critical link that crosses White Oak Bayou and Studewood Street, is *still* closed. It's been out of commission since January 2025 due to damage from a rain event, and repairs were delayed again in February due to equipment issues. So, you're looking at detours, which means more interaction with Houston traffic and less uninterrupted trail time. It’s a pain, and frankly, a safety concern for runners and cyclists forced onto less-friendly routes.

<AlertBox type="info">
### MKT Trail Detours
If your route usually involves the Hogan Street pedestrian bridge on the MKT Trail, plan for alternative crossings. TxDOT is still waiting on equipment, pushing back the expected reopening further into spring 2026.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, no immediate flooding or severe wind warnings for today, which is something. But honestly, between the air you're breathing and the detours you're dodging, it's not exactly a pristine day for an outdoor PR. Be smart, stay safe, and maybe hit that treadmill or indoor gym if you're feeling extra sensitive.
