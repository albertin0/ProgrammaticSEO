It's a typical Houston "overcast" day, but don't let that fool you into thinking it's a perfect outdoor workout day. Far from it.

<LungsJointsScore score={4} />

<BulletList>
*   That "moderate" air quality is hiding a real gut punch for your lungs, thanks to fine particulate matter. Don't be a hero.
*   "Feels like" temperatures are hitting triple digits, even if the actual thermometer says otherwise. You know what that means: sweat and potential trouble.
*   Construction on our greenways is a perpetual Houston hazard, and it's particularly messing with the White Oak Bayou trails right now, and for years to come.
</BulletList>

Forget your romantic notions of a breezy run along Buffalo Bayou, unless you've got some heavy-duty lung filters. Today, our city's air quality is officially in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category with an Air Quality Index (AQI) of 103, driven by fine particulate matter (PM2.5). Yeah, "moderate" pollen is almost an afterthought when your lungs are essentially trying to filter tiny bits of industrial exhaust. If you've got asthma, COPD, or just a general aversion to feeling like you've smoked a pack after two miles, seriously reconsider your outdoor plans. Even healthy folks might feel that burning sensation.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**AQI is Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups!** With PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and an AQI of 103, anyone with respiratory issues, children, and older adults should absolutely limit time outdoors. Everyone else, be aware of symptoms like coughing or throat irritation and cut your workout short.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the weather. Sure, it's "overcast" at 24.7°C (about 76.5°F), but the "feels like" temperature is reportedly hitting a scorching 100°F. That's Houston summer in May, folks – the humidity is cranked up, making every stride feel like running through soup. Keep that hydration up, and for crying out loud, don't push for a personal record today. It's just not worth the heat stroke. While isolated showers are possible this morning, they won't be enough to truly cool down this concrete jungle.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Triple-Digit "Feels Like" Temps!** Don't trust the thermometer. High humidity makes it feel significantly hotter, pushing heat stress risks. Hydrate constantly and listen to your body – this isn't the day to go all out.
</AlertBox>

And because it's Houston, there's always construction. If you were planning a peaceful long run on the **White Oak Bayou Greenway**, specifically the Little White Oak Bayou Trail, near the I-45/I-10 interchange, think again. That section, between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, is completely shut down for ongoing connector ramp work and is expected to stay that way through *2028*. Yes, 2028. There are detours, but they often involve surface streets, which are exactly what you're trying to avoid on a run or bike. The MKT Trail Hogan Pedestrian Bridge, while likely reopened after its own rehabilitation saga, sits right in that messy zone, with the trails beneath it still impacted by the larger freeway project. Factor in extra time, and probably extra frustration, if you're trying to navigate that area on foot or bike. Other major thoroughfares like I-10 West are also seeing lane reductions due to significant reconstruction projects expected to continue until mid-2026.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**White Oak Bayou Greenway Closure:** The Little White Oak Bayou Trail is closed near the I-45/I-10 interchange until *2028*. Expect significant detours, likely onto less-friendly surface streets. Plan your routes carefully or pick another trail.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Plan Your Indoor Backup (or Just Chill):** With poor air quality and high heat stress, maybe today's the day for some cross-training indoors, or honestly, just take a rest day. Your body will thank you.
</AlertBox>
