Look, I get it. We're runners. We're tough. But even tough runners don't go out during a thunderstorm. The environmental data straight up says "Thunderstorm," and while some other forecasts might try to tell you it's "mostly sunny" for June 30th, the *actual* conditions are volatile, and frankly, dangerous. Just last week, we saw heavy rain causing significant flooding and even sewage backups in areas like Candlelight Oaks. Do you really want to risk running through that? No, you don't. It's not just a nuisance; it's a health hazard.

### Construction Chaos Continues

If the weather wasn't enough to deter you, the ongoing construction certainly should. Beyond the White Oak Bayou Greenway's daytime closure near N. Shepherd, which runs for at least three months, the Brays Bayou Trail is still impacted by the SH 35 expansion. And let's not even start on the various bike lanes getting demolished or closed for "drainage projects" like the Austin Street Bike Lane from Elgin to Gray. Trying to navigate these closures, especially in a downpour, is a recipe for a twisted ankle or worse.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DO NOT RUN OUTSIDE DURING A THUNDERSTORM.** Lightning kills. Flash floods are incredibly dangerous. If you see lightning or hear thunder, get inside immediately. Roads and trails can flood rapidly, and standing water often hides hazards like debris, open manholes, or raw sewage.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**CHECK LOCAL CLOSURES BEFORE YOU HEAD OUT.** Multiple trails and bike lanes, including sections of the White Oak Bayou Greenway and Brays Bayou Trail, are impacted by long-term construction projects. Don't assume your usual route is open.
</AlertBox>
