Today's a classic Houston stew of "don't push it" factors. We've got a "Mainly Clear" sky but don't let that fool you into thinking it's pristine out there. The Air Quality Index (AQI) is sitting at a rather unwelcome 103, which is categorized as "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups". The main culprit? PM2.5, those tiny particulate matter nasties that dig deep into your respiratory system and can cause all sorts of cardiovascular headaches. If you've got asthma, heart conditions, or just value your lung capacity, today is not the day for heroics. Even healthy folks might feel it if you're out there too long.

Then there's the heat. While March sometimes teases us with cool fronts, today's 28.2°C is just plain warm. Add in the humidity that's usually hanging around Houston, and you're looking at a recipe for rapid dehydration and overheating, especially when your body's already working overtime to filter questionable air.

## Trail Troubles: Still an Obstacle Course

If you're planning on hitting some of the city's greenways, prepare for diversions. That damn pedestrian bridge along the MKT Trail in the Heights, near White Oak Bayou and Studewood Street, is *still* closed. It's been out of commission since January 2025 due to structural damage, and repairs are continually delayed, with the latest holdup being a missing crane as of late February. This means if you rely on that crossing, you're getting shunted onto roads designed for cars, not runners or cyclists. It's a pain and frankly, a bit dangerous.

And it's not just the Heights. A section of the Buffalo Bayou Trail on the north side, near Johnny Goyen Park, is also temporarily closed. They're doing some work on a retaining wall and slope to improve visibility, which is good in the long run, but right now, it's another detour pushing you into traffic. Plus, don't forget the White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail) between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center. That stretch is closed until *2028* for IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work. So, yeah, plan your routes carefully, or just stick to the treadmill.
