## Houston: Hot, Hazy, and Under Construction

Look, I love hitting the pavement as much as the next runner, but today is not the day to push your limits. We're staring down a March 26th that feels more like early summer, with temperatures hitting a predicted 86°F (30°C) and the kind of humidity that makes you feel like you're breathing soup. The NWS has been forecasting highs in the mid-to-upper 80s all week, and our nights aren't cooling down much either, which means your body doesn't get a break. That's a recipe for quicker dehydration and heat fatigue.

### Air Quality & Allergy Alert

Forget what your fitness tracker says about "optimal" conditions. Our Air Quality Index is sitting at 103, which is "Moderate," but the dominant pollutant is PM2.5. This fine particulate matter can irritate your respiratory system, especially if you're out there huffing and puffing. And for those of us who experience Houston allergies as a seasonal sport, today's tree pollen levels are "Very High." Specifically, Oak pollen is having a field day, along with Hackberry and Ash. If you're prone to seasonal sniffles, a watery-eyed jog down the **Buffalo Bayou Park** trails might just turn into a full-blown snot fest.

### Trail Troubles: Construction Chaos Continues

As if the air and heat weren't enough, our beloved city is still in a perpetual state of construction. If your route takes you anywhere near the downtown area, be aware that the **White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail)** between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is still closed for the IH-45 southbound to I-10 westbound connector ramp work. This isn't a quick fix; the closure is expected to last through 2028, so plan your detours carefully. And don't forget the ongoing I-10 White Oak Bayou elevation project, which continues to cause lane reductions and delays. It's enough to make you just want to stay home.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction Navigation:** If you *must* run outside, check Houston TranStar or Houston Bike Guide for the latest on trail and road closures. Better yet, stick to well-known, open paths and be prepared for unexpected detours. Assume any path less traveled might be a construction zone.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, or just stay inside. Your lungs and joints will thank you.
