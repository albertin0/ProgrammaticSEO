### Air Quality Alert: Breathe at Your Own Risk

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) has been tracking elevated particulate matter in the air, with Saharan dust plumes and residual smoke from New Mexico wildfires possibly filtering into parts of Texas. While not every particle is local, your lungs don't care about its passport. An AQI of 103 means that if you've got asthma, emphysema, or just lungs that prefer clean air, today is not your day to set a PR on the Buffalo Bayou Greenway. Even healthy individuals might experience some irritation with prolonged exposure.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**HIGH PM2.5 Levels:** Today's dominant pollutant is PM2.5, which means tiny particles are getting deep into your lungs. Sensitive groups (kids, elderly, anyone with respiratory issues) should seriously limit outdoor exertion. Maybe just... don't.
</AlertBox>
