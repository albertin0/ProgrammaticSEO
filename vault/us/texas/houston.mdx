<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Heat Stroke is a Real Threat, Even Early in the Day**

Look, it's August in Houston. The reported 27.1°C (80.8°F) sounds tolerable, but with 91% humidity, it *feels* like 31°C (87°F) right now. By afternoon, the heat index is forecasted to hit a brutal 103-107°F (40-41°C) inland. If you're out in direct sunlight, Houston Methodist doctors say it can feel another 10-15 degrees hotter. Your body just can't cool itself effectively when the air is this saturated. This is how people end up in the ER, so don't push it. Even the UIL for high school sports is mandating modifications or cancellations when the Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) gets too high, and Houston's August average is in that danger zone.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Don't Forget About Your Lungs (and Nose)**

While the AQI is "only" 79 today, putting it in the moderate category, the dominant pollutant is PM2.5. This fine particulate matter is what gets deep into your lungs. Houston and Harris County have a history of struggling with PM2.5 levels, and a federal judge just upheld stricter standards because our air quality has been consistently above safe limits. Add to that the moderate pollen counts, and anyone with allergies or asthma needs to be extra cautious. If you're prone to respiratory issues, today is not the day for an intense, outdoor sprint session.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Flash Flood Roulette in Effect**

Parts of north Harris County were under a flood advisory last night and this morning due to heavy rains. The forecast for today includes a 75% chance of rain, with isolated afternoon thunderstorms that could bring torrential rainfall. We're talking localized flooding in a flash. You know the drill: if you see water over the road, turn around. It's not worth risking your life or your car. And don't even think about running through it; who knows what's lurking beneath that murky water.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Trail Troubles Continue**

If you had plans for a long run on some of our beloved greenways, you might need a Plan B. The Brays Bayou Trail is still closed at Spur 5 for the SH 35 expansion, a project that seems to be stretching into eternity. And the White Oak Bayou Greenway has multiple headaches: intermittent closures near Shepherd daily until 5:30 p.m. for flood control work (with no clear detour, mind you!), and a longer-term closure between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center for I-45/I-10 connector work. These aren't minor detours; they can seriously disrupt your route, often forcing you onto less safe streets.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Your PT's Unsolicited Advice (But Seriously, Listen)**

*   **Hydrate like it's your job.** You should have started yesterday. Water, electrolytes – not just during, but before and after.
*   **Move it indoors.** Seriously. Hit the treadmill, the pool, anything with AC. Your body will thank you.
*   **If you *must* be outside:** Go before 7 AM. Stick to shaded routes. Keep it short. Wear light, loose-fitting clothing. And carry your phone so someone can call for help when you inevitably bonk.
*   **Listen to your body.** Muscle cramps, dizziness, nausea, headache? Stop. Immediately. Find shade, get water, cool down. Heat illness can escalate fast.
</AlertBox>
