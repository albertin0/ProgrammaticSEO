## Today's Workout Hazards in H-Town

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Seriously, Your Lungs Are Under Attack Today
It's an **Ozone Action Day** for the Houston, Galveston, and Brazoria areas. This means the air is brewing a cocktail of pollutants, and high levels of ozone are expected. Our AQI is sitting at 103 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, putting us firmly in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category. Even if you're a seasoned runner, pushing yourself today could lead to respiratory irritation, shortness of breath, and a general feeling of crud. Kids, the elderly, and anyone with existing respiratory issues? Stay inside, keep that AC running, and don't even think about it. The air quality is forecast to be "very poor" overall, with pollution potentially peaking this afternoon.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Muck: Construction and Event Detours
Your favorite trails might be a no-go, or at least a pain in the asphalt. The massive **I-10 White Oak Bayou elevation project** means long-term lane closures and trail impacts around the Studemont Street area, and that mess isn't clearing up until 2028. If you're near Buffalo Bayou East, specifically around **Tony Marron Park**, you'll find it closed for a significant expansion. Construction for Marron Park Way is also ongoing, affecting accessibility.

And forget a peaceful run through parts of **Memorial Park**. The Bayou City Art Festival has the Picnic Loop shut down until October 12th, and several parking lots are closed to the public through the weekend. Plus, the western Outer Loop Trail is still closed until 2027. If you head to Mason Park this morning, expect crowds for the Barrio Dogs Tails & Trails event. Same deal at Sims Bayou Park for the Breast Cancer Walk. You'll be spending more time navigating crowds or detours than actually working out.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### If You *Must* Go Out...
Temperatures are sitting at a warm 28.2°C (82.8°F) and it's mainly clear. Hydrate like it's your job, even if you're just walking. If you decide to brave the outdoors, stick to lighter activities and shaded areas. Pay attention to your body – any irritation, dizziness, or unusual fatigue is a sign to call it a day and head back indoors. Seriously, don't be a hero, no one's giving out medals for lung damage.
</AlertBox>
