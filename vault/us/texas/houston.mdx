### Air Quality: Don't Breathe Too Deeply

Today's AQI is sitting at a grumpy 103, with fine particulate matter (PM2.5) being the main culprit. That's not terrible for everyone, but if you've got asthma, allergies, or any kind of lung issue, or you're just generally sensitive to bad air, you should seriously reconsider that long run. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) even forecasts PM2.5 to be at the "lower end of the 'Moderate' range" for Houston today, which matches our "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" reality. AccuWeather goes as far as to say the air quality is "Poor" and "unhealthy for sensitive groups," recommending reduced time outside if you're feeling symptoms.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**AQI 103: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups!** Keep your high-intensity outdoor workouts to a minimum, especially if you're usually affected by poor air quality. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

### Weather: Houston Summer Being Houston Summer

Yeah, it's 26.3°C (around 79°F) and technically clear or mostly cloudy in the morning, but let's be real – it *feels* a whole lot hotter. We're looking at "feels like" temperatures of 31-32°C (87-90°F) because the humidity is still hanging around like an unwelcome guest. While the "Extreme Heat Warning" from earlier in the week has passed, the general forecast for the weekend was "hot with highs in the mid to upper 90s" and "dangerous heat continues across the Houston area." That Gulf Coast humidity is no joke and can turn a good workout into a dangerous situation fast.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Humidity Hangover:** Even though the temperature might look okay on paper, the high humidity means your body struggles to cool itself. Expect to sweat profusely, and watch out for signs of heat exhaustion. Stay hydrated, seriously.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Conditions: Still a Mess in Places

Remember Tropical Storm Bertha? While the immediate flood risk has "ended" for Houston as of yesterday, some areas might still be waterlogged or have debris. More importantly, the city's perpetual state of construction means many of our best running paths are still shut down. The **Brays Bayou Trail** is still closed at Spur 5 due to the SH 35 expansion. And if you usually hit the **Buffalo Bayou Trail** near downtown, expect detours as it's been closed since January to fix a blind spot. Don't even get me started on the **White Oak Bayou Greenway** near Houston Avenue, which is basically a construction zone through 2028 for the IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Plan Your Route (Again):** Check Houston TranStar for specific road closures and Houston Bike Guide or Houston Parks Board for trail updates before you head out. These closures aren't going away anytime soon, so get used to finding alternative routes.
</AlertBox>
