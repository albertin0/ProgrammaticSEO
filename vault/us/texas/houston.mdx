Look, I get it. You've got that marathon on the calendar, or maybe you just genuinely enjoy stewing in your own juices while running. But today, July 28, 2026, is not the day to push it outside. The weather folks are calling for actual temperatures around 100°F (37.8°C), but it's the *heat index* — that lovely combination of heat and our infamous Gulf Coast humidity — that's going to make it feel like 108-110°F. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous, especially if you're out there during peak sun hours (noon to 7 p.m., per the advisory). Your body's ability to cool itself is severely compromised in these conditions, and heat exhaustion can quickly turn into heat stroke. Don't mess with that.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### **Extreme Heat Advisory in Effect**
A Heat Advisory has been issued for the Houston area today. Expect "feels like" temperatures up to 110°F. Hydration is not enough; seek air conditioning, reduce activity, and listen to your body.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. AQI 103 means it's unhealthy for sensitive groups, and that "sensitive" category is broader than you think. Anyone with asthma, respiratory issues, or even just working harder than usual is at risk. PM2.5, the dominant pollutant, consists of tiny particles that get deep into your lungs, and guess what? Increased breathing during exercise means you're inhaling more of it. So while you're battling the heat, you're also giving your respiratory system a nice dose of crud.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### **Unhealthy Air Quality for Sensitive Groups**
With an AQI of 103 and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, today's air quality is not ideal for sustained outdoor exercise, particularly for those with respiratory conditions. Consider indoor alternatives.
</AlertBox>

And because Houston loves to keep things interesting, your usual routes might be a minefield of orange cones. The Brays Bayou Trail, for instance, remains impacted by the ongoing SH 35 expansion at Spur 5, which frankly, feels like it's been going on since the dinosaurs roamed this city. If you're planning on a run or ride along the White Oak Bayou Greenway, be aware there are intermittent daily closures near Shepherd due to flood control work, which can last for months. Nothing like a surprise detour into traffic when you're already feeling like a grilled cheese sandwich.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### **Trail Updates & Construction**
Always check local city/county parks and recreation sites for real-time trail closures, especially around construction zones. Major projects like the Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 and intermittent closures on the White Oak Bayou Greenway can derail your workout.

Seriously, find an indoor treadmill, hit the gym, or maybe just do some burpees in your air-conditioned living room. Your lungs, joints, and sanity will thank you.
