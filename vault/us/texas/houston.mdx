## The Air You Breathe

Let's not sugarcoat it. An AQI of 103 is "Moderate," but it means the air quality is acceptable, though for some pollutants there may be a moderate health concern for a very small number of people who are unusually sensitive to air pollution. PM2.5, our dominant pollutant today, is that fine particulate matter that really digs deep into your lungs. If you've got asthma, COPD, or are just prone to a cough, seriously reconsider your long runs or push it indoors. The Houston Health Department issues "Asthma Air Aware Day Warnings" for conditions like these for a reason.

## On the Ground: Hazards & Headaches

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Chaos at Memorial Park:** The Memorial Groves project is still a beast, keeping portions of the Outer Loop Trail out of commission at Memorial Park until late 2027. That means heavier traffic on the Seymour Lieberman Trail. Watch your step, watch for others, and know that some benches and fitness stations might be inaccessible.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**White Oak Bayou Greenway Intermittent Closures:** If you're on the White Oak Bayou Greenway near N Shepherd, expect disruptions. They're doing flood control work, which is important, but they're closing sections daily from 7 AM to 5:30 PM for about six months with no detour signs. Plan for impromptu reroutes onto less-than-ideal street connections.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is NOT Optional:** With that muggy air and temperatures pushing into the low 80s, you're going to sweat. A lot. Even under overcast skies, dehydration is a real threat. Carry more water than you think you need, especially on those longer hauls. Don't be that person collapsing on the Buffalo Bayou Trail because you thought a small water bottle would cut it.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Heads Up for Tomorrow (Election Day):** Today might be okay, but a cold front is rolling in for Election Day, bringing a high chance of showers and thunderstorms. We're talking potentially heavy rain, strong winds, hail, and a Level 1 out of 4 risk for excessive rainfall and flooding in Southeast Texas. If you have outdoor plans for Tuesday, seriously, *seriously* reconsider them. Slow-moving downpours could leave water standing on roads and in low-lying areas, making some spots impassable.
</AlertBox>

Look, I get it. You want to train. But today isn't exactly prime conditions. The air is iffy, it's sticky, and good luck finding a clear stretch of trail without a detour or a new barricade. Maybe hit the gym, or save your big efforts for Wednesday when cooler, drier air is expected to finally settle in.
