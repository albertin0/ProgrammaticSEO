## The Air is Thick, Literally

Let's talk about that air quality. Forget "mainly clear" skies—what you can't see is the real problem. Houston's AQI is hitting 103, predominantly from fine particulate matter (PM2.5). This isn't just a sniffle-and-sneeze kind of day; this stuff gets deep into your lungs and bloodstream, potentially causing serious health issues, especially if you're pushing your cardiovascular system. If you've been wondering why things feel a bit hazy or your throat's scratchy, it's the pollution. The fallout from that warehouse blaze in Southeast Houston earlier this week, spewing "heavy metals and toxic combustion byproducts into the air," certainly isn't helping matters.

## Weather Incoming: Get Your Workout Done *Early* (If at All)

While it's currently clear, the nice weather is a trap. Temperatures are already warm, hovering around 28.2°C, and Friday saw highs up to 84°F with gusty winds. The real kicker for today, Saturday, April 4th, is the incoming cold front. Rain chances are climbing this afternoon and evening, and we're looking at widespread showers and thunderstorms, likely between 3 PM and 11 PM. These aren't just sprinkles; meteorologists are warning of "damaging wind gusts approaching 60 mph and hail at least an inch wide," plus "localized pockets of flooding, particularly in areas that are flood-prone or have poor drainage." There's a street flooding threat for all of Southeast Texas.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Severe Weather Incoming!** Do NOT plan outdoor workouts for late afternoon or evening. Damaging winds and localized flooding are real threats. "Strong north winds" are also expected to kick up on Sunday, making things even more unpleasant.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Troubles: Construction Never Sleeps

Beyond the weather and air, don't forget the constant headache of Houston construction. If you usually run or bike along the **White Oak Bayou Greenway**, be aware that the section between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center has been closed since January 2026 and will remain shut down through 2028 due to I-45/I-10 connector ramp work. There are detours, but they add time and complexity. Similarly, the MKT Trail Hogan Pedestrian Bridge over White Oak Bayou is undergoing rehabilitation, with some sources saying it should reopen in spring 2026, meaning it's likely still inaccessible. This means navigating street traffic, which is always less safe for runners.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Construction Closures on Bayou Trails.** The White Oak Bayou Greenway near downtown and the MKT Trail Hogan Pedestrian Bridge are impacted by long-term construction. Plan alternative routes or stick to paved roads with sidewalks.
</AlertBox>
