## The Air is Thick (and not with opportunity)

Look, I love our city's trails as much as the next runner, but the Air Quality Index clocking in at 103 for PM2.5 is a serious buzzkill for your respiratory system. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality even predicted "Moderate" PM2.5 for our area today, likely from a mix of local gunk and some less-than-fresh air drifting in from elsewhere. Those fine particulates are sneaky, getting deep into your lungs and causing trouble. If you've got asthma, allergies, or any other lung issues, today is absolutely *not* the day to test your limits. Honestly, even if you feel like a ironman, why willingly inhale that crud?

<AlertBox type="danger">
### AQI: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (103 PM2.5)
Limit prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors. Sensitive individuals should avoid outdoor activity entirely. This isn't just about feeling a little winded; it's about long-term lung health.
</AlertBox>

## Soggy Trails and Persistent Closures

Remember that rain yesterday? Yeah, it saturated the ground pretty well, and while the major bayous held their own, expect plenty of muddy sections and potential standing water, especially on trails near creeks and low-lying areas. Keep an eye out for street flooding if you're navigating urban routes.

And speaking of navigation, our trails continue to be a minefield of construction. The **White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail)** between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is a no-go for the long haul, closed through 2028 for IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work. There are detours, but frankly, who needs the extra headache of trying to figure out where to run when you're already dealing with mediocre air? Also, keep in mind Fonde Park is currently closed until June 15th for trail resurfacing.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Watch for Wet Trails & Detours
Between yesterday's rain and ongoing construction, many trails are either muddy, slippery, or completely closed. The long-term closure on the White Oak Bayou Greenway is still active. Plan your routes carefully, or better yet, opt for a treadmill session.
</AlertBox>

## Pollen & Heat Check

Pollen is holding steady at a "Moderate" level. Combine that with the PM2.5, and your sinuses might stage a full-blown rebellion. Our temperature today is a warm 28.2°C (about 82.8°F), and while it's not "extreme heat advisory" hot, Houston's humidity is always a factor. Doctors regularly warn about how brutal our summer humidity can be, making even moderate temperatures feel much worse and putting extra strain on your body. Don't get complacent just because the thermometer isn't redlining.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate & Listen to Your Body
If you absolutely *must* run outside, do it early, stay hydrated, and wear light, loose clothing. Pay attention to how your body feels. With the air quality and humidity, overheating can happen faster than you think.
</AlertBox>
