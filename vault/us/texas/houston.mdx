### Mind Your Lungs, Houston!
While an AQI of 60 is technically "acceptable for most individuals," it's worth noting that sensitive groups might still experience "minor to moderate symptoms from long-term exposure". Given the PM2.5 dominance, if you're asthmatic or have other breathing difficulties, consider keeping your run shorter or moving it indoors. That stuff gets *everywhere* in your system.

## Trail Closures & Construction Chaos

Alright, let's talk about the real joy killer for Houston runners and cyclists: the never-ending construction. If your route relies on the White Oak Bayou Greenway near downtown, prepare for a long-term detour. The segment between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center has been closed since January 2026 and is expected to stay that way, with only intermittent reopenings, all the way through 2028, thanks to the I-10 Elevation Project. Seriously, 2028. You could run a full marathon season in the time this thing is shut down.

And it's not just White Oak. The Buffalo Bayou Trail near downtown is also closed "to fix a blind spot" since January 31, 2026, with maps and detour info supposedly available. Then there's MacGregor Park, where portions, including sections of the trail, have been closed for renovations since August 2026. Don't even get me started on the continuous downtown street closures for various projects. You'll spend half your workout trying to figure out which concrete barrier means "go home" and which means "scoot around."

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Navigate With Caution!
These aren't minor inconveniences. The White Oak Bayou Greenway closure between Houston Ave and Castillo Community Center is a *multi-year* thing. If you're commuting or training, you're going to need a plan B, C, and probably D. Detours often push you onto less safe, more crowded sidewalks or streets. Stay vigilant and be aware of traffic if you find yourself rerouted onto roads.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate, You Sweaty Beast
With temperatures feeling like 29°C (85°F) and those humidity levels, heat-related illness is always a risk. The Houston Health Department and OEM regularly warn about it in the summer and even into the fall. Drink way more water than you think you need, wear light clothing, and if you start feeling dizzy or nauseous, stop. Now. No PR is worth heatstroke.
</AlertBox>
