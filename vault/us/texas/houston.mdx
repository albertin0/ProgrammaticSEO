## The Air: A Houstonian's Bane

Look, I'm not a corporate wellness blogger telling you to "embrace the grind" when the air itself is trying to send you home. Houston's air quality today, with an AQI of 103 and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, means it's unhealthy for anyone with existing respiratory issues, young children, or the elderly. This isn't just some abstract number; fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is tiny enough to get deep into your lungs and even bloodstream, aggravating asthma and causing breathing problems. We've got a history of industrial pollution in Harris County, and while the city works on long-term solutions, today, you're breathing it.

Add to that the "moderate" pollen levels, and if you’re one of the many Houstonians who feels like "moderate" is a lie when oak pollen is going full tilt, you're not wrong. Expect itchy eyes, sniffles, and that general feeling of being unwell if you push it too hard outside.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **AQI 103 is Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups!** If you have asthma, COPD, heart disease, or are otherwise sensitive, reconsider strenuous outdoor activity. Everyone else, take it easy and watch for symptoms like coughing or shortness of breath.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Avoid White Oak Bayou (Partially)

If you were planning a run along the White Oak Bayou Greenway, particularly the Little White Oak Bayou Trail section, you're going to hit a wall – literally. The segment between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center has been closed since January 15, 2026, and it's not expected to reopen until 2028 due to ongoing work on the I-45/I-10 connector ramps. There's a detour, sure, but do you really want to navigate construction zones with this air and humidity? Probably not.

Meanwhile, a heads up for later this month: the Texas Children's Houston Open will severely impact access and parking at Memorial Park from March 26-29. Maybe hit up Hermann Park or the Rice University Loop for your long runs instead.
