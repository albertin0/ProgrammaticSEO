## The Air You Breathe (Or Don't)

Look, that 22.9°C temperature is a dream for September in Houston. But don't let the clear skies fool you into thinking it's all sunshine and PRs. Our Air Quality Index is sitting at a solid 103 right now, with fine particulate matter (PM2.5) being the dominant pollutant. For anyone with asthma, allergies, or just a general desire to *not* hack up a lung, that's firmly in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" territory. You're trying to improve your cardio, not test your lung's ability to filter urban grime.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**AQI 103: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups!** If you're a "sensitive group" (and many runners are, whether they admit it or not), consider taking your workout indoors today. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

And as if PM2.5 wasn't enough, we've got moderate pollen levels floating around too. So, your sinuses get to join the party with your lungs. Good times.

## Trail Trouble & Detour Drama

It wouldn't be Houston if a few of our beloved greenways weren't undergoing some "improvements" or just plain shut down. The Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 remains a no-go, a victim of the endless SH 35 expansion. Plan your longer runs with alternative routes, because that project is still holding runners hostage for the foreseeable future.

Over on the White Oak Bayou Greenway, things are perpetually in flux. While some long-standing bridge repairs (like the Studewood bridge) are thankfully done, and flood control work near Shepherd *might* be wrapping up this month, other sections remain intermittently closed for concrete panel replacement until November. And don't forget the I-10 White Oak Bayou elevation project still causing headaches with lane reductions and ongoing bridge demolition near Studemont Street. Always check the Houston Bike Guide or TranStar for the latest before you head out, unless you enjoy surprise detours onto busy streets with zero shoulder.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Check Before You Roll/Run:** Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 is a definite no-go. White Oak Bayou Greenway also has intermittent closures near Shepherd through November due to flood control work. Don't assume your usual route is clear!
</AlertBox>

On a slightly brighter note, Memorial Park had some "Wellness Walks" yesterday, and Buffalo Bayou Park has a "Nature Walking Tour" today, so if you're sticking to well-maintained main loops, you should be fine, just watch for increased foot traffic from those events. Grimes Park, however, is permanently closed as of September 1st for HISD development. Another one bites the dust.
