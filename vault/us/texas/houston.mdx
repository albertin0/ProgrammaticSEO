If you're looking to pound the pavement outside today, just don't. Seriously, don't.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
  - Today is a wash-out, literally. We're under a Flood Watch, and the forecast has us swimming through rounds of heavy rain and scattered thunderstorms, courtesy of some charming tropical moisture hanging out in the Gulf. Street flooding is already a problem, and it's only going to get worse.
  - Houston's air quality is officially "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" with an AQI of 103 and PM2.5 leading the charge. Layer that on top of moderate pollen, and your lungs will feel like you're running through a dusty swamp.
  - The combination of ongoing heavy rain, high humidity, and potential tropical storm conditions means treacherous footing, poor visibility, and a significant risk of heat exhaustion if you're out there pushing it.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We runners are a stubborn bunch. We think we can outsmart the weather, outrun the pollen, and hop over any construction detour thrown our way. But today, June 17, 2026, Houston is actively trying to take you down. And not in a "good workout" kind of way.

We've got a Flood Watch continuing across Southeast Texas, with periods of heavy showers and storms that could lead to rapid street flooding, especially in low-lying areas. There's even a Tropical Storm Watch for coastal counties, bringing strong winds and more rain. This isn't just about getting a little wet; it's about being swept away or getting stuck in high water. Even if you don't drown, navigating flooded streets significantly increases your risk of joint injuries from unseen potholes or debris.

And while the official forecast might hint at "Mainly Clear" later, don't let that fool you. The current reality on the ground, and what the local news is reporting, is persistent heavy rain and scattered thunderstorms throughout the day. Combine that with our infamous humidity – which doctors at Houston Methodist have warned can be *more* dangerous than heat alone because it prevents your body from cooling itself efficiently – and you're setting yourself up for a bad time. Even at 28°C (around 82°F), high humidity makes it feel much hotter and puts extra strain on your cardiovascular system.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Immediate Flash Flood Risk:** Roads and trails, including sections around the White Oak Bayou Greenway, are highly susceptible to flash flooding. Avoid underpasses, low-lying areas, and any street with standing water. "Turn around, don't drown" isn't just a catchy phrase, it's how you stay alive.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Air Quality Alert:** With an AQI of 103, the air is unhealthy for sensitive groups. This means if you have asthma, allergies, or any respiratory issues, you should seriously consider keeping your workout indoors. Even if you don't, expect some lung irritation.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Trail Closures:** If you *must* venture out, be aware that several popular trails are currently impacted by long-term construction. The White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail) between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is closed until 2028. Pay attention to detour signage; don't just blast through orange fencing.
</AlertBox>
