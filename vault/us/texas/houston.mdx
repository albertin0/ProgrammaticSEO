## Today's Vibe: Hot, Humid, and Hazy, with a Side of Orange Cones

Look, it's August in Houston. Did you *really* expect a crisp, cool breeze? Today's going to feel like you're running through a hot, wet blanket. We're looking at temperatures around 26-31°C (79-87°F), but factor in the humidity, and it'll feel more like 31-32°C (88-90°F) out there. Sensitive folks, especially those with respiratory issues, might find the Air Quality Index (AQI) of 60, with ozone being a dominant pollutant, makes things a bit tougher. It's listed as "Fair" for good reason; don't push it if you feel off.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Be a Hero in This Heat

The "feels like" temperature is the real enemy here. When your sweat can't evaporate, your body struggles to cool down. You're already losing electrolytes and minerals, not just water. Hydrate *before* you're thirsty, wear loose, moisture-wicking gear, and seriously consider cutting your workout short or moving it indoors if you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or get muscle cramps. Houston Methodist doctors aren't kidding when they say humidity can be more dangerous than heat alone.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Trouble: More Closures Than a Saturday Morning Donut Shop

If you're planning a long run or ride on the greenways, be prepared for detours. The **Brays Bayou Trail** is still a mess down by Spur 5, thanks to that never-ending SH 35 expansion. They're saying 2026 for completion "if we're lucky," so don't hold your breath for a clear path there anytime soon.

Meanwhile, the **White Oak Bayou Greenway** near Shepherd is facing intermittent closures daily from 7 AM to 5:30 PM for a solid six months due to flood control work. Good luck finding a clearly marked detour, because apparently, that's optional. And forget about cruising through Midtown on Alabama Street; that bridge is closed for demolition until 2027. So, if you're out there, bring your patience and maybe scope out your route on a map *before* you head out.

Always have an alternative route in mind when you're dealing with Houston's perpetual construction zones.
