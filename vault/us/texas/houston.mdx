Forget your PRs, folks. Today is a prime example of why running in Houston requires more than just decent shoes; it requires a strong will to ignore common sense. While the sky might be "mainly clear," it's a deceptive clarity. What's clear is that the air isn't doing your lungs any favors, and the heat? It's downright oppressive. You're not going to "crush it" out there, you're going to get crushed *by* it.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Index Warning
With forecasted heat index values climbing to a brutal 105-107 degrees Fahrenheit, heatstroke and exhaustion are very real risks. Hydrate aggressively *before* you step outside, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, don't push it. This isn't a badge of honor; it's a fast track to the ER.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. A delightful 103 AQI means if you have asthma, are elderly, or just generally enjoy breathing, today is not your day to test those limits. The dominant pollutant is PM2.5, which is that fine particulate matter that goes straight to your lungs and hangs out. If you *must* go out, keep it short, easy, and maybe wear a mask, though frankly, you're just putting a band-aid on a gaping wound.

Speaking of gaping wounds, our trail system continues to be a patchwork of closures. Don't even think about a smooth, uninterrupted long run on the **White Oak Bayou Greenway** if you're headed downtown. That segment between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center? Closed until *2028* for I-45/I-10 interchange work. Yes, two thousand twenty-eight. Plan your detours, people, or just accept you'll be doing laps around a smaller section. And if MacGregor Park is your usual stomping ground, know that large sections, including parts of the trail, have been closed for renovations since mid-August.
