## What's Messing With Your Outdoor Plans Today

Let's cut to the chase: Today is not your day for a PR, or even a particularly pleasant long run. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) forecast has Houston's PM2.5 levels hanging out in the "Moderate" to "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" range. AccuWeather straight up calls our air quality "Poor" for today, meaning if you've got asthma, emphysema, or just generally enjoy breathing, you should seriously reconsider that outdoor session. That haze isn't just humidity, folks; it's residual smoke and aerosols drifting in from, well, everywhere south of us. Coupled with those infamous light Houston winds, it's just stagnating.

Then there's the pollen, a consistent thorn in our side this time of year. While not "high," moderate levels are still enough to tickle your sinuses into a full-blown attack, especially when you're sucking in lungfuls of that questionable air quality. So, you're looking at a double-whammy: tiny particulates irritating your lungs *and* allergens making your eyes water and nose run. Good times.

### Houston's Never-Ending Construction Saga

As if the air wasn't enough, navigating Houston's trails right now is like playing a frustrating game of "Guess the Detour." The **Brays Bayou Trail**, for instance, is still closed at Spur 5 due to the never-ending SH 35 expansion. That's a major greenway trail, folks, not some tiny side path. You want to run from Hermann Park towards UH? Plan on hitting a wall or getting very, very lost.

And it's not just Brays. The White Oak Bayou Greenway (specifically the Little White Oak Bayou Trail) is a no-go between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, shut down through 2028 for the I-45/I-10 connector project. Even Herman Park Drive is closed from today through May 22nd. So, good luck trying to get a scenic loop in. This city is constantly under construction, which is great for future infrastructure, but a royal pain in the ass for your present workout plans.
