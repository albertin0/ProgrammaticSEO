## The Rundown: Don't Be a Fair-Weather Fool

Look, I get it. The sun's out, the temperature is sitting pretty at 20.1°C (about 68°F), and it *feels* like a perfect spring day for a run or ride. But this is Houston, and we're still shaking off yesterday's deluge. Central Houston saw some flash flooding with a Stage 2 alert, and while those storms have mostly moved on, the ground is saturated. We're still under a Flood Watch, particularly for those counties hugging the coast, so expect creeks and low-lying areas to be holding onto some serious water. Forget about hitting that Strava PR on any iffy terrain. Mud, standing water, and erosion are your enemies today.

### Air & Allergies: Breathe Easy, ish.

Your lungs aren't getting a free pass either. Our AQI is sitting at 52, which is "Moderate," with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. Couple that with a "Moderate" pollen count, and anyone with asthma, allergies, or other respiratory issues needs to pay attention. Harris County doesn't exactly have a stellar track record for air quality, so don't push it if you feel tightness in your chest or your nose starts running like a leaky faucet. The good news is there isn't any significant wildfire smoke or Saharan dust expected to make things worse today, but residual smoke from burning activities in Mexico and Central America *could* be filtering in.

### Trail Trouble: Your Usual Haunts Might Be Hosed

Planning on hitting your favorite trail? Double-check before you head out. The Brays Bayou Trail, specifically at Spur 5, is still a no-go thanks to that never-ending SH 35 expansion. Seriously, that project feels like it's been going on since before I laced up my first pair of running shoes. You're also out of luck on parts of the White Oak Bayou Greenway (the Little White Oak Bayou Trail section between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center) due to IH-45/I-10 connector ramp construction that's slated to last until 2028.

Downtown is a mess with the FIFA Fan Fest installation ongoing near Bastrop Promenade, so if you're trying to weave through that, be prepared for detours and crowds. And remember that construction at Memorial Park for that new hike-and-bike trail along Loop 610? That's still humming along, so watch for heavy equipment and rerouted paths if you're in that neck of the woods.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Flood Alert: Stay Away From Standing Water!
Seriously, don't mess with floodwater. It's often contaminated with sewage and chemicals you don't want on your skin or in your lungs. If a trail looks flooded, turn around. Your workout isn't worth a trip to the emergency room or a nasty infection.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Watch Your Step: Saturated Ground and Construction Zones
With all the rain we've had, trails are likely slick, muddy, and potentially unstable. Combine that with ongoing construction, especially around Memorial Park and the bayous, and you've got a recipe for twisted ankles or worse. Stick to paved surfaces or dry, well-maintained routes.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Check Your Route, Especially Downtown!
Before you head out, do a quick search for road or trail closures specific to your area, particularly if you're training near any of the major construction projects or downtown events like the FIFA Fan Fest. Plan a backup route or be ready to pivot.
</AlertBox>

Overall, today's a day for cautious activity. Choose your path wisely, listen to your body, and don't assume that because the sun's out, all is well in the Bayou City.
