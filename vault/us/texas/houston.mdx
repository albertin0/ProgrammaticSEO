## The Full Breakdown

Let's be real, Houston in August is basically a sauna that occasionally tries to drown you. Today is no different, despite the "Mainly Clear" skies this morning. That just means more direct sun to bake you.

The National Weather Service has dropped a Heat Advisory on all of southeast Texas, effective until 7 PM CDT on Sunday. We're talking actual temperatures nudging the low 100s, but the *feels like* temperature, thanks to our infamous Gulf Coast humidity, is expected to hit a disgusting 106 to 112 degrees Fahrenheit. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous. Your body's ability to cool itself by sweating is severely hampered when the air is already saturated.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat Index!
Seriously, this isn't a drill. With heat index values between 106 and 112°F, heat illnesses like cramps, exhaustion, and stroke are a real threat. Hydrate *before* you're thirsty, wear loose, light clothing, and stick to air conditioning or early morning/late evening hours. Don't push it. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get inside immediately.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. The AQI of 70, driven by PM2.5, is technically "moderate." However, for anyone with asthma, allergies, or other respiratory sensitivities, it's enough to cause irritation. We're still getting wisps of wildfire smoke from the Pacific Northwest and the ever-present Saharan dust plume. It’s not a full-blown "stay inside" alert for everyone, but if you’re coughing more than usual or your throat feels scratchy, your body is telling you something.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### WARNING: Moderate Air Quality for Sensitive Lungs
If you've got respiratory issues, or just want to breathe easy, today's PM2.5 levels might feel rough. Keep an eye on any symptoms and consider shorter, less intense workouts.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble: White Oak Bayou Greenway is a Construction Zone

Good luck trying to get a continuous run in on some of our favorite spots. The Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 is still snarled by the SH 35 expansion. But the real headache today is the **White Oak Bayou Greenway**. Multiple segments are impacted. You've got ongoing, daily closures near N Shepherd, expected to last around six months, for Harris County Flood Control District work. And let's not forget the bigger, longer-term closure between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center for the IH-45/I-10 connector ramps project, which isn't slated to open until 2028. They say there's a detour for that one, but as any seasoned runner knows, "detour" often means "longer, hotter, less-shaded, and probably confusing."

<AlertBox type="info">
### PRO TIP: Plan Your Route (and Your Bailout)
If you insist on hitting the trails, especially around White Oak Bayou, *research your route beforehand*. Expect detours and closures. Bring extra water, tell someone where you're going, and don't be a hero if that heat index starts cooking you. Early morning is your best bet, but even then, that humidity will be a gut punch.
</AlertBox>
