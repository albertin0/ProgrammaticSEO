## Today's Run: Hot, Sticky, and Hazy, with a Side of Detours

Look, it's June in Houston. You know the drill. That "clear" sky might sound inviting, but our notorious humidity ensures that 77 degrees feels more like an oven set to "broil" by mid-morning, with heat indices projected to hit the low 100s. Your sweat won't evaporate, it'll just sit there, making your heart and lungs work overtime. As a PT, I can tell you that kind of sustained effort in high humidity is how you end up with heat exhaustion or worse, especially if you ignore the warning signs like muscle cramps or dizziness. Hydrate aggressively before, during, and after, and consider moving your longer efforts to the evening when that Gulf breeze might actually show up.

Then there's the air. The AQI is clocking in at 103, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) forecast confirms PM2.5 will be in the "Moderate" range today, partly due to urban particulate matter and lingering atmospheric conditions, even a dissipating Saharan dust plume from earlier in the week. This means tiny airborne particles can irritate your respiratory system. If you've got asthma, allergies, or any kind of lung sensitivity, this is your cue to seriously consider an indoor alternative or at least shorten your outdoor time. Even for the rest of us, it's not ideal for pushing hard efforts.

And because Houston wouldn't be Houston without it, construction continues its relentless march. If you frequent the Brays Bayou Trail, specifically around Spur 5, expect closures due to the ongoing SH 35 expansion. This isn't a new issue, and these projects can drag on forever, so scout your alternative routes *before* you head out. Don't rely on muscle memory right now; you'll just get frustrated and add unnecessary mileage trying to navigate a construction maze. Similarly, the city's preparing for the FIFA World Cup 2026 with road and sidewalk improvements, especially downtown, which means more localized disruptions.
