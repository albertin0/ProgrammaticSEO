Listen up, Houston. If your idea of a good outdoor workout today involves swimming through street gutters or inhaling a questionable particulate cocktail, then by all means, hit the trails. Otherwise, maybe rethink that run.

<LungsJointsScore score={4} />

<BulletList>
*   **Rain, Rain, Go Away (It Won't):** We're in for a multi-day storm pattern with heavy rain and a significant flood risk. Expect street flooding in low-lying areas. You don't want to be caught miles from your car when a downpour turns your favorite route into a bayou.
*   **Lung-Challenging Air:** That "Mainly Clear" sky is a bit deceiving. Our AQI is sitting at 103 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. This means the air quality is officially "Moderate," but it's on the higher side. Combine that with moderate pollen and lingering smoke from Mexican agricultural fires, and your lungs might be doing more work than your legs.
*   **Trail Turmoil:** All that rain turns our beloved dirt trails into slick, muddy hazards. Think twisted ankles, busted knees, and a whole lot of laundry. Stick to pavement if you must, but even then, watch for standing water.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We're runners. We like to pretend a little rain makes us tougher. But there's a difference between a light drizzle and navigating a potential flash flood. We're looking at 3 to 5 inches of rain possible over the next few days, and a Stage 2 flood alert is already in effect through Memorial Day Weekend. That means even if it's not pouring *right this second*, conditions can change fast, and the ground is already getting saturated.

On top of the rain, that AQI isn't doing anyone any favors. While yesterday saw showers that might wash out some particulates, the high relative humidity and continued influence from Saharan dust and residual smoke mean your lungs are still getting a workout they didn't ask for. If you've got asthma or any other respiratory issues, seriously consider taking it indoors today.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Flooding Risk is Real:** Heavy rainfall means many Houston roads and underpasses are prone to flooding. Do NOT try to run or bike through standing water. Turn around, don't drown. This is especially true near natural low points like Buffalo Bayou Park, where water levels can rise rapidly.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Zone Ahead:** Even if you avoid the floods, ongoing road construction can present unexpected detours and uneven surfaces. Houston TranStar reports various lane closures and projects on major arteries like IH-10 and I-45 today. Keep an eye out for construction zones, especially if you're hitting urban routes.
</AlertBox>
