### Air Quality: Don't Breathe Deeply Today

Seriously, Houston, the air is not your friend. We've got an AQI of 103, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, meaning the air is unhealthy, especially if you're sensitive to that crud. On top of that, the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality has issued an Ozone Action Day for the Houston area for today. This combo means your lungs are going to be working overtime trying to filter out both particulate matter and ground-level ozone, a respiratory irritant. Inland areas, in particular, are prone to higher ozone and PM2.5 readings. If you have asthma, allergies, or any respiratory issues, consider this your stern warning: stay inside.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Danger: Double-Whammy Air Pollution!** With an AQI of 103 (PM2.5) and an Ozone Action Day, outdoor exertion is highly discouraged for sensitive groups and should be limited for everyone else. Think twice before pushing it.
</AlertBox>

### Weather: Warm and Ready to Wear You Out

At 28.2°C, it's not the inferno of August, but it's still plenty warm enough to contribute to heat stress, especially when paired with the returning humidity later in the weekend. You'll sweat, and with the high humidity that's typical for Houston, that sweat won't evaporate effectively, making your body work harder to cool down. Hydration is key, but honestly, so is common sense.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Warning: Keep an Eye on the Heat.** Even though it's "only" 28.2°C, factor in the expected mid-90s highs and returning humidity. Stay hydrated with more than just plain water if you're out there.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: Good Luck Finding a Clear Path

Forget your long, uninterrupted runs on some of the city's favorite greenways. The **White Oak Bayou Trail** is still a hot mess of closures and construction. The segment between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, which is your main artery to downtown, has been shut down since January 2026 and isn't expected to fully reopen until 2028, thanks to I-45/I-10 connector ramp work. That's not a temporary inconvenience; that's a multi-year commitment to detours.

Down near North Shepherd, the Harris County Flood Control District has been replacing concrete lining, and while one segment might reopen this month, the construction is immediately shifting west of Durham Drive, with work expected through mid-2027. Plus, those daily closures from 7:30 AM to 5:30 PM mean even if a section is technically "open" for part of the day, it's not reliably clear for a full workout.

And don't even get me started on the Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 or parts of MacGregor Park. Those are also facing significant, ongoing closures due to construction. If you're running downtown, forget Polk Street between Chartres and Jackson for bikes or pedestrians; you'll be rerouted via Rusk Street.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Heads Up: Plan Your Route Carefully.** Check Houston Public Works or Houston Parks Board sites for the latest on trail closures, especially around White Oak Bayou and Brays Bayou. Have a backup plan, or prepare for more pavement than you bargained for.
</AlertBox>
