Alright, Houston, let's cut to the chase. If you're looking at that "Mainly Clear" sky and thinking it's a good time for a long run, your brain might already be cooking. We're staring down another day where the heat index isn't just high; it's advisory-level extreme. The National Weather Service is calling for heat indices up to 112 degrees, and those overnight lows aren't even dipping below the upper 70s or 80s. Your body's natural cooling system is going to be working overtime just trying to keep you from literally overheating.

And if the heat doesn't get you, the air quality might. With an AQI of 103, and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, this air is classified as "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups." That means if you've got asthma, COPD, or just a general aversion to feeling like you're breathing through a wet sock, outdoor exertion should be severely limited. Even healthy folks might experience throat irritation or difficulty breathing with prolonged exposure.

Beyond the atmospheric torture, don't forget the ground-level chaos. Good luck finding a clear path on some of our favorite spots. The White Oak Bayou Greenway, a personal favorite, has sections near North Shepherd experiencing intermittent closures for flood control work, and let's not forget the longer-term shutdown between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center due to that endless I-45/I-10 connector ramp project, expected to last until 2028. Even parts of the Buffalo Bayou Trail near Downtown have been closed since January to fix a "blind spot." And if you were heading north, the east side of the Sam Houston Multi-Use Trailhead is still closed because of a wildfire response, even though the fire's contained. It’s a maze out there.

### DANGER: EXTREME HEAT ADVISORY IN EFFECT
With heat index values hitting 106-112°F, heat stroke is a very real threat. Limit outdoor activity, hydrate *before* you're thirsty, and know the signs of heat-related illness (dizziness, nausea, lack of sweating). Move your workout inside, or save it for another day. Seriously.
</AlertBox>
