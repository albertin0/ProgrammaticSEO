### The Air You're Breathing (or trying to)

Let's talk about the air. The AQI is clocking in at 103, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. What does that mean for your lungs? It means those fine particulate matters are floating around, ready to get deep into your respiratory system. Even if you don't *feel* it immediately, repeated exposure isn't doing you any favors long-term. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) even put Houston on the radar for "Ozone/PM2.5" issues today. For sensitive folks—and honestly, that's most of us pushing hard outside—this isn't just a nuisance; it's a legitimate health concern.

Then there's the pollen. It's only "Moderate" today, but combined with the PM2.5, it's a one-two punch for your airways.

### Your Run, Interrupted: A Tour of Closures

Houston is a city under perpetual construction, and this weekend is no exception. If you had plans to hit some of the city's prime running real estate, you're likely out of luck.

The **Bayou City Art Festival** has taken over downtown, meaning major closures on **Allen Parkway** and surrounding streets through Monday morning. Good luck trying to get any kind of uninterrupted run or ride in that area.

Meanwhile, if you were eyeing **Memorial Park**, forget about it during the day. **Memorial Loop Drive** is closed for the 2026 Texas Children's Houston Open, and public access is only allowed from 8:00 PM to 4:00 AM nightly. So unless your idea of a good run involves dodging golf carts in the middle of the night, find another spot.

And the construction? It's relentless. The **Brays Bayou Trail** is still shut down at Spur 5 thanks to the SH 35 expansion, a project that's been dragging on forever with no clear end in sight. Similarly, the **White Oak Bayou Greenway** (specifically the Little White Oak Bayou Trail section) is a no-go between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center. That closure is for I-45/I-10 connector ramp work and is slated to last through 2028. Seriously, if you're a regular on these trails, you're practically a local expert in construction detours by now.
