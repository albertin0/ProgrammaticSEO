<AlertBox type="danger">
**Hazardous Air & Ozone Alert:** Don't let those "Mainly Clear" skies fool you. The National Weather Service (NWS) Houston/Galveston office, alongside the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ), has slapped an Air Quality Alert on our city for today. Conditions are ripe for producing high levels of ground-level ozone pollution, which, combined with the reported 103 AQI for PM2.5, shoves us squarely into the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category. If you're sensitive – and honestly, who *isn't* sensitive to breathing smog? – you're looking at increased respiratory symptoms. Think twice about pushing those intervals.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Brutal Heat Index:** Forget the 28.2°C (83°F) ambient temperature; that's just a number. What truly matters in Houston's August hellscape is the "feels like" temperature, and today that's a soul-crushing 102-107°F. The NWS is warning of persistent hot temperatures and heat indices well into the triple digits. This isn't your grandma's "a little warm" advice; this is straight from Houston Methodist doctors who are seeing the fallout from our city's brutal summer conditions. They explicitly state that humidity can be *more* dangerous than heat alone because it actively prevents your body from cooling itself through sweat evaporation. Headaches, dizziness, muscle cramps – ignore these at your peril.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Chaos Continues:** Just when you thought the air and heat were your only enemies, don't forget the maze of closures on our beloved greenways. If your go-to is the **Brays Bayou Trail**, be aware that sections, particularly around Spur 5, remain closed due to the ongoing, seemingly never-ending SH 35 expansion project, expected to last through 2026. Over at **Buffalo Bayou Park**, the Houston Parks Board has had portions of the trail near Downtown (specifically the north side between Sabine Street Bridge and Tapley Tributary) shut down since January for what they're calling "blind spot" fixes. And for those who favor the **White Oak Bayou Greenway**, brace yourself. There are "intermittent closures" near Shepherd for flood control work, often without so much as a proper detour sign. Plus, the Little White Oak Bayou Trail segment between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is on a long-term hiatus until 2028 because of the IH-45/I-10 connector ramp construction. Seriously, if you insist on running outside, have a backup plan, a backup for your backup plan, and enough water to fill a kiddie pool.
</AlertBox>
