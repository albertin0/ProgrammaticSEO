## Why today isn't the day:

<BulletList>
*   **Thunderstorms & Lightning Galore:** Forget your long run; you're more likely to become a lightning rod. Expect "scattered showers and storms [bringing] brief heavy rain, lightning, and gusty winds," especially as the day heats up. The forecast calls for "afternoon convection/sea breeze storms through the Fourth of July weekend," so this isn't a quick pop-up shower. Getting struck is a very real, very dumb way to end your fitness journey.
*   **Oppressive "Feels Like" Triple Digits:** Even if the rain holds off for a minute, the humidity is still going to wallop you. "Heat index values, or humidity-driven “feels like” temperatures, will be in the triple digits across Southeast Texas by 3 p.m. Wednesday." This isn't just uncomfortable; it's straight-up dangerous for your heart and lungs because "when the air is already saturated with moisture, sweat stays on the skin instead of evaporating and cooling the body." You're just asking for heat exhaustion or worse.
*   **Trails Are a Mess (and Potentially Closed):** With heavy rain predicted, expect our bayou trails to become their usual muddy, flooded selves. While Buffalo Bayou Park is usually a go-to, any significant downpour can make sections impassable. Plus, parts of the Brays Bayou Trail are still closed at Spur 5 due to ongoing construction, making detours a headache you don't need today.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### IMMEDIATE DANGER: Thunderstorms & Lightning!
Do not risk being outside during active thunderstorms. Lightning kills. Seek immediate shelter in a substantial building or a hard-topped vehicle. Running with headphones in means you might not even hear the thunder until it’s too late. This isn't a "push through" kind of hazard.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### HIGH HEAT INDEX & POTENTIAL FLASH FLOODING
Even if the storm passes, the air will still be thick and heavy, making your body work overtime to cool down. Drink copious amounts of water if you absolutely *must* go outside later, and pay attention to your body. Also, watch for localized flash flooding, especially underpasses and low-lying trail sections. The Houston area is prone to this, and with "heavy rain increasing flash flood risks," it's a legitimate concern.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your Best Bet: Embrace the Indoors
Today is prime time for a treadmill session, some yoga in your living room, or hitting up one of Houston's many indoor gyms. Save your outdoor miles for a day when the sky isn't trying to smite you and the air isn't trying to suffocate you. Your lungs and joints (and common sense) will thank you.
</AlertBox>
