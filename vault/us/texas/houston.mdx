## The Air You Breathe (or Don't)

Look, I love our city, but sometimes running here feels like training for an apocalypse. Today's AQI is 103, which officially puts us in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category, primarily due to PM2.5. If you're one of those "sensitive groups" – which includes damn near anyone with lungs that aren't made of steel after years of Houston living – you need to seriously limit your outdoor exposure. This isn't just about a little cough; Harris County has an actual "F" grade for ozone levels, and conditions like today trigger Asthma Air Aware Day Warnings.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Unhealthy Air Quality!** AQI is 103 (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups). Avoid prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors, especially if you have respiratory issues, heart disease, are elderly, or are a child. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Troubles: The Saga Continues

If you're planning a long run on some of our beloved greenways, prepare to reroute or get creative. The **White Oak Bayou Greenway** is still a mess. There are intermittent daily closures near N Shepherd, running from 7 AM to 5:30 PM, and these are expected to continue for another six months due to flood control work. Even worse, the section of the Little White Oak Bayou Trail between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center? Closed through *2028* for I-45/I-10 connector ramp construction. There's a detour, but it's a detour.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Widespread Trail & Road Closures.** Expect significant detours and blocked paths on White Oak Bayou Greenway, Memorial Park (western Outer Loop), and Brays Bayou Trail. Downtown streets also have ongoing construction closures. Plan your route meticulously.
</AlertBox>

And don't even get me started on Memorial Park's western Outer Loop, which is basically a ghost town until late 2027 thanks to the Memorial Groves project. The Seymour Lieberman Trail loop is mostly open, but some amenities are gone. Brays Bayou Trail is still closed at Spur 5, likely through *this entire year* for highway expansion.

Downtown is a minefield of closures too, with Polk, Chenevert, Jackson, and Clay Streets impacted by the Convention District Transformation Project. Major freeways are still seeing weekend construction closures as of this morning, meaning getting to your preferred outdoor space might be half the workout itself.

## Temperature & Humidity

It's 28.2°C (about 83°F) and "muggy." While we're past the "extreme heat advisory" days of early October where the heat index hit 106°F, this isn't exactly crisp autumn air. Combined with the poor air quality, your body's going to struggle to regulate temperature effectively. Drink more water than you think you need.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip: Hydrate Aggressively.** Even if it "only" feels muggy, our bodies work harder in these conditions. Dehydration creeps up fast when you're exerting yourself in Houston's humidity.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, or better yet, stay inside. Your choice.
