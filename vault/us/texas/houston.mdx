## The Rundown: Don't Be a Hero, Houston

Look, it's May in Houston. This isn't exactly prime running weather, but it's not impossible if you're smart. The biggest concern today is the air quality. An AQI of 103 means fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is floating around, ready to mess with your respiratory system. We've got ongoing reports about industrial pollution, like the recent complaints against Freeport LNG, contributing to the general atmospheric funk that can lead to long-term health issues like asthma and cancer for those living nearby. So, even if you don't feel it immediately, you're still exposing your lungs to more than ideal.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### AQI 103 & PM2.5: Your Lungs Are Not a Filter
Seriously, don't think your body can just "power through" bad air. When the AQI is in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" range, and PM2.5 is the main culprit, you're inhaling microscopic particles that dig deep into your lungs and can even enter your bloodstream. This is especially rough if you have pre-existing conditions. Cut back on intensity, or just take it inside.
</AlertBox>

If you're dead set on getting those outdoor miles in, consider your route carefully. Along the popular trails in **Buffalo Bayou Park**, you'll be exposed to the same lovely air quality as the rest of the city. Remember, the humidity will make even a moderate effort feel harder. Hydration is key, and don't be afraid to take walk breaks or shorten your planned distance.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate, Hydrate, Hydrate (and Then Hydrate Some More)
It's warm and humid. You're sweating more than you think. Carry water, drink before you feel thirsty, and replenish electrolytes. Don't be that person cramping up under a bridge.
</AlertBox>

Also, while not directly impacting trail running, the Houston TranStar reports a bunch of road construction and closures on major arteries like IH-10 and IH-610. This means more traffic congestion, which usually translates to more vehicle emissions contributing to our already delightful air quality. Just something to keep in mind if you're commuting to your workout spot.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Early Bird Catches... Slightly Less Bad Air
If you absolutely *must* run outside, get out there early. Temperatures are lower, and typically, the air quality is *slightly* better before traffic and heat really get cooking. It's not a magical cure, but every little bit helps.
</AlertBox>
