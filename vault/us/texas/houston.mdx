## Today's Environmental Breakdown: More Sweat, Less Glory

### Heat & Humidity: Houston's Summer Special (read: Hell)

It's August in Houston, so obviously, it's going to be a swamp out there. But today, it's particularly egregious. Temperatures are hitting 28.2°C (82°F) right now, but the "feels like" factor is pushing well into the upper 90s and easily triple digits later. We're talking 106°F to 108°F possible heat index values. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous. Houston's humidity is infamous for screwing with your body's ability to cool itself, turning a casual jog into a full-blown emergency if you're not careful. Doctors have been warning about this all summer, and today is no different.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** An extreme heat warning is in effect for much of the region. High "feels like" temperatures mean a significant and increased risk of heat-related illness, especially for anyone spending extended time outdoors. Limit strenuous activity to early morning or late evening, or just stay inside.
</AlertBox>

### Construction & Closures: Your Favorite Trails Are a Minefield

If the heat and crappy air weren't enough, good luck trying to actually *run* on some of our better trails. The Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 is still closed due to the ongoing SH 35 expansion project, and that thing isn't going anywhere anytime soon. Don't even get me started on the White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail) between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, which is completely shut down for IH-45/I-10 connector work until 2028. And then you've got the intermittent closures along the White Oak Bayou Greenway near Shepherd for flood control. This isn't just an inconvenience; it forces runners and cyclists onto main roads, adding a layer of traffic-related danger to your already risky outing.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Plan Your Route (and Your Bailout):** Seriously, hydrate like your life depends on it (it kind of does). Aim for pre-sunrise or post-sunset if you absolutely must be outside. Wear light, moisture-wicking gear. Know the signs of heat exhaustion – dizziness, nausea, weakness – and stop immediately if you feel them. And for the love of all that is holy, check TranStar's daily construction schedules before you head out. These closures are notorious for popping up and derailing your plans.
</AlertBox>
