## The Air: Don't Breathe Too Deep

Seriously, Houston. An Ozone Action Day means conditions are *prime* for creating high levels of ozone pollution. Emissions from cars and industry react with our lovely heat and sunshine, trapping that nasty stuff at ground level. This isn't just a nuisance; it aggravates respiratory problems. AccuWeather has our AQI as "Poor" and explicitly states it's unhealthy for sensitive groups. So, if you've got asthma, COPD, or just like your lungs, think twice about pushing it today.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Ozone Action Day in Effect!
The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) has declared an Ozone Action Day for the Houston, Galveston, and Brazoria areas. Today's AQI of 103 means the air quality is Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups due to high ozone levels. Reduce prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors, especially in the afternoon. Consider moving your workout indoors.
</AlertBox>

## Navigating the Urban Maze (and its Closures)

If you absolutely *must* get out there, be prepared for detours. Construction is a constant here, and it's still messing with some key running and biking routes. For instance, the White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail) between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is shut down until 2028 because of I-45/I-10 connector ramp work. Don't try to sneak around the fencing; it's there for a reason.

While the Buffalo Bayou Trail between Waugh Drive and the Rosemont Bridge on the north side is apparently "NOW OPEN" after a drainage project, other segments, particularly the Kinder Trail east of downtown, are still undergoing improvements and closures are to be expected. If your route involves the Brays Bayou Trail, be aware that it's closed at Spur 5 due to the SH 35 expansion.
