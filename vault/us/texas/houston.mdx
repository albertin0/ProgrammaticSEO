## The Heat Is On, And It's Just Getting Started

That "Mainly Clear" sky might look inviting, but don't be fooled. While the actual temperature hovers around 28.2°C (81°F to 83°F), the humidity is cranking up the "feels-like" factor into the upper 90s (around 37°C) today, and forecasters are saying we're looking at 102-104°F (39-40°C) feels-like temps by midweek. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's a legitimate hazard. Houston's Gulf Coast humidity means your sweat, which is supposed to cool you down, just sits there. Your heart and lungs work overtime, and heat-related illnesses can sneak up faster than a driver cutting you off on Buffalo Speedway.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Excessive Heat Risk
With "feels-like" temperatures soaring, the risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke is significant. Limit strenuous outdoor activity, especially during peak sun hours. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or excessively fatigued, stop immediately and get into the shade or air conditioning.
</AlertBox>

## Breathe Easy? Not So Much.

Our AQI is currently 103, which officially puts us in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category due to PM2.5. This means if you have asthma, other respiratory issues, or heart conditions, you *will* feel it. But honestly, even if you don't, you might find yourself feeling a bit sluggish. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) notes that light residual smoke from distant fires in Mexico and Central America, plus a lingering Saharan dust plume, are contributing to our fine particulate matter levels. High humidity also plays a role in keeping those particles hanging around.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Air Quality Alert: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups
An AQI of 103 (PM2.5) means active children and adults, and people with respiratory diseases such as asthma, should limit prolonged outdoor exertion. Consider taking your workout indoors today.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Troubles: Still Under Construction

If you were planning to hit some of our usually fantastic greenways, be aware of ongoing headaches. The **White Oak Bayou Greenway** (specifically the Little White Oak Bayou Trail section between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center) is still shut down for extensive IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work, and it's not expected to reopen until 2028. There *are* detours, but they're not always runner-friendly, so plan your route carefully. Same goes for parts of the Brays Bayou Trail, which is still seeing closures at Spur 5 thanks to the ongoing SH 35 expansion.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Check Trail Status Before You Go
Construction closures are a constant reality in Houston. Always check local trail or city public works websites for the latest on detours, especially for popular routes like the White Oak Bayou Greenway or Brays Bayou Trail. Be safe, stay visible if you're forced onto streets, and respect those construction barriers.
</AlertBox>
