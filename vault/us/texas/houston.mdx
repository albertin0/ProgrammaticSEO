## The Air is Thick With... Everything But Oxygen

Look, our AQI is currently sitting at 58, which is technically "Moderate." But don't let that fool you into thinking it's fine. The dominant pollutant is PM2.5, and that's the nasty stuff that gets deep into your lungs. On top of that, we've got a fresh plume of Saharan dust moving in today. This isn't just a pretty sunset opportunity; it's a respiratory hazard, especially for anyone with allergies, asthma, or just a desire to breathe deeply. If you're out there pushing hard, you're just inviting irritation and inflammation. Trust me, your lungs will thank you for taking it easy.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Saharan Dust & PM2.5 Warning!
Limit your time outdoors and avoid strenuous activity, particularly if you have respiratory sensitivities. This isn't just hazy skies; it's microscopic irritants for your lungs.
</AlertBox>

## Weather Whiplash and Wetlands

You step outside and it's thundering. Great. But even if that clears up, don't be fooled by the actual temperature of 26.2°C (around 79°F). The heat index, which is what your body *actually feels*, is soaring into the triple digits, potentially hitting 104-105°F. That kind of humidity and heat combination is a recipe for heat exhaustion or worse, even if you're just walking.

Add to that, the ground is likely saturated from previous rains this month, and with active thunderstorms, trails will be slick, muddy, and potentially prone to localized flooding. Lightning is an obvious risk during a thunderstorm, so staying inside is your best bet until things really clear up and cool down.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Extreme Heat Index & Thunderstorm Alert
Expect dangerously high "feels like" temperatures (104-105°F) with a real thunderstorm risk. Stay hydrated, listen for thunder, and seriously consider an indoor workout.
</AlertBox>

## Construction Chaos and Closed Trails

Good luck trying to piece together a decent outdoor route today. The Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 is still closed thanks to the SH 35 expansion, and their "detours" are, to put it mildly, an insult to trail users. You've also got significant, long-term closures along the White Oak Bayou Greenway, particularly between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, projected to last through 2028. Trying to navigate these areas means fighting vehicle traffic, which is dangerous on a good day, let alone when you're distracted by the heat and dust.

Oh, and if you were planning on heading downtown, remember the FIFA Fan Fest is in full swing at Bastrop Promenade and surrounding streets all day. Expect road closures and general mayhem if you try to get near that area.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Trail Closures & Detour Difficulties
Avoid Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 and large sections of the White Oak Bayou Greenway due to ongoing, poorly marked construction closures. Find alternative routes or stick to neighborhood streets, but beware of increased vehicle traffic.
