## Today's Forecast: Heatstroke & Haze

If you woke up thinking, "I need to get my run in," let me stop you right there. Today, August 19, 2026, is a hard no for serious outdoor workouts. We're looking at actual temperatures climbing into the upper 90s and low 100s, but it's the "feels like" temperature that'll really bake you, hovering around a brutal 106°F to 110°F. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's a straight-up health hazard. Humidity, as we all know here in the Bayou City, is often more dangerous than the heat itself, preventing your sweat from doing its job to cool you down. Doctors at Houston Methodist have been shouting this from the rooftops: heat-related illness escalates fast, even for seasoned runners.

And while you're battling the oppressive heat, your lungs will be fighting their own war. The Air Quality Index (AQI) is at a lovely 103, which is "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups," with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. But wait, there's more! We also have an Air Quality Alert in effect for elevated ozone levels across Houston, Galveston, and Brazoria. So, you get a double whammy of respiratory irritants. Whether you're sensitive or not, inhaling this cocktail while pushing your heart rate is just asking for trouble. Light density smoke from scattered burning activities and Saharan dust plumes are also contributing to the fine particulate matter.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat Advisory!
With "feels like" temperatures nearing 110°F, heatstroke and heat exhaustion are very real threats. Symptoms like muscle cramps, dizziness, nausea, and extreme fatigue mean you need to STOP immediately and get inside. Hydration is key, but plain water isn't always enough to replace lost electrolytes.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Treachery: Mind the Closures

Thinking of hitting a greenway to escape the concrete jungle? Think again. Parts of the White Oak Bayou Greenway are closed. Specifically, a section west of Studemont Street and south of I-10 has temporary closures for infrastructure repairs. And don't even get me started on the Little White Oak Bayou Trail between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center – that section is completely shut down until *2028* due to IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work. If your usual route takes you near Buffalo Bayou Park downtown, be aware that a portion of the trail on the north side, near Johnny Goyen Park, is also temporarily closed for safety improvements at the confluence of White Oak and Buffalo Bayou. And for those further south, Brays Bayou Trail is still closed at Spur 5 because of that never-ending SH 35 expansion.

It's a hot, hazy, and construction-riddled mess out there. Save your PR attempts for a day when the air isn't trying to kill you and the heat isn't actively trying to boil your insides. Hit the gym, find a pool, or just chill out. Your body will thank you.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality Alert: Limit Outdoor Activity
With an AQI of 103 and elevated ozone, limiting outdoor exertion is highly recommended, especially for children, the elderly, and those with respiratory issues. Early morning or late evening might seem better, but the heat and ozone persist.
</AlertBox>
