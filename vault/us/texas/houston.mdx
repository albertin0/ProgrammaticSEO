## The Air You Breathe (or Don't)

Look, I get it. The temperate 19°C (66°F) and sunshine feel like a gift. But your lungs are telling a different story. The Air Quality Index (AQI) is clocking in at 103, putting us firmly in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category. The dominant pollutant is PM2.5, those tiny particles that love to burrow deep into your respiratory system.

And it's not just the usual urban grime. A stagnant weather pattern is letting ground-level ozone build up, especially as the day heats up. Plus, if you've noticed a hazy tinge, that's likely smoke from agricultural fires in southern Mexico being swept north by persistent southerly winds, further mucking up our air. Even small scattered local burning activities are adding to the PM2.5 soup.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Sensitive Lungs, Stay Indoors:** If you have asthma, COPD, or are just generally sensitive to pollution, today is NOT the day for outdoor exertion. Active children and adults in sensitive groups should limit prolonged outdoor activity. This isn't a suggestion; it's basic self-preservation.
</AlertBox>

## Your Usual Routes? Good Luck.

It feels like every other week there's another trail closure, and this week is no different. Forget your usual easy loops; you'll be dodging barricades like it's a field sobriety test.

The **MKT Trail** bridges near Studewood and Hogan are still closed, with the Hogan bridge possibly out of commission for another *two to three years* if we don't keep the pressure on. A pedestrian bridge along the MKT Trail at White Oak Bayou and Studewood Street has seen repair delays, pushing back its reopening. A portion of the trail near White Oak Bayou is closed for a pedestrian bridge project, expected to last about 90 days.

Don't even think about a clear run on **Brays Bayou Trail** at Spur 5; that's still shut down for the never-ending SH 35 expansion, potentially until Spring 2026. The **White Oak Bayou Greenway** (specifically the Little White Oak Bayou Trail) between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is closed through 2028 for IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work. And the **Buffalo Bayou Trail** near Downtown has been closed since January 31, 2026, to fix a "blind spot". If you were planning to drive to a park, good luck around Herman Park Drive, which is closed until May 22nd.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Construction Zone, Not a Race Course:** Seriously, these closures aren't just minor detours. They often force pedestrians and cyclists onto unsafe street sections with fast-moving traffic. Find an alternative, or hit the treadmill. Your life isn't worth saving five minutes.
</AlertBox>

## Don't Forget the Water

While the immediate skies are clear, Houston is always a couple of pop-up storms away from a flash flood. We just had a Mother's Day weekend with significant storm and flood risk, and saturated soils are still a concern in places. Rice University just put out research in April warning about hidden flood dangers and how current flood maps don't fully capture the risk, especially with rapid development. Harris County Flood Control District projects are underway, but some have faced delays.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Houston's Always-On Flood Risk:** Even on a clear day, stay aware of recent rainfall and check local resources for any lingering high water, especially in low-lying areas. Houston's flooding isn't always from current rain; sometimes it's yesterday's downpour finding new ways to surprise you.
</AlertBox>
