## The "Fall" That Isn't: Houston's Heat Strikes Again

Alright, Houston, let's be real. It's October 12th, and we're still sweating like it's August. The forecast is telling us a delightful 30.1°C (86.2°F), which sounds almost tolerable, right? Wrong. Factor in that glorious Gulf humidity, and it *feels like* a brutal 36°C (97°F) out there. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's straight-up dangerous for sustained outdoor activity. Your body is going to struggle to cool itself when the air is already saturated. Houston Methodist has been shouting about this for months – high humidity means your sweat just sits on your skin, not evaporating, which makes you overheat faster and strains your heart and lungs. Unless you're trying to win a prize for "most heatstroke-prone runner," maybe stick to the AC during peak hours (that's 10 AM to 4 PM, for the uninitiated).

## Trail Closures and Detours

If you're planning to hit the trails, particularly anywhere near I-10, prepare for some rerouting. The I-10 White Oak Bayou Elevation Project is ongoing, with significant closures. Specifically, the I-10 HOV connector between Franklin Street and Studemont Street closed on October 9th for demolition and reconstruction and isn't expected to reopen until *Summer 2028* for westbound traffic. This project aims to eventually create new bike trails along White Oak Bayou, which sounds great for the future, but right now it means potential headaches and inaccessible sections around our beloved White Oak Bayou Greenway. Always check local city resources or social media for the latest on trail access before you head out, because getting a few miles into your run only to hit a construction wall is peak Houston frustration.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Trail Alert: White Oak Bayou Greenway:** Expect ongoing disruptions and closures around the I-10 corridor due to the White Oak Bayou Elevation Project. Plan alternate routes or be ready for detours, especially if your path usually takes you under or near the I-10 overpasses.
</AlertBox>
