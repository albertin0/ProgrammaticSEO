## Lungs: Choking Hazard

Alright, let's talk about the air. That AQI of 103 for PM2.5 isn't a suggestion, it's a warning. If you're one of the "sensitive groups"—which, let's be honest, could be anyone on a bad day—you should seriously reconsider sustained outdoor exertion. This fine particulate matter can irritate your lungs and, combined with the moderate pollen count, it's a double whammy for your respiratory system. There's been lingering smoke and haze in the area from agricultural fires down in Mexico, carried up by our lovely southerly flow, and that's not helping our PM2.5 situation.

## Joints: Slip and Slide Edition

The weather today is a classic Houston mixed bag, with a definite lean toward "stay inside." We've got scattered thunderstorms and heavy rain rolling through, making anything from the sidewalks on Main Street to the trails around Memorial Park slick and potentially dangerous. While the severe thunderstorm watch from overnight was expected to clear by daybreak, that doesn't mean the rain and general wetness are gone. Poor visibility and hydroplaning aren't just for cars; they're for runners too. And seriously, don't tempt fate with lightning.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Watch Your Step!** Heavy rain means slippery surfaces. If you *must* run outside, stick to known, well-lit paths and treat every patch of pavement like an ice rink.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Construction Continues

As if the weather and air weren't enough, don't forget about our ongoing infrastructure "improvements." The **White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail)**, specifically between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, is still closed for the IH-45 and I-10 connector ramp construction. This closure is apparently sticking around until 2028, so if that's your usual route, you're still on detour duty. Plan your runs accordingly, because those orange fences aren't just for decoration.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Detour Ahead:** If you usually hit the White Oak Bayou Greenway, remember that section near Houston Avenue is a no-go zone for the foreseeable future. Know your alternate routes before you head out.
</AlertBox>

Look, I love running as much as the next maniac, but today's conditions are stacked against you. Prioritize being able to breathe and not breaking an ankle.
