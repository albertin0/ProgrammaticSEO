## Why today is *not* the day for PRs:

<BulletList>
*   **Ozone Action Day.** The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) has declared an Ozone Action Day for the Houston area today, with afternoon levels expected to hit "unhealthy for sensitive groups." Even if your personal AQI monitor says "moderate," official alerts are saying otherwise for ozone, which can mess with your lungs. Harris County already got an "F" for ozone from the American Lung Association, so this isn't a surprise.
*   **Sticky Heat is Back.** While the morning is clear at 28.2°C, temperatures are climbing into the low 90s (around 32-33°C) this afternoon, with heat indices flirting with triple digits. Houston's heat season is already cranking, and ERs are seeing heat-related illnesses. You'll be sweating buckets, and your body will struggle to cool itself.
*   **Trail Trouble Everywhere.** Forget planning a straightforward route. Major segments of popular trails are shut down. The **Brays Bayou Trail** is still closed at Spur 5 due to the ongoing SH 35 expansion. Up north, the pedestrian bridge on the **MKT Trail** near White Oak Bayou and Studewood Street is *still* out from last year's construction mishap and rain, forcing unsafe detours. Don't even think about the **White Oak Bayou Greenway** between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center; it's blocked until 2028 for IH-45/I-10 connector work.

</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Ozone Alert! Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups

Today's air quality, particularly for ozone, is forecast to be "unhealthy for sensitive groups." This means if you have asthma, other respiratory conditions, or are older or very young, you should seriously limit or avoid outdoor exertion. Even healthy individuals might experience discomfort or irritation. Keep an eye out for any lingering haze from those agricultural fires down in Mexico, too; they've been contributing to our air woes.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Mercury – and the Humidity

With temperatures soaring and humidity making it feel even hotter, heat exhaustion and stroke are real threats. This isn't just "uncomfortable"; it's legitimately dangerous. Hydrate aggressively, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, don't leave kids or pets in cars. If you do go out, stick to shaded areas and listen to your body.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route (and Your Backup Route)

Seriously, if you're hitting the pavement or what's left of the trails, check current construction and closures. Houston TranStar has a list longer than my last marathon for various highway and ramp closures that could affect your run even if you're not directly on a trail. Consider hitting the gym or finding a short, known-safe neighborhood loop instead of trying to navigate a trail system that's more Swiss cheese than solid path right now. Early mornings are always better for heat and sometimes air quality, but even then, be smart.
</AlertBox>
