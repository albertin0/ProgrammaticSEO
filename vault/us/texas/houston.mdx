## The Full Breakdown

Today, June 26, 2026, Houston is serving up its usual summer special: a steaming platter of heat and humidity, topped with a side of questionable air. The mercury might only hit the low to mid-90s, but with that infamous Houston humidity, the heat index is forecasted to soar to a truly dangerous 104 to 107 degrees. That's "limit strenuous outdoor activities between noon and early evening" territory, according to the National Weather Service, and frankly, I'd say extend that window. Your body is going to be fighting to regulate its core temperature from the moment you step outside. Dehydration and heat-related illnesses are not a joke, folks.

Then there's the air. Our AQI is sitting at 103, with PM2.5 as the main culprit. While city officials are downplaying it, air monitors caught spikes in particulate pollution downwind of that tire fire at Mammoth Metal Recycling earlier this week. If you're anywhere near the Kellogg and Lawndale streets area, or if you have any kind of respiratory condition, just assume the air is trying to kill your lungs. Staying indoors or wearing a mask is not an overreaction. And get ready for a Saharan dust plume to make things hazier over the weekend.

If you *must* run, be prepared for some frustrating detours. The **White Oak Bayou Greenway** near N Shepherd is currently closed daily from 7:00 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. for flood control work, which could last six months. And don't forget the Little White Oak Bayou Trail closure for the IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work, which is expected to last through 2028. Even the Brays Bayou Trail is still closed at Spur 5 due to the never-ending SH 35 expansion. It's like they're actively trying to keep us off the trails.
