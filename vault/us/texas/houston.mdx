### Ozone Action Day & Unhealthy Air
Today, August 31, 2026, the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) has declared an **Ozone Action Day** for the Houston, Galveston, and Brazoria areas. Coupled with an Air Quality Index (AQI) of 103 and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, the air quality is considered unhealthy for sensitive groups. This means if you have asthma, allergies, or any respiratory issues, you should seriously consider keeping your workout indoors. Even healthy individuals might experience irritation to their throat and difficulty breathing with prolonged exposure.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Underestimate the Humidity
It's Houston in late August. Even when the temperature isn't scorching, the humidity is always lurking. Doctors at Houston Methodist consistently warn that high humidity is often a bigger issue than heat alone, as it prevents your body from effectively cooling itself. Hydrate well *before* you start, dress in loose, moisture-wicking gear, and know the signs of heat-related illness: muscle cramps, weakness, headaches, dizziness, nausea, extreme fatigue, or confusion. Stop immediately if you experience any of these.
</AlertBox>

### Trail and Park Construction Updates
Your usual routes might be looking a little different, or completely blocked off. The western portion of the **Memorial Park Outer Loop Trail** is closed until 2027 for the Memorial Groves construction. If that's your usual stomping ground, you'll need a new plan. Elsewhere, sections of the **Buffalo Bayou Trail** near Downtown are closed indefinitely for "blind spot" fixes. Over at **MacGregor Park**, significant renovations began on August 17th, closing parts of the trail, tennis center, and playground. Even downtown, you'll find pedestrian and bike traffic along Polk Street (between Chartres and Jackson) discontinued due to ongoing construction, with rerouted cycling paths available via Rusk Street. Plan your route or hit the treadmill.
