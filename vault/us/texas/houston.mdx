## Air Quality Alert: Don't Breathe Deeply Today

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) forecast for Houston today, July 23rd, shows an "Ozone/PM2.5" concern, with PM2.5 levels potentially reaching the middle of the "Moderate" range. Our official numbers show an Air Quality Index (AQI) of 103, which is firmly in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category due to those fine particulate matter levels. This isn't some distant wildfire smoke making an appearance from Canada this time, though we've certainly had our share of that this summer. This is local stuff, likely exacerbated by our usual urban emissions and stagnant air, especially around the Ship Channel.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**AQI & Pollen Warning:** With an AQI of 103 (PM2.5), sensitive groups (children, older adults, and people with lung conditions like asthma) should avoid outdoor exertion. Everyone else should significantly reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor activity. Moderate pollen adds to the respiratory burden, so expect a double whammy if you're prone to seasonal sniffles.
</AlertBox>

## Humidity, Heat, and Hydration (Again!)

Yesterday, Houston was under an "Extreme Heat Warning" with "feels like" temperatures soaring up to a brutal 115 degrees Fahrenheit (46°C), prompting the city to open over 100 cooling centers. While today's actual temperature is a slightly less scorching 82 degrees Fahrenheit (28.2°C) and "Mainly Clear" right now, the humidity is still a beast at 82%. Remember what the docs at Houston Methodist keep saying: our Gulf Coast humidity is often *more* dangerous than just high heat alone because it stops your sweat from actually cooling you down effectively. Your body works overtime to regulate its temperature, and that puts extra strain on your heart and lungs, even on a day that "feels" a bit cooler.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Non-Negotiable:** Even without an extreme heat warning today, our persistent Houston humidity means you *will* dehydrate faster than you think. Drink water before, during, and after your workout. Seriously, don't skimp. Avoid caffeine and alcohol before outdoor activity; they'll just make things worse.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Trouble: White Oak Bayou Greenway Continues to be a Mess

If you were planning on hitting the White Oak Bayou Greenway for your run or ride, think again. The Little White Oak Bayou Trail, specifically the stretch between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, has been shut down since January 15, 2026, and is expected to stay that way through *2028*. This is for ongoing work on the IH-45 southbound to I-10 westbound and I-10 eastbound to IH-45 northbound connector ramps. There are detours, but let's be real, navigating freeway construction detours on a bike or on foot is a pain in the asphalt. And don't even *think* about ducking under those orange construction fences; they're there for a reason, likely involving heavy machinery and falling debris.
