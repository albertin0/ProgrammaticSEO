## The Full Rundown: Sweat, Sneezes, and Shoddy Signage

Let's cut to the chase. The air quality index is holding strong at a "Good" 35, which is a win for your lungs. But that's about where the good news ends if you prefer comfortable outdoor exercise. We're staring down temperatures in the low 80s, and with our famous humidity, it'll feel even hotter. Factor in the moderate pollen, and you've got a recipe for heavy breathing and maybe an allergy flare-up.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Hydrate, Hydrate, Hydrate!** With temperatures pushing into the 80s and the "feels like" temperature higher, dehydration is a real risk. Drink plenty of water before, during, and after your workout. Don't be a hero; carry a bottle, or plan your route around water fountains you *know* are working.
</AlertBox>

Weather-wise, expect mostly clear skies this morning, but isolated to scattered showers and storms could pop up this afternoon and evening, especially north of I-10. Nothing too wild today, but the forecast for the weekend shows increasing chances of rain and even heavy downpours, with a marginal risk of excessive rainfall Saturday into Sunday. So, if you're planning a long weekend run, keep an eye on the radar. Slick surfaces are no joke.

### Navigating the Urban Obstacle Course

Now, about those trails. If you're hoping for a serene long run along the bayous, you're going to be disappointed. The city's a constant construction zone, and your beloved routes are often the casualties. The significant, long-term closure of the White Oak Bayou Greenway between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is a prime example, running through 2028. That's not a short-term headache; that's a whole new training plan for anyone who relies on that stretch. Similar story for the Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 – still shut down.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Trail Closures are NOT Suggestions.** The White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail) between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center is officially closed through 2028. Don't try to sneak through; it's a safety hazard and an active construction zone. Find your detours *before* you head out, and for the love of your ankles, pay attention to signage around the Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5 too.
</AlertBox>

Other smaller, but equally annoying, disruptions include the Cleburne Street Bike Lane at 288 and the completely demolished Austin Street Bike Lane downtown. It feels like half our bike infrastructure gets torn up faster than it's built. Just be prepared for rerouting, sharing the road with cars, and generally less-than-ideal conditions on Houston's cycling and pedestrian paths.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Plan Your Route (and a Backup):** Seriously, between the heat and the closures, winging it today is a bad idea. Check local city and park websites for the most up-to-date information on trail status. Have a plan B, and maybe even a plan C, especially if your usual loop involves the bayous or major thoroughfares.
</AlertBox>

In short, it's Houston. We make do. Just be smart, stay hydrated, and don't expect any personal bests when you're navigating construction cones and trying not to overheat.
