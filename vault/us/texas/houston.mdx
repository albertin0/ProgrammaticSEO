## The Air Quality: Don't Breathe Too Deeply

Look, I'm not a meteorologist, I just run through whatever atmospheric soup this city serves up. Today's special is a "Moderate" AQI of 103, with PM2.5 taking center stage as the dominant pollutant. This isn't exactly "crisp spring air" we're talking about, folks. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality forecast for today, March 23rd, mentioned that light winds and warm temperatures could keep ozone in the "Moderate" range, often accompanied by PM2.5.

Factor in the "Moderate" pollen levels, and if you're prone to seasonal allergies or have any respiratory sensitivities, you're looking at a double-whammy. It's not just the stuff blooming; there was a prescribed burn at the Houston Arboretum in Memorial Park last week that pumped out a fair bit of smoke. While that was a few days ago, the particulates can linger, especially with lighter winds, exacerbating our already compromised air quality. So, if you feel like you're sucking down a dusty, flower-filled exhaust pipe, you're probably not far off.

## Weather: Early Summer Vibes, Not Spring

It's March 23rd, not June 23rd, but you wouldn't know it looking at the thermometer. We're hitting an unseasonably hot 28.2°C (that's 82.76°F for you old-school folks) today, under mainly clear skies. Houston's been seeing an exceptionally warm March, with temperatures consistently in the 80s, which is well above our climate normals. Prepare to sweat, and then sweat some more. Staying hydrated isn't just a suggestion; it's a desperate plea from your body.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Unseasonable Heat — Hydrate or Suffocate
Temperatures pushing into the mid-80s are no joke, especially when your body isn't fully acclimated to summer heat yet. Carry more water than you think you need, wear light-colored, moisture-wicking gear, and for the love of all that is holy, don't ignore early signs of heat exhaustion.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Closures & Construction: The Usual Houstonian Headache

If you're a regular on the trails, you already know the drill: Houston is constantly under construction. And today is no exception. If you planned on hitting the White Oak Bayou Greenway, especially near downtown, you're still out of luck. The sections between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center are shut down for ongoing IH-45/I-10 connector ramp work, and they expect this mess to last through *2028*. Yeah, you read that right. 2028. It's a prime example of "here for a good time, not a long time" if "good time" means "running into orange cones."

The I-10 White Oak Bayou elevation project is causing significant disruptions, with lane closures and bridge work continuing through at least mid-2026. Don't even get me started on the Brays Bayou Trail, which is still experiencing closures at Spur 5 due to the SH 35 expansion, with an optimistic (read: probably false) return to normalcy sometime this year. Always check your route before you head out, unless you enjoy unexpected detours through busy streets.
