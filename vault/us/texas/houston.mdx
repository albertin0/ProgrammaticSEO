Look, I get it. We want to get out there. But today, July 7th, is a prime example of why you need to stay smart, not just strong, when running in this city. While the air quality is surprisingly decent for an H-Town summer morning, those lung-busting heat index numbers and the impending storms are screaming "treadmill" or "pool laps."

The NWS Houston/Galveston office is predicting heat indices well into the triple digits for the entire week, hitting 100-108°F. Doctors at Houston Methodist are pretty clear: our humidity is a bigger problem than just the temperature because it stops your sweat from actually cooling you down. That means your body is working overtime, and heat-related illness can sneak up on you faster than a Houston pothole. If you *must* run outside, stick to the early, early morning. We're talking before the sun even thinks about cracking the horizon. And if you feel any muscle cramps, weakness, dizziness, or nausea, for the love of all that is holy, STOP.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat & Humidity Alert:** Heat index values are expected to hit 100-108°F today and throughout the week. This is a severe risk for heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Exercise outdoors with extreme caution, or better yet, move it inside.
</AlertBox>

Then there are the afternoon storms. Our current weather says "Clear," but the forecast is calling for scattered thunderstorms today, bringing the fun combo of heavy rain and lightning. Nothing ruins a run faster than a bolt from the blue or wading through impromptu bayous. Keep an eye on the radar if you're stubborn and insist on going out, and if you hear thunder, get inside. Don't risk it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Afternoon Storm Risk:** Scattered thunderstorms with heavy rainfall and lightning are possible this afternoon. These conditions are dangerous for outdoor activities.

And let's talk about our beloved trails. If your usual route involves the **Buffalo Bayou Park** greenway, specifically the north side between Waugh Drive and Rosemont Pedestrian Bridge, or near Johnny Goyen Park, be aware there are still closures and detours in effect. Same goes for the Brays Bayou Trail at Spur 5, which is an ongoing construction nightmare until at least 2026. And don't even get me started on the White Oak Bayou Greenway (Little White Oak Bayou Trail) between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center; that section is shut down until *2028* for IH-45/I-10 connector work. These aren't minor inconveniences; they're major reroutes that can add miles and frustration to your workout.
