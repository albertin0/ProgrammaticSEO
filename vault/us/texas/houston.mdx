## What's Messing with Your Mileage

Look, Houston running is a commitment, and today, that commitment comes with a side of respiratory roulette and persistent detours. We're under an "Ozone Action Day" for the Houston–Galveston–Brazoria region. This means ground-level ozone is elevated, and if you're out there huffing and puffing during strenuous exercise, especially this afternoon when temperatures are predicted to hit the low 90s (around 33°C) with rising humidity, your lungs are going to feel it. Even though the overall Air Quality Index is currently showing 53 (Moderate), the ozone alert is a specific warning to ease off the gas for your own good. Add in the "Moderate" pollen count, and if you already deal with seasonal allergies or asthma, you're essentially signing up for a bad time.

### Perpetual Potholes & Trail Tribulations

And then there's the never-ending story of Houston's infrastructure. If your preferred long run takes you along the White Oak Bayou Greenway, particularly between Houston Avenue and the Castillo Community Center, you're out of luck. That section of the Little White Oak Bayou Trail has been shut down since January 15, 2026, and it's not expected to reopen until 2028 because of the IH-45/I-10 connector ramp construction. So much for seamless connectivity. There are detours, sure, but they often mean pavement pounding or navigating less-than-ideal routes. The Brays Bayou Trail is also still dealing with closures at Spur 5 due to the SH 35 expansion. It's like the city wants you to just give up on your outdoor goals.
