## The Houston Summer Grind: More Brutal Than Usual

Look, I get it. We're runners. We're used to sweating buckets out here. But today, Monday, July 20th, isn't just a "tough it out" kind of day. We're still reeling from a weekend where the heat index pushed well into the triple digits, and that pattern isn't letting up. Meteorologists are even talking about our first 100-degree actual temperature days hitting later this week. That "Mainly Clear" condition might look inviting, but it just means more direct sun beating down on you, adding to the thermal load.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat and Humidity Advisory
Seriously, folks, the combination of mid-to-upper 90s temperatures and high humidity creates genuinely dangerous conditions. Heat index values are expected to remain well over 100°F. This isn't just discomfort; it's a direct threat to your health, putting massive strain on your cardiovascular system. Symptoms like headaches, dizziness, nausea, and confusion can escalate rapidly into heatstroke. Stay indoors during peak afternoon hours.
</AlertBox>

And if the heat isn't enough to curb your enthusiasm, the ongoing trail closures certainly will. Good luck trying to get a decent long run in on sections of the White Oak Bayou Greenway, for instance. A significant portion near Shepherd is closed daily until November 19th for flood control work. And let's not forget the Brays Bayou Trail closure at Spur 5. These aren't minor detours; they mess with your rhythm, your mileage, and sometimes, your sanity.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail Closures Galore
Expect disruptions. The White Oak Bayou Greenway near Shepherd is closed daily until 5:30 PM due to construction, and there's no official detour posted, leaving you to figure out safe street-level alternatives. Always check local trail updates before heading out to avoid a frustrating dead end.
