Look, I know we all love charging up those trails in the Franklin Mountains State Park, but you need to know this: **the interior of the park is closing down starting 5 PM today, October 11th, and won't reopen until 2 PM on October 15th.** That's a solid four days of no access. Don't be "that guy" who ignores the signs and gets stuck or fined. Plan your mountain runs for next week. The primitive camping area, which was closed, *just* reopened today, so maybe you can still hike in for that if you're quick, but general interior access is a no-go this afternoon.

While the morning looks beautiful, don't get complacent. Our friends at ABC-7 StormTrack are telling us to expect scattered showers and thunderstorms this afternoon, especially for those of you running or riding east of the Rio Grande. Some of these could dump brief, heavy rain and bring some gusty winds. If you're out on the Paso del Norte Trail or any of the arroyos, remember how quickly a "brief downpour" can turn into a flash flood scenario here. We all saw what happened in late September.

### Afternoon Storm Risk
Keep an eye on the sky, especially if you're east of the Rio Grande. Afternoon thunderstorms are possible and could bring heavy rain and gusty winds, leading to localized flash flooding. Don't be caught off guard.
</AlertBox>

Construction around El Paso is a perennial hazard, and today is no different. We've got I-10 widening closures on the westside between Anthony and Transmountain, with full closures for bridge deck placement until Monday. Even if you're not driving, this means traffic headaches and potential detours around your usual running routes if you cross over or near the freeway. The Mountain to River Trail also has ongoing construction for new sidewalks and ADA access at Borderland & Doniphan, and a new parking area at Paseo del Norte & Transmountain Drive. Just be aware of your surroundings, and don't assume a trail is clear if it's near a major construction zone.
