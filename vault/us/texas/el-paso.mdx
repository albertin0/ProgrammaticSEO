## Air Quality & Your Lungs

That "Moderate" AQI with PM2.5 being dominant is a big neon sign to pay attention, especially if you're usually bothered by allergies or have any kind of respiratory sensitivity. Even if you don't, sustained exposure to these fine particulates isn't good for anyone. The Saharan dust is playing a role, so don't expect it to magically clear up after a quick shower.

## Afternoon Storms & Trail Troubles

While the morning looks clear and the temperature is a pleasant 24.6°C (about 76°F), don't get complacent. Those afternoon thunderstorms are notorious for popping up fast and bringing more than just a little sprinkle. We're talking gusty winds and enough rain to turn our desert trails into muddy, slick messes, or worse, flash flood zones. Even the NWS El Paso is noting a low threat of flash flooding.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Potential for localized flooding and gusty winds this afternoon!** If you're planning a run at Franklin Mountains State Park, especially on the more exposed or lower elevation trails, be hyper-aware of rapidly changing weather. Turn around if you see dark clouds or hear thunder. That dirt turns to grease faster than you can say "twisted ankle."
</AlertBox>

## Construction Chaos

Seriously, El Paso, pick a lane. Or rather, don't, because half of them are closed. There are continuous closures on Darrington Road, various lane closures on I-10 around mile markers 76-79, and even work on Socorro Road. This isn't just a headache for drivers; it messes with pedestrian and cycling routes too. A "continuous closure" on Purple Heart Highway (Loop 375) southbound between Montana and Spur 601 was expected to lift this morning, but stay vigilant – these things often get extended or cause ripple effects.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Plan your route carefully!** Check local traffic advisories for ongoing construction. If your usual running path involves crossing or running alongside major roads like I-10 or Loop 375, expect delays, detours, and potentially unsafe pedestrian conditions. Stick to established, well-lit areas if you're out there. And as always, hydrate like it's your job, especially with afternoon temps pushing into the low 90s.
</AlertBox>
