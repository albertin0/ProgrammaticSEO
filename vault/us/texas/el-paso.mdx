### Construction Chaos: Your Real Hazard Today

Here’s where things get dicey. El Paso's got more orange cones and detour signs than a politician has promises, and a good chunk of that construction is actively happening *today*. If your route takes you anywhere near I-10, Loop 375 (Purple Heart Highway), or US-54, you need to be hyper-aware.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Major Road Construction is ONGOING.** Expect lane closures, sudden detours, and heavy equipment. Specifically, areas around the I-10 eastbound exit to Redd are impacted today for drilled shaft operations. The I-10 westbound exit ramp to Transmountain is continuously closed until August 2026. This isn't just an inconvenience; it's a legitimate hazard if you're trying to share the road or cross traffic.
</AlertBox>

Crews are working on everything from median columns to illumination on SH-178 (Artcraft) and the Borderland Expressway. Even the Montana Widening Project had overnight work shifting traffic lanes, so expect new configurations near Joe Battle Boulevard. This means unpredictable traffic patterns, uneven surfaces, and potential debris. Stay off the shoulders if you can, and assume drivers are distracted by the chaos, because they probably are.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Be Mindful of Intersections:** With various lane closures and traffic switches, especially around Joe Battle Boulevard and Montana Avenue, intersections can be particularly hazardous. Look twice, then look again. Assume no one sees you.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Trail Time?** Given the road construction, today might be a perfect day to hit the trails at Franklin Mountains State Park or McKelligon Canyon if you can get there safely. You'll escape the pavement pandemonium and enjoy that great weather without worrying about getting clipped by a cement mixer. Just make sure you've got your water and know your route.
</AlertBox>

So, yeah, get out there. The air is clear, the sun is shining, and it's not too hot. Just pick your battle wisely. Stick to the trails if you can, or at least scope out your street route for new construction headaches before you commit. Your lungs and joints might be good, but your common sense needs to be on high alert for those El Paso roads.
