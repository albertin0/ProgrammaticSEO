Look, I get it. You've been waiting for those crisp fall mornings. And yeah, the news reports some "cool starts" in the 50s and 60s this week. But by the time most of us are lacing up after work or trying to squeeze in a lunchtime miles, it's a full-on sauna. This isn't just about being uncomfortable; 90 degrees in October is asking for trouble.

Seriously, if you're planning on hitting the Katy Trail or pushing some loops around White Rock Lake, you need to adjust your expectations. While there are no official "extreme heat warnings" today – those usually kick in closer to 105°F and higher – 90 degrees with direct sun is plenty hot enough to cause heat exhaustion or worse. Remember that burn ban Dallas County put in place back in July? It might technically still be active, which should tell you something about how dry things can get around here, even if we aren't seeing active fire alerts today.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Heat is the Real Hazard Today:** Don't be a hero. Hydrate aggressively *before*, *during*, and *after* any outdoor activity. Electrolytes are your friend. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or get that tell-tale chill despite the heat, STOP. Get to shade, get water, and cool down immediately. Your PR isn't worth passing out on a trail.
</AlertBox>

On the trail front, remember that ongoing construction near Garland Road and White Rock Trail, specifically between E. Lawther Drive and San Rafael Drive, is still a thing. They're messing with wastewater mains, so expect potential reroutes or narrowed paths. Also, if you're driving anywhere near I-30 to get to a trailhead, be aware that sections of Cesar Chavez, Cadiz, and Akard Street are fully closed and detoured for the massive I-30 Canyon project. No pedestrian access in those work zones, either. It's a mess, so plan your approach.

Today isn't the day for breaking records unless that record is "most water consumed while *not* running." Get out early, stay hydrated, and don't push it.
