Look, I get it. We're runners, we like to suffer. But today, Mother Nature, or maybe just the *Dallas* nature, is really trying to test your limits. The actual temperature might read 32.7°C (90.86°F), but the humidity from Tropical Storm Bertha making its way across the region means the heat index, that "feels like" temperature, is going to be brutal. We're talking heat index values potentially reaching 105-108°F. This kind of heat increases your risk for heat exhaustion and heat stroke, no matter how "fit" you think you are. Seriously, your body's ability to cool itself is severely compromised in these conditions.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** A Heat Advisory is in effect through Thursday evening. Heat index values are projected to be well over 100°F. Limit outdoor activity to early morning or late evening, if at all. Stay hydrated, take frequent breaks in the shade or AC, and wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing. Know the signs of heat-related illness like confusion, dizziness, and rapid pulse.
</AlertBox>

Even if you're stubborn and insist on getting those miles in, be aware of ongoing construction. For instance, a significant portion of the **White Rock Trail** between E. Lawther Drive and Winsted Drive is closed until September for wastewater main grouting. They've got detours set up, but let's be real, a detour in this heat is just adding insult to injury. E. Lawther Drive between Garland Road and Winfrey Point Way is also shut down to vehicle traffic to help trail users detour safely, but you're still adding distance and complexity to your run. Check the City of Dallas Park and Recreation website for maps, or just assume your favorite loop is probably messed up.

As for the air, your lungs will be dealing with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant in the moderate AQI. While Dallas isn't under the extreme wildfire smoke advisories that hit the Midwest earlier this week, 64 AQI with particulate matter isn't exactly fresh mountain air. Your respiratory system is already stressed in the heat, so adding particulate matter to the mix won't do it any favors.
