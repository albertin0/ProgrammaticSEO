Today in Dallas, we're looking at a pretty mild 24.1°C (about 75°F) with clear skies right now, which is a nice break from the usual inferno. The Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at 33, which is firmly in the "Good" category, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. So your lungs are getting a relative break from the smog. But don't start cheering yet, because while today's numbers are decent, the Dallas-Fort Worth area consistently gets an "F" for ozone pollution in the American Lung Association's annual report, ranking among the worst in the nation for unhealthy days. That's a long-term problem that doesn't just disappear on a nice day.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### White Rock Lake Trail Closure: Seriously, Don't Go There
Listen up, runners, walkers, and cyclists: a significant chunk of the **White Rock Trail is currently CLOSED.** The City of Dallas started grouting work on an abandoned wastewater main adjacent to Garland Road and the trail back in mid-June, and this project isn't wrapping up until September 2026. Specifically, the section between E. Lawther Drive and Winsted Drive is shut down. There are detours in place, and E. Lawther Drive between Garland Road and Winfrey Point Way is also closed to vehicular traffic to help reroute trail users. This isn't some minor detour; this is a full-on "find another trail" situation. A sinkhole opened up earlier this year, so they're not messing around.
</AlertBox>

Weather-wise, don't let the current clear skies fool you. Scattered showers and storms are expected to continue today and tomorrow, with "heavy rain and flooding" being the primary concern. While the worst of the flash flood warnings from Monday might have passed, we're not out of the woods. Rain chances will gradually taper off later in the week, and then we're back to our regularly scheduled programming of oppressive heat, with highs in the mid-to-upper 90s returning after today, and heat index values potentially hitting 105 by Sunday.

Pollen is currently at a moderate level, so if you're prone to seasonal sniffles, you might still feel it.

With scattered storms and potential localized flooding, especially if you're venturing out on less-maintained paths, always know your route and have an exit strategy. Water crossings can become dangerous quickly.

### The Heat is Coming Back
While today is mild, the forecast for the next few days has temperatures climbing back into the mid-90s, with heat indices pushing triple digits. Hydrate aggressively now to prepare for the return of the real Dallas summer. Remember, Texas doesn't believe in mandated worker heat protections, so you're on your own out there.
