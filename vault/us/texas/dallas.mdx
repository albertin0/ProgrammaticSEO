## Dallas, What Are You Doing Out There?

Look, I love a good run around White Rock Lake as much as the next masochist, but today is straight-up disrespectful to your body. We're talking 97°F actual temperature, but with the humidity, it's going to *feel* like 104-105°F out there right now. The National Weather Service has slapped us with a Heat Advisory until 8 PM, and they're not messing around: heat indices could easily climb to 109°F. Dallas has been flirting with record-breaking August heat all month, hitting 100°F or more nearly every day, and today is no different. If you insist on hitting the pavement or trails, do it before the sun fully commits to trying to kill you, and keep it short. Hydration isn't just a suggestion; it's a lifeline.

### Your Lungs Are Not a Filter

Now, let's talk about the air you'll be breathing. My intel from earlier might have shown a deceptively low AQI, but the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) and the National Weather Service have both issued an **Ozone Action Day** for Dallas-Fort Worth today. This means conditions are ripe for ground-level ozone to reach levels considered "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups." So, if you've got asthma, COPD, or just like your lungs working properly, consider this your red flag.

And it gets better. Remember that Ross Fire still chewing up acres out in Palo Pinto County? Yeah, that smoke is headed our way. Winds are pushing that lovely plume southeast, so expect reduced visibility and an extra dose of particulate matter for your airways to contend with. So, while one source might give you a mild AQI, the *combination* of ozone and smoke makes it a genuinely bad day for deep breaths outdoors. AccuWeather is already calling the overall air quality "Poor" for sensitive groups for today.

### Afternoon Storm Roulette

As if heat and questionable air weren't enough, there's a chance of scattered showers and thunderstorms rolling through this afternoon and evening. And these aren't just your gentle summer sprinkles. We're talking potential frequent lightning and gusty winds that could easily top 40 mph. Getting caught out on an exposed trail like the Katy Trail or even near the Trinity River with lightning overhead is just asking for trouble.
