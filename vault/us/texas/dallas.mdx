<AlertBox type="danger">
### Stay Hydrated or Get Cooked
Mid-80s with high humidity means your body is working overtime just to keep cool. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are no joke, even in October. Bring more water than you think you need, especially if you're hitting the Katy Trail for more than a quick stroll. Don't be that person collapsing at Reverchon Park.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Roadworks
While trails like the newly opened (hopefully) sections of White Rock Trail should be fine, navigating city streets might be a pain. Plan your route to avoid major construction zones like the I-30 corridor, especially if you're cycling or running near busy roads. There's nothing like dodging traffic *and* potholes to ruin your zen.
</AlertBox>

### Early Bird Gets the... Less Sweaty Worm
If you absolutely *must* get your miles in outside, aim for first thing in the morning. Temperatures will be slightly cooler, and the sun won't be beating down quite as hard. Once that afternoon sun hits its stride, paired with the humidity, it's going to feel a lot warmer than the number on the thermometer. Listen to your body – it's smarter than your Strava PR.
