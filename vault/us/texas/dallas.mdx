### The Reality Check: Don't Be a Hero

Look, I get it. We runners are a dedicated, sometimes stubborn, bunch. But trying to set a PR on a flooded trail or a mudslide is just asking for a trip to my office with a sprained ankle or worse.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Active River Flood Warning:** The National Weather Service issued a River Flood Warning for Dallas County until 8:00 PM CDT today, April 5, 2026. This warning specifically mentions a significant threat to property or life in Dallas County, with bike trails along the Trinity River corridor potentially covered by up to 2 feet of water. STAY OFF THESE SECTIONS. It’s not a challenge; it’s a hazard.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, those trails along the Trinity River are probably more suitable for ducks right now. You might think you can "power through" a bit of water, but hidden debris or washed-out sections are a real risk. Your ego isn't worth a broken bone or a nasty bacterial infection.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Dirt Trails are Likely Closed or Treacherous:** Many unpaved trails, especially if they're not well-drained, will be slick and soft. Cedar Hill State Park, for example, had all its dirt trails closed as of April 2nd. Assume other natural surface trails in the area are in similar shape. Save yourself the ankle twist and the trail damage.
</AlertBox>

If you absolutely *have* to get outside, stick to paved paths or roads away from flood-prone areas. The good news is the air quality is great (AQI 46, dominant pollutant PM2.5, which is low) so your lungs won't be complaining. Just watch your footing and don't try to outsmart Mother Nature. She's got a pretty good track record of winning that fight.
