## Hazards & Your Health

### <AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning incoming!**
The National Weather Service has an Extreme Heat Warning active from 1 PM CDT today through Tuesday evening. This means dangerously hot conditions are expected. Heat stroke and exhaustion are real, serious threats. Avoid outdoor activity during peak afternoon hours.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, folks. If you *must* be outside this morning, get it done before 10 AM, and stay in the shade. That 26.7°C (around 80°F) partly cloudy morning will quickly morph into a furnace. Your body is going to be working overtime just to keep cool. Adding intense physical activity on top of that is just asking for trouble. Dehydration happens faster than you think, and once your core temperature starts climbing, it's a slippery slope to heat exhaustion or, worse, heat stroke. Signs include dizziness, nausea, throbbing headache, and a rapid, strong pulse. Don't be a hero.

### <AlertBox type="warning">
**White Rock Lake Trail Closure!**
Heads up, White Rock Lake regulars! Construction work to grout an abandoned pipeline means the trail between E. Lawther and Winsted is **CLOSED** until September 2026. Detours are in place, and E. Lawther between Garland Road and Winfrey Point Way is also closed to vehicular traffic for safety. Don't plan your long run there today without a serious detour plan.
</AlertBox>

If you were planning to escape the concrete jungle heat by hitting the iconic **White Rock Lake Trail**, think again. Major construction has parts of it completely shut down. This isn't some minor inconvenience; it's a full closure that forces you onto less ideal, possibly busier, or less shaded routes. Planning is key if you insist on going out, but honestly, consider an indoor alternative today.

### <AlertBox type="info">
**Listen to your body (and some common sense).**
If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or excessively fatigued, stop immediately. Seek shade, hydrate, and cool down. There's always tomorrow, or a gym with AC.
</AlertBox>

Don't push it today. Your fitness journey isn't a race to see who can get heatstroke first. Be smart, stay safe, and maybe hit up an indoor track or the elliptical instead.
