Forget trying to get your outdoor miles in today, Dallas. Unless you're training for a triathlon that involves swimming through street runoff, you should probably stay inside.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
*   **Heavy rain and flooding are the real hazards.** North Texas got hammered by storms yesterday from the remnants of former Hurricane Polo, and that mess is sticking around.
*   A **Flash Flood Watch** remains in effect for Dallas, Denton, and Tarrant counties through this morning, with more rain expected. That "overcast" weather report means more than just cloudy skies; it means saturated ground and potential standing water everywhere.
*   Pollen is only "Moderate" and the air quality is "Good" today, which would usually be a win, but it's completely overshadowed by the waterlogged conditions.
</BulletList>

You might be eyeing the cool 23.4°C (74°F) and thinking it’s perfect for a run, but I'm here to tell you it's a trap. Flash flooding has already been reported across parts of Dallas and Collin counties. We're talking more than 4 inches of rain in some DFW areas, which is more than we saw in July, August, and September combined. That kind of deluge doesn't just disappear overnight.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DO NOT ATTEMPT TO RUN OR BIKE ON FLOODED TRAILS.** This isn't just about getting your shoes wet. Heavy rainfall has caused sanitary sewer overflows in areas like Fort Worth, meaning you could be running through some seriously nasty, contaminated water. "Turn around, don't drown" isn't just for cars. Stay safe, stay clean.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, trying to hit your stride on the **Katy Trail** or around **White Rock Lake** today is just asking for a busted ankle or worse. Those low-lying sections along the creek banks and near underpasses? Absolutely treacherous. The National Weather Service is still warning that up to 6 inches of rainfall could be seen through Friday evening.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Even if a trail *looks* clear, assume it's slippery, muddy, and covered in debris you can't see. The ground is saturated, making it easy to twist an ankle on hidden roots or uneven pavement. Plus, with the recent storms, there's always a chance of downed branches or other hazards.
</AlertBox>

There's a "Spooky Sprint" race listed for today, October 2nd, but if you're planning on running it, double-check for cancellations or route changes. Given the widespread flooding and safety alerts from yesterday and into today, any outdoor event should be approached with extreme caution. The State Fair of Texas even had a delayed opening yesterday for safety.

<AlertBox type="info">
If you absolutely *must* get a workout in, consider moving it indoors. Or at the very least, stick to well-lit, paved urban routes that are clearly draining, and stay far away from any standing water. Check local news for real-time flood updates before you even think about stepping out.
</AlertBox>
