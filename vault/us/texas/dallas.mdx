Look, I love hitting the pavement as much as the next maniac, but today in Dallas demands a tactical approach to your outdoor workout. First off, let's talk about the elephant in the room: the heat. While the thermometer might say a balmy 25.7°C (78°F) right now, the high is pushing 100°F (38°C) later, and with the humidity, those "feels like" temperatures are soaring into the 105-106°F range. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous. We've been under this heat dome for what feels like forever—56 days without measurable rain, 45 days over 100°F this year already. Hydration is non-negotiable. Don't be that person collapsing on the Santa Fe Trail because you thought a single water bottle was enough.

Then there's the air. Our AQI is sitting at a "Good" 44, which sounds great on paper, but don't let that lull you into a false sense of security. The Ross Fire, still burning strong out in Palo Pinto and Jack counties, is pushing smoke plumes east and southeast into North Texas. While it might not trigger a full-blown "Unhealthy" warning everywhere, if you're sensitive to particulates, you're going to feel it. I'm seeing "fair" ozone levels peaking around 9 AM. If your lungs feel scratchy, cut it short. It’s not a badge of honor to wheeze through your run.

## Know Your (Blocked) Routes

Forget a peaceful downtown run today. The RNC Midterm Convention at the American Airlines Center means Victory Avenue, Nowitzki Way, and parts of Houston Street are basically a no-go zone until Friday. It's a security perimeter, not a running track. Your DART Victory Park Station stop? Closed. So if you were planning to zip in and out, think again.

### Triple-Digit Heat Advisory in Effect!
Despite the clear skies this morning, temperatures are forecast to hit 100°F (38°C) with heat index values up to 105-106°F (41°C) later today. This is extreme heat. Adjust your workout times to early morning or late evening, if at all. Stay in the shade, wear light clothing, and hydrate aggressively *before*, *during*, and *after* any outdoor activity. Watch for signs of heat exhaustion or heat stroke in yourself and others.
