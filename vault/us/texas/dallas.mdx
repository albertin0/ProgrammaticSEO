## Dallas: The Oven is Open

Let's be blunt: attempting any serious outdoor workout in Dallas today is just plain dumb. I'm a runner, I get it, the urge to get out there is strong. But your common sense should be stronger. We're staring down the barrel of what's predicted to be the hottest week of the summer, with daily highs hitting 103-105°F (39.4-40.5°C). It's 32.7°C (91°F) right now, and it's barely morning. Don't even get me started on the overnight lows, which barely drop into the *80s* (29-33°C), so there's no real reprieve. This isn't just "a bit warm"; it's dangerous. Heat stroke is no joke, and neither is the recovery. Trust me, I've seen enough dehydrated runners wheeled into ERs to know.

### Ozone Alert: Don't Breathe Deeply

On top of the oppressive heat, our air quality is taking a dive. The Air Quality Index (AQI) is sitting at 53, with ozone being the main culprit. The North Central Texas Council of Governments (NCTCOG) confirms that DFW still isn't meeting federal ozone standards, and this kind of heat and sunlight just cooks up more of it. Today is an "Ozone Action Day" for Dallas-Fort Worth, which means the air is considered "unhealthy for sensitive groups." If you have asthma, allergies, or any kind of respiratory issue, you'll feel this in your chest, and even if you don't, prolonged exposure isn't doing your lungs any favors.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### EXTREME HEAT AND OZONE WARNING!
Avoid strenuous outdoor activity, especially during peak afternoon hours. If you absolutely MUST be outside, keep it short, easy, and stay hydrated. Your body isn't meant to perform in these conditions.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk & Local Annoyances

Good news, though not really helpful today: the section of White Rock Lake Trail along Garland Road that was closed for sinkhole repairs reopened back on August 7th. So, that's one less obstacle if you *insist* on melting.

However, if you typically use the **Chisholm Trail**, heads up: the south side at Independence Parkway is closed starting *today* for several days. Crews are pulling sediment to improve drainage. The north side of the trail is still open, but navigating closures in this heat is just another reason to bail on your outdoor plans.
