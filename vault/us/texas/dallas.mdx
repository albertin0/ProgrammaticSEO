## The Full Breakdown

### Weather Woes: Heat, Humidity, and Downpours

It might be clear and relatively pleasant right now at 79°F (26.4°C) with 80% humidity, but don't let that fool you. Dallas is about to get a whole lot stickier and stormier. Temperatures are expected to climb quickly into the 90s, potentially reaching 96°F (35°C), with the humidity making it feel even more oppressive. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's a recipe for heat exhaustion and heatstroke.

To add insult to injury, we've got scattered to heavy thunderstorms rolling in today, bringing a good 65-75% chance of rain, especially as the day progresses. The National Weather Service is even warning about an "isolated flood threat" through Monday, so those underpasses and low-lying trail sections could become impromptu swimming pools.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Extreme Heat & Thunderstorms!** Avoid midday and afternoon outdoor activity. The combination of high temperatures and humidity is dangerous, and pop-up thunderstorms can bring lightning and localized flooding quickly. If you *must* be outside, do it at dawn, stay hydrated, and know the signs of heat illness.
</AlertBox>

### Air Quality & Allergens

Our provided data shows a "Good" AQI of 44 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. However, the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) forecasts the Dallas-Fort Worth area to be in the "Moderate" range for PM2.5 today. This is partly due to "light density smoke" from distant burnings. So, your lungs might not love that long run, especially if you're sensitive. Pollen is also moderate, so expect some sneezes if you're prone to seasonal allergies.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Moderate Air Quality & Pollen.** The forecasted "Moderate" PM2.5 means sensitive individuals might experience symptoms. Couple that with moderate pollen, and it's not ideal for deep breathing exercises outside.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: Construction Continues

If your go-to is the White Rock Trail, be aware that construction crews are still working on a wastewater main between E. Lawther Drive and San Rafael Drive. This has been ongoing since mid-June and means navigating around work zones. Always be mindful of equipment and diversions. Beyond the trails, there's general road construction across the city, including TxDOT projects that might affect your drive to a trailhead.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip: Plan Your Route (and Hydrate!)** If you absolutely need to hit the trails, go early to beat the worst of the heat and storms. Pick a shaded route and carry plenty of water – more than you think you need. Consider indoor alternatives like a gym or pool, or at least a shorter, lower-intensity workout.
</AlertBox>
