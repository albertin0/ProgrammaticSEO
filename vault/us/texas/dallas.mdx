## Today's Dallas Rundown: Hot Enough for Ya?

Alright, let's cut to the chase: it's March 21st, and North Texas is already acting like mid-July. We're looking at highs around 94 degrees today. That's not just "warm," that's "stay indoors unless your workout involves a pool and a large umbrella" kind of heat. This isn't just a one-off, either; temperatures are expected to hover in this range through the weekend, potentially hitting 95 degrees by Sunday. Your body is not acclimated to this yet, so attempting your usual ten-miler at noon is a recipe for heat exhaustion, or worse. Hydration isn't just a suggestion; it's the only thing keeping you from becoming a human raisin.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat & Wildfire Risk!
Seriously, this isn't a drill. The unseasonable heat, dry vegetation, and low humidity mean an elevated wildfire risk across the region. If you absolutely MUST be outside, go early in the morning or late evening. Carry more water than you think you need, wear light-colored, breathable clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, tell someone your route. Your internal thermostat isn't ready for this summer preview.
</AlertBox>

### White Rock Lake Trail Update: Still Keeping an Eye on That Hole

Remember that sinkhole on the White Rock Lake Trail near Lawther Drive and the spillway? The city says they've stabilized it and reopened that section as of March 13th. "Stabilized" and "reopened" are nice words, but they're still "monitoring" it for ground movement and admit they *might* need to close it again to fill an abandoned pipeline. Even the Dallas Running Club is considering rerouting their April race. So, if you're hitting White Rock, maybe be extra vigilant, especially if you're a creature of habit who runs that exact spot. It's probably fine, but "probably" isn't a physical therapy diagnosis I'd rely on.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### White Rock Lake Trail: Use Caution Near the Sinkhole Site
While reportedly "stabilized" and "reopened," the area near Lawther Drive and the spillway on the White Rock Lake Trail remains under monitoring due to a past sinkhole. Be aware that future closures for maintenance are still a possibility. Pay attention to signage and any local alerts.
