## Dallas Daily Grind: May 31, 2026

Alright, let's talk turkey about hitting the pavement or trails today. It's May 31st, and Dallas is doing its usual dance between pleasant mornings and simmering afternoons. Right now, it's a clear 24.7°C (about 76.5°F) with some humidity. Feels a bit muggy, actually. The good news is the Air Quality Index (AQI) is a nice, low 29, and the dominant pollutant is listed as Ozone/Other.

However, a word to the wise: while the AQI might look green, the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) forecast for Dallas-Fort Worth today is "Ozone/PM2.5." We've had "Ozone Action Day" alerts earlier this week, with conditions ripe for ground-level ozone to build up due to heat and sunlight, particularly in densely populated areas. So, while it's good *now*, those clear, sunny skies and a forecasted high of 94°F (34°C) for the afternoon mean ozone could be brewing later in the day. Your morning run around White Rock Lake? Probably fine. Your 3 PM slog? Maybe rethink it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Afternoon Ozone & Heat Advisory
The good AQI won't last all day. With temperatures heading into the mid-90s this afternoon and a "Ozone/PM2.5" forecast for Dallas, ground-level ozone could spike, especially during peak sunlight hours. If you're sensitive to air pollution or have respiratory issues, consider moving your workout indoors or to early morning/late evening. Also, stay hydrated if you brave the heat; it'll feel hotter than the thermometer reads thanks to that humidity.
</AlertBox>

Pollen is currently at a moderate level, so if you're one of those lucky souls whose eyes and nose decide to wage war with nature this time of year, grab your meds before you head out.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration is Your Best Friend
With clear skies and rising temperatures, dehydration is a real threat. Don't wait until you're thirsty to drink water. Carry a bottle, refill often, and consider electrolytes if you're doing anything over an hour. You'll thank me later when you're not cramping up on the Katy Trail.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, I haven't seen any major reports of trail closures or significant construction specifically blocking our beloved running paths in the last 24 hours. The Loop Dallas project is still pushing forward, connecting trails like the Trinity Forest Spine Trail, but major construction impacting current access seems to be planned for "late next year." There are some overnight road closures on I-35E frontage roads until 10 AM today that might affect your drive to a trailhead, but not the trails themselves. No wildfires are choking the air, and we're not under any severe thunderstorm warnings for Dallas itself today, though "western Texas" has a marginal risk. So, no epic natural disasters to worry about. Just your standard Dallas May.
