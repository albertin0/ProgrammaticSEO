## The Lowdown on Dallas Dirt

So, the skies are clearing and it's going to be a decent day for a run, walk, or ride – *if* you pick your path wisely. We had severe thunderstorms rolling through yesterday, and while the rain might have moved on by early this morning, the ground hasn't forgotten.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Muddy Trails Ahead (Probably)
Given yesterday's downpour, expect plenty of slick spots and standing water, especially on unpaved trails. Running on muddy trails isn't just a pain; it can be a recipe for ankle sprains or knee tweaks. Your feet will sink, your gait will change, and you'll put extra stress on those stabilizer muscles trying not to face-plant. Pick pavement or know where you're stepping.
</AlertBox>

### White Rock Woes & Cedar Hill Closures

If your usual loop involves the **White Rock Lake Trail**, heads up: there's ongoing construction for gabion wall improvements, which means portions of parking lots might be closed off for equipment. Just another layer of navigation you don't need when you're trying to hit a decent pace.

And for those of you who love to get properly dirty, the **DORBA Trail at Cedar Hill State Park is CLOSED** as of May 10th due to wet conditions. Don't be "that guy" who ignores the signs and tears up the trail for everyone else. Respect the closures; they're there for a reason, both for your safety and to preserve the trail system. Other trails in Cedar Hill State Park are open, but use common sense and check the conditions before you go.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Post-Storm Trail Etiquette
If you do hit a trail that's open but clearly still a swamp, consider turning back. If you must go on, stick to the center of the trail to avoid widening it, and try not to blast through puddles. Save the epic splashes for your post-run shower.
</AlertBox>

In short, get out there and enjoy the pleasant weather, but be smart about where you're putting your feet. Your body will thank you, and so will the Dallas Park and Recreation Department.
