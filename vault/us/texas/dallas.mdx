### Why You Should Rethink That Outdoor Session

Seriously, let's talk sense. That 22.5°C (72.5°F) "clear" morning forecast you might have seen? That's probably before the sun really gets down to business turning our city into a convection oven. By afternoon, it's a different beast entirely. We're talking about conditions where even a casual stroll can lead to heat exhaustion if you're not careful. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; it's about legitimate health risks.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat & Heat Index Warning!** Temperatures are forecast to climb into the high 90s, with "feels like" temperatures potentially reaching 105°F due to humidity. This level of heat poses a high risk of heat-related illnesses like heatstroke. Short heavy downpours are also possible, which could lead to temporary street flooding.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the storm threat. While severe weather isn't *guaranteed*, those chances of strong winds and lightning by late afternoon and evening are enough to make any exposed activity a gamble. Rapid weather changes are possible, and you could go from sunshine to a downpour and lightning in minutes.

If you absolutely *must* get out, make it an extremely early morning run or stick to an indoor gym. Hydrate relentlessly, and by relentlessly, I mean you should be pre-hydrating, hydrating during, and post-hydrating like it's your job. Forget pace goals; today is about survival if you choose to brave the elements.
