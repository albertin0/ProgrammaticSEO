## Why today is *definitely not* the day:

<BulletList>
*   Heavy rainfall and flash flood warnings are plastered across DFW, meaning streets and trails are compromised. Expect significant ponding and fast-moving water in low-lying areas.
*   Slippery surfaces, hidden debris, and reduced visibility due to gusty winds are a recipe for twisted ankles, scraped knees, and worse.

Look, I get it. October usually brings that sweet, crisp air that makes you want to hit the pavement or the trails. But Mother Nature has other plans today. We're talking about the remnants of former Hurricane Polo bringing a deluge of tropical moisture, and it's making a mess of North Texas.

Right now, we're under a flash flood watch through Friday morning, and there are flash flood *warnings* in effect for Dallas, Denton, and Tarrant counties. Forecasters are calling for 2 to 4 inches of rain, with isolated spots potentially seeing over 6 inches. That's not "a little drizzle"; that's "stay inside and watch a movie" rain.

### Trail Terror: White Rock Lake and Beyond

Areas around **White Rock Lake** are notorious for flooding, and guess what? It's been singled out as having the most flooding incidents in Dallas over the last five years. Even if that construction on the White Rock Trail between E. Lawther Drive and Winsted Drive finished up last month as planned, a fresh downpour like this means soft ground, potential washouts, and slick spots galore. Don't test your luck out there, folks.

If you're usually navigating the urban sprawl, be aware of ongoing road closures that aren't helping matters. Portions of Inwood Road and easements along the Dallas North Tollway are closed for water/wastewater main construction until spring 2027. And downtown? Major closures persist on Cesar Chavez Boulevard, Cadiz Street, and Akard Street, with Good Latimer Expressway narrowed. Some of these work zones explicitly prohibit pedestrian access, which, with flooding, becomes even more dangerous.

The air quality, bless its heart, is actually in the "Good" range (AQI 33), with the rain probably washing out most pollutants. But a good AQI won't save you from a submerged trail or a gust of wind knocking you off your feet. As for your allergies, while overall pollen is "Moderate," grass pollen is still on the high side, and ragweed is declining but present. So, even indoors, those sniffles might persist.
