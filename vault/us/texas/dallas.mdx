## Why today isn't the day (unless you're a glutton for punishment):

<BulletList>

*   **Heat Dome is REAL:** We're officially under a "potentially historic" heat wave, folks. Temperatures are soaring into the high 90s and low 100s, but the real killer is the heat index, which will hit 100-110°F (40-43°C) and higher in some spots. Even at 27.0°C (80°F) with overcast skies, that humidity is going to make it feel absolutely brutal. Nighttime offers little reprieve, staying in the upper 70s.
*   **Saharan Dust Invasion:** A huge plume of Saharan dust has decided to settle over North Texas today and tomorrow. Expect hazy skies and, if you've got respiratory issues, expect those lungs to feel it. Even with a decent AQI, dust + heat is a combo for misery.
*   **Gusty Winds, Just for Fun:** As if the heat and dust weren't enough, we've got sustained southerly winds of 15 to 25 mph with gusts up to 40 mph. Trying to run against that in this heat is a special kind of hell.
*   **Trail Trouble:** If your go-to is **White Rock Trail**, be aware that construction is ongoing. Portions between E. Lawther Drive and Winsted Drive are closed, forcing detours, and E. Lawther Drive itself has closures for vehicular traffic to aid trail users. It's a mess, just what you need on a blazing hot, dusty, windy run.

</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat Index Values

The National Weather Service is calling for heat index values (what it *actually* feels like) to reach 100-110°F and potentially up to 115°F (46°C) today. Prolonged outdoor activity can quickly lead to heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Seriously, reconsider your outdoor plans, especially between 2 PM and 7 PM, which is the hottest part of the day. If you *must* be outside, stay hydrated, take frequent breaks in the shade, and for the love of all that is holy, do NOT leave kids or pets in cars.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### WARNING: Saharan Dust & Sensitive Lungs

That hazy sky isn't just picturesque. It's a 5,000-mile journey of Saharan dust. While the overall Air Quality Index is good at 43, sensitive groups (asthma, allergies, etc.) should limit prolonged outdoor exposure today and tomorrow. Your lungs already have enough to deal with in this heat.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### PSA: Concrete is a Heat Trap!

If you're walking around urban areas, like near the Dallas Cowboys stadium for any World Cup hype, be aware that concrete and asphalt can reach blistering temperatures of 122°F (50°C). Stick to grassy areas, seek shade, and keep an eye on how you're feeling. Those hot surfaces radiate heat right back at you.
</AlertBox>
