## Hotter Than a Dallas Summer, In Spring

Look, I've run more miles on the Katy Trail than I care to admit, and this kind of heat in mid-March is just asking for trouble. We're already seeing local news reminding folks about heat safety for outdoor workers and events planned later in the day, which usually means they're worried about everyone else too. My advice? If you're not out there right now, literally, this minute, you're going to be battling heat exhaustion, not PRs.

### Your Lungs: Holding Their Own (Mostly)

The AQI being a cool 28 is a silver lining, I guess. You can breathe easy, even if your whole body is screaming. Just remember that "Moderate" pollen still means some of you will be sneezing and sniffling, especially with this kind of dry heat kicking up dust and whatever else is trying to invade your sinuses. If you've got seasonal allergies, even a "moderate" day can feel like a "high" day when you're pushing yourself.

### Your Joints: Just Trying to Survive the Swelter

Your joints themselves probably won't complain *too* much about the heat directly, but dehydration will make everything feel creaky and awful. And if you push too hard in this kind of temperature, you're looking at a higher risk of muscle cramps, strains, and just generally feeling like hammered dog crap for the rest of the day. Trust me, I've seen it. I *am* it sometimes.

  The "Mainly Clear" skies mean full sun exposure. Even early in the day, that sun is intense. Slather on the sunscreen, and seriously, carry more water than you think you need. Or better yet, stick to the treadmill. There's no shame in it when the alternative is heatstroke.
