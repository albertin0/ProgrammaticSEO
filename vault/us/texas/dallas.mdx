### Weather & Air Quality: Don't Let the "Clear Skies" Fool You

You might look outside and see clear skies and think, "Great, perfect running weather!" You'd be dead wrong. Dallas is currently experiencing "unprecedented warmth" for October, with highs hitting 93°F (34°C) yesterday and a forecast for around 90°F (32°C) today, October 10th. That's not fall; that's summer trying to sneak in one last heat stroke.

And then there's the air. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) has issued an "Ozone Action Day" for the Dallas-Fort Worth area, which is continuing today. The Air Quality Index for ozone is firmly in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" range (101-150 AQI). This means if you have asthma, any other respiratory issues, or even just sensitive lungs, you should seriously reconsider that long run outside. Even if you're a granite-lunged beast, you're still working harder to breathe this garbage in.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Extreme Heat & Ozone Action Day!**

With temperatures soaring to 90°F and an "Ozone Action Day" active, outdoor exercise, especially during the afternoon, is a bad idea. Ozone pollution at these levels can significantly irritate your respiratory system. If you must run, do it at sunrise, keep it short, and hydrate aggressively. Otherwise, hit the treadmill or the weights. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

### Local Hazards & Hot Spots

The big event today isn't on the trails, but it *will* impact the city: the Texas Longhorns vs. Oklahoma Sooners football game at Cotton Bowl. Expect massive traffic and congestion, especially around Fair Park and major arteries leading into downtown. If your usual route involves navigating this area or relies on quick access to places like the Trinity Strand Trail, plan for delays and extra car exhaust fumes.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Cotton Bowl Traffic May Affect Your Commute**

Even if you're not going to the game, the Texas vs. Oklahoma showdown at the Cotton Bowl today will create significant traffic jams across Dallas. This could make getting to your favorite spots like White Rock Lake or the Katy Trail a headache. Factor in extra travel time and consider how increased vehicle emissions might further worsen the air quality on routes near major roads.
</AlertBox>

**Pro Tip: Early Bird Catches the... Less Horrible Air**

If you're absolutely committed to outdoor activity, get it done as early as humanly possible. The heat and ozone typically build through the day. Those pre-dawn miles on the Katy Trail are going to be your best bet today for avoiding the worst of the atmospheric cocktail Dallas is serving.
