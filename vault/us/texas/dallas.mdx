## Today's Dallas Rundown: Hot, Humid, and Hazy

Alright, Dallas runners, let's talk turkey. Today, November 5, 2026, isn't exactly the postcard-perfect running weather you dream about. We've got a concoction of heat, suffocating humidity, and less-than-stellar air quality brewing, especially for your afternoon miles.

The mercury is climbing to a high of about 27°C (around 81°F). While that might sound tolerable for some, the crucial detail is the nearly 90% humidity clinging to the air. That's not just "damp"; that's "feel like you're breathing water" territory. This combination makes it incredibly difficult for your body to cool itself through sweat evaporation, significantly increasing your risk of overheating. You'll be working twice as hard just to maintain your pace, and your joints will feel the added strain from your core temperature trying to skyrocket.

Then there's the air. Our Air Quality Index is currently in the "Moderate" range at 52, with ozone being the primary troublemaker. These ozone levels are expected to be at their highest during the afternoon and early evening. If you have any kind of respiratory sensitivity, or even if you don't, prolonged exposure during an intense workout could leave you feeling rough, like you've been sucking on exhaust fumes. It's not ideal for getting those deep, lung-expanding breaths you need for a good run.

And for those of you who love your consistent loops on the Katy Trail, be advised: there's new construction at the south end near Houston Street for "The Loop Plaza." This is part of the city's grand vision for a connected 50-mile trail system, but for now, it means a temporary reroute. It's only a minor inconvenience, adding about two minutes to your run through that section, but it's something to be aware of to avoid any last-minute surprises or navigation headaches.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Stress Risk:** With temperatures around 27°C and nearly 90% humidity, heat exhaustion and heatstroke are serious concerns. Hydrate aggressively, wear light, breathable clothing, and consider moving your workout indoors or to the very early morning hours. Listen to your body – dizziness, nausea, or excessive sweating are not signs of a "hard workout," they're signs of trouble.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ozone Impact on Lungs:** A moderate AQI driven by ozone means sensitive groups should limit outdoor exertion, especially in the afternoon. Even healthy individuals might experience some irritation. Consider reducing your intensity or duration, or opting for an indoor workout today.
</AlertBox>
