## What to Watch Out For

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Heat Wave Incoming!**
Seriously, Dallas, it's going to be a scorcher. Today's high near 94°F (34°C) with a heat index approaching 100°F is just the start. Over the weekend, expect temperatures to push 100°F and heat index values near 105°F. Hydration is non-negotiable. Plan your workouts for early morning or late evening, and don't be a hero. Those gusty 15-25 mph south winds, with 30+ mph gusts, might *feel* cooler, but they're just circulating that hot, humid air and won't provide much relief from the muggy overnight lows near 80°F.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Moderate AQI & Pollen Party**
Our Air Quality Index is currently sitting at a moderate 61, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. This means if you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, you might feel it. Couple that with "Moderate" pollen levels, and it's a double whammy for your lungs. The real kicker? Saharan dust is forecast to move into North Texas late Sunday into Monday, which will further reduce air quality, especially for sensitive groups. Maybe opt for an indoor workout or keep your outdoor efforts short and low-intensity today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **White Rock Trail is CLOSED (Parts of it)**
Listen up, White Rock Lake devotees: A significant portion of the White Rock Trail is **CLOSED** due to wastewater main grouting. Specifically, the stretch *between E. Lawther Drive and Winsted Drive* is completely shut down for public safety. This construction started mid-June and is expected to last until September 2026. Don't try to sneak through; there are detours, but it means no continuous loop. Find an alternative route, or hit a different park like Katy Trail for your mileage today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Downtown Shuffle**
If your usual routine takes you downtown, be aware of the ongoing Kay Bailey Hutchison Convention Center redevelopment. The City Council just greenlit the project yesterday, which involves changes to the Jefferson and Houston Viaducts. Expect potential traffic and pedestrian disruptions, especially if you cycle or walk through that area. The Houston Viaduct will eventually be reserved for public transit, bicycles, and pedestrians, so keep an eye out for those changes.
</AlertBox>
