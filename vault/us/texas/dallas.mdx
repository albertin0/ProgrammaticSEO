## The Rundown

Alright, Dallas, let's talk turkey. Or rather, let's talk about trying not to *become* turkey out on the Katy Trail today. The primary hazard? The damn heat. We're looking at nearly 91 degrees with clear skies, which means you're absorbing every single ray the sun can throw at you. My joints are screaming just thinking about a long run in that kind of heat, and your lungs won't be thrilled either.

Yesterday brought some serious weather, with parts of Dallas and Collin counties under severe thunderstorm warnings, featuring hail and 60 mph wind gusts. Even if your favorite trail wasn't directly in the crosshairs, there's a good chance low-lying areas, especially around spots like White Rock Lake, are still holding onto some moisture or mud. Puddles might seem innocent, but a misstep on slick ground is a quick way to a sprained ankle and a week on the couch. And guess what? More rain chances are sticking around for the rest of the week, with heavy rainfall and flash flooding concerns ramping up for Thursday.

Air quality is actually decent right now, hovering at an AQI of 40, which is "Good." But with "Ozone/Other" as the dominant pollutant on such a hot, sunny day, that could easily shift for sensitive individuals or if you're out there during peak heat. Always better to be cautious.

Beyond the immediate weather, don't forget we're in Dallas, which means *construction*. There are over $830 million in highway projects tearing up our roads this year, including major work on I-30 and I-35. While this is mostly for drivers, it means dust, detours, and general disruption that can spill over into surrounding areas, even if your specific running path isn't directly closed. No *new* trail closures due to construction were announced in the last 24 hours specifically within Dallas, but the background hum of progress (and concrete mixers) is always there.

## Safety First, Bragging Rights Second

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### DANGER: Extreme Heat
  Seriously, this isn't the day to push your limits. With temperatures pushing 91°F and clear skies, heat stroke is a very real threat. If you absolutely *must* get out there, go before 8 AM or after 8 PM. Anything in between is flirting with disaster. Hydrate *before* you go, carry more water than you think you need, and don't be a hero.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ### CAUTION: Slippery Trails & Allergy Attack
  That "Moderate" pollen count combined with potentially damp or muddy spots from yesterday's storms is a recipe for an irritating and potentially injury-inducing workout. Consider sticking to paved surfaces or treadmills if your allergies are acting up, or if you're worried about slick conditions around places like the overflow areas of White Rock Lake.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Pro Tip: Ditch the Cotton
  Seriously, get some moisture-wicking gear. Cotton holds sweat, and in this humidity, you'll feel like you're running in a wet blanket. Light colors, breathable fabrics – your skin will thank you.
</AlertBox>
