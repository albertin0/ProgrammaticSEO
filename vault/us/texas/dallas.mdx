## The Rundown

Alright Dallas, it's one of those days where the numbers look good on paper, but you gotta use your head. The air quality is stellar, a solid 36 AQI means your lungs are getting a break from all the usual city grime. But don't get too comfortable. That clear sky is letting the sun absolutely bake us, and we're heading into some seriously unseasonable heat. We're talking 80s and potentially low 90s Fahrenheit by the afternoon, and your body probably hasn't acclimated to that kind of oven yet.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Early-Season Heat Warning
Temperatures today are going to climb significantly, pushing into the upper 80s and low 90s, which is well above average for March. The National Weather Service has even declared "First Alert Weather Days" for the unusually hot conditions through the weekend. You are *not* acclimated to this. Plan your runs for early morning or late evening, carry water, and don't push the pace. Heat illness is a real threat, even in March.
</AlertBox>

On the pollen front, we're sitting at a "Moderate" level. So if your sinuses are already staging a coup, you know who to blame. Cedar, Elm, Oak – they're all probably having a party out there. Pop an antihistamine before you head out, and maybe consider a quick shower post-workout to wash off any lingering allergens if you're particularly sensitive.

## Local Trail Talk

Speaking of getting out there, if your go-to is **White Rock Lake Trail**, listen up. There's a section of the trail closed due to a sinkhole near the spillway and San Rafael Drive. This thing's apparently 20-40 feet deep, so yeah, definitely avoid that area. Dallas Park & Recreation closed it down between Lawther Drive and the spillway, so plan your route accordingly or find an alternate spot for your miles. Don't be "that guy" trying to sneak around the barriers – it's there for a reason.

Also, be aware of general road construction around the city, particularly anything involving the I-30 Canyon Improvement Project downtown. While it's mostly traffic-related, pedestrian access can be restricted in active work zones, so keep an eye out if your route takes you near those areas.
