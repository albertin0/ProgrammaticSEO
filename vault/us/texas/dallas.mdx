## Why today isn't the day to push it (or even lightly jog it)

<BulletList>
*   **It's a hot, humid mess.** We're looking at highs of 36°C (97°F) with humidity sitting around 73%. That's not "sweaty glow," that's "risk of heat stroke" weather, and it feels even worse on your lungs. Hydration isn't optional; it's survival.
*   **Air quality is in the "meh" zone, with extra junk.** Our AQI is sitting at 57, which is "Moderate," and the air quality forecast shows ozone and fine particulate matter (PM2.5) possibly hitting the lower to middle "Moderate" range across DFW. Add to that some lighter smoke lingering from scattered burnings, and you're essentially breathing in a low-grade cocktail of irritants. Your lungs already work harder in the heat; don't make them filter that nonsense too.
*   **Construction, construction everywhere.** Whether you're dodging traffic downtown around the Kay Bailey Hutchison Convention Center demolition or navigating the lingering impacts of the White Rock Trail wastewater main project which was expected to finish this month, hazards abound. Don't assume a smooth path.
</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Heat Stress is REAL!
Seriously, 36°C (97°F) with high humidity is no joke. Your body's ability to cool itself is severely compromised. Symptoms like cramping, excessive sweating, dizziness, and rapid pulse are your body screaming for help. Don't ignore them. Schedule your runs for before sunrise or don't schedule them at all.
</AlertBox>

### Know Your Local Obstacles

If you insist on venturing out, especially around the downtown area, be prepared for significant disruptions. The ongoing $888 million I-30 Canyon project has multiple road closures, including Cesar Chavez Blvd, Cadiz St, and Akard St, with *no pedestrian access allowed in the closed work zones*. Seriously, don't try to be a hero and hop barriers. You'll end up twisting an ankle or worse, getting yelled at by a foreman.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### White Rock Trail Update: Proceed with Caution
Construction near Garland Road and White Rock Trail (between E. Lawther Drive and San Rafael Drive) was slated to wrap up this month. While it *should* be done, expect residual construction debris, uneven surfaces, or temporary detours that might still be in place. Always stay aware of your surroundings, and if a section looks sketchy, turn around. No PR is worth a trip to the ER.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Look Ahead: Monsoon Season Approaching?
While today is mostly dry, meteorologists are warning of significant changes. Heavy rain, hail, and flooding threats return to North Texas by Wednesday and Thursday, with a flood watch in effect from Wednesday afternoon. If you're planning runs later this week, keep a very close eye on the forecast. Your long run might turn into a swim.
</AlertBox>
