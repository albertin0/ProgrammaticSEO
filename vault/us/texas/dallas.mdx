## Today's Dallas Workout Rundown: March 13, 2026

If you absolutely, positively *must* hit the pavement or dirt today, prepare yourself. We're looking at some absurdly high temperatures for mid-March, not to mention the usual DFW springtime annoyances.

### The Heat is On (and it's not a good thing)

Let's just get this out of the way: 91 degrees in March is ridiculous. While other parts of the Southwest are bracing for triple-digit heat waves, Dallas isn't far behind, and our bodies are nowhere near ready for this kind of early-season baking. Hydration isn't just a suggestion; it's a survival tactic. Don't be that person collapsing on the Santa Fe Trail because you thought you could power through.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** The temperature is 32.7°C (91°F). This is dangerous, especially for strenuous outdoor activity. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real. Limit your time outdoors, go during the coolest parts of the day (which won't be very cool), and hydrate aggressively. Your body isn't used to this in March, so the impact will be more significant.
</AlertBox>

### What's Up with Your Lungs?

The good news is our Air Quality Index is looking decent at a cool 46, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. So, you're not inhaling a bunch of smog today.

But then there's the pollen. It's only moderate, but if you're one of the unfortunate souls who feels every sneeze from a blossoming tree, you'll still notice it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Pollen Alert:** "Moderate" pollen might not sound like much, but it's enough to trigger symptoms for allergy sufferers. If your eyes are already watering just thinking about it, reconsider your outdoor plans or load up on antihistamines.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk & Roadblocks

If your go-to is the **White Rock Lake Trail**, you're in for a rough time. A significant portion of the trail between Lawther Drive and the spillway is still shut down because of a sinkhole. Honestly, don't try to go around it. You're just asking to end up in some questionable mud or worse. Beyond that, expect ongoing construction along sections of the trail and Anderson Bonner Park for gabion wall improvements. It's a mess, and it’s not letting up anytime soon.

And for those who drive *to* their workouts, be aware that Dallas is a perpetual construction zone. There are still significant lane closures on I-20 and I-30, especially at night, from ongoing TxDOT projects. Plan your routes accordingly, and don't expect smooth sailing, even if you're just trying to get across town to a less-sinkholed trail.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Local Tip:** If White Rock Lake Trail is your usual haunt, maybe hit the Katy Trail for a change of scenery today, but be mindful of increased crowds, especially with the Mavericks playing downtown tonight. Or, consider an indoor workout to beat the heat and avoid the trail chaos.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, stay safe out there. Or just stay inside.
