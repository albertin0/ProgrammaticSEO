Look, I'm all for getting your miles in, but Dallas in mid-October shouldn't feel like July. We're still getting hammered with summer temperatures. That 32.7°C might not scream "danger" to some of you tough Texans, but let me tell you, your body feels every single degree. Dehydration is a real thing, and heat exhaustion can sneak up on you faster than a cyclist on the Katy Trail without a bell.

The sun is blazing in Dallas today, and frankly, it's not the ideal running weather. With temperatures hitting a sweltering 32.7°C (that's pushing 91°F), you're not just working out; you're actively trying to melt.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning (for your brain)
Seriously, 32.7°C is no joke. While it might not be an official "Extreme Heat Warning" from the National Weather Service (they typically issue those for sustained temps of 105°F/40.5°C or higher for two consecutive days), your body still reacts negatively to this kind of heat. Even with a good AQI of 46, heatstroke and dehydration are major risks.
</AlertBox>

Pollen levels are moderate, so if you're one of the many Dallasites with seasonal allergies, expect that glorious combination of sweat and sniffles. At least the air quality is good, so you won't be huffing smog while you're huffing and puffing from the heat.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Stay Hydrated and Go Early (or Late)
With this kind of heat, hydration isn't just a suggestion, it's a survival strategy. Drink plenty of water before, during, and after your workout. Consider moving your run to very early in the morning or later in the evening to avoid the peak sun. If you're running the Trinity Trails, remember there isn't always a ton of shade, so plan your route accordingly or bring extra water.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, folks, be smart. Dallas is beautiful, but it's not worth passing out on a trail because you ignored the thermometer.
