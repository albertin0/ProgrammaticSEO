### Heat: The Austin Summer Standard

You live here, you know the drill. August in Austin means scorching. Today, expect temps to climb to the high 90s, with the "feels like" temperature easily topping 100°F. The National Weather Service reported a heat index of 102°F at Austin Camp Mabry yesterday, and today isn't looking much cooler. There are no *official* severe heat warnings right now, but that doesn't mean your body isn't under stress. That 28.4°C (83°F) at 7:57 AM UTC is already warm, and it's only going to get worse as the sun climbs. The humidity is also in the high 50s and 60s, making it feel even hotter.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Extreme Heat Risk!** Hydrate *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. Seriously. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get inside immediately and cool down. This isn't a game; heatstroke is a real, potentially fatal threat.
</AlertBox>

### Construction Chaos: Mind Your Step (and Your Detours)

The real pain point for runners today, beyond the heat, is the ongoing construction and trail closures. If you're planning a run along the **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail** around Lady Bird Lake, be prepared for some major headaches. The I-35 Capital Express Central project is a beast, and it's actively impacting the trail. The east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing has been closed since February 2026 and will remain so for roughly a year, so don't count on it. Plus, a significant reroute on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building is in effect until March 2027 due to Austin Energy's electrical work.

And it's not just Lady Bird Lake. If you typically hit the **Shoal Creek Trail**, a major section between Shoal Creek Blvd and the Janet Fish Bridge is closed for repairs through late August 2026. You'll be detoured onto the sidewalk along N Lamar Blvd, which isn't nearly as pleasant.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Trail Closures & Detours!** These aren't minor inconveniences. Check The Trail Conservancy and Austin Parks and Recreation websites *before* you head out. Unexpected detours can put you on uneven pavement, busy streets, or add miles you weren't planning for, increasing injury risk.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**PRO-TIP:** When construction forces you onto sidewalks, be extra vigilant for uneven surfaces, unexpected obstacles, and less space than you're used to. Your ankles will thank you.
</AlertBox>

So, if you absolutely *must* get out there, go early, keep it short, stay on good pavement to avoid extra ankle strain from detours, and carry more water than you think you need. Or, you know, just embrace the a/c.
