### Austin's Atmospheric Adventures

Today, the air quality index (AQI) is at 59, which falls into the "Moderate" category due to fine particulate matter (PM2.5). This isn't exactly "crisp mountain air," and if you're sensitive, you might feel it. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) indicates that Austin's PM2.5 AQI is predicted to be at the lower end of the "Moderate" range today. AccuWeather, for what it's worth, is calling Austin's air quality "Unhealthy for sensitive groups" today with an AQI of 85, so keep that in mind if you have respiratory issues. On top of the local pollutants, we're getting a dose of Saharan dust and possibly some residual smoke from fires further south, contributing to the haze.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**PM2.5 on the Prowl:** With PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and some Saharan dust in the mix, take it easy on those lung-busting sprints. If you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, seriously consider an indoor workout, or at least cut back on the intensity.
</AlertBox>

Pollen levels are holding steady at Moderate, so if you're an allergy sufferer, you already know the drill: hit the Zyrtec and maybe wear some shades to keep the yellow stuff out of your eyes.

### The Heat is On (or About to Be)

While our current temperature is sitting pretty, this is Austin in June. The National Weather Service has been clear: we're headed for some serious heat. We’re talking heat index values that feel like a kick to the face if you're not careful. This isn't the time to be a hero and push through a mid-day run.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Imminent (or Present):** NWS forecasts suggest central Texas will see high heat index values in the coming days. If you're out there, hydrate *constantly*, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, don't leave kids or pets in a car. If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or confused, get inside immediately.
</AlertBox>

### Construction: The Austin Runner's Nemesis

Ah, construction. Our city's unofficial mascot. If you're planning a run or ride on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail today, be aware that sections on the north shore, near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club, are rerouted through March 2027. This is thanks to Austin Energy doing some underground electrical work. Pay attention to signage and staff.

Then there's the I-35 Capital Express Central Project, which continues to be a headache. The east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing is closed for about a year as they build a new Lady Bird Lake Bridge. You'll need to use the South Congress crossing or the shiny new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley. And don't even *think* about that Riverside Drive pedestrian ramp to the Boardwalk; it's closed through 2029.

If you're a fan of the Shoal Creek Trail, brace yourself. The section between Shoal Creek Boulevard and the Janet Fish Bridge is closed until late June for maintenance, so you're looking at a detour onto the N. Lamar Blvd sidewalk. The 5th Street Bridge area of Shoal Creek Trail is also undergoing repairs, estimated to last until mid-June. And if you go further north, they just started a new underpass project at FM 2222, which means more potential lane closures and detours on Shoal Creek Boulevard.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Detour Dynamo:** Seriously, check the latest trail conditions before you head out, especially if you're hitting the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail or Shoal Creek Trail. Be prepared for unexpected detours, slow down, and share the rerouted paths with courtesy.
</AlertBox>

Portions of The Great Lawn at Zilker Park, as well as the lawns at Auditorium Shores and Vic Mathias Shores, are also seeing intermittent closures for fertilizer application through tomorrow, June 26. The trails themselves are open, but watch out for equipment.

Looks like a good day to hit the weights indoors or explore some lesser-known neighborhood streets if you're determined to get outside. Just stay vigilant, Austin.
