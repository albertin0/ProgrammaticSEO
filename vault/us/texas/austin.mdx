## Air Quality: Don't Be a Hero

You see that "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" label? That's not a suggestion; it's a warning from your future self. Even though some local forecasts might call it "Fair" or "Good," the PM2.5 at 119 is what we're actually dealing with, and that fine particulate matter gets deep into your lungs.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: High AQI (119 - PM2.5).** If you have respiratory issues, heart conditions, or are just generally not trying to wreck your airways, *do not* engage in strenuous outdoor exercise today. Keep your windows closed. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Lady Bird Lake is a Construction Zone

If you're planning a long run around Lady Bird Lake, be prepared for some serious detours, especially on the north shore. The I-35 Capital Express Central project is a multi-year beast, and it's heavily impacting the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail.

The east pedestrian path and the northern sidewalk on Riverside Drive are shut down through 2029. The east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing? Closed for roughly a year since February. There's a long-term detour on the north shore, west of I-35, that's expected to be in place until 2033. And don't even think about parking under the I-35 bridge; that lot is closed for the foreseeable future.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Major, ongoing construction on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail near the I-35 corridor.** Expect significant detours, closures of pedestrian paths, and no parking under the bridge. Plan your route meticulously or, better yet, choose a different part of the trail or a completely different park.
</AlertBox>

## Heat and Hydration: It's Austin in May

It's not summer yet, but this is Austin. A "mainly clear" day at 28.4°C is plenty warm enough to get you into trouble if you're not smart. Hydrate before, during, and after. Wear light clothing. And for the love of all that is holy, don't forget the sunscreen.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip: Beat the Heat (and AQI).** If you absolutely *must* get outside for a workout, do it at the crack of dawn. Temperatures will be slightly lower, and while the AQI might still be rough, at least you're avoiding the hottest part of the day. Stick to shaded routes if you can find them, like parts of the Barton Creek Greenbelt, but even then, keep it mellow.
</AlertBox>

Your body is your temple, or at least your ride-or-die vehicle. Treat it with respect, especially today.
