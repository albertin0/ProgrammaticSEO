Today's temperature is a glorious 13.1°C (around 55°F) with clear skies. That's prime running weather, folks. Your joints are going to thank you for this mild, cool air. No slipping on ice, no baking in the relentless Texas sun. A truly *perfect* day for mobility, keeping that "Joints" part of the score high.

However, let's get real about those lungs. The Air Quality Index shows a "Good" 30, which sounds great on paper. But don't let that fool you entirely. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) issued an "Ozone Action Day" for yesterday, October 21st, warning of atmospheric conditions favorable for producing high levels of ground-level ozone. And the forecast for today, October 22nd, still lists "Ozone/PM2.5" for the Austin area.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ozone Can Sneak Up On You:** Ground-level ozone can be a real respiratory irritant, making breathing harder and potentially worsening conditions like asthma. While the overall AQI is low, sensitive individuals, especially those doing prolonged outdoor exertion, should still be mindful. Think twice about those super-intense intervals along Barton Creek Greenbelt today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Allergy Annoyances:** If you're susceptible to pollen, consider timing your outdoor activities for earlier in the day when counts are often lower, or pop an antihistamine before you head out. Don't be that person sniffling through your entire long run.
</AlertBox>

Finally, if your preferred scenic route is the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around Lady Bird Lake, be aware that the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building has an ongoing reroute. Austin Energy is busy installing underground electrical infrastructure, so expect detours and maybe a slightly muddier path in spots. This isn't a *hazard* per se, but it can be a nuisance and add a few unexpected turns to your usual loop. Keep an eye out for signage and be respectful of any crews still working.

So, go run, Austin. Enjoy the fantastic weather. Just be smart about your breathing and maybe give those electrical workers some space.
