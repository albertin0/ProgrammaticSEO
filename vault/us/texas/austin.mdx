## Today's Austin Grind: Clear Mornings, Stormy Evenings, and Endless Detours

Don't let that "mainly clear" weather report fool you into thinking it's a perfect day for a long run down the **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail**. While the air quality is surprisingly good, clocking in at a breezy 26 AQI, the forecast for the afternoon and evening is looking pretty gnarly. We're talking severe thunderstorms with potential for large hail and damaging winds, plus significant rainfall that could lead to flash flooding across the I-35 corridor through tomorrow morning. High humidity will make that 83-degree temperature feel closer to 90, so hydrate like your life depends on it – because, well, it kind of does.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Evening Storms & Flash Flood Risk
A Level 1-2 risk for severe thunderstorms is expected this evening and overnight, bringing large hail, damaging wind gusts, and heavy rain. Austin is specifically under a Level 2 of 4 flash flooding risk for Thursday. If you're planning an evening workout, reconsider. If you absolutely *must* be outside, stay alert for rapidly changing conditions and do NOT, under any circumstances, try to cross flooded roads or trails. "Turn around, don't drown" isn't just a catchy phrase, it's how you stay alive.
</AlertBox>

And speaking of trails, good luck navigating them without hitting a roadblock. The **Shoal Creek Trail** near the Fifth Street Bridge is reportedly closed *through* today for pipe and conduit repairs following a March fire. So, check before you go. The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail also has a long-term closure at Willow Street due to construction, and significant reroutes along the north shore are expected from September 2025 through 2033 due to the I-35 Capital Express Central project. That I-35 pedestrian bridge closure on the east side? Still in effect, redirecting you to South Congress or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge. Basically, if you thought you knew the trails, you don't. Plan alternate routes or prepare for some creative detours.

<AlertBox type="warning">
