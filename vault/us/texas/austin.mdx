## Austin Weather: Still Hot, Still Humid, Still August

Alright, folks, it’s August 4th, and if you thought we'd caught a break from the heat, think again. The National Weather Service is forecasting "moderate heat risk impacts" all week, with maximum temperatures possibly hitting 101°F and heat indices soaring up to 109°F in some parts of Central Texas. Even though our current 28.4°C (about 83°F) might feel manageable right now with clear skies, the reality is that August is historically the warmest month here, and this one is leaning "drier and warmer than normal." If you're planning an outdoor session later, you're playing with fire, metaphorically speaking, but also, given our fire risks, almost literally. Austin area officials have noted we're in the "historically hottest part of the summer" and seeing a rise in heat-related emergency calls. The American Heart Association has been pushing this for a while: shift your workout to the early morning or head indoors.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Risk Throughout the Day

Temperatures are expected to climb well into the triple digits, with high humidity making it feel even worse. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's a serious health threat. Dehydration and heat force your heart to work harder. Watch for dizziness, rapid pulse, headaches, and nausea. The City of Austin advises avoiding outdoor activities during peak temperatures (roughly noon to 3 p.m.).
</AlertBox>

## Trail Closures & Obstacles: Lady Bird Lake is a Maze

If you're one of my fellow Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail loyalists, prepare for more headaches. The trail remains a construction zone in several key areas. The access point at Willow Street has been closed for ages, and it's still shut down. More significantly, the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building is rerouted through March 2027 as Austin Energy installs underground electrical infrastructure. You'll need to slow down and pay attention to signage if you're trying to navigate that section. Also, the I-35 pedestrian bridge closure, part of the Capital Express Central Project, is still in effect from February 2026 for approximately a year. You won't be able to cross between the north and south shores on the east side of I-35, so plan to use South Congress or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley. And don't forget the Waterloo Greenway project, Phase 2, which continues to cause detours along Waller Creek and the Butler Trail near the Emma S. Barrientos Mexican American Cultural Center. Expect these detours through the end of August. Basically, if you're hitting the Butler Trail, expect to be rerouted, slowed down, or forced to find another path.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail: Major Detours Ahead

Multiple sections of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail are experiencing long-term closures and detours due to ongoing infrastructure projects. Always pay attention to signage and consider alternative routes. Your usual loop might be a lot longer, or completely blocked off.
</AlertBox>

## Air Quality & Allergens: Just Annoying

Air quality is sitting at a "Moderate" AQI of 54, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. For most people, this is fine, but if you're in a sensitive group – asthmatic, elderly, or have respiratory issues – you might experience some minor irritation from prolonged exposure. Given the heat, you're probably not spending "prolonged" time out there anyway, right? Pollen is also moderate. So, nothing severe here, but just another reason to perhaps skip that brutal afternoon run.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Moderate Air Quality and Pollen

With an AQI of 54 and moderate pollen, sensitive individuals might feel some minor respiratory or allergic symptoms. Stay aware of your body's signals, especially when combined with the heat.
</AlertBox>
