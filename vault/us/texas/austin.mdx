## Today's Outdoor Rundown

That moderate pollen count might be a nuisance for some, but the clear skies and comfortable temperature make it tempting to ignore. Don't. If you're prone to seasonal sniffles, you'll know it. Take your meds, or stick to the gym.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Pollen Alert:** While the temperature is prime, cedar fever season might be over, but "moderate" pollen can still hit you hard. Check your personal allergy forecast before you commit to a long outdoor session.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble & Urban Obstacles

Our beloved Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail is still a construction zone in parts. Starting this June and running through March 2027, the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building has a reroute in effect. This is for Austin Energy to bury some electrical lines, so expect the usual slowdowns and keep an eye out for staff guiding traffic.

And speaking of our trails, the Shoal Creek Trail is closed between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge for trail maintenance until late August 2026. You’ll be detoured to the sidewalk along N. Lamar Blvd.. Honestly, sometimes it feels like every trail is under construction. It's a testament to how much we use them, I guess, but it sure makes planning a long run a pain.

There's also ongoing work at Roy G. Guerrero Colorado River Metropolitan Park, with a trail reroute near the east side of the new pedestrian bridge until late May 2027. So, if your usual loop takes you through there, be ready to improvise.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Major Trail Detours:** Don't just blindly head out on your usual route. The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail and Shoal Creek Trail both have significant reroutes and closures. Check the Austin Parks and Rec site *before* you lace up.
</AlertBox>

Road construction is also a constant here. The intersection improvements at Dessau Road and Childress Drive are expected to continue for about three months, so if you're running or cycling in that area, anticipate delays and crews guiding traffic. It's not directly on a major running trail, but it impacts how you get to and from your workout.
