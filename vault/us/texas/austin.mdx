## Austin's Outdoor Obstacle Course

Beyond the atmospheric delights, you've got to contend with Austin being… well, Austin.

The first weekend of **ACL Festival** is in full swing, turning the area around Zilker Park into a logistical nightmare. While they *claim* the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail and Barton Creek Greenbelt are open, good luck getting to them without battling traffic jams, street closures, and festival-goers who clearly haven't received the memo on pedestrian etiquette. Barton Springs Road, Stratford Drive, and Azie Morton Road are all seeing significant closures. If your usual route takes you anywhere near Zilker, reroute or reconsider. Don't be that person trying to weave through thousands of concert-goers just to get your miles in. You'll end up frustrated, probably run over someone's cooler, and your joints won't thank you for the jerky movements.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### ACL Festival Chaos Around Zilker
Expect major street and parking lot closures around Zilker Park through today, October 3rd. Barton Springs Road and Stratford Drive are particularly impacted. While trails *might* be open, access will be a headache, and you'll contend with crowds. Plan alternative routes far away from the festival grounds.
</AlertBox>

And let's not forget the never-ending I-35 Capital Express Central Project. There are long-term detours on sections of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail west of the I-35 bridges, which will be rerouted through September and October. Plus, the Austin Convention Center area is a construction zone for the "Unconventional ATX" project, meaning closures on Trinity, Red River, E 3rd, and E 2nd streets through 2028. Essentially, if you run downtown, you're playing a real-life game of Frogger.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Lingering Wet Conditions
Austin experienced heavy rainfall and flood watches through yesterday, October 2nd. Although the watch has expired, expect potentially slippery, muddy, and uneven surfaces on trails, especially along creeks and low-lying areas. Stay clear of any standing water – six inches is enough to knock you off your feet.
</AlertBox>

Overall, today's not the worst day to get out there if you're careful, but it's far from ideal. Listen to your body, respect the air quality, and for Pete's sake, avoid Zilker Park unless you're there for the music.
