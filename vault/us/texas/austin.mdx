## Austin's Air: More Haze Than Hype Today

Look, Austin usually boasts some decent air, but today isn't one of those days. The AQI clocking in at 119 due to PM2.5 is a legit concern for anyone with sensitive lungs, and honestly, it's not ideal for the rest of us either if you're pushing hard. While some sources might say our air is generally "good," a 119 means we're firmly in the "moderate" to "unhealthy for sensitive groups" territory. That little "Austin Fire" you might have heard about? Don't worry, that's burning in the Mt. Hood National Forest in Oregon, not here. So at least we're not dealing with local wildfire smoke on top of everything else.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**AQI Warning: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups!** With an AQI of 119 and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, those with heart or lung disease, older adults, and children should avoid prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors. Everyone else should reduce prolonged or heavy exertion. Your lungs aren't meant to filter this crud.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Chaos Continues

If you're heading out to your favorite spot, double-check the closures. The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail along Lady Bird Lake is still a maze of construction. The north shore, near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club, has reroutes through March 2027 for electrical infrastructure. And don't even get me started on the I-35 Capital Express Central Project, which is causing closures, including the I-35 pedestrian bridge, likely through early next year. You'll need to use the South Congress bridge or the shiny new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge if you want to cross Lady Bird Lake in that area.

Even the Shoal Creek Trail is under siege, with closures between Shoal Creek Blvd and the Janet Fish Bridge until late August for resurfacing. You'll be detouring onto the sidewalk along N Lamar Blvd, which is just delightful. Roy G. Guerrero Colorado River Metropolitan Park also has trail reroutes near its new pedestrian bridge, also lasting well into next year.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Plan Your Route (Seriously):** Don't just show up expecting clear sailing. Check The Trail Conservancy and Austin Parks and Recreation websites for the latest on detours on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail and other local spots. Assume there's construction, because there probably is.
</AlertBox>
