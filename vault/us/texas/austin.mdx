Today is a day to think twice before you lace up, Austin.

  <li>Moderate pollen levels are a constant companion this time of year, adding insult to respiratory injury.</li>
  <li>Multiple significant trail detours and closures mean you'll be dodging traffic or taking unexpected routes, increasing your risk of an ankle twist or a grumpy run.</li>

Look, I get it. The sun's out, and it's a decent 9.6°C (heading up to a comfortable 25°C later) for March, but let's be real about what you're stepping into. That AQI of 119 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant isn't just a number; it's a heads-up that your lungs might be working overtime to filter out some crud. If you've got asthma, allergies, or just a general aversion to feeling like you've been sucking on tailpipes, maybe keep your workout indoors today.

And then there are the trails. Good luck having a seamless run around Lady Bird Lake. The I-35 Capital Express project is a gift that keeps on giving, closing the pedestrian bridge on the east side of I-35 until at least 2029. So, if you usually cross there, you're looking at a detour all the way to South Congress just to get to the other side. And don't forget the Riverside Drive Pedestrian Ramp to the Boardwalk, also shut down for the same reason through 2029.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  The Great Lawn at Zilker Park is intermittently closed today until 2:00 p.m. for fertilizer application. While parking and trails are technically open, expect some weird detours and keep an eye out for staff directing traffic. Nobody wants to run through fresh fertilizer.
</AlertBox>

Heading further west? Don't plan on a clear shot on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail near Lou Neff Road; there's a detour routing you onto the road itself for maintenance this month. And if you're a Barton Creek Greenbelt regular, remember that long-standing streambank repair near Toomey Road that's had a detour in place since February 2025 is still ongoing, expected to wrap up mid-2026.

It's a construction zone out there, folks. Between the air quality and the obstacle course that Austin's trails have become, today might be better spent on the treadmill or doing some strength work. Your lungs, knees, and ankles will thank you.
