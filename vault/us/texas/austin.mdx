## Workout Hazards & Local Impact

Let's be real, Austin summers are brutal, and this one's no different. Today, the moderate AQI, driven by PM2.5, means if you're out huffing and puffing, you're getting a generous dose of whatever fine crud is in the air. This isn't just about itchy eyes; it's about what you're dragging into your lungs. Throw in "moderate" pollen on top, and allergy sufferers are going to feel like they're running through a cotton field.

Then there's the heat. While the current 25.3°C (77°F) is pleasant, the afternoon high of 96°F (36°C) and those "feels like" temps pushing past the upper 90s are no joke. Hydration becomes a full-time job, and ignoring it is how you end up in the ER, not setting a new PR.

### Trail Updates: Patience, Austin

If your go-to is the **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail**, prepare for some navigational headaches. There are ongoing reroutes on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building, expected to last until March 2027, thanks to Austin Energy doing underground work. And don't even think about a quick crossing at the east-side I-35 pedestrian bridge; it's closed for about a year as part of the Capital Express Central Project. You'll be taking a scenic detour to South Congress or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch Your Step & Lung Capacity on the Butler Trail.** With reroutes for utility work and bridge closures, you'll encounter unexpected turns and potentially uneven surfaces. Slow down, especially near the Confluence, and remember that PM2.5 air quality coupled with exertion means those detours could feel extra long.
</AlertBox>

The **Shoal Creek Trail** is also a no-go between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge. It's closed until "late June" for maintenance, so assume it's still inaccessible today. You'll be shunted onto the N. Lamar Blvd. sidewalk, which, let's be honest, is not the same vibe.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**High Heat Advisory for Afternoon Workouts.** Temperatures are climbing, with "feels like" values approaching triple digits. If you *must* be outside, avoid peak heat (10 AM to 4 PM), pre-hydrate, and carry more water than you think you need. Heat exhaustion and stroke are real, people. Don't be a statistic.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Consider Indoor Alternatives or Early Birds Only.** Today's combination of air quality, pollen, and rising heat makes an early morning workout your best bet. If you're susceptible to respiratory issues or allergies, an indoor gym or a nice long stretch session in the AC might be the smartest move.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, Austin. Don't let your training plans override common sense.
