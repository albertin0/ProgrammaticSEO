## Don't Cook Yourself, Austin

Look, I love running as much as the next masochist, but 83 degrees before November? That's just a recipe for heat exhaustion. The sky's "mainly clear," which means that beautiful Texas sun is going to beat down on you with full force. Save your PR attempt for when the weather decides to act like fall.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning
Stay hydrated. Seriously. Carry more water than you think you need. Consider moving your workout indoors or to early morning/late evening. Watch for signs of heat cramps, heat exhaustion, or heatstroke – dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating, confusion. If you feel any of this, STOP. Get somewhere cool, drink water, and elevate your feet. Your body isn't a microwave.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Chaos: The Butler Trail Edition

If you were planning a serene long run around Lady Bird Lake, you might want to rethink your definition of "serene." The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail is currently a labyrinth of construction detours, thanks to the I-35 Capital Express Central Project and Austin Energy's undergrounding efforts.

For starters, the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building has been rerouted since June and will stay that way until March 2027. So, say goodbye to your usual flow there. The south shore boardwalk near I-35, while its specific weekly closure passed, continues to have *limited* access through early 2027 for a new pedestrian bridge. And if you typically cross I-35 on foot at the east side, forget about it. That crossing has been closed since February and could be shut down until 2029.

Essentially, large sections of one of Austin's most popular running routes are undergoing major surgery. You'll find yourself rerouted onto sidewalks along W. Riverside Drive or other less-than-ideal paths. Even Lou Neff Road in Zilker Park is closed through the holiday season for the Trail of Lights, so don't count on that loop being clear.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Construction Zone Ahead!
The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail is heavily impacted by ongoing construction. Expect unexpected detours, reroutes onto less safe sidewalks, and potential bottlenecks. Plan your route meticulously, or better yet, find an alternative. Stay alert for construction vehicles and uneven surfaces. Don't assume your usual path is open.
</AlertBox>
