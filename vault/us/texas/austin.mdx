## The Rundown: August 12, 2026

The air quality might be a decent AQI of 40, but don't let that fool you into thinking it's an easy day out there. The real killer today is the heat, and it's not playing around. We're looking at a forecast high of 100°F, and by midday, the "feels like" temperature will have you convinced you're running on the surface of the sun. A Heat Advisory was in effect yesterday, and those conditions are sticking around, with heat index values hitting dangerous levels. If you absolutely *must* get your miles in, set that alarm for pre-dawn. Seriously, if the sun's up and glaring, you should be done or finding an air-conditioned treadmill.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### Extreme Heat Hazard: Avoid Midday and Afternoon Workouts
  Temperatures are climbing to 100°F, with heat index values soaring up to 110°F. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real risks. Plan your outdoor activity for the very early morning and keep it short. Seriously consider moving your workout indoors after 9 AM.
</AlertBox>

On the trail front, there's still plenty of construction to navigate, especially if you're a regular on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail. Austin Energy is busy installing underground electrical infrastructure on the north shore between Brazos and Trinity Streets, meaning reroutes and narrowed paths are in effect through March 2027. Keep your head up and watch for workers and equipment. Don't be that runner who gets in the way because they're glued to their watch. Also, if you were planning a cool dip at Pease Park Splash Pad today, good news: it's expected to open with normal hours after maintenance yesterday. Just remember, staying hydrated is critical, even when you're just walking around.

  The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail has ongoing construction and reroutes, particularly on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building. A section of the trail under the overhanging cliff at Hamilton Pool Preserve remains closed due to falling rocks, so don't plan on that scenic loop.

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Stay Hydrated and Listen to Your Body
  With these temperatures, hydration is paramount. Drink more water than usual, even if you don't feel thirsty. Wear lightweight, light-colored, loose-fitting clothing and take frequent breaks in the shade. If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or excessively fatigued, stop immediately and get to a cooler place.
</AlertBox>
