## The Rundown: Don't Be a Heatstroke Statistic or a Flood Victim

Alright, let's get real. It's July in Austin, and that means even a "mild" morning can turn into a sweaty death march by noon. While your Lungs & Joints Score isn't in the dumpster today, that's largely thanks to surprisingly good air quality (AQI 49, with Ozone/Other as the dominant pollutant – barely a blip). But don't let that fool you; the ground beneath your feet and the air above your head have other plans.

First, and this is a big one: **STAY AWAY FROM LADY BIRD LAKE AND OTHER RIVERFRONT TRAILS.** The City of Austin issued a full-on waterway ban starting yesterday, July 17th, and it's sticking around until Tuesday, July 21st, at 5 PM. Why? Because recent flooding in the Hill Country and subsequent floodgate operations by the Lower Colorado River Authority mean faster flows, increased debris, and critically, *riverfront trails that are unsafe to navigate and susceptible to flash flooding.* So, if your usual route involves hugging Lady Bird Lake, especially near the Longhorn Dam or where Barton Creek meets the lake, find a new path. Seriously, this isn't a suggestion; it's a direct warning from the city.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Waterway Ban & Unsafe Riverfront Trails
**AVOID** Lady Bird Lake, Lake Austin, the Colorado River downstream of Longhorn Dam, and *all riverfront trails* until Tuesday, July 21st, 5 PM. Faster currents, debris, and flash flooding risk make these areas extremely dangerous for runners and all recreation.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the ongoing saga of our perpetually-under-construction city. If you frequent the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, you're used to navigating orange cones. Expect more of that. There are active narrowing sections on the north shore near the Confluence for emergency repairs this month. Plus, a longer-term reroute in the same area continues through March 2027 for electrical infrastructure. Even the Roy G. Guerrero Colorado River Metropolitan Park trail has a reroute near its new pedestrian bridge until late May 2027. And let's not forget the I-35 Capital Express Central Project, which will cause short segments of the Lady Bird Lake trail to close periodically.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail Closures & Construction Everywhere
Be ready for detours and narrow sections on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail and other popular paths. Keep an eye out for construction zones, especially near the Confluence and I-35. Slow down and follow signage.
</AlertBox>

Finally, let's talk about the heat. While the morning might start around a muggy 80°F (27°C) feeling like 86°F (30°C), temperatures are forecast to climb to 94-96°F (34-36°C) today, with higher humidity. We've had Extreme Heat Warnings already this summer (even if not today), with heat index values hitting 113-118°F, so this isn't a drill. Limit your intense workouts to early morning or late evening, carry plenty of water, and listen to your body. Don't push it.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration & Heat Safety
It's July. It's Austin. Drink more water than you think you need, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothes, and for Pete's sake, run early or late. Don't be a hero; nobody wants to scrape you off the pavement.
</AlertBox>
