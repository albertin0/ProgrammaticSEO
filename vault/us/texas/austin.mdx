## The Good, The Bad, and The Annoying

Let's cut to the chase. The temperature at 17.3°C (that's a pleasant 63°F for you old-schoolers) and clear skies means you *want* to be outside. And honestly, for your lungs, it's a solid win. The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a sweet 28, with "Ozone/Other" as the dominant pollutant, which basically means it's cleaner than your average Tuesday in July. Both WeatherBug and AccuWeather concur; it's "Fair" and generally acceptable for most outdoor activities. Even IQAir is calling it "good" with no expected health risks. So, go on, get those miles in without feeling like you're sucking down exhaust.

Now, for your nose and eyes, it's a different story. We're still in "Moderate" pollen season. While the searches didn't break down exactly *what* is trying to kill your sinuses today, "moderate" is enough to turn a run into a snot-filled slog for some of us. Consider it a light hazing.

## Austin's Ever-Present Obstacle Course: Construction & Closures

This is where your "joints" score takes a hit, mostly from the mental strain of constantly re-planning your route. Austin's infrastructure is perpetually under construction, and today is no exception. There are currently no city-wide weather warnings or advisories, so no excuses there, but the trails? Oh, the trails are a mess.

The **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail** around Lady Bird Lake is still a minefield of detours and closures. Access at Willow Street? Closed. Construction on the north shore near Brazos Street and the I-35 Bridge? Ongoing. You've got long-term utility work and intermittent detours west of the I-35 bridges running through January 2027. And don't even think about the pedestrian boardwalk ramp along the northbound I-35 frontage road at Riverside Drive – that thing's closed until 2029. Planning a run along the lake, particularly on the east side of I-35, means you're going to be dodging barriers and probably adding some serious mileage to find an open path.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Expect significant detours and potential delays if your route involves the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, especially near the I-35 corridor and downtown. Plan your route with an alternative or two in mind, because "temporary" in Austin often means "forever."
</AlertBox>

And it's not just the lake. The **Shoal Creek Trail** between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge is *still* closed for maintenance, with a detour shunting you onto the sidewalk along N. Lamar Blvd. There's also that perennial landslide area between 26th-27th Street with its own detour. Plus, don't forget the ongoing William Cannon Drive Corridor project, with lane closures on McKinney Falls Parkway and various intersection improvements.

<AlertBox type="danger">
Boat ramps and swimming at **Emma Long Metropolitan Park** and **Commons Ford Ranch Metropolitan Park** are closed until November 30th due to Lake Austin drawdown. If you were planning a cool-down dip after your run, tough luck.
</AlertBox>

In short, today's a great day to get out and move, but be prepared for some navigational challenges. The air is good, the weather is pleasant, but the city is still under construction. Happy trails, if you can find them.
