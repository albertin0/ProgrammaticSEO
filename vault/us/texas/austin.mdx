## The Air: More Than Just Pollen Today

Look, "moderate" pollen is standard Austin summer fare. You deal with it. But today, you're getting a side of Saharan dust, too. The Air Quality Index is sitting at 52, which is technically "moderate," but with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and news outlets confirming a denser plume of dust moving inland across Texas, those with sensitive lungs are going to feel it. This isn't just about sniffles; these fine particulates can dig deep into your respiratory system. Think twice about pushing those intervals around Lady Bird Lake this afternoon.

## The Heat: Summer's Early Grin

Current temps are hovering around a bearable 26.4°C (79.5°F) this morning, but don't let that fool you. Forecasts for today show highs around 94°F, and with high humidity, the "feels like" temperature is going to be significantly higher. We're staring down the barrel of a heat wave this week, and Monday (tomorrow) could be Austin's first triple-digit day of the year. This ain't a drill; heat-related illnesses are a real risk, especially when your body can't properly cool down.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Dust & Humidity Combo:** The Saharan dust combined with increasing humidity is a double-whammy for your lungs and overall comfort. If you have asthma or other respiratory issues, seriously consider an indoor workout.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Don't Mess with Texas Heat:** Even if it feels okay initially, rising temperatures and high humidity drastically increase your risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Early mornings or late evenings are your only outdoor windows. Hydrate constantly.
</AlertBox>

## Your Favorite Trails? Expect Detours.

If your go-to is the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, prepare for some navigation challenges. Significant portions of the north shore are rerouted from now through March 2027 as Austin Energy shoves underground electrical infrastructure along the Lady Bird Lake shoreline. That means slowing down and following directions from on-site staff, which isn't exactly ideal for hitting your pace goals. The Shoal Creek Trail between Shoal Creek Boulevard and the Janet Fish Bridge was also closed for maintenance until "late June 2026", so if it's open today, it's just barely. And let's not forget the ongoing Willow Street access closure to the Butler Trail near the 98 Red River project.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction Zone Ahead:** The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail has multiple long-term detours, particularly on the north shore. Expect slower going and plan alternate routes if you want uninterrupted mileage.
</AlertBox>
