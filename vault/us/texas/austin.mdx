Look, I love running as much as the next masochist, but today isn't the day to try and PR your half marathon outside. The air quality in Austin is sitting at an AQI of 119, thanks to PM2.5. If you've got asthma, allergies, or just a general desire to not cough up a lung, stay indoors. Even for the rest of us, running in this kind of air is just dumb. Light morning winds aren't helping, potentially increasing urban fine particulate matter. The City of Austin even flags warm, sunny days with light winds as contributing to ground-level ozone, and while PM2.5 is the issue today, the message for sensitive groups remains: limit outdoor activity.

And let's not even get started on the heat. While the current temp is manageable, we're heading for near triple digits, and the "feels like" temperature is going to be straight-up dangerous. You'll be drenched in sweat before you even hit the first mile marker on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail. Dehydration and heat exhaustion are no joke.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Air Quality & Extreme Heat
An AQI of 119 (PM2.5) means the air is unhealthy for sensitive groups, and everyone should consider reducing prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors. Combine that with a projected heat index over 105°F, and you're looking at a recipe for respiratory issues and heat-related illness. Hydration won't save you if you're not smart about this.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the ongoing saga that is Austin's infrastructure. The I-35 Capital Express Central Project has turned vast swaths of our beloved city into a construction zone. Forget a seamless run along the north shore of Lady Bird Lake; you'll be dodging detours and construction fences thanks to Austin Energy's underground electrical work through next March. The east-side I-35 pedestrian bridge over Lady Bird Lake has been closed since February, forcing runners to divert to South Congress or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge. Seriously, just assume there's construction somewhere that's going to mess up your route.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail Navigation Nightmare: I-35 Capital Express Central Project
Expect significant detours and closures around the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, especially near the I-35 corridor and on the north shore. Plan your routes in advance, be prepared for reroutes onto sidewalks, and definitely slow down. Construction is also ramping up in Northwest Austin, especially around the Pennybacker Bridge on 360.
</AlertBox>

If you absolutely *must* get out, hit it early, and keep it short. Seriously, *early*. Before the sun starts really cooking and before the PM2.5 levels climb even higher.
