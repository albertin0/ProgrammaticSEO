Look, I get it. It's Sunday, and the weather app probably told you "Clear." That's the Austin summer special: clear, sunny, and about 10 degrees hotter than you think it is. While the air quality index is a respectable 22 (good news for your actual lungs, mostly), with Ozone/Other as the dominant pollutant, don't let that lull you into a false sense of security. That 25.4°C (77.7°F) temperature you see this morning is just the starting gun for what’s coming. The mercury is set to hit a forecasted high near 98°F (36.7°C) today, and with our famous Central Texas humidity, it'll *feel* like 100-103°F (37.8-39.4°C) by the afternoon. Some models even suggest pockets could hit 108°F (42.2°C) during peak heat. If you're out there between 10 AM and 6 PM, you're actively playing with heat exhaustion, plain and simple.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Advisory: It's Going to Be Brutal

Heat index values today are predicted to exceed 100°F (37.8°C). This isn't just "warm," it's dangerous. Limit strenuous outdoor activity to early morning or late evening. If you must be outside, take frequent, shaded breaks, and drink way more water than you think you need. Your body doesn't adapt to this, it just breaks down.
</AlertBox>

On the trail front, if you were planning a nice long run on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, get ready for some unplanned detours. The north shore, specifically near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building, is getting torn up for underground electrical infrastructure. This reroute is expected to last until March 2027, so settle in for the long haul. And don't forget the behemoth that is the I-35 Capital Express Central Project; segments of the Lady Bird Lake trail near I-35 will continue to see closures for bridge rebuilding throughout 2026. The east-side pedestrian crossing over I-35 has been closed since February 2026, so plan to use the South Congress bridge or the newer Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge. Basically, if you're hitting Lady Bird Lake, keep your head up and your eyes peeled for construction signs and flaggers.

It's also worth a sober thought that today marks one year since the devastating July 5, 2025, floods that ravaged parts of Travis County, including Big Sandy Creek. While we're not facing those conditions today, it's a stark reminder of how quickly weather can turn in Texas and the long-lasting impact it can have. Always stay aware, even when the forecast looks "clear."

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Annoying but Necessary Trail Detours
Construction on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail on the north shore, near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club, means reroutes. Expect impacts from the I-35 Capital Express Central project as well, particularly around the I-35 bridge crossings. Slow down, pay attention to signage, and share the rerouted paths respectfully.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration is Your Best Friend
With "very high heat and humidity" being the standard for July in Austin, proper hydration isn't optional. Pre-hydrate starting *now*. Carry more water than you think you need, and consider electrolytes. Your body will thank you.
</AlertBox>
