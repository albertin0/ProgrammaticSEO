## Local Hazards You Need To Know

Alright, let's talk real Austin. First off, if you were planning on a long run down the **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail** around Lady Bird Lake, be aware. Even without direct flooding, those underpasses can collect standing water in a hurry during a decent downpour. You don't want to wade through mystery puddles, trust me.

We've got reports of localized heavy rain throughout the morning. This isn't just a "get a little wet" situation; it's a "flash flood potential" situation. Keep an eye on local news for road closures, especially low-water crossings. We've all seen how quickly Shoal Creek can turn into a raging river, right?

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### Lightning & Flash Flooding Risk
  This is a full-blown thunderstorm, not just some dramatic clouds. Lightning is a serious, life-threatening hazard. Do NOT be outside during a lightning storm. Seek shelter immediately. Furthermore, flash flooding is a real possibility. Turn around, don't drown.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ### Slippery Surfaces & Tree Limbs
  Wet trails are treacherous. Concrete paths will be slick, and unpaved sections, particularly on trails like the Barton Creek Greenbelt, will be muddy and prone to erosion. Watch out for fallen tree limbs or debris washed onto trails by heavy rains and high winds.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Indoor Alternatives Exist, Folks
  Look, I get it. You love your outdoor runs. But sometimes, a treadmill at the gym or a killer bodyweight session at home is the smarter play. Your joints (and your life) will thank you. Save the heroics for race day, not for dodging lightning.
</AlertBox>

Honestly, today's a great day to catch up on laundry, foam roll, or just, you know, not risk an ER visit. If you absolutely *must* get out, wait until the storm passes completely, then proceed with extreme caution on paved surfaces. And for the love of all that is holy, bring water. It's still Austin in June, even with the rain.
