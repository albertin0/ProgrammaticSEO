## The Good, The Bad, and The Sweaty

Look, mornings in Austin right now are a tease. You step outside, feel that relative cool, and think, "Yeah, I've got this." Don't be fooled. We're staring down another day where the mercury is going to climb well into the mid-to-high 30s Celsius (90s Fahrenheit), possibly even brushing 38C (100F) later on. Your body is going to feel that. Even with the "Mainly Clear" skies, the sun is a brutal taskmaster in late August. Hydrate like your life depends on it – because, out there, it kind of does. No major weather warnings or advisories for Austin today, thankfully, but that heat itself is a hazard.

### Trail Trouble & Construction Chaos

If your usual loop involves the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, prepare for some navigation. It's a patchwork of detours right now. You've got long-term utility work west of the I-35 bridges that's been rerouting the trail since August 20th and will continue through September and October. Plus, more reroutes on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club are ongoing until next March for electrical infrastructure. And if you typically hit the Shoal Creek Trail, it's still largely closed between Shoal Creek Blvd and the Janet Fish Bridge, with a detour pushing you onto the N Lamar Blvd sidewalk. Seriously, check the websites before you head out, or you'll be adding extra mileage to your run in the form of frustrated backtracking.

### Keep an Eye Out

<AlertBox type="info">
The good news is Austin's Air Quality Index is currently sitting pretty at 39, which is "Good." So, no wildfire smoke choking your lungs today, despite the high wildfire risk and burn ban across Travis County. This is for *our* Austin, not that one in Oregon that's actually on fire.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
Yes, there was a house collapse in South Austin this past Thursday *after* high winds. While no current high wind advisories are in place for today, always be aware of your surroundings, especially if you're near older structures or areas with recent storm damage.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
Don't underestimate the heat, even on a clear day. The long-range forecast had late August pegged as one of the hottest periods, and it's delivering. If you're running midday, you're asking for trouble. Stick to early mornings or late evenings. Drink more water than you think you need. Seriously.
</AlertBox>
