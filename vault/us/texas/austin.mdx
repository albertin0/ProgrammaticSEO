### Current Hazards

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Waterways Closed: Lady Bird Lake and Riverfront Trails are OFF-LIMITS.**
Following last week's historic Hill Country flooding, the Lower Colorado River Authority (LCRA) has been opening floodgates from the Highland Lakes, sending a surge of water and debris downstream. This means Lady Bird Lake, Lake Austin, and the Colorado River downstream of Longhorn Dam are officially closed to all watercraft until **5 PM on Tuesday, July 21st**. More importantly for runners and cyclists, authorities are strongly urging everyone to **avoid riverfront trails** near these waterways. Fast currents and hidden underwater debris make these areas seriously unsafe. Don't be "that guy" who thinks they know better.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Air Quality is Rough on the Lungs.**
Our Air Quality Index (AQI) is 119, primarily due to PM2.5. This isn't just a number; it means the air is "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups." If you have asthma, allergies, or just sensitive lungs, you'll want to reduce your time outdoors. Even healthy individuals might experience some throat irritation or difficulty breathing during prolonged activity.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail Construction & Detours.**
If you're planning on running the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, be aware of ongoing construction. There’s a long-term reroute on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building, expected to last through March 2027, for Austin Energy installing underground electrical infrastructure. Also, parts of the trail near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club are narrowed for emergency repairs this month, and the Confluence lattice bridge is closed. Pay attention to signage and on-site staff, and slow your roll through these areas.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Heat is building, but not today.**
While the "feels like" temperature is creeping up this week, forecast to hit 100-105 degrees Fahrenheit by Wednesday, today's actual 25.4°C (77.7°F) is relatively mild for late July in Austin. However, with 90% humidity, you'll still be sweating buckets. Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate.
</AlertBox>
