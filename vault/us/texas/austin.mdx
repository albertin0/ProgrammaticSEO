## Today's Austin Workout Rundown: Dodge the Heat & Detours

Alright, folks, let's talk about hitting the pavement or trails in Austin today. The sun's out, which sounds great, but don't let that fool you. We're staring down another classic Austin August day, which means the mercury is heading north, fast. While your weather app might show a pleasant 27°C (80.6°F) right now, just remember, August is typically the hottest month of the year for Central Texas, with daily highs expected to soar into the high 90s and even triple digits. Factor in the moderate air quality with PM2.5 hanging around and moderate pollen, and your lungs might be working overtime even before you hit your stride.

### Heat Advisory: It's Already Brewing

You know the drill. Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate. And then hydrate some more. The heat is the biggest challenge today. Even if you're an early bird, that sun is relentless, and pavement radiates heat long after dawn. The Texas A&M Forest Service even notes that August is the peak month for wildfires due to hot, dry conditions across the state, though thankfully no direct heavy smoke impacts are noted for Austin's air quality today. Still, high temps affect everyone, so listen to your body and cut your workout short if you start feeling anything off.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch for Heat Exhaustion:** Keep an eye out for dizziness, excessive sweating, nausea, and cramping. If you feel any of these, stop immediately, find shade, and cool down. Don't be a hero; heat wins every time.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Closures & Construction Chaos

Now, for the fun part: trying to actually *run* somewhere. If your usual go-to is the **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail**, be prepared for some navigation challenges. The I-35 Capital Express Central Project is a long-haul beast, and it's messing with our beloved trail. The east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing has been closed since February 2026 for bridge construction and isn't expected to reopen for about a year. This means you can't easily cross Lady Bird Lake on the east side of I-35. Your options are to head west to South Congress or try out the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley, which opened back in February.

And it's not just the Butler Trail. The **Shoal Creek Trail** is also experiencing significant closures between Shoal Creek Boulevard and the Janet Fish Bridge for resurfacing work, expected to last until late August 2026. There's also an ongoing detour around a landslide area near 26th-27th Street. These aren't minor inconveniences; they require proper detours, adding length and potentially less ideal running surfaces to your planned route.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**I-35 Trail Closures Are Real:** Don't assume you can just power through. The I-35 Capital Express Central Project means *major* detours and closures, especially around the Lady Bird Lake section of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail. Plan your route carefully to avoid hitting a dead end.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Local Tip:** If you're planning a long run, consider loops or out-and-back routes that avoid the I-35 corridor and the closed sections of Shoal Creek. Stick to neighborhood streets if you need uninterrupted miles, or head to parks like Zilker earlier in the day to beat the worst of the heat and crowds.
</AlertBox>

Don't let this scare you indoors completely, but be smart about it. Today calls for extra vigilance, especially with the heat and the urban obstacle course our trails have become.
