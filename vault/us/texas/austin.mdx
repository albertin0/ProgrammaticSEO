## Austin's Outdoor Workout Lowdown: May 16, 2026

Alright, Austin, let's talk turkey about getting your sweat on outside today. The good news? The air's not trying to actively murder your lungs, and the overcast sky means you won't be melting into the Barton Springs pool deck by 9 AM. The AQI is a sweet 38, and a balmy 22.6°C (about 72°F) is practically begging you to get out there. Sounds ideal, right?

Wrong.

While your lungs might be happy, your joints are probably going to be doing some fancy footwork to navigate all the urban chaos.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Gaps (and the Detours)

Seriously, if you had plans to hit the **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail** near the I-35 bridges, or even part of the **Shoal Creek Trail**, you need to adjust. There's so much construction going on, it's like a perpetual urban obstacle course. The pedestrian ramp to the Boardwalk along northbound I-35 frontage road at Riverside Drive is closed until 2029, and major reroutes are in effect on the north shore of Lady Bird Lake due to the TxDOT I-35 Capital Express Central project through *2033*. That's not a typo. Thirty-three. Expect significant detours and blocked access points. Find another way or prepare to get creative with your mileage.

And speaking of Shoal Creek, sections are straight-up closed. Between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge, it's shut down until May 18 for maintenance. The Fifth Street Bridge area? Closed for pipe and conduit repairs until May 20. Seriously, check the City of Austin Parks and Rec closures before you head out, or you'll be doing more frustrated walking than running.
</AlertBox>

### Flash Flood Forecast

Okay, no *active* major flooding as of this morning, but don't get complacent. "Severe weather/Flash Flooding" is in the forecast, and heavy rains *can* lead to flash flooding. With the day being overcast and increasing rain chances for next week, keep an eye on the sky and check ATXfloods.com if you're planning to run near any low water crossings. Remember the golden rule: Turn Around, Don't Drown. That goes for your run, too.
