## What's Out There Today: The Good, The Bad, and The Orange Cones

Listen, the air quality is actually *good* today with an AQI of 32. That's practically a miracle for your lungs, so take advantage of it before cedar season decides to make an unwelcome encore. However, that's about where the "easy" news ends.

### Construction Chaos on Your Favorite Routes

If you're planning a long run or ride around Lady Bird Lake, especially on the north shore, prepare for some navigation challenges. The I-35 Capital Express Central project has our main artery, the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, in a perpetual state of rerouting. They're even building a massive dock west of I-35 to ferry construction materials, which means increased noise and activity. Good luck finding your zen with all that going on.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail: Expect Major Detours!**
The I-35 Capital Express Central project has significant and long-term reroutes on the north shore of the Lady Bird Lake Trail, continuing through 2033. Additionally, the pedestrian ramp to the Boardwalk along northbound I-35 frontage road at Riverside Drive is closed through 2029. Be ready for unexpected turns, heavy equipment, and general chaos.
</AlertBox>

And it's not just the big lake trail. The Shoal Creek Trail between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge is still closed for maintenance until May 18th. So much for a serene jog there. And if you usually connect to the Red Line Trail or Lance Armstrong Bikeway, be aware of the East 5th St & Red Line Trail improvements impacting several networks.

### Heat Check: It's May, Y'all

While we're not hitting triple digits *yet*, 28.4°C (83°F) with clear skies is enough to get you sweating buckets. We've seen May bring 90s and even 100-degree days in Austin before. Your body isn't fully acclimated to peak summer heat just yet, so don't push it like it's a cool October morning.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Not Optional.**
Even at 83 degrees, Austin heat is no joke. Carry more water than you think you need, especially if you're out there between noon and 3 PM when the sun is strongest. Don't wait until you're parched; sip constantly. Headaches, dizziness, or nausea? Get to shade, cool down, and hydrate *immediately*. Those are early signs of heat exhaustion.
</AlertBox>

### Wildfire Watch

The AQI is good today, which means no active smoke plumes are messing with our air. However, it's worth remembering that Austin and Travis County issued a disaster declaration earlier this year due to increased wildfire risk, and wildfire remains a significant threat in Central Texas thanks to climate change, drought, and flammable vegetation. Always be aware of your surroundings, especially if you're venturing into greenbelts or more remote areas.
