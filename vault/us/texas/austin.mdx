## Don't Be a Heat Casualty, Austin

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** Austin-Travis County EMS has been busy with heat-related illness calls all month. Today's forecast high of 92°F, coupled with high humidity, means it's going to *feel* hotter than that. If you're running midday, you're asking for trouble. Stick to early mornings or late evenings, hydrate aggressively, and wear light, loose clothing. Take breaks in the shade. Your run isn't worth a trip to the ER.
</AlertBox>

## Your Favorite Trails Are a Mess

If you're planning a long run around Lady Bird Lake, good luck. The **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail** is basically an obstacle course. There are multiple, overlapping construction projects that are narrowing sections and forcing detours.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Butler Trail North Shore Closures:** Expect narrowing near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building for emergency repairs *throughout July*. Plus, another section on the north shore in the same general area is being rerouted *through March 2027* for Austin Energy electrical work. You'll need to slow down significantly or find alternative routes. The Confluence lattice bridge is entirely closed.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**I-35 Capital Express Central Project Impacts:** The I-35 project means long-term disruptions to the Butler Trail. Short segments will be closing for about a week at a time, up to three times a year, in 2026. The parking lot under the I-35 bridge is also closed, and the east-side pedestrian crossing over I-35 on Lady Bird Lake has been closed since February for bridge construction and will be for about a year. Plan to cross Lady Bird Lake at South Congress or use the Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley.
</AlertBox>

The good news? Air quality is actually pretty decent today. Our AQI is low, and while there's some Saharan dust and distant wildfire smoke floating around the state, it's expected to remain mostly aloft and won't significantly impact our ground-level air quality today.

In short: Get out there, but be smart about it. Hydrate, pick your times, and for the love of all that is holy, pay attention to those trail detour signs. They're there for a reason, even if they feel like a personal attack on your Strava segment.
