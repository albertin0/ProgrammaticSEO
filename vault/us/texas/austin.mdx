## The Rundown for June 10, 2026

Alright, Austin, let's talk brass tacks. It's Wednesday, and while the overcast skies might trick you into thinking it's a perfect morning, the air quality isn't doing your lungs any favors. With an AQI of 119 and fine particulate matter (PM2.5) being the main culprit, you're looking at air that's firmly in the "Moderate" zone. That means sensitive groups, like anyone with asthma or respiratory issues, should seriously reconsider intense outdoor workouts. Even if you're a seasoned marathoner, why make your lungs work harder than they need to?

<AlertBox type="warning">
**AQI is Moderate (119):** PM2.5 levels are elevated. If you're sensitive, or just value clean air in your lungs, consider an indoor workout or dialing back the intensity today. Your body will thank you.
</AlertBox>

And let's not pretend that mild-ish morning temperature is going to last. We're on the cusp of some serious summer heat, with the National Weather Service forecasting temperatures to climb into the mid-to-upper 90s by the weekend. Humidity is also a factor, making that 25.4°C feel a lot more oppressive. Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate, and consider shifting your runs to the very early morning or late evening if you insist on being outside.

### Trail Troubles: Lady Bird Lake and Beyond

If you're a regular on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, you already know the drill: construction, construction, and more construction. Starting this month and running through March 2027, the north shore near the Austin Rowing Club is being rerouted for Austin Energy's underground electrical work. Add to that the ongoing I-35 Capital Express Central Project, which has parts of the north shore rerouted until *2033* and the east-side pedestrian crossing on I-35 closed for a year. Seriously, planning a long run around Lady Bird Lake right now requires the navigation skills of a seasoned explorer.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail Reroutes & Closures:** Significant sections of the north shore are rerouted or closed due to ongoing construction for both Austin Energy and the I-35 Capital Express Central Project. Expect delays, detours onto less safe sidewalks, and frustration. Check the latest maps before you head out.
</AlertBox>

The Shoal Creek Trail isn't much better, with closures between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge expected until late June. And let's not forget the indefinite closure further north due to a landslide. Basically, if you prefer trails to pounding the pavement, you're going to have to be flexible or get creative.
