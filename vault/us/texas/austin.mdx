Listen up, Austin. Today is another one of those days where the city is actively trying to keep you from enjoying a decent outdoor workout.

<LungsJointsScore score={4} />

<BulletList>

*   **Your Lungs Will Hate You:** We've got an AQI of 119, firmly in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" range, thanks to PM2.5. Toss in moderate pollen, and you're looking at a recipe for a wheezing good time.
*   **Sticky & Soggy:** While the severe weather from last night has *mostly* cleared out, a Flood Advisory was in effect for parts of Travis and Hays counties until the wee hours of Monday morning. Expect lingering damp, potentially slick, and muddy conditions on unpaved trails. Plus, it's already 83 degrees and heading back to the 90s later this week. Hydrate, people.
*   **Playgrounds Are Out, Trails Are… Questionable:** All Austin Parks and Recreation facilities are shut down today for staff training. That means no access to pools, splash pads, or even staffed rec centers. Parks and green spaces remain technically open, but good luck finding an open restroom or water fountain. Barton Springs Pool is closed after its early morning "swim at your own risk" period.
*   **The Ann and Roy Butler Trail is a Minefield of Detours:** Construction on the I-35 Capital Express Central project continues to chew up sections of our beloved Lady Bird Lake loop. Don't even think about using the east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing; it's closed for a year to build a new bridge. Your options are now the west-side sidewalk, South Congress, or the recently opened Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley. The pedestrian ramp to the Boardwalk at Riverside Drive is also shut down through 2029. And if you usually hit the Shoal Creek Trail, forget it – parts are closed until May 18th.

#### Air Quality Alert!
The AQI is currently 119 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. This means it's unhealthy for sensitive groups. If you have respiratory issues, heart conditions, or are older or pregnant, consider taking your workout indoors. Even healthy individuals might experience symptoms with prolonged exposure.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Trail Conditions & Closures
Recent heavy rainfall and ongoing construction projects mean you'll encounter detours and potentially slick, muddy spots on popular trails, especially the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail. Pay attention to signage and consider alternative routes. Specifically, the Shoal Creek Trail from Shoal Creek Blvd. to Janet Fish Bridge is closed until May 18, 2026.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Hydration is Key, Even if it's Not "Extreme" Heat
At 83 degrees Fahrenheit, you're going to sweat. With many city facilities closed today, public water fountains might be inaccessible. Carry more water than you think you need.
</AlertBox>

Look, I get it. We're runners. We like to suffer. But sometimes, even I have to admit the conditions are just stacking up too high. If you absolutely *must* get out there, avoid the peak heat of the day, stick to well-known, paved routes where you can avoid the mud and construction chaos, and for the love of all that is holy, check the air quality *again* before you step out the door. Your lungs will thank you, and your physical therapist (me) will be slightly less annoyed when you show up with a respiratory infection instead of a sprained ankle from a construction detour.
