## Today's Outdoor Rundown: Austin, August 17, 2026

Alright, let's cut to the chase. If you *must* get out there, be smart, be careful, and for the love of all that is holy, bring water. Lots of it.

### The Heat is No Joke

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning!
Today's high is expected to hit a brutal 103°F (39°C). This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous for sustained outdoor activity. Heat exhaustion and heatstroke are real, and they can hit you faster than you think, especially if you're pushing it. Adjust your expectations, scale back your distance, and consider taking your workout indoors. Seriously, nobody needs to be a hero out on the Barton Springs Greenbelt in this kind of oven.

### Trail Closures & Urban Obstacles

If you're planning a run or ride around Lady Bird Lake, be aware. The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail is facing a significant reroute on the north shore near the Confluence and the Austin Rowing Club building. This electrical infrastructure project is going to be ongoing until March 2027. Expect staff on-site guiding you, so don't be a jerk and ignore them.

Adding to the fun, the I-35 Capital Express Central Project has shut down the pedestrian bridge on the east side of I-35, and that's going to be closed for about a year. You won't be able to cross between the north and south shores there. Your alternatives are the South Congress bridge or the newer Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley. And don't even think about using the Riverside Drive pedestrian ramp to the Boardwalk — that's closed until 2029. It's a mess out there, so plan your routes carefully, or you'll be doing more unexpected detours than actual training.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Widespread Construction & Detours
Key trails like the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail have significant detours and closures. The east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing over Lady Bird Lake is closed for roughly a year as part of the Capital Express Central Project. Always check local alerts and be prepared for reroutes, especially if your route involves downtown or the lakefront.
</AlertBox>

### Air Quality & Pollen Check

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is currently sitting at a "Good" 46, with ozone as the dominant pollutant. While the NWS hasn't declared an Ozone Action Day today, warm, sunny days like this *do* contribute to ground-level ozone, which isn't great for your lungs, especially if you're sensitive. Keep an eye on it.

Pollen's moderate today. Nothing to write home about, but if you're prone to allergies, you'll feel it.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Ozone on the Horizon
Despite a generally "Good" AQI of 46, ozone is the dominant pollutant. Austin often sees elevated ozone levels on hot, sunny days. While no Ozone Action Day is currently declared, keep this in mind if you have respiratory sensitivities or plan to be out during peak sun hours.
