## The Daily Rundown: What's Out There Today

Today, May 29, 2026, the air quality is surprisingly good, clocking in at an AQI of 28, with ozone as the main culprit. That's a green light for your respiratory system, so go ahead and suck in that Austin air (what little of it isn't exhaust, anyway). The temperature is a mild 28.4°C (about 83°F) and it's mainly clear. Don't let that lull you into a false sense of security, though. We're on the edge of "Austin summer," and the heat warnings for Central Texas are already talking triple digits, potentially making this our hottest May ever. Hydration isn't a suggestion, it's a fundamental requirement.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Austin Heat: It's Coming For You
While today isn't scorching, AccuWeather's monthly forecast shows daily highs for May ranging from 88° to 94°F, with a tropical storm threat looming for late May. "Warm and dry conditions for the next few days before rain chances return" means things are heating up before any relief. Plan your longer runs for early mornings, carry more water than you think you need, and don't be a hero. Heat exhaustion is a real buzzkill.
</AlertBox>

### Construction Zone: Welcome to Austin's Permanent State

Ah, construction. The true spirit animal of Austin. Our trails are perpetually under siege.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Lady Bird Lake Trail (Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail) — Proceed With Extreme Caution (or Avoid)
If your plan was a peaceful loop around Lady Bird Lake, think again. The north shore, near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building, is getting rerouted starting in June 2026 and continuing through March 2027. They're installing underground electrical infrastructure. That means sharing space, slowing down, and following staff instructions. Don't be "that guy" who thinks the rules don't apply to them. Furthermore, trail access at Willow Street is shut down for roughly 64 months due to the 98 Red River project.

And let's not forget the I-35 Capital Express Central Project. That beast of a project means a long-term detour starting early 2025 and lasting a cool 8 years. The I-35 pedestrian bridge over Lady Bird Lake has been closed since February 9, 2026, for about a year. If you need to cross, you're hoofing it to South Congress or using the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley, which thankfully opened back in February. Seriously, if you're not planning your route around current construction, you're not running in Austin.
</AlertBox>

The good news? The Shoal Creek Trail closure near the Fifth Street Bridge for pipe repair is scheduled to *end today*, May 29. So, maybe a tiny win there. But remember, another section of Shoal Creek Trail (24th to 29th along N. Lamar) is indefinitely closed due to a landslide, and the section between Shoal Creek Blvd. and Janet Fish Bridge is closed until late June. So, you win some, you lose a lot.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Watch Out For E-Bikes (and Be Smart if You're on One)
Austin police are getting more complaints about reckless e-bike riders. They're running stop signs, flying down sidewalks and trails, and generally making a nuisance of themselves. If you're on foot, keep your head on a swivel. If you're on an e-bike, for the love of all that is holy, slow down on trails, follow traffic laws, and wear a helmet (especially if you're under 18, it's required by ordinance). Nobody wants to be part of a "reckless e-bike accident" statistic.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Wildfire Awareness: It's Not Just for Summer Anymore
May is National Wildfire Awareness Month, and for good reason. We've already had 19 fires burn over 1,800 acres in Central Texas this year. While recent rains might have greened things up, that just means more fuel when it inevitably dries out. Be mindful of anything that can cause a spark, especially if you're out near the greenbelts or more undeveloped areas. One stray cigarette butt or dragging trailer chain can ruin everyone's day (and property).
</AlertBox>
