Look, I'm all for getting your miles in, but let's be realistic. Today, May 2nd, 2026, we're waking up to the aftermath of some decent rain yesterday, which had a Flood Watch in effect for Central Texas until last night. While the skies are clear and the temperature a crisp 10.5°C (that's a lovely 51°F for you Celsius-averse folks), those "nicer, brighter, and drier" conditions don't magically un-muddy the trails. Expect some slick spots, especially on unpaved surfaces along the Greenbelt or the lesser-maintained sections of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike and Bike Trail.

Speaking of the Ann and Roy Butler, if that's your usual stomping ground, brace yourself. The I-35 Capital Express Central project is still going strong, which means "significant north shore trail reroutes" are a daily reality until 2033. Remember that pedestrian ramp to the Boardwalk near Riverside Drive? Closed until 2029. And if you typically cross on the east side of I-35, that pedestrian crossing has been shut down since February 9, 2026, for about a year while they rebuild the Lady Bird Lake Bridge. Your best bet for crossing the lake around there is the South Congress bridge or the shiny new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley, which thankfully opened back in February.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Hazards:** Recent rainfall means many unpaved trails, especially those along Barton Creek Greenbelt, might be muddy, slippery, or even partially submerged in low-lying areas. Choose your routes wisely and wear appropriate footwear.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, your lungs are getting a break. The Air Quality Index is a sweet 37, which is firmly in the "Good" category. You can breathe easy, literally.

Don't be that person surprised by a "Trail Closed" sign after a two-mile jog, only to have to backtrack. Your knees, and your sanity, will thank you.
