## The Rundown

Look, I love running the Butler Trail as much as the next masochist, but you need to understand that sections of it are a hot mess right now. Forget smooth sailing, because Austin Energy is tearing up the north shore near The Confluence and the Austin Rowing Club for underground electrical work. That reroute's been in effect since June and is slated to continue through March 2027. Seriously, slow your roll and pay attention to the signage.

And if you usually cross Lady Bird Lake on the east side of I-35, guess what? That pedestrian crossing has been shut down since February for the new bridge construction, and it's not reopening for about another year. Your options are the west-side I-35 sidewalk or going way out of your way to South Congress or the Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley. Choose wisely, especially if you're trying to hit that Monday morning run with the Airflow Summit crowd behind the Hyatt.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Butler Trail Navigational Nightmare:** Multiple long-term construction projects mean significant reroutes and closures on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, especially on the north shore near The Confluence and the I-35 pedestrian crossing. Plan your route carefully and expect delays and detours.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the heat. Your phone might say it's a clear 29.7°C (85.5°F) out, which isn't the *worst* we've seen this month, but don't get complacent. This is still late August in Central Texas. The "clear" condition means the sun's beating down, and we're always just a humidity spike away from a real feel temperature that'll knock you on your ass. Remember those "triple digits expected again throughout the week" warnings from a couple weeks ago? That heat lingers. Head out early or late, hydrate like it's your job, and don't be a hero.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heat Advisory (My Version):** While the thermostat might be hovering in the mid-80s, the sun is relentless. Expect the "real feel" to be higher. Stay hydrated, wear light clothing, and don't push yourself during the hottest parts of the day. Heat exhaustion is a real thing, even if you're "used to it."
</AlertBox>

Finally, for those of you with sniffles, pollen's sitting at a moderate level today. The air quality is actually quite good with an AQI of 20, dominant pollutant being Ozone/Other, so your lungs are mostly in the clear from industrial nastiness. But trees and grasses might still be trying to mess with you.
