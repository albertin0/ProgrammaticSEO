## What You Need to Know Before You Go

It's September 23rd, and while the heat isn't trying to murder you for once, Austin's still got its unique blend of challenges for outdoor enthusiasts.

### Air Quality: Don't Breathe Too Deeply (Especially Later)

That AQI of 57 might sound "Moderate," but let's be real – the air quality forecast from the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) for today specifically mentions ozone and PM2.5 reaching "lower to middle of the 'Moderate' range" in the afternoon and early evening. AccuWeather straight up calls it "unhealthy for sensitive groups" at AQI 53, which is right in our ballpark. If you've got asthma, allergies, or just lungs that prefer actual oxygen, consider an earlier workout or keep it indoors. Nobody wants to feel like they ran a marathon breathing through a sock.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Sensitive Lungs Beware:** With PM2.5 and ozone levels pushing into "unhealthy for sensitive groups" territory in the afternoon, scale back your outdoor intensity, especially if you have respiratory conditions. Early morning might be your best bet if you want to run outside.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Troubles: Butler Trail & I-35 Construction Continues

Our beloved Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail is still playing dodgeball with construction crews. A significant reroute on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building is in effect until March 2027. You'll be navigating detours while Austin Energy does its thing installing underground electrical infrastructure. Pay attention to signage and don't try to be a hero blazing your own path through a construction zone. Your knees will thank you.

Speaking of construction, the never-ending I-35 Capital Express Central Project means disruptions around the interstate are ongoing. The pedestrian crossing on the east side of I-35 near Lady Bird Lake is closed for about a year as they build a new bridge. Your options are to cross at South Congress or use the Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge, which, thankfully, is now open. Good news on the MLK Jr. Boulevard bridge over I-35 though – it's fully operational, and crews are just finishing up shared-use paths. Still, expect traffic and potential delays if your route takes you through these areas.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Major Trail Detours on Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail:** The north shore near the Confluence has a long-term reroute in place. Be prepared for navigation changes and slower going.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**I-35 Crossings:** If you usually cross I-35 near Lady Bird Lake, remember the east-side pedestrian crossing is closed. Use South Congress or the Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge.
</AlertBox>

### Greenbelt & Other Trails: Mostly Dry, Watch for Crowds

For our mountain biking friends, most of the popular trails like Emma Long, Mary Moore Searight, Brushy, and Bull Creek are showing "Predicted Dry" or "Observed Dry" conditions. That's good news after any recent pop-up showers, but Flat Creek and Reveille Peak Ranch are currently closed, so check your specific trail's status before you head out. With clear skies and pleasant temperatures, expect some of the more accessible greenbelts and parks, like Zilker Park, to see a good crowd.

Overall, it's a decent day to get out, but be smart about the air and prepare for those pesky detours on your usual routes.
