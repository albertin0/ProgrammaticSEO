Forget hitting those beloved trails today, Austin. Seriously, just don't.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
*   **Thunderstorms are rolling through.** Current weather: thunderstorm. That's not just a little drizzle, folks. Lightning, slick surfaces, and reduced visibility are your new running buddies.
*   **Flooding is a very real, very recent threat.** We've seen significant flash floods this month, closing dozens of low-water crossings. Even if the rain briefly lets up, standing water and dangerous currents can pop up faster than a new condo tower.
*   **Construction continues to chew up our favorite spots.** The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail is basically a giant obstacle course in places, and other popular routes aren't faring much better.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. The urge to get out there is strong, especially when the temperature isn't outright trying to melt your face off at a balmy 26.3°C (though don't get too comfortable, we've got warnings for some truly brutal heat indices coming our way next week, possibly hitting 100-110°F). But today, Mother Nature herself is giving you a hard pass.

You've got an active thunderstorm making its rounds. That means lightning, sudden downpours, and surfaces slicker than a politician's promise. And in Austin, "thunderstorm" often means "flash flood warning waiting to happen." We just had a serious dose of that mid-month, with over a hundred low-water crossings shut down across Travis and Williamson counties. Unless you've trained for an Ironman *swimming* through urban runoff, stay home.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Immediate Danger: Thunderstorms & Flash Flooding.** Do NOT risk it. Lightning kills, and floodwaters are incredibly deceptive. Turn around, don't drown. This isn't a suggestion, it's a safety decree.
</AlertBox>

Even if you *thought* you could dodge the storms, good luck navigating the maze that is our current trail system. The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail along Lady Bird Lake is currently a patchwork of detours. The north shore, near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club, is rerouted through March 2027 for Austin Energy's electrical work. And don't forget the east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing, which has been closed since February for bridge construction, forcing you to hoof it to South Congress or the new Wishbone Bridge. Shoal Creek Trail between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge is *supposed* to be wrapping up its maintenance closures this month, but with everything else, assume it's still a headache.

Your lungs? They're probably fine today, with a Moderate AQI of 54 (dominant pollutant PM2.5) and Moderate pollen. That's about the *only* good news. But breathing easy won't help if you're swept away or struck by lightning.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Persistent Hazard: Construction Chaos.** Large sections of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail are impacted by long-term construction projects. Expect detours, narrow paths, and reduced access. Plan your routes carefully, or better yet, opt for a treadmill today.
</AlertBox>

Save yourself the soggy shoes, the potential for a lightning strike, and the frustration of constant detours. Hit the gym, do some yoga in your living room, or spend some quality time with a foam roller. Your physical therapist (me) and the emergency services will thank you.
