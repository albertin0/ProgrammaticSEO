Listen, nobody likes running in a downpour, and nobody likes driving through a flooded street. Governor Abbott and the National Weather Service aren't sugarcoating it: heavy rainfall and flooding threats are coming to a significant portion of Texas, including our stretch along the I-35 corridor and the Hill Country. Today, Tuesday, September 29th, is your window. Take it.

## Today's Trail Talk: Lady Bird Lake and Zilker Park

If you're out around Lady Bird Lake, know that while the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail is technically open, you'll be dealing with some detours. There are reroutes on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club for electrical work until next March. Also, that I-35 pedestrian crossing on the east side has been closed since February for bridge construction, so you'll need to use the west side or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley. Don't be a hero; use the designated crossings.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**ACL Festival Prep:** While the main trails like the Butler Hike-and-Bike and Barton Creek Greenbelt are staying open, expect significant street and parking lot closures around Zilker Park and Barton Springs Road as they prep for ACL. If you're driving to Zilker, have a backup plan or expect to walk a mile to even get to the start. Barton Springs Pool access will also be limited to the Azie Morton Rd. entrance on festival weekends.
</AlertBox>

### Construction Zone Headaches

Beyond ACL, construction continues to be the constant companion of every Austin runner. Roadway and sidewalk improvements are ongoing near Red River Street and Cesar Chavez Street, so watch for temporary lane closures and pedestrian reroutes. Downtown is a minefield of projects, including the Congress Avenue Urban Design Initiative, which means ongoing lane and sidewalk closures between Cesar Chavez Street and 7th Street. Keep your head up and eyes peeled; drivers here are already distracted enough.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Future Flood Risk:** This is the big one. Starting Wednesday and intensifying into Thursday and Friday, we're looking at widespread heavy rain and a significant flash flood risk. The Weather Prediction Center has placed us under a Level 3 of 4 flood risk for Thursday. "Turn Around, Don't Drown" isn't just a catchy phrase; it's how you stay alive. Seriously, check local weather updates before stepping out mid-week.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Don't Forget Hydration (Even When Overcast):** While it's not blazing, Austin humidity is always a factor. Keep that water bottle handy, even with the clouds. And if you're hitting the trails, remember those moderate pollen levels. If you're sensitive, pre-dose your antihistamines.
</AlertBox>

Today's okay, maybe even pleasant. But consider it your last hurrah for uninterrupted, dry outdoor workouts for a few days. Plan accordingly, stay safe, and for the love of all that is holy, do not drive into standing water.
