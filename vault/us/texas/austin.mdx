## Today's Breakdown

Let's be real. Mother Nature is trying to drown us, and then poison us with whatever's floating in the air. The "mainly clear" condition the weather apps might be showing you is a cruel joke, given the flood watch in full effect for the entire day. The National Weather Service is screaming about heavy rain, especially early, and while they're forecasting cooler temps, the 28.4°C (83.1°F) we're seeing right now, combined with that humidity from the incoming or recent storms, means you'll be sweating buckets into air that's actively trying to irritate your respiratory system.

If you *insist* on getting outside, and I know some of you do because you're stubborn like that, here's what you need to know.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Flash Flood Risk is Real
Seriously, don't mess with flooded roads or low-water crossings. "Turn Around, Don't Drown" isn't just a catchy phrase; it's how you stay alive. Areas around Barton Creek and Shoal Creek are notorious for flash flooding. This isn't the day to test your new trail shoes against a raging torrent.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality and Allergies: A Double Whammy
With an AQI of 119 and "Moderate" pollen levels, your lungs are going to hate you. That PM2.5 irritant combines with whatever cedar or oak pollen is still hanging around. Expect itchy eyes, sneezing, and maybe a nice wheeze to go with your workout.

### Mind the Muck and Detours
Even if you find a spot that isn't actively flooding, trails are going to be muddy, slick, and generally unpleasant.
