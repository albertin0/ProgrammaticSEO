## Today's Outdoor Rundown: Sweat, Thunder, and Traffic Cones

Alright, Austin, let's talk about hitting the pavement or trails today, April 25, 2026. The environmental data says "Thunderstorm" and while the forecast indicates a "marginal risk" for severe storms, you know how quickly things can change around here. What you *can* count on is the heat. Temperatures are cruising into the low 90s, but the real kicker is that heat index, making it feel like a suffocating 100-103°F during peak afternoon hours. That's not just uncomfortable, that's dangerous.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Heat Stroke & Lightning Risk!**
With heat index values soaring into the low 100s, heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real threats. Combine that with scattered thunderstorms and the potential for lightning, hail, and damaging winds, and you've got a recipe for a bad time. Stay hydrated and for the love of all that's holy, if you hear thunder, get inside!
</AlertBox>

Beyond the weather, Austin's favorite perpetual state – construction – is still wreaking its usual havoc. The I-35 Capital Express Central project is a beast, and it's making its presence known, especially around Lady Bird Lake. If you're planning a scenic jog on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, know that the east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing is closed for about a year as they build the new Lady Bird Lake Bridge. You can still use the west-side sidewalk, but don't expect a seamless experience. Also, the Riverside Drive Pedestrian Ramp to the Boardwalk? Closed through 2029. Factor in various other road construction projects around town, and your workout might turn into an unplanned detour marathon.
