Today is NOT the day to set a PR on the trail, unless your goal is heatstroke or an unscheduled trip to the ER. Seriously, stay smart out there, or better yet, stay inside.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
*   **Forget "hot," we're in "brutal."** Austin is under an Excessive Heat Warning with actual temperatures expected to hit 103-107°F, and it'll *feel* like a soul-crushing 105-110°F. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous.
*   **Smoky skies are back.** There's an Air Quality Advisory in effect until noon today due to smoke from the Austin Fire or Narrows Fire, pushed our way by slight westerly winds. So, on top of feeling like you're running in a sauna, you'll be breathing in more than just humidity.
*   **Trail access is still a mess.** Between I-35 Capital Express Central construction and various utility projects, sections of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail and Shoal Creek Trail are rerouted or outright closed. Don't expect a clear run, and certainly don't expect easy access to water.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning in Effect!** Do not underestimate Central Texas August heat. Temperatures are hitting triple digits, with heat index values pushing 110°F. If you *must* be outside, keep it short, stick to the shade, and consume more water than you think you need. Consider moving your workout to the *very* early morning or late evening, but even then, conditions will be tough. Your joints might appreciate the heat, but your heart and lungs won't.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Smoke in the Air.** That "moderate" AQI might look okay on paper, but an Air Quality Advisory is in place until noon due to local wildfires. If you have any respiratory issues – asthma, allergies, or just a general aversion to coughing up a lung – consider an indoor alternative. Running through wildfire smoke and ozone is a recipe for a bad day, and possibly a worse tomorrow.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction & Closures Continue on Popular Trails.**
The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around Lady Bird Lake is experiencing multiple detours. Specifically, TxDOT's long-term utility work west of the I-35 bridges, which started yesterday, means a reroute that will last about two months. The east-side I-35 pedestrian bridge remains closed for its year-long replacement project. Over on the Shoal Creek Trail, sections between Shoal Creek Blvd and the Janet Fish Bridge are closed for repairs, and parts near Pease Park are still blocked from old landslides. If you're planning a long run, expect detours and be prepared for less-than-ideal surfaces. Also, the drinking fountain and showers near the Zilker Park Sand Volleyball Courts were shut off yesterday for repairs. Plan your hydration strategy carefully – don't rely on those.
</AlertBox>

Look, Austin is a great city for getting outside, but today ain't it. Save the epic long runs for a day when the air isn't trying to cook you alive and burn your lungs, and when our trails aren't a maze of construction cones. Hydrate, find some AC, and maybe do some strength work indoors. Your body will thank you, and frankly, I'd rather see you on the starting line of the next race than in my PT clinic because you decided to prove a point to the sun.
