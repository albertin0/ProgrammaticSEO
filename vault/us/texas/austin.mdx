## Today's Outdoor Reality: More Hazard Than Heroics

Alright, Austin, let's cut to the chase. You woke up to overcast skies and a mild 22.7°C (71-72°F), which usually sounds like prime running weather, right? Wrong. The National Weather Service is calling for a risk of severe storms today, May 9th, with heavy rain, large hail, and lightning possible from Friday afternoon through Saturday morning. We're also looking at a low risk of isolated flash flooding, with some areas potentially getting 1 to 2 inches of rain. So, if you were planning on hitting the trails, you might just get to experience an impromptu swim, or worse, become a human lightning rod. Your lungs also aren't getting a free pass with a moderate AQI of 55 (thanks, PM2.5) and moderate pollen levels.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Storm's a-Brewin': Don't Be a Dummy
Seriously, large hail and lightning are no joke. If you hear thunder, get inside. Don't "just finish this last mile." Your PR isn't worth a trip to St. David's or worse. Isolated flash flooding can turn harmless underpasses into death traps in minutes. Stay away from low-water crossings.
</AlertBox>

## Construction Carnage and Commencement Chaos

If the weather doesn't deter you, the sheer volume of urban disruption might. Our beloved Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, while a gem, is currently a patchwork of closures. You've got trail access at Willow Street shut down for a good 64 months because of the 98 Red River project. And don't even get me started on the TxDOT I-35 Capital Express Central project, which means "significant north shore trail reroutes" from now through 2033. Oh, and that east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing? Closed for roughly a year to build a new Lady Bird Lake Bridge. So much for an uninterrupted loop.

Downtown, the Congress Avenue Urban Design Initiative has construction between Cesar Chavez and 7th Street until Summer 2027. And let's not forget the Austin Convention Center redevelopment that's ongoing through late 2028.

Adding to the fun, it's UT Commencement weekend. Expect major congestion and delays around campus, with street closures affecting Robert Dedman Dr., Clyde Littlefield Dr., San Jacinto Blvd., and more, well into today, Saturday, May 9th. Trying to navigate that on foot or bike will be a frustrating exercise in dodging confused parents and caps-and-gowns.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Gaps (and the Grads)
If you insist on hitting the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, be prepared for diversions and unexpected detours. Don't assume your usual path is open. Around the UT campus, traffic and pedestrian volumes are insane due to graduation ceremonies. Avoid the area if your workout doesn't involve collegiate celebrations.
</AlertBox>
