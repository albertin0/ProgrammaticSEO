### Weather & Air Quality: Don't Sweat the Small Stuff, Sweat the Big Stuff

Right now, the air's pretty decent with an AQI of 45, which is in the "Good" range. Dominant pollutant is ozone, but at this level, it's not a huge concern for most. The temperature right now is a comfortable 16.9°C (about 62°F), which sounds lovely, right? WRONG. Don't be a rookie. This afternoon, we're staring down projected highs near 89°F (31.6°C). That's almost 15 degrees above average for late March, folks. This isn't just "warm," it's "let's all remember what a real Texas summer feels like before summer even officially starts" warm.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Extreme Heat Potential:** That 89°F forecast for today isn't a suggestion; it's a warning. Hydrate like your life depends on it (because out there, it kinda does). Plan your runs for early morning or late evening if you can.
</AlertBox>

### Hazards & Headaches: Construction, Heat, and Wildfire Woes

Let's get real about the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail. It's a mess, and it's not getting better anytime soon around the I-35 corridor. TxDOT's I-35 Capital Express Central Project means the east-side pedestrian crossing over I-35 on Lady Bird Lake has been closed since early February 2026, and it's staying that way for roughly a year. If you usually cross there to get from north to south shores, you're looking at a detour all the way to South Congress or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley. Oh, and the pedestrian ramp to the Boardwalk near Riverside Drive is also closed until 2029. Factor in ongoing "Confluence" construction near Waller Creek, which also affects parts of the Butler Trail, and you've got a recipe for navigational frustration.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Major Trail Detours:** The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around I-35 and Riverside Drive is severely impacted by long-term construction. Expect significant detours and closures. Check The Trail Conservancy's website before you go, or prepare to add some unplanned miles to your route.
</AlertBox>

Beyond the concrete jungle, the dry, unseasonably warm conditions are cranking up the wildfire risk across Central Texas. The Texas A&M Forest Service has the state at a "Preparedness Level 3" for wildfires, with elevated-to-critical fire weather projected for a good chunk of the western two-thirds of the state through next week. The city had a burn ban in parks last year, and with conditions like these, assume any spark is a bad idea. Don't be the person who accidentally torches a greenbelt because you flicked a cigarette butt.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Wildfire Awareness:** With high temperatures and dry air, the wildfire risk is elevated. Be extra cautious with anything that could cause a spark, especially if you're venturing off paved paths into the drier areas of our greenbelts.
</AlertBox>

There's also construction happening on Shoal Creek Greenbelt and along South Lamar Boulevard, so if those are your usual stomping grounds, expect some lane and sidewalk closures.

All in all, today's a mixed bag. The air quality is good, but the heat is a factor, the pollen is annoying, and trail detours are a significant pain. Be smart, stay hydrated, and don't assume your usual route is clear.
