### Lungs: Breathing Easy? Mostly.

The good news for your lungs is that the Air Quality Index (AQI) is holding strong at a "Good" 28 today, with Ozone/Other listed as the dominant pollutant. That means you're generally not battling lung-irritating smog just from the air itself. However, don't get too comfortable. AccuWeather's forecast for today *does* hint at "Poor" air quality for sensitive groups, so if you typically react to air changes, pay attention to your body.

Pollen levels are sitting at a "Moderate" rating. For many of us, that's a mild annoyance at worst, but if you're one of the unlucky ones with a severe allergy, you know the drill: pre-medicate, shower immediately post-run, and maybe just embrace the treadmill.

### Joints: Prepare for the Sweatfest and Trail Tetris

Now for the part where your joints (and every other cell in your body) take a beating: the heat. Today is forecasted to hit a scorching 38°C (100-101°F) with a "feels like" temperature that could easily push past 40°C (105-109°F). This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous, especially if you're out there pushing it.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** We are firmly in the historically hottest part of summer, and heat-related illnesses are no joke. Know the symptoms of heat exhaustion and heat stroke, and *do not* mess around. Dehydration hits fast, and it can be deadly.
</AlertBox>

On top of the heat, Central Texas is currently under a "Very High" fire danger rating, with a fire weather watch in place due to hot, gusty winds. This means conditions are ripe for wildfires to spark and spread quickly. While no immediate fire-related trail closures were reported in the last 24 hours, the situation can change rapidly.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**High Fire Danger:** Keep an eye on local news for any sudden advisories or trail closures, particularly if you're planning a run in more wooded areas like the Barton Creek Greenbelt. One spark is all it takes.
</AlertBox>

Finally, our perennial companion, construction, continues its reign. Portions of the **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail** around Lady Bird Lake are still rerouted on the north shore, particularly near the Confluence and the Austin Rowing Club building, due to Austin Energy installing underground electrical infrastructure. This is expected to last through March 2027. The I-35 Capital Express Central Project has also closed the east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing for roughly a year, so plan to use South Congress or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley if you're trying to cross the lake there.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Trail Construction Advisory:** If you're running the Butler Trail, expect detours and narrower sections. Slow down, be courteous to other trail users, and keep an eye out for construction equipment. The same goes for any attempts to navigate around the I-35 Capital Express Central Project.
</AlertBox>

So, if you must run outside today, go early, go short, hydrate constantly, and listen to your body. Or just hit the gym, because AC is a beautiful thing.
