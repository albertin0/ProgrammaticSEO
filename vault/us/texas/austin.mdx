## Austin's Outdoor Workout Reality: June 28, 2026

Alright, Austin, let's talk brass tacks. The forecast might be a bit of a mixed bag depending on when you step outside, but the short version is: today's not exactly prime running weather. The skies opened up (or are about to) with thunderstorms, making for slick surfaces and potential lightning strikes. Even if those clear by mid-morning, you're just trading the rain for the typical Austin summer sauna. Expect highs in the 90s, with "feels like" temperatures easily hitting the triple digits later on.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Thunderstorm Alert!** If you hear thunder, you're too close to lightning. Get inside immediately. Trails, especially exposed areas like along Lady Bird Lake, become extremely dangerous. Don't be "that guy" who gets zapped for a PR.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble Ahead

If you were planning a long run or ride on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, prepare for some serious detours and slowdowns. The north shore, particularly near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building, is rerouted from June 2026 through March 2027 for Austin Energy's electrical infrastructure work. That means you'll be slowing down, navigating new paths, and potentially sharing space with more equipment than usual. The east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing is also closed for about a year as they build the new Lady Bird Lake Bridge. If you need to cross, head to South Congress or the newer Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge.

Don't even think about the Shoal Creek Trail between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge if you were planning on it; it's still closed for maintenance through late June. And for those looking to cool off, Deep Eddy Pool is shut down for repairs through today, reopening tomorrow. Barton Springs Pool, thankfully, is back open after its recent flooding closure, but parts of the Khabele Bathhouse are still under renovation.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Slippery When Wet:** After a thunderstorm, trails are slick, especially over roots and rocks. Watch your footing or risk a twisted ankle, or worse, a trip to the ER. Remember the recent flooding Austin saw in early and mid-June – ground saturation means things will get muddy fast.
</AlertBox>
