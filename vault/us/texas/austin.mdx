## The Good, The Bad, and the Allergy-Inducing

Let's start with the immediate good news: The air quality is looking solid. Your lungs won't be fighting off a chemical attack today with an AQI of 43 and the dominant pollutant being Ozone/Other. In fact, the TCEQ's forecast for Austin on November 2nd is "Good" for all pollutants. That's a win for those deep breaths up and down *that* hill you love to hate.

Now for the "meh": Pollen. It's sitting at "Moderate" today. For some of us, that's just a sniffle or two. For others, it means a full-blown snot-fest, itchy eyes, and generally feeling like you've run a marathon before you even tie your shoes. If you're in the latter camp, pop those antihistamines now and consider sticking to indoor workouts or hitting the trails early before the daily pollen peak.

## Weather Whiplash: Enjoy Now, Brace for Later

The temperature right now is a glorious 23.6°C (that's about 74.5°F for you Fahrenheit folks) and it's partly cloudy. The National Weather Service expects highs to reach around 85°F today. It’s practically perfect running weather. *But*, and this is a Texas-sized "but," don't let it fool you.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Severe Weather Alert: Tonight through Tuesday Morning!**
A cold front is barreling through Central Texas tonight (Monday evening) into early Tuesday morning. Expect showers and thunderstorms, some of which could be *severe* with gusty winds and localized hail. Rain is projected to clear by 9 a.m. Tuesday, bringing cooler temps in the 70s for the rest of the week. If you’re planning an evening run, re-evaluate. Seriously. Don't be "that guy" caught in a hailstorm.
</AlertBox>

## Austin's Perpetual State of Disrepair: Trail & Road Construction

If you've been running in Austin for more than five minutes, you know our trails are basically obstacle courses sponsored by TxDOT. Today is no different. The main villain remains **the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around Lady Bird Lake**.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Major Butler Trail Closures & Detours:**
Several key sections of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail are still a mess. The east-side pedestrian crossing at I-35, a vital link, has been closed since February 2026 for new bridge construction and could remain shut until *2029*. If you usually cross there, TxDOT's detour sends you along Riverside Drive to the west sidewalk of the I-35 bridge. Also, parts of the south-shore boardwalk access will be limited through early 2027, and the trail access at Willow Street is a long-term closure due to construction (64 months, if you're counting). Plan your route meticulously or prepare to improvise (and probably add a few miles).
</AlertBox>

Don't forget the **Shoal Creek Trail** either; that popular stretch between Shoal Creek Blvd and the Janet Fish Bridge is still closed for repairs through November 2026. Your detour there is the sidewalk along the west side of N Lamar Blvd.

TxDOT's massive I-35 Capital Express project is still chugging along. While the MLK Jr. Boulevard bridge is thankfully rebuilt and reopened, expect ongoing work related to drainage tunnels and other frontage road widening projects across the city. Even Loop 360 is seeing ongoing construction between Bold Ruler Way and the Pennybacker Bridge through late 2026.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction Navigation Pro-Tip:**
Always check The Trail Conservancy's website for the latest updates on Butler Trail detours before you head out. These closures are long-term and can drastically change your usual loop. For road projects, DriveTexas.org is your best bet for real-time traffic impacts.
</AlertBox>

So, enjoy the pleasant weather while it lasts, dodge the pollen, and for the love of all that is holy, don't ignore those trail closure signs. Or, you know, just run on a treadmill. It's less scenic, but you won't get hailed on.
