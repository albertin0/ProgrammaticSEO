## The Full Breakdown

Look, if you're determined to get outside, your lungs are probably fine. The air quality index is sitting pretty at 36, which is firmly in the "Good" range with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. No smoke advisories or anything actively trying to give you an asthma attack today, which is a rare win.

But that's where the good news pretty much ends.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Heat Index Warning!
The actual temperature might be around 27.5°C (81.5°F), but the heat index could push it closer to a brutal 38°C (100°F) later today. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's dangerous. Heat exhaustion and stroke are real, even in mid-October when your body thinks it should be cooling off. Plan for early morning or late evening if you *must* run outside, and carry more water than you think you need. Seriously.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Troubles

The construction gods are still actively working against your peace of mind. The south shore Boardwalk section of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail at I-35 is closed starting today and will stay that way through Sunday, October 18th. This is part of the ongoing I-35 Capital Express Central Project. You'll need to use alternative access points or detours, so check The Trail Conservancy's website before you lace up.

And speaking of detours, the Shoal Creek Trail between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge is still closed until November for trail maintenance. Landslides have also blocked sections in Pease Park. If Shoal Creek is your usual jam, you'll be rerouted to the N. Lamar Blvd sidewalk.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Water Access Restricted
If you were planning to cool off in Lake Austin after your workout, forget about it. The boat ramps and swimming areas at Emma Long Metropolitan Park and Commons Ford Ranch Metropolitan Park are both closed from today through November 30th due to Lake Austin being drawn down.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Smart Out There
Even with the good AQI, the combination of high heat, humidity, gusty winds, and scattered showers means you need to be prepared. Dress light, hydrate continuously, and be aware of slippery surfaces. If you see lightning, find shelter immediately. And please, for the love of all that is holy, respect those trail closures. They're for your safety, not just to annoy you.
</AlertBox>
