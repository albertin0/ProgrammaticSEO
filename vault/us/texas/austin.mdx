Look, I love hitting the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail as much as the next masochist, but today is not the day to try for a PR. The temperature, even at 28.4°C (that's 83°F for the rest of us, but feels a lot worse), is a red herring. The real danger is the heat index, which is forecasted to soar to a brutal 105 to 112 degrees Fahrenheit through Independence Day weekend. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous, and heat stroke is no joke. If you *must* be outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and hydrate like your life depends on it – because it might.

Then there's the air. Our AQI is sitting at a lovely 119 due to PM2.5, thanks to that charming Saharan dust cloud that's decided to park itself over Central Texas. This means if you have any kind of respiratory issues – asthma, chronic bronchitis, or just a general preference for breathing easily – you should seriously limit your time outdoors. Even for healthy individuals, prolonged exposure to this stuff can lead to throat irritation and difficulty breathing. So, while fireworks are lighting up the sky later for the Fourth of July, the air itself is already providing its own kind of "special effects."

And speaking of the Lady Bird Lake Trail, portions of the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building are rerouted until March 2027 for Austin Energy's underground electrical work. Plus, the east-side I-35 pedestrian bridge closure means you can't cross between the north and south shores on that side for about a year. Plan your route accordingly, or better yet, just stay home.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning!
Heat indices are expected to peak between 105 and 112 degrees Fahrenheit. Intense exercise can quickly lead to heat exhaustion or heat stroke, which can be fatal. If you experience dizziness, nausea, headache, or extreme fatigue, get indoors and cool down immediately.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Unhealthy Air Quality for Sensitive Groups
The Air Quality Index is 119 due to PM2.5 from Saharan dust. People with asthma, chronic bronchitis, or emphysema should minimize outdoor exertion. Consider taking your workout indoors today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Trail Detours
If you insist on hitting the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, be aware of ongoing reroutes on the north shore due to construction. The east-side I-35 pedestrian bridge is also closed; use the South Congress or Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley instead.
</AlertBox>
