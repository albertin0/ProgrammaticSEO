### Air Quality & Respiratory Rant

Seriously, Austin, what are we doing to ourselves? An AQI of 119 with PM2.5 is a red flag. This isn't just "allergies," folks; these are tiny particulate matter messing with your lungs and cardiovascular system. If you have asthma, COPD, or just enjoy, you know, breathing easily, today is not the day for heroics. Keep your outdoor sessions short, low-intensity, or just take it inside. Your PT (me) says your lungs will thank you.

### Weather Woes: Thunderstorms & Flooding

"Overcast" sounds benign, but don't let it fool you. We're looking at heavy thunderstorms throughout the day and rain showers into the night. This isn't a drizzle; we're talking about a significant chance of rain and a real threat of localized, even life-threatening, flash flooding later this week. Low-water crossings, especially those around Onion Creek or even parts of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, can become dangerous very quickly. If you see water over a road or trail, turn around. Seriously, "Turn Around, Don't Drown" isn't just a catchy phrase; it's how you don't become a local news story.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Flood & Thunderstorm Alert: Stay Indoors!
Heavy thunderstorms and rain are in the forecast for today, carrying a level one flood threat. This increases the risk of flash flooding, especially in low-lying areas and near creeks. Do not attempt to cross flooded roads or trails. Postpone outdoor activities until conditions improve.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk & Construction Chaos

If you were planning a long run around Lady Bird Lake, be aware that the I-35 Capital Express Central Project is still a beast, permanently closing the northbound Riverside Drive exit and the Holly Street entrance and exit. There are bypass lanes and temporary U-turns, but it still means navigating construction mess near a popular running spot. Combine that with the flood risk, and maybe stick to an indoor track if you absolutely *must* run today. And don't even think about the Greenbelt if it starts raining; those trails become slick death traps faster than you can say "sprained ankle."
