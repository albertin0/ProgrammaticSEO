### Heat: The Real Danger Today

The biggest red flag waving today is the heat. Our current temperate 78°F (25.9°C) is just the calm before the storm. The forecast pegs today's high around 99-100°F (37-38°C), and with humidity, it'll *feel* a lot hotter, potentially up to 111°F. We've already hit triple digits twice this year by July 21, so this isn't new territory, but it's still dangerous.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** A Heat Advisory is in effect for parts of Central Texas from noon to 7 PM today. Heat index values between 108-111°F are expected. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's straight-up dangerous for outdoor activity. Hydrate *constantly*, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, take frequent shade breaks or just stay indoors during peak heat.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: Mind the Mess

If you're heading out to hit some pavement, be ready for detours. The **Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail** is still under siege from various projects. There's a long-term reroute on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club that started in June and will last until March next year, thanks to Austin Energy's electrical infrastructure work. Plus, Austin Water's emergency repairs are also narrowing parts of that same north shore section this month.

Then there's the behemoth I-35 Capital Express Central Project, which has thrown the whole area around Lady Bird Lake into an 8-year construction frenzy. You can't even cross the east-side I-35 pedestrian bridge over the lake right now; it's closed for roughly a year. TxDOT suggests using South Congress or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley, which opened in February.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Chaos Continues:** Expect significant detours and closures along the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, particularly near the Austin Rowing Club and all I-35 crossings over Lady Bird Lake. Slow down, pay attention to signage, and adjust your route.
</AlertBox>

### Water Wisdom: Don't Be Fooled

The good news is the waterways ban on Lady Bird Lake and the Colorado River south of Longhorn Dam was lifted yesterday. The bad news? City leaders still urge caution, advising to avoid "riverfront trails which may be unsafe to navigate and are susceptible to flash flooding at this time due to raised water levels." Bacteria levels also take a few days to return to normal after heavy flows. If you do decide to get in or on the water, be extra vigilant for debris and unexpected currents, especially near dams or recent flood areas.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Non-Negotiable:** With these temperatures and humidity, staying hydrated isn't a suggestion, it's a command. Drink water *before*, *during*, and *after* any outdoor activity. Electrolytes aren't a bad idea either.
</AlertBox>
