That's a "Good" rating, folks, meaning you can breathe easy without feeling like you're sucking down exhaust fumes. The dominant pollutant is just "Ozone/Other," which for us, is practically a spa day for your respiratory system. But here's the rub: While your lungs are happy and the sun's out, your feet might be in for a rough ride. Austin's trail scene is a permanent construction zone these days, and today is no different. If you're heading out to the beloved Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around Lady Bird Lake, be prepared for some navigational gymnastics. The access at Willow Street is still shut down for the 98 Red River project, a long-term commitment that's got another 60-some months to go. Even more relevant for your immediate future, starting in *just a few days* (June 2026), a major reroute kicks off on the north shore near the Confluence and Austin Rowing Club building. This beauty is for Austin Energy to install underground electrical infrastructure, and it's set to last through March 2027. So, you'll be slowing down, dodging equipment, and following detour signs.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Lady Bird Lake Detours Ahead:** If your route includes the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, especially near the north shore by the Confluence, expect significant reroutes starting in June. Pay attention to signage and on-site staff; they're there for a reason.
</AlertBox>

Other spots aren't immune either. The Shoal Creek Trail has ongoing closures, including a section between Shoal Creek Blvd. and the Janet Fish Bridge until late June for maintenance. There's also an indefinitely closed segment from 24th to 29th along North Lamar due to a landslide that still hasn't been fully addressed. So much for a seamless run.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration Isn't Optional:** Even with clear skies and a pleasant start, Austin heat can sneak up on you. Drink plenty of water *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. Don't wait until you're thirsty; that's too late.
</AlertBox>

In short, today's great for getting outside *if* you know where you're going and you're ready to navigate some urban trail chaos. Otherwise, hit the gym or embrace the detours with a good dose of Austin patience.
