## Today's Vibe: Hot, Hazy, and Holy ACL!

Let's cut to the chase: October 9, 2026, is the second weekend of the Austin City Limits Music Festival. That means Zilker Park is essentially a no-go zone unless you've got a wristband and a deep tolerance for crowds and overpriced beer. While parts of the park, like the Great Lawn, are completely shut down until October 18th, some of your go-to workout spots *are* technically open. The Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around Lady Bird Lake is still accessible, as is the Barton Creek Greenbelt and Barton Springs Pool. But here's the kicker: getting to any of these spots near Zilker? Good luck. Barton Springs Road and Stratford Drive are basically glorified pedestrian thoroughfares or closed entirely through the weekend, and parking anywhere near the area is a fool's errand. Forget about cruising in; you're walking, biking, or taking a heavily detoured shuttle.

### Weather & Air: The Usual suspects

The good news on the air front is that our AQI is a decent 52, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. No "don't breathe too deeply" alerts today. Pollen, however, is hanging out at a "Moderate" level. If you're susceptible to cedar fever's cousins, expect some sniffles or watery eyes, especially if you're hitting the trails near any oak or ragweed concentrations.

Now, about that weather. While your phone might say "overcast," the National Weather Service is calling for continued hot weather, pushing into the upper 80s to mid-90s (Fahrenheit, obviously) across the region. Combine that with moderate humidity, and you've got a recipe for feeling sluggish if you don't hydrate like it's your job. Seriously, it's not a dry heat, and even with some cloud cover, you'll still be sweating buckets. There are no major weather warnings for Austin, so no flash floods or unexpected freezes to worry about, just classic Austin heat.

### Construction Corner: Perpetual Detours

The I-35 Capital Express Central Project continues its long-term impact on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail, particularly near the I-35 bridges. Expect ongoing detours and reroutes in that area. While the main trail is still usable, keep your head on a swivel for unexpected shifts, construction vehicles, and general disruptions. It's a marathon, not a sprint, with that I-35 project, so get used to it.
