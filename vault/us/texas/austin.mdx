## The Good, The Bad, and The Sweaty

Let's start with the small mercies: the air quality is actually decent today. An AQI of 26 means your lungs can breathe easy, at least from industrial pollutants. That's a rare win in September, which historically is one of Austin's worst months for ozone. So, if you're just worried about PM2.5 and smog, you're golden.

Now, for the reality check.

### Pollen's Petty Revenge

While the general AQI is good, your sinuses might beg to differ. Pollen levels are still firmly in the "Moderate" category, and some sources are even calling it "High." Specifically, ragweed is making its annual climb, and weed pollen in general is high. If you're one of the unfortunate souls whose immune system declares war on tiny plant reproductive cells, you'll be feeling it. Pre-dose your antihistamines and maybe save that long run for after a good rain washes some of this crud away. Don't be that person hacking up a lung and blaming the nonexistent "bad air." It's just mother nature being a jerk.

### Heat Advisory (For Your Brain)

The temperature right now might feel pleasant at 25.5°C (78°F), but don't be fooled by the morning breeze. The mercury is set to hit the mid-90s, potentially reaching 98°F today, and it’s going to *feel* even hotter thanks to our lovely humidity. The forecast for the next week shows temperatures consistently in the mid-90s to low 100s. That's prime time for heat-related illness. Don't be a hero; hydrate early, run smart, and reconsider that noon interval session. Public libraries and parks are open as cooling centers if you overdo it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heat's a Sneaky Killer:** Current temps are comfortable, but they're climbing. Expect mid-90s with high humidity. Plan your workouts for early morning or late evening, carry water, and listen to your body. Heat stroke is not a badge of honor.
</AlertBox>

### Construction: The Austin Marathon's Forever Obstacle Course

Seriously, I'm starting to think the city just likes moving cones around for fun. If you're trying to get a run in on the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail around Lady Bird Lake, be ready for a frustrating experience. The **east-side I-35 pedestrian crossing is closed** until next year for the new bridge construction, so you'll need to use the west side, South Congress, or the new Wishbone Pedestrian Bridge at Pleasant Valley to get across the lake.

And let's not forget the **Shoal Creek Trail**. It's still a mess, with sections closed indefinitely due to old landslides and other parts under maintenance until at least November 2026. You'll be detoured onto busy N. Lamar Blvd sidewalks, which, let's be honest, aren't exactly ideal for a peaceful run.

Add to that the ongoing, extensive I-35 Capital Express Central Project with its rolling lane closures, ramp closures, and general mayhem impacting traffic and access points all over downtown and beyond. And the Congress Avenue Urban Design Initiative is also chewing up sidewalks and lanes between Cesar Chavez and 7th Street.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Trail & Road Closures are NO JOKE:** Major sections of the Ann and Roy Butler Hike-and-Bike Trail and Shoal Creek Trail are impacted by long-term construction and closures. Check specific detours before you head out, especially if you're planning on crossing Lady Bird Lake or running through Pease Park. The city's a construction zone right now.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip for Runners:** If you’re hitting a popular trail like the Ann and Roy Butler, be extra aware of detours, construction equipment, and other users. Consider less affected trails further from downtown, or hit up some neighborhood streets early to avoid the heat and the chaos.
</AlertBox>
