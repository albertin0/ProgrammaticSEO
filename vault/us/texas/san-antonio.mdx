## The Not-So-Great News: Local Hazards

### Smoke & Stench

Okay, so the official AQI is good, but let's talk about that lingering smell. There's a mulch fire near the Nelson Gardens/Brush Recycle Center on the Southwest Side, close to Loop 1604, that's still smoldering. The San Antonio Fire Department says it's not a threat to structures, but winds have been pushing that smoke across parts of the city. Runners in Northwest Bexar County even reported a smoky odor from what UTSA Police Department called an approved controlled burn. So, while the air isn't *dangerous*, you might still catch a whiff, especially if you're downwind. Listen to your body – if your throat gets scratchy, maybe hit the treadmill.

### Trail Closures & Debris

This is where it gets tricky. We've had some strong winds earlier this week, with gusts up to 50-55 mph across Bexar County, which means downed branches and general debris could still be on the trails, especially in more wooded areas. Also, recent heavy rains caused flooding, shutting down the Leon Valley DPS Mega Center just a couple of days ago on March 18th. While the sun's out now, low-lying areas, particularly along river greenways like portions of the **Leon Creek Greenway**, could still be muddy, slick, or have standing water. The City Parks & Rec website explicitly warns that trails may have mud and debris after rain events.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Construction Zone Chaos!**
Forget just mud, construction is a persistent pain. Multiple sections of the Salado Creek Greenway (Tobin Park Trailhead), Culebra Creek Greenway, and the Leon Creek Greenway (under the 1604 overpass) are dealing with closures or intermittent disruptions due to TxDOT projects and other re-construction. The San Antonio River Authority also notes ongoing stem-density maintenance on the Mission Reach through Spring 2026, which means temporary closures there too. Don't just show up expecting your usual route to be clear. Check the City of San Antonio Parks & Rec website or social media *before* you head out, or you'll be doing an unexpected cross-training session of dodging "Trail Closed" signs.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip for River Runners**
If you're hitting any of the greenway trails, especially those near creeks or the San Antonio River, be extra vigilant. Even if the main path looks clear, tributary crossings or underpasses could still be wet, muddy, or have accumulated junk from recent high water. Better safe than face-planting in a puddle.
</AlertBox>
