Look, I get it. We're runners. We like to suffer. But there's a fine line between "pushing your limits" and "getting airlifted to University Hospital because you ignored common sense." This isn't just "hot," it's dangerously hot. Your body's ability to cool itself is severely compromised when the heat index climbs this high. Even if you're a seasoned San Antonio endurance monster, today is the day to seriously reconsider that outdoor long run or intense interval session.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning!
Heat index values between 103-107°F are expected today. This significantly increases the risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Limit strenuous outdoor activity, especially between 10 AM and 6 PM. If you MUST be outside, hydrate constantly with water and electrolytes, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, and take frequent shade breaks. Symptoms of heatstroke include a body temperature of 103°F or more, red/hot/dry skin, lack of perspiration, confusion, and fainting. This is an emergency – call 9-1-1 immediately if you or someone else experiences these.
</AlertBox>

### Moderate Pollen & Ozone Considerations
While overall pollen is moderate, combine it with the elevated ozone levels present on hot, sunny days, and it's a double-whammy for your respiratory system. If you're prone to allergies or have any kind of lung sensitivity (asthma, COPD), you'll feel it. Consider wearing a mask designed for particulates or sticking to indoor workouts.
