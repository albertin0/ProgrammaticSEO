<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat & Heat Index
Afternoon temperatures are going to climb into the mid-90s, with "feels like" temperatures hitting 100-107°F. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous. The city's been harping on this for weeks for a reason. If you *must* be outside, avoid strenuous activity between 2-7 p.m. and stay hydrated like your life depends on it – because it kinda does.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### WARNING: Saharan Dust Has Arrived
Starting late Sunday evening, a plume of Saharan dust is making its grand entrance, peaking Monday afternoon. While "widespread air quality impacts are expected to remain limited" by official metrics, those microscopic particles can still trigger respiratory irritation and inflammation, especially if you're out huffing and puffing. Expect hazy skies and possibly reduced visibility. If you have asthma or other lung issues, take extra precautions today and definitely tomorrow.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### FYI: Trail Closures & Maintenance
Don't be surprised if your go-to running spots are still a mess. Portions of the Leon Creek Greenway, particularly near Loop 1604, are closed for TxDOT construction until mid-2027. The Salado Creek Greenway near Tobin Park also has intermittent closures. Check the City of San Antonio Parks & Recreation website before you head out, or you'll just be adding frustration to your workout. Also, remember recent heavy rains (like last week's flash floods) mean some unpaved trails or low-water crossings could still be muddy or have debris, even if the warning is gone.
</AlertBox>

Look, I get it. You want to get out there. But today, San Antonio is trying to bake you, and then give you a side of dust to inhale. Maybe hit the gym or embrace the early morning hours before the sun really starts doing its thing and the dust settles in. Or just find a nice air-conditioned spot to plan tomorrow's run (which will probably also be hot and dusty).
