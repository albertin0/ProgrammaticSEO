Another day, another chance to get out there, San Antonio, but today you'll need to keep your eyes peeled. While the air quality is pretty sweet, Mother Nature's got a few curveballs heading our way, and our trails are still a mess in spots.

<LungsJointsScore score={6} />

<BulletList>
*   **Rain, Rain, Go Away, Or Don't, But Either Way, Stay Alert:** We've got light drizzle right now, but forecasters were calling for "repeated rounds of rain" and "flash flooding" potential overnight into Thursday morning, especially around the Alamo City. Soils are already saturated, which means any additional rain could turn into a real problem quickly.
*   **Watch for Water on the Roads (and Trails):** Bexar County is at a Level 2 out of 4 risk for flash flooding today, so those low-water crossings you usually breeze through? They could be death traps. The San Antonio River Authority and Bexar County are even running a "Floods Don't Care" campaign, which tells you all you need to know about how seriously to take this.
*   **Trail Obstacles Still a Thing:** Good luck finding a completely clear path. Parts of the Leon Creek Greenway under the 1604 overpass are still seeing periodic closures for TXDOT construction. And if you're a fan of the Mission Reach, be aware that sections are closed for erosion repair. Always check for current closures before you head out, or you'll be doing an unexpected cross-country adventure.
</BulletList>

## Weather Woes and Wet Trails

Today's 22.1°C (around 72°F for those of us not on the metric system) with light drizzle sounds pleasant enough for a jog. But don't let that fool you. The National Weather Service warned of severe storms with potential hail and damaging winds hitting parts of South Central Texas yesterday, and the risk of "efficient rainfall producing storms and associated flooding will increase by Thursday into the weekend." We had some strong winds and lightning overnight, too.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Low Water Crossings: Turn Around, Don't Drown!
Seriously, I can't say this enough. With all the rain we've had and more expected, any low water crossing could be deceptively deep and dangerous. Bexar County just rolled out a new flood gate system because people aren't getting the message. Don't be "that guy." "Floods Don't Care if you drive a Tesla or an F-150." Don't assume your big truck will save you.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Slick Surfaces and Muddy Adventures
Even if you avoid the outright flooded spots, expect slick roads, sidewalks, and especially muddy trails. A light drizzle on already wet ground means less traction. Twist an ankle, tweak a knee – that's a one-way ticket to my office. Be extra careful on the greenways, like the Salado Creek Greenway, which often gets mucky after even moderate rain.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route, Check Closures
Before you lace up and hit the door, do a quick check for trail closures. The City of San Antonio Parks and Recreation website is usually your best bet. There's plenty of construction around, like the new playscape at Brackenridge Park and ongoing work along various sections of our trail system. The last thing you want is to hit a dead end five miles out.
</AlertBox>

Overall, if you're itching to get some miles in, stick to known dry routes, wear appropriate gear for wet conditions, and for the love of all that is holy, *stay away from flooded areas*. Your workout isn't worth a trip to the ER or worse.
