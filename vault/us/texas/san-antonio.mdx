## The Daily Grind: What to Expect Out There

Look, the air quality (AQI 23, "Good") is actually decent for a July day in San Antonio, with no immediate wildfire smoke or major particulate issues. Your lungs will mostly thank you. But your entire body? That's another story.

### Heat Advisory: It's Hot, Deal With It (Safely)

The biggest challenge today is the heat and that classic South Texas humidity. Our current temperature is around 28.1°C (82.6°F), but that's just the start. Daily highs are expected to consistently hit the mid-to-upper 90s this week. The City of San Antonio themselves warn against strenuous activity during the hottest part of the day (typically 2-7 p.m.) and for good reason. Heat exhaustion and stroke are real, folks.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Extreme Heat Risk!** Hydrate like it's your job, and then hydrate some more. Carry more water than you think you need. Consider moving your workout to early morning or late evening. If you start feeling weak, dizzy, or nauseous, stop immediately, find shade, and cool down. This isn't a drill.
</AlertBox>

### Trail & Road Blocks

Construction continues its relentless march across San Antonio. If you're a fan of the **Leon Creek Greenway**, be aware that a portion near Loop 1604 (north of the Valero Trailhead) is shut down for TxDOT construction, and it's not reopening until mid-2027. That's a long haul, so find an alternate route or just grit your teeth and deal with the detour signs. Other city parks like Falcone Park and Flores Park have playgrounds closed for renovations through July, and even some parking lots are undergoing work, like at Pickwell Park. Always check the City of San Antonio's park and trail closure pages before you go.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Construction Ahead!** Specific sections of the Leon Creek Greenway and other city parks are impacted by long-term construction projects. Plan your routes, especially if you're running or cycling, and look for official detours. Don't go around safety fencing.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**PRO TIP: Listen to Your Body.** With the heat, even "good" air quality doesn't mean you should push your limits. Take frequent breaks, seek shade, and if you're not feeling 100%, dial it back or opt for an air-conditioned workout. Your joints (and brain) will thank you.
</AlertBox>
