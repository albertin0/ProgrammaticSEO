### Today's Rundown

Yesterday was a washout, literally. San Antonio got pounded with heavy rain, leading to flash flood warnings that lasted through Friday afternoon and widespread road closures. We're talking up to 36 roads closed across the county, and several parks shuttered due to high water. While the active rain has cleared out for today, the aftermath is no joke. The National Weather Service warned of "life-threatening flash flooding of creeks and streams," and that kind of water doesn't just evaporate overnight.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Post-Flood Hazards: Turn Around, Don't Drown (Even After the Rain!)**

Don't be a hero. Just because the sun's out doesn't mean the danger is gone. Creeks like the Salado Creek were inundated with water. That means trails that run alongside them, or even cross them, could be severely compromised. Hidden sinkholes from washed-out pavement, slippery mud, and accumulated debris are all serious threats. Furthermore, there's a real risk of contaminated water from sewage overflows, and yes, even rafts of stinging fire ants looking for new high ground. Avoid any standing water on trails or roads, and assume anything submerged is treacherous. Your PR isn't worth a trip to the emergency room or an expensive water rescue.
</AlertBox>

Temperatures are cool, hovering around 11.4°C (that's low 50s for you Fahrenheitors), with clear skies. Honestly, perfect running weather *if* the ground wasn't actively trying to eat your ankles. Air Quality Index (AQI) is sitting pretty at 40, putting us in the "Good" to "Fair" range, which is fine for most folks, though sensitive groups might still notice something with prolonged exposure. The dominant pollutant is still Ozone, which is our usual San Antonio special, but at these levels, it's not your primary concern today.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ongoing Construction & Closures**

Beyond the flood mess, San Antonio's perpetual state of improvement means there are always *other* things trying to trip you up. The Oxbow Trail along the Museum Reach, for example, was closed for maintenance until May 1st. It *might* be open now, but check local signage. Other trails, like parts of the Leon Creek Greenway, see periodic closures for TXDOT projects, and several park playgrounds and even the Elmendorf Lake Park parking lot are closed for various construction or repairs. Always check the City of San Antonio Parks & Rec page or San Antonio River Authority for specific trail alerts before you head out, especially for greenways.
</AlertBox>
