### Air Quality & Pollen Breakdown

The good news is the overall Air Quality Index (AQI) for San Antonio is currently 44, which is squarely in the "Good" category. Your lungs can breathe a sigh of relief, for now. However, the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) predicts that ozone levels could hit the higher end of the "Good" range later this afternoon and early evening, thanks to light winds and the heat. So, if you're sensitive, maybe hit that run earlier.

Pollen is clocking in at a moderate level today. If you’re one of the many locals who suffer, make sure you’ve taken your meds before you head out, or you’ll be snotting your way around Government Canyon State Natural Area by mile two.

### San Antonio: The City That Never Stops Building (Or Closing Trails)

Look, I get it, progress and all that. But it feels like every other week another one of our favorite spots is shut down. Today is no different. We're looking at widespread construction closures impacting trails and parks across the city.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, trying to plan a route without hitting a detour or a completely closed section is a workout in itself. Take the **Leon Creek Greenway**, for instance. A significant portion north of the Valero Trailhead, up to The Rim, is closed for TxDOT construction, and it's not expected to reopen until mid-2027. That's right, *mid-2027*. So much for those long, uninterrupted runs.

And it’s not just the greenways. Classen-Steubing Ranch Park has trails closed through September, and Elmendorf Lake Park's playground and even its *parking lot* are out of commission for months. Southside Lions Park also has closures affecting sections of the Salado Creek Greenway and a walking bridge project starting September 8th. So, check the City Parks and Rec site *before* you lace up, unless you enjoy running into orange cones and construction fencing.

### Heat Index is a Sneaky Bastard

While the morning might feel nice at 25.6°C (78°F) and "Mainly Clear" skies, don't be fooled. Stay hydrated. Seriously. And maybe reconsider that midday long run.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Hydrated, Stay Safe

With those heat index numbers, hydration isn't just a suggestion; it's a survival strategy. If you're heading out for anything more than a quick jaunt, carry water. Plan routes that have water stops or loops back to your car for refills. And for the love of all that is holy, don't ignore the warning signs of heat exhaustion. Nobody wants to be *that* person paramedics are scraping off the Salado Creek Greenway.
</AlertBox>
