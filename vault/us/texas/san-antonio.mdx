## The Aftermath of the Storm

Yesterday, San Antonio got absolutely drenched. We saw flash flood warnings plastered everywhere, lasting well into Monday night. The kind of rain that cancels major Fiesta events, including the beloved Texas Cavaliers River Parade, and knocks out power for thousands. Even if the skies are clearer this morning, that water has to go somewhere, and it means our beloved trails are almost certainly a swampy mess. The San Antonio River Authority warned everyone yesterday to "Stay Weather-Aware This Fiesta" and check for high water and low-water crossing closures. They weren't kidding.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Danger: Lingering Flooding & Slick Trails!**
Many roads were closed yesterday due to high water, and while those might be clearing, low-lying areas, underpasses, and unpaved trails will retain water. "Turn Around, Don't Drown" isn't just for cars; it applies to your running shoes too. Expect slick conditions, hidden debris, and potential washouts.
</AlertBox>

## Perpetual Closures and Construction Headaches

As if the rain wasn't enough, we've got our usual dose of ongoing closures. If you're planning on hitting the San Antonio River Walk, specifically the Museum Reach, you'll find the Oxbow Trail closed until May 1st for some project work. So much for a scenic riverside jog there.

And let's not forget the never-ending Loop 1604 expansion. A section of the Leon Creek Greenway under the 1604 overpass, north of the Valero Trailhead, is also subject to periodic closures throughout April due to TxDOT construction. It's always something.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Alerts: Plan Your Route!**
*   **Museum Reach (Oxbow Trail):** Closed for maintenance until May 1st. Find an alternate route.
*   **Leon Creek Greenway (1604 Overpass):** Expect periodic closures due to TxDOT construction this month. Check conditions before you head out.
</AlertBox>

## Air Quality & Pollen Report

On the upside, the Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at 38, which is generally good news for your lungs. Even though "Ozone/Other" is the dominant pollutant, at this AQI, it's not a major concern today.

However, it *is* April in Texas, and that means pollen. We're looking at a moderate level today. If you're one of the many who suffer, you know what that means: itchy eyes, runny nose, and possibly a cough that makes every breath feel like a struggle.
