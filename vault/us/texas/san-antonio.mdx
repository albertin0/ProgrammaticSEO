## The Good, The Bad, and The Orange Cones

Today, your lungs and legs are mostly in agreement that it's a decent day to get some miles in. We've finally caught a break from the oppressive heat dome that's been parked over South Texas, making an early morning or late afternoon run feel much less like a death march. The "Good" air quality means you won't be huffing anything nasty, which is always a bonus in a city that often struggles with ground-level ozone.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Get Cooked (Even If It Feels Cooler)
While 80 degrees is a vast improvement, it's still San Antonio. You still need to hydrate like your life depends on it. Carry water, know your limits, and if you feel woozy, knock it off. Heat exhaustion is no joke, even on "mild" days for us.
</AlertBox>

Here's where things get tricky, and where my inner curmudgeon really shines. Our beloved greenway system is under siege. You practically need an engineering degree and a crystal ball to navigate some sections right now.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Trail Apocalypse: Know Before You Go
Seriously, folks, *check your routes*. We've got massive, long-term construction closures everywhere. A significant chunk of the **Leon Creek Greenway** near Loop 1604 is shut down until mid-2027 thanks to TxDOT. The **Salado Creek Greenway** is a mess, with sections south of Southside Lions Park closed for repairs until October, and other parts from Gembler Road to Houston Street closed indefinitely. Even the **Culebra Creek Greenway** near Tranquil Creek is out of commission for about another week. Don't even get me started on the ongoing upgrades and playground closures at various parks like Classen-Steubing Ranch Park. If you try to run your usual loop blind, you're going to hit a barricade. Or worse, a giant hole.
</AlertBox>

And it's not just the greenways. General construction is ramping up, including major TxDOT Loop 410 improvements near the airport starting this month, which can mean more traffic headaches if you're driving to a trail.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Park Facilities - Minor Hiccups
Planning a pit stop at a park? The water fountain's bottle filler at Confluence Park is out for maintenance, and the restrooms at Espada Park are closed due to a water leak. Plan accordingly if you rely on those.
</AlertBox>

My advice? Embrace the urban jungle. If your usual trail is blocked, explore a different neighborhood, or hit the pavement instead of the dirt. Just be hyper-aware of your surroundings, especially near construction zones. It's a decent day to be outside, but you'll have to earn those miles by outsmarting the orange cones.
