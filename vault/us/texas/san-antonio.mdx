## The Heat is No Joke, San Antonio

Yesterday we finally broke 100 degrees for the first time this year, and today's heat index is cranked up even higher. We're not talking about a casual jog; we're talking about conditions that can land you in the ER. The National Weather Service has been clear: limit strenuous outdoor activity to the early morning or evening. And by "early morning," I mean before the sun really decides to remind you it's July in South Texas.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Advisory: Heat Index up to 112°F
A Heat Advisory is in effect for Bexar County until 8 PM CDT today. Heat index values could reach 112 degrees, posing a serious risk for heat-related illnesses. Stay hydrated, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, don't leave kids or pets in cars. Ever.
</AlertBox>

## Don't Forget Your Lungs

That "moderate" AQI might look okay at a glance, but with PM2.5 being the main culprit and the forecast creeping into unhealthy territory, your respiratory system is under stress. This isn't just for people with asthma; anyone can feel the effects of these tiny airborne particles. If your chest feels tight or you're coughing more than usual, it’s a sign to back off.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups"
Today's AQI is forecast to reach 103, which means sensitive groups should reduce prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. Even if you're not in a "sensitive group," this level of PM2.5 is not doing your lungs any favors.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Closures and Other Headaches

Our beautiful greenways are a mixed bag right now. Construction is still a beast. If you had plans to run the Leon Creek Greenway north of the Valero Trailhead, forget about it; that section's closed until mid-2027. And don't be surprised by intermittent closures on the Salado Creek Greenway either, thanks to more TxDOT projects. Oh, and if you were hoping for a serene paddle, the southern SASPAMCO Paddling Trail is still closed from log jams. Always check the City of San Antonio Parks and Recreation or River Authority sites before you head out.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route (and Your Bailout)
Always check current trail conditions before you leave the house. Construction and flood damage can close sections without much warning. Have a backup route in mind, or better yet, hit a treadmill or track with AC. And carry more water than you think you need. Seriously.
</AlertBox>

If you insist on an outdoor session, keep it short, keep it slow, and listen to your body. Don't be that person we have to scrape off the trail.
