## The S.A. Sweat Session Debrief

Look, I get it. The sky might be "mainly clear" as of this morning, and the thermometer says 24.5°C (that's a deceptive 76°F for you Celsius-challenged folks). But don't let that fool you into thinking it's a pleasant day for a long run or a strenuous bike ride. This isn't your grandma's Texas heat; this is the kind that grabs you by the throat.

The *real* issue today is the "RealFeel" temperature. AccuWeather's showing it at a brutal 101°F, and we're looking at an Extreme UV Index of 12. That's not just "warm," that's "your-body-is-cooking-from-the-inside-out" territory. The City of San Antonio itself is putting out warnings about avoiding strenuous activity during the hottest part of the day, typically between 2 p.m. and 7 p.m., due to the high risk of dehydration, heat stroke, and exhaustion. If you insist on getting out, make it early, keep it short, and stay in the shade. Preferably near a water fountain that actually works.

### Trail Conditions & Construction Chaos

If the heat wasn't enough, some of our favorite spots are still a hot mess. The Escondido Creek Parkway trails are currently closed due to flooding, and over on the Mission Reach, the Camino Coahuilteca is underwater. Planning to hit the Salado Creek Greenway? Good luck. Sections of it are closed for maintenance and TXDOT construction, and will be until winter 2026. You'd think they'd have these things sorted out, but this is San Antonio, folks. Always something.

And don't even get me started on the general construction across town. Loop 1604 and I-10 are a perpetual nightmare, and while that's mostly a driving headache, it means detours and potential hazards if you're trying to bike or walk near those areas. The VIA Rapid Green Line project along San Pedro Avenue also means you'll hit intermittent sidewalk closures there.
