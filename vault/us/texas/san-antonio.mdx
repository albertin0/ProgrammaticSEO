## Run Down on Today's Hazards

Look, the morning might feel deceptively nice right now at 25.9°C (about 79°F), but don't get complacent. The National Weather Service is still talking about near-record heat, with San Antonio expecting highs in the upper 90s to triple digits, and heat index values making it feel like 106-108 degrees later today. Hydration isn't a suggestion; it's survival. That "mid-summer heat is still hanging around across South-Central Texas" despite the calendar.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Afternoon Heat Incoming!** While it feels okay this morning, expect temperatures to soar dramatically by afternoon. We're still in a pattern of "upper 90s to near 100 degrees" for the weekend. If you're running past 10 AM, you're looking at a completely different, much hotter beast. Plan accordingly or move your workout indoors.
</AlertBox>

### Construction Everywhere You Look

Forget a nice, long, uninterrupted run on your favorite paths. Construction is an absolute nightmare across the city's trail system. From the Salado Creek Greenway to the Leon Creek Greenway, you'll find "ongoing closures" and "periodic closures" for everything from dam repairs to bridge beam setting to general maintenance.

For example, a portion of the Leon Creek Greenway at Loop 1604 is closed for TxDOT construction until mid-2027. Garza Park is practically a hard hat zone, with most of it closed until January 2027 for trail and park upgrades. Even some portions of the Culebra Creek Greenway are shut down. Trying to navigate these closures on a long run is a recipe for frustration and potentially unsafe detours onto busy roads.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures are Widespread:** Do not assume your usual trail is open. Check the City of San Antonio Parks and Recreation website *before* you head out. Expect detours, muddy sections after any rain (though none is expected today), and heavy equipment in many areas. The Leon Creek Greenway, Salado Creek Greenway, and other popular routes have significant, ongoing closures.
</AlertBox>

## El Niño on the Horizon

While it's not affecting your run today, forecasters are talking about a "super El Niño" that's gaining strength. This means a potentially much wetter and colder fall and winter for Texas, with increased chances for rain and flooding extending into spring 2027. So, while we're sweating it out now, start thinking about those waterproof trail shoes for the coming months.
