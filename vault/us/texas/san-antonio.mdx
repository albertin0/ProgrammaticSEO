## Why today is *not* ideal for crushing PRs

<BulletList>
*   **Flooding is no joke.** We've had enough rain to turn parts of our greenways into murky rivers. Don't be "that guy" who thinks they can power through. You'll end up with a nasty infection, or worse, swept away.
*   **Heat and humidity are back with a vengeance.** While the thermometer might only say 28°C (82°F) early on, it's climbing fast, and the humidity will make it feel like you're running in soup. You'll be sweating buckets before you hit mile one.
*   **Construction traffic is a weekend warrior's nightmare.** Good luck getting to some of your favorite spots without hitting major TxDOT headaches.
</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Stay OFF Flooded Trails and Roads!
Seriously, I can't stress this enough. San Antonio and Bexar County are under a flood watch, and some areas have seen significant rainfall. The San Antonio River Authority reports closures at Jackson Nature Park, River Crossing Park, Graytown, and Helton due to flooding from Cibolo Creek and the San Antonio River. Even if you *think* it looks shallow, just six inches of moving water can knock you down, and hidden dangers like displaced animals, live wires, or sewage are lurking. Your lungs won't thank you for inhaling that funk. Turn around, don't drown. It's not a suggestion, it's how you stay alive.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Heat (and the Roads)
While yesterday's extreme heat warnings might not be active, it's still Texas in July. Temperatures are forecast to hit the mid-90s today, and with the humidity, the "feels like" temperature will be higher. Limit strenuous outdoor activities, especially between 10 AM and 6 PM. And don't forget the ground can be scorching hot on your joints and paws if you're out with a four-legged friend. Also, if you're planning on hitting the Leon Creek Greenway, be aware that portions around Loop 1604 are still closed due to TxDOT's never-ending construction. You're going to hit major delays on Loop 1604 and I-10 this weekend anyway, so plan your routes carefully, or just stay local.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate or Die-drate!
If you *must* venture out, make hydration your religion. Water isn't enough; grab some electrolytes. And for crying out loud, tell someone where you're going and when you expect to be back. A small, early morning window might be tolerable for a shorter effort, but don't push it. There's always tomorrow, assuming you don't end up in Cibolo Creek.
</AlertBox>
