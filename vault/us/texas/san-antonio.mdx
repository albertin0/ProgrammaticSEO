Alright, San Antonio, listen up. Today's a mixed bag, so don't just roll out of bed and hit the pavement without a plan.

<BulletList>
*   Clear skies and relatively mild temperatures mean you won't be slogging through the humidity, which is always a win in March. Expect highs around 70 degrees later, but mornings are still brisk.
*   Moderate pollen levels are hanging around, so if your allergies kick in with cedar, oak, or ragweed, you're going to feel it. Pop your meds early.
*   Major trail closures and ongoing construction are the real buzzkills, especially if your usual route takes you along the greenways.
</BulletList>

Today, March 12, 2026, the air quality is actually looking pretty good with an AQI of 45, which is firmly in the "Good" range. Dominant pollutants are ozone or "other" vague stuff, but nothing to trigger your emergency inhaler. So, your lungs should be pretty happy on that front, unless the pollen monster gets you.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Morning Chill & Wind
While it's clear out, a cold front pushed through, making early morning temps feel colder than the stated 14.9°C (59°F). Expect wind chills in the 30s this morning, thanks to breezy winds with gusts up to 30 mph. Your joints won't thank you if you're not properly warmed up. Layer up, seriously.
</AlertBox>

Now for the bad news, if your legs prefer actual trails to concrete. The City of San Antonio's Parks and Rec department seems to be in a perpetual state of "improvement," which translates to "major headaches" for runners. The **Leon Creek Greenway** is particularly impacted right now. A significant chunk under the Loop 1604 overpass near the Valero Trailhead is closed through April for TxDOT construction. And let's not forget the new $2.9 million expansion project for the Leon Creek trails from Grissom Trailhead to Culebra Road, which started construction in February and won't wrap up until August 2027. So, if you were planning on a nice long uninterrupted haul out there, you'll be rerouting.

Similarly, parts of the **Salado Creek Greenway** around Tobin Park Trailhead are intermittently closed, and the trailhead itself is shut down. And if you frequent the **San Pedro Creek Culture Park**, expect closures on the east bank trail between Cameron Street and the lower trail at two access points due to ongoing work. It's like a game of whack-a-mole with these closures – check the city's parks site before you head out, or you'll be doing an unexpected urban adventure.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Trail Hazard: Mud, Debris, and Diversions
Recent heavy rains earlier this week brought potential for severe storms and heavy rainfall. While today is drier, many unpaved trails are likely still slick, muddy, or littered with debris. Even the paved sections might have standing water or washout areas. Don't go flying around blind corners, especially on the greenway systems. You don't want to end up with a sprained ankle just because you missed a "Trail Closed" sign covered in mud.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Flood Aware Even on Clear Days
San Antonio is a flood-prone city, and it's National Flood Awareness Week for a reason. Even if it's clear today, local waterways can rise fast. Always use common sense near creeks and low-water crossings. The San Antonio River Authority even has HALT (High Water Alert Lifesaving Technology) sensors, so check BexarFlood.org if you're planning a route near any crossings.
</AlertBox>

So, yeah. Get out there, but be smart about it. Stick to known open routes, embrace the layers, and maybe scope out a new neighborhood loop if your favorite trail is a construction zone.
