Look, I get it. The calendar says October, but Mother Nature's still got her summer vacation shades on. Forecasts are calling for low-90s today, but with the sticky, humid air blowing in, the "feels like" temperature is going to push past the triple-digit mark for hours this afternoon. If you *insist* on getting out there, be smart about it. That kind of heat isn't just uncomfortable, it's dangerous. And guess what? Tuesday and Wednesday are expected to be even worse, with heat index values possibly hitting 105°F. Don't be that person getting carted off the trail.

Beyond the scorching conditions, if you planned on hitting the trails, you might run into some roadblocks. The City of San Antonio's Parks and Recreation department has ongoing closures. For example, a portion of the **Leon Creek Greenway** near Loop 1604, specifically north of the Valero Trailhead to The Rim, is still closed due to TxDOT construction and isn't expected to reopen until mid-2027. Similarly, you'll find closures at **Classen-Steubing Ranch Park**, where both Prickly Pear Path and Sotol Descent trails are out of commission through the end of October for construction. Don't even think about trying to navigate some sections of the Salado Creek Greenway either, as various portions are periodically or indefinitely closed for maintenance and repair work.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Heat Exhaustion & Heatstroke Are Real
With heat index values over 100°F, your body is working overtime just to keep cool. Don't push it. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get indoors immediately and cool down. This isn't a drill.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail Closures & Construction Ahead
Check the City of San Antonio Parks and Recreation website for specific closures *before* you head out. Don't waste your time driving to a trailhead only to find a "closed" sign. Especially with work on sections of Leon Creek Greenway and other parks, your usual loop might be cut short.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Early Birds & Night Owls Only
If you absolutely *must* get your miles in, stick to very early mornings or late evenings when the sun isn't directly overhead and the heat index *might* be slightly less brutal. Hydrate like your life depends on it, because frankly, it does. Bring more water than you think you need.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, or better yet, wait for that cold front to roll through later this week. I hear the high 70s are coming for the weekend, now *that's* something to look forward to.
