That AQI of 59 might look benign at first glance, but with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and hints of wildfire smoke and Saharan dust in the air, your lungs know the difference. Pair that with moderate pollen, and it's not exactly prime breathing conditions for anyone pushing hard, especially if you're sensitive.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Chaos Continues:** If your workout route involves the **Leon Creek Greenway** near the Loop 1604/I-10 interchange, you're in for a rough time. TxDOT's ongoing "improvements" mean closures north of the Valero Trailhead all the way to The Rim, and these aren't just weekend annoyances; they're set to last until mid-2027. Additionally, portions of the Salado Creek Greenway, including sections near the Tobin Park Trailhead and from Gembler Road to Houston Street, are facing periodic or temporary shutdowns. Check the city's parks and trails site before you head out, or your run might turn into an unexpected adventure in detours and road running.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Don't Mess with August Heat:** While the current temperature sits at a manageable 28.1°C (82.6°F), forecasts for later today push us into the low 100s for actual temperature and even higher for the "feels like" temp, potentially reaching 109°F due to humidity. The National Weather Service even warned of "moderate heat risk impacts" for the region. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's a straight-up health hazard. City officials have been urging residents to avoid strenuous activity during the hottest part of the day, generally between 2 p.m. and 7 p.m. Stay hydrated, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, don't push it in the middle of the day. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real and deadly. If you're feeling lightheaded, nauseous, or just plain *bad*, get indoors or into the shade immediately and cool down.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**AQI & Pollen Prep:** With PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and moderate pollen counts, sensitive individuals (asthma, allergies) should consider an indoor workout or significantly reduce intensity. If you're heading out, maybe pop an antihistamine and definitely check how your lungs feel. That slight haze isn't just picturesque sunsets; it's a mix of smoke and dust that can irritate your respiratory system.
</AlertBox>
