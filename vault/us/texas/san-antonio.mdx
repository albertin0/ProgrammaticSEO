## The Rundown

Alright, let's cut to the chase. It's September 1st, and Mother Nature apparently thinks we're all training for some kind of hellish desert ultra. Today's temperature is sitting at a balmy 28.1°C (that's 82.6°F for those still on Fahrenheit), but don't let that "Mainly Clear" condition fool you. The forecast is screaming a high of 98°F (37°C) with a heat index that's going to make it *feel* like a staggering 104°F (40°C). Forget PRs; you'll be lucky to avoid heat stroke if you push it too hard outside after about 9 AM.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat & Heat Index
With a forecast high of 98°F and a heat index potentially reaching 104°F, heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real threats. Hydrate excessively *before* you go, during, and after. Consider moving your workout indoors or to the very early morning/late evening. No, seriously. Don't be a hero.
</AlertBox>

## Trail & Road Woes

If you're planning on hitting the trails, you're going to need a map, a compass, and probably a healthy dose of patience. Several sections of our beloved greenway system are still closed or undergoing maintenance. For instance, a chunk of the **Leon Creek Greenway at Loop 1604** is shut down for TxDOT construction until well into mid-2027. And let's not forget the **Culebra Creek Greenway** near Tranquil Creek, which just started a two-week closure on Monday. Even the Riverwalk trail between Mission Rd. and Roosevelt Rd was recently closed for "bushwhacking" that made it too dangerous to pass. Seriously, who bushwhacks the Riverwalk?

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Construction Chaos & Trail Closures
Don't assume your usual route is open. Check the City of San Antonio Parks & Recreation website for closures, especially along the Leon Creek and Salado Creek Greenways. You might find yourself rerouting onto busy roads, which brings its own set of hazards.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, there are currently no active flash flood warnings for San Antonio. However, the mention of Tropical Storm Edouard tracking towards Texas is a good reminder to stay vigilant in the coming days. For now, it seems like the immediate impact is minimal, but don't count out residual damp spots or unstable ground from previous rain on unpaved trails.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Gear Up Smart
If you do head out, light-colored, moisture-wicking clothing is your best friend. Sunscreen is non-negotiable. And for the love of all that is holy, carry more water than you think you need. Or, you know, just stay home and lift some weights in the AC. Your choice.
</AlertBox>
