## The Heat Index is the Real Enemy

Seriously, folks, 24.5°C with an overcast sky sounds pleasant on paper, but this ain't a paper workout. We're looking at "feels like" temperatures that could hit 100 degrees or more by late afternoon, thanks to those high dew points. Your body struggles to cool itself when the air is already saturated with moisture, making you a prime candidate for heat exhaustion or, worse, heatstroke. This isn't some corporate wellness blog fear-mongering; this is basic physiology in a South Texas summer.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Deceptive Heat Index!
The actual temperature might seem mild, but with high humidity, the "feels like" temperature (heat index) will be dangerously high today, especially from late morning through the evening. Strenuous outdoor activity is not recommended during peak heat. Hydrate aggressively, take frequent breaks in the shade, and seriously reconsider your long run.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Mind the Closures and Mud

If you're planning on hitting up the **Salado Creek Greenway**, especially around Tobin Park Trailhead or the Gembler Road to Houston Street section, be aware that there are periodic and even long-term closures for construction and maintenance. Don't just assume your usual route is clear; check the City of San Antonio Parks and Rec website before you head out. Even where trails are "open," after recent heavy rains, you're likely to encounter mud, debris, and potentially slippery surfaces, especially in low-lying areas or near creek crossings. Don't be that person who ignores the "DO NOT ENTER" sign and ends up getting pulled out of a muddy ditch by our already overworked emergency services.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### CAUTION: Trail Conditions and Closures
Recent heavy rainfall means many trails, particularly low-lying sections of greenways like the Salado Creek, might still be muddy, slippery, or have standing water. Furthermore, several sections of the Salado Creek Greenway are under periodic or long-term construction closures. Always check official city resources for current trail status before heading out.
</AlertBox>
