## Your Daily Dose of Reality: Construction & Wind

Look, you live in San Antonio, so you already know our city's in a constant state of "improvement." Today's no different. While the cooler temps and good air might tempt you, you're going to hit some snags if you're out trying to log miles.

### Trail Troubles are Real

Several of our beloved greenways are currently a mess, thanks to ongoing projects. Don't even think about hitting certain sections of the **Leon Creek Greenway** without checking the latest updates. Specifically, the portion under the Loop 1604 overpass near the Valero Trailhead is seeing periodic closures through April due to TXDOT construction. If your route relies on that, find an alternative or be prepared to navigate detours. Likewise, if you usually run or bike the Culebra Creek Greenway, be aware that the trail within Culebra Creek Park connecting to it has been closed for reconstruction since mid-February for approximately three months.

And for those who frequent the Salado Creek Greenway, expect intermittent closures near the Tobin Park Trailhead. It’s all for "improvements," which usually means dust, heavy machinery, and unexpected reroutes for you.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Construction Chaos on Greenways:** Major sections of Leon Creek, Culebra Creek, and Salado Creek Greenways are experiencing closures and construction impacts. Check the City of San Antonio's Parks and Recreation updates or plan your route carefully to avoid frustrating detours.
</AlertBox>

### Watch the Wind, Especially for Fire Risk

Today's overcast skies might hide the sun, but they won't stop the wind. Forecasters predicted breezy conditions with gusts up to 25 mph for today. These aren't hurricane-force winds, but they're enough to make your run feel harder, kick up dust, and dry out vegetation. This dryness, coupled with the wind, means there's an elevated fire risk.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Wind Advisory:** Those breezy winds can make long runs feel tougher. Stay hydrated, and if you're on exposed trails, be mindful of any loose debris.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Elevated Fire Risk:** With breezy conditions, please avoid any outdoor burning. A stray spark can quickly become a serious problem in our dry South Texas landscape.
</AlertBox>

### Pollen and Your Lungs

"Moderate" pollen is often a sneak attack. It's not "high" enough to make headlines, but if you've got seasonal allergies, you'll feel it. The trees are doing their thing, and while the AQI is good, that doesn't account for what nature's directly spraying into your airways.
