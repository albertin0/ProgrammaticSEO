## Today's Rundown: Sweat & Swerve

Look, the skies are clear, the actual air quality isn't actively trying to kill your lungs (a rare win for summer in SA), but let's be real: this heat is no joke. While your phone might say it's a pleasant 25.4°C (77.7°F) right now, the "feels like" temperature is already climbing, and yesterday we were seeing it push towards triple digits thanks to the humidity. You know how it goes here; the sun will be a relentless beast by midday. The City of San Antonio explicitly advises against strenuous activity during the peak heat hours, typically 2-7 p.m. Plan your run, ride, or walk accordingly, or you'll be one of those folks I see slogging it out looking like a melted popsicle.

### Trail Troubles & Traffic Traps

Now, about those trails. While the rain from earlier in the week has mostly cleared out, don't assume everything's pristine. Areas like the Salado Creek Greenway are still dealing with construction closures. You've got periodic closures near the Tobin Park Trailhead and a complete shutdown from Gembler Road to Houston Street until winter. That's a long haul, folks. Expect mud, debris, and potentially still-high water levels in low-lying sections or creek crossings. Even Government Canyon State Natural Area had all trails closed just yesterday due to rainfall and isolated flooding, so don't be surprised if some spots are still squishy.

And if you're driving to your favorite spot, remember the ongoing headache at the Loop 1604 and I-10 interchange. There are significant lane and frontage road closures until Monday morning for bridge construction. This isn't just a minor inconvenience; it's a "plan a different route or add an hour to your commute" kind of situation.

### Trail Closures & Mud: Don't Be That Person
Respect posted trail closures, especially around construction zones like sections of the Salado Creek Greenway and San Pedro Creek Culture Park. A "closed" sign means closed for a reason, usually your safety. Flash floods can recede, but the damage, mud, and slick surfaces remain. Don't risk an injury for a few extra miles.
