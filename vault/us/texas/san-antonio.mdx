## Today's Rundown: Clear Skies Now, Danger Ahead

Alright, San Antonio, it's July 21st, and right now, the morning is clear and a relatively mild 25°C (77°F). Don't let that fool you into thinking it's prime summer running weather all day, or for the rest of the week. While we don't have any *active* extreme heat advisories for *right this moment*, the National Weather Service is predicting a rapid climb. Wednesday is set to be the hottest day of the year so far, with temps hitting 38°C (100°F) in town and a scorching 40°C (105°F) south of the city, with heat index values making it feel even worse. We're talking a "moderate to major heat risk" across the region on Wednesday and Thursday. So, if you're lacing up, do it early, and don't push it.

## Trail & Road Hazards: The Usual San Antonio Shuffle

If you're planning to hit the trails, you'll need to do some homework. Several parts of our beloved greenway system are currently impacted. For example, a significant portion of the **Leon Creek Greenway** at Loop 1604 (north of the Valero Trailhead) is closed for TxDOT construction until mid-2027. Over on the Salado Creek Greenway, the section to the Tobin Park Trailhead is seeing intermittent closures, and the stretch from Gembler Road to Houston Street is completely shut down for maintenance.

And let's not forget the never-ending construction. The VIA Rapid Green Line project is active along San Pedro Avenue, meaning intermittent lane and sidewalk closures. Cyclists and pedestrians need to be extra cautious. Plus, there's ongoing TxDOT work on Loop 1604 and I-35 that's always a fun surprise.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Flooding & Trail Closures**: Parts of Jackson Nature Park and lower sections of River Crossing Park, Graytown, and Helton are still closed due to recent flooding from Cibolo Creek. Do not ignore these closures. "Turn around, don't drown" applies to trails too, folks.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pet Safety - Screwworm Alert**: This is a weird one, but serious. There's a screwworm alert for our parks, including the San Antonio River Trail area. Inspect your pets daily for unusual wounds or maggots. If you see something, call the Texas Animal Health Commission.
</AlertBox>

Also, it looks like the El Chupacabra de San Antonio 5K/10K race might be happening today. While the specific location is vague, be aware that events can block smaller roads or trail sections.

My advice? If you're going out, go early. Bring more water than you think you need. Stick to paved areas if you're worried about screwworms and muddy, potentially washed-out trails. And for Pete's sake, keep an eye on the forecast for Wednesday. That's when things get truly gnarly.
