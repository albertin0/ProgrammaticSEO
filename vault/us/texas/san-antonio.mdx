## Your Daily SA Workout Debrief

Let's get real: October 6th in San Antonio isn't winning any awards for prime outdoor workout conditions. That AQI of 95, with PM2.5 being the main culprit, is a legitimate concern, especially for us runners who are sucking down air faster than a vacuum cleaner. While the TCEQ forecast said "Good" a few days ago, today they're calling for "Ozone/PM2.5" in the lower "Moderate" range for San Antonio. Doesn't exactly inspire confidence, does it? The city's already in "Serious Nonattainment" for ozone, so our air isn't doing us any favors to begin with.

And then there's the never-ending construction. I get it, progress, future, blah blah. But when TxDOT is shutting down major sections of I-35 and Loop 410 *nightly* for their multi-billion dollar expansion projects, you know that means more car exhaust hanging around, even if you're hitting the trails. If your go-to route involves getting near these arteries, expect a side order of fumes with your workout.

Remember last week's flooding? Roads were closed all over the place. While the waters have receded, the San Antonio River Authority is still warning about mud and debris on trails after rain events. We've got a chance of rain returning later this week, so some of those unpaved greenway paths might still be a mess. Don't be "that guy" who sprains an ankle trying to leap a puddle.

Speaking of trails, if you had plans to hit up Gilbert Garza Park for a quick loop, forget it. That park's loop trail, multipurpose field, and softball field #2 are *closed until January 2027* for the Zarzamora Creek Greenway Trail project. You'll be rerouting for a while there. Also, a section of the Leon Creek Greenway near Loop 1604 and under I-10 near the Marquis Trailhead is still shut down due to TxDOT work and a fallen highway overpass. So, much for your long run uninterrupted.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch for localized smoke.** While not a major wildfire event in Bexar County today, "small, scattered burning activities across Southeastern Texas may continue to produce light density residual smoke and slightly elevate PM2.5 levels over this region of the state." This, combined with the general AQI, is a double-whammy for your lungs.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Don't ignore that AQI number.** If you have any respiratory issues or just feel "off," cut your outdoor session short or take it indoors. AQI 95 with PM2.5 isn't great, and pushing it won't make you tougher, just more congested.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate.** It’s still warm out there, even if it feels "cooler" than last month. You're losing fluids, and that "Mainly Clear" sky means the sun is beating down. Also, consider an early morning or late evening run to dodge the highest temps and potentially better air.
</AlertBox>
