## The Full Rundown: What to Expect Out There

Look, I get it. The 19.4°C (67°F) and overcast skies make it feel like prime running weather, a welcome reprieve from the brutal heat we usually bake in. But that's a trap, folks. We've got a significant chance of scattered showers and thunderstorms today, and those storms can drop heavy rain *fast*.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**SERIOUS FLOOD RISK:** A Flood Watch is in effect for Bexar County and much of south-central Texas. This means creeks can rise rapidly, and low-water crossings become death traps. Remember the May 1st incident where flash floods led to a fatality and numerous road closures near San Pedro Creek? "Turn Around, Don't Drown" isn't a suggestion; it's how you stay alive. Check BexarFlood.org before you even consider leaving your driveway.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Status & Construction Headaches

The Howard W. Peak Greenway Trails System, bless its ever-expanding heart, is a perennial construction zone. Right now, parts of the Salado Creek Greenway are periodically closed due to TXDOT construction and general maintenance. There's also ongoing work at Tom Slick Park impacting trail access, expected to run through May. Don't be surprised if your usual route is either fenced off, a muddy mess, or completely underwater. SARA explicitly warns about Camino Coahuilteca and River Front Parkway being closed due to high water.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**CHECK BEFORE YOU GO:** Many San Antonio trails, especially those along our numerous creeks, are prone to flooding and closures after rain. Assume any trail near a creek or low-lying area is compromised. Period.
</AlertBox>

### Air Quality & Allergy Report

Our air quality today is actually decent, sitting at an AQI of 45. That's a green light for your lungs. However, pollen is hanging out at a moderate level. If you're one of the many San Antonians who get hammered by seasonal allergies, you might still feel some sniffles and sneezes, especially with lingering moisture in the air.
