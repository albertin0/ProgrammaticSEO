## Today's San Antonio Grind

Look, I get it. We're runners. We like to pretend a little heat builds character. But character-building heat that’s pushing 95°F by late afternoon with an already muggy start to the day isn't "character." It's just asking for trouble. This September has been brutal, continuing a streak of being one of the hottest on record, and today's no exception. Leave the heroics for race day (which, by the way, isn't today, so calm down).

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: It's a Sauna Out There
Temperatures are forecast to hit the mid-90s, potentially topping out around 95°F later today. While no *official* heat advisory has been issued for this specific moment, the prolonged heat in San Antonio means your body is already working overtime. Hydration isn't just a suggestion; it's survival. Go early, go short, or go inside. Don't be that person getting carted off by EMS from a casual jog down your street. You know who you are.
</AlertBox>

### Air You Can (Almost) Taste

Our AQI is clocked at 56, falling into that "Moderate" category with ozone and other pollutants as the dominant players. For most of us, it’s not going to kill you, but if you've got asthma, COPD, or just generally enjoy breathing without a metallic aftertaste, maybe take it easy. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality even suggested ozone and PM2.5 could reach the "upper end of the 'Moderate' range" in parts of the city today. It's not a full-blown air quality alert, but it's enough to make you consider the treadmill.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Air Quality for Sensitive Lungs
An AQI of 56 means the air quality is "generally acceptable," but sensitive groups (read: anyone who finds themselves coughing or wheezing when they push it) might experience some symptoms with prolonged exposure. Consider adjusting your intensity or duration, especially if you're hitting those high-traffic areas.
</AlertBox>

## Pollen & Trail Hassles

Our pollen count is holding steady at a "Moderate" level. So, if you're sneezing through your run already, prepare for more of the same. Keep those allergy meds handy.

And, because nothing makes a planned workout more irritating than a spontaneous detour, be aware of ongoing trail closures. A significant stretch of the **Leon Creek Greenway** at Loop 1604, specifically north of the Valero Trailhead to The Rim, is still shut down due to TxDOT construction and will be until mid-2027. You also can't access the Garza Park Loop Trail until January 2027 for the Zarzamora Creek Greenway Trail Project. Even parts of Classen-Steubing Ranch Park are closed through *this month* for construction. Check the city's parks and rec site before you head out, unless you enjoy backtracking and running through dusty construction zones.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route: Trail Closures & Upcoming Events
Various sections of greenway trails, including parts of the Leon Creek Greenway and Garza Park, remain closed for construction. Always check the City of San Antonio Parks & Recreation website for the latest closures before heading out. Also, a heads-up: several races, including the SNIPSA Race for the Rescues 5K and the Golden Harvest Half Marathon & 5k/10k, are scheduled for this weekend. While they likely won't block *your* specific trail today, they mean more people are out, and tomorrow's routes might be impacted.
</AlertBox>

My advice? Embrace the gym, or get your outdoor miles in before the sun decides to actively try and melt you.
