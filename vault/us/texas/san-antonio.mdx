## The Air You Breathe (or Don't)

Let's talk about that air. An AQI of 95, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, is firmly in the "Moderate" category, but it's a hair's breadth from "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups." If you've got asthma, COPD, or just feel like your throat gets scratchy after a run, today is *not* the day to test your limits. Forget trying to break your personal best on the Salado Creek Greenway; your lungs will hate you for it. This isn't wildfire smoke, thank god, but it's still particulate matter that gets deep into your respiratory system.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### PM2.5 is Your Lung's Arch-Nemesis
These tiny particles are small enough to bypass your body's natural defenses and get straight into your lungs, and even your bloodstream. Moderate AQI might sound okay, but sustained exposure or high exertion can definitely irritate your airways. If you feel any chest tightness, coughing, or shortness of breath, cut your workout short. Seriously, it's not worth it.
</AlertBox>

## Heat & Hydration: Don't Be a Statistic

It's 28.1°C (that's 82.6°F for you Fahrenheit folks) and "Mainly Clear." Translation: the sun is already beating down, and there's no cloud cover to save you. We all know how fast the mercury climbs here. By noon, it'll feel like running inside a pizza oven. If you're hitting the Mission Reach trails, remember that shade is sparse. Carry more water than you think you need, and for the love of all that is holy, don't wait until you're dizzy to start drinking. That's called dehydration, and it's a one-way ticket to a bad time, possibly with paramedics.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Early Bird Gets the... Less Sweaty Worm
If you're dead set on an outdoor workout today, your window is closing fast. Get out there before 8 AM, or resign yourself to the treadmill. Trying to run in the midday San Antonio sun when it's this clear and already warm is just asking for trouble.
