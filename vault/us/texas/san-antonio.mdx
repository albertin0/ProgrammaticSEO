## The Air: Not Exactly Crisp Hill Country Air

Look, San Antonio is hot. We all know this. But what often gets overlooked is what we're actually *breathing* when we're trying to push ourselves on those long stretches of the Salado Creek Greenway. Today, with an AQI clocking in at 95 and PM2.5 as the main culprit, it means there's a decent amount of microscopic junk floating around. This isn't like wildfire smoke where you can see the apocalypse, but these tiny particles can still irritate your respiratory system, especially for those of us with sensitive lungs or existing conditions. Think of it as a subtle sandpapering of your airways.

Add to that the "Moderate" pollen count – probably from a mix of ragweed and various grasses that decide August is their time to shine – and you've got a double whammy for your respiratory system. It's not enough to keep you indoors entirely, but it's certainly enough to make that tempo run feel a whole lot harder than it should.

### Weather & Hydration: The Usual San Antonio Tango

The temperature is sitting at a balmy 28.1°C (about 82.6°F) with mainly clear skies. This means two things: it's already warm, and the sun's going to be beating down on you without much cloud cover to offer a reprieve. Starting your workout early is always the move here, but today, it's non-negotiable if you plan on going for more than a quick neighborhood stroll. You'll be sweating, so hydrate, hydrate, hydrate. And then hydrate some more. Don't be that person collapsing on the River Walk because you thought a single bottle of water would cut it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
    The combination of moderate PM2.5 and moderate pollen means your lungs and sinuses are in for a rough ride. If you feel any chest tightness, unusual coughing, or excessive sneezing, dial it back or head indoors. This isn't the day to PR your breathing.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Conditions & Local Buzz

No major news on widespread trail closures or extreme conditions like flooding or construction blocking the popular routes around town in the last 24 hours. So, if you're planning on hitting up McAllister Park or exploring parts of the Leon Creek Greenway, you should be fine in terms of physical obstructions. Just remember to be mindful of fellow runners, cyclists, and the occasional wildlife that thinks it owns the path.

### Safety Check: When a Good Day Goes Sideways

Always tell someone where you're going and when you expect to be back. Carry your phone. Maybe some ID. This isn't rocket science, but people get complacent. With the heat and potential for air quality issues to worsen throughout the day, having a backup plan or an emergency contact is just smart.

<AlertBox type="info">
    Consider wearing a lightweight, moisture-wicking mask if the PM2.5 starts to bother you. It won't filter everything, but it can help reduce your exposure to airborne irritants. Just be aware it might make breathing harder, so listen to your body.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
    Heat exhaustion and heatstroke are real threats, even when it "only" feels warm. If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get to shade and cool down immediately. Don't be a hero; your health isn't worth a few extra miles.
</AlertBox>
