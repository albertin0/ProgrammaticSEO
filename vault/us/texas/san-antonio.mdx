## The Heat is On (Literally)

Look, I know 24.6 degrees Celsius sounds lovely if you're European, but here in San Antonio, it's just a placeholder for the inferno that's coming. The National Weather Service is predicting actual temperatures to hit 101-102 degrees Fahrenheit by 4 PM today, with humidity pushing the "feels like" temperature—your heat index—up to a nasty 105 degrees. That's a moderate heat risk, folks, and frankly, moderate is generous when your sweat can’t even evaporate. This isn't the day to try for a PR on the Mission Reach at high noon. Seriously, you'll regret it when you're seeing mirages of Big Red and tacos.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Afternoon Triple-Digit Heat!**
Plan your outdoor workouts *early* or just don't. Hydration isn't a suggestion; it's the only thing keeping you from becoming a heat casualty. Take more water than you think you need, and don't be a hero.
</AlertBox>

## Breathing Easy (Mostly)

The good news, if you can call it that, is our Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at 50, which is "Good." So, while there's some hazy nastiness from distant Pacific Northwest wildfires floating high above us, it's not expected to mess with our ground-level air quality. You won't be inhaling ash, just humid, hot air. However, San Antonio has a history of ozone issues, and with all that sunshine cooking up pollutants, it's always something to keep an eye on, especially if you've got sensitive lungs.

Pollen is moderate today, especially for mold spores and those stubborn weeds. If you're susceptible, you know the drill: pre-medicate, shower immediately after your run, and try to avoid rolling through any freshly mown grass near O.P. Schnabel Park. Your seasonal allergies don't care about your Strava segments.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Alert: Closures on Greenways**
Parts of our beloved greenway system are still impacted. A significant portion of the Leon Creek Greenway near Loop 1604 (from the Valero Trailhead north to The Rim) is closed for TxDOT construction, potentially until mid-2027. And if you typically hit Friedrich Wilderness Park, all trails there are *temporarily closed* as of mid-July. Check city parks and trail websites before you head out, especially for your longer runs.
</AlertBox>

Overall, get out early, respect the heat, and watch your step. Or, you know, hit the gym. No judgment.
