## The Concrete Jungle Strikes Again: Trail Closures Everywhere

Alright, San Antonio, if you're planning a long, uninterrupted run on your favorite greenway, you might need to recalibrate your expectations. The city is a patchwork of construction zones, and our trails are feeling the brunt of it.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**MAJOR TRAIL CLOSURES & CONSTRUCTION:** The sheer volume of ongoing projects means you need to check your route twice.
*   The Culebra Creek Park trail is closed for a three-month reconstruction.
*   Sections of the Leon Creek Greenway under the 1604 overpass are seeing periodic closures thanks to TXDOT.
*   A big chunk of the Salado Creek Greenway, specifically from Gembler Road to Houston Street, is shut down until *winter 2026*. If you were planning a long haul there, reroute entirely. Intermittent closures also affect the portion leading to Tobin Park Trailhead.
*   Even the Brackenridge Park Trail has electrical issues, with lighting out on the north side. Repairs are supposedly happening, but use caution, especially if you're out near the Josephine Street inlet after sundown.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, the air itself isn't trying to kill you with smog. Our AQI is a respectable 50. However, don't ignore that "Moderate" pollen level. If you're sensitive, you might still feel like you've run through a field of cotton balls. Keep those allergy meds handy.

The weather is actually pretty decent for a run, all things considered. Overcast skies at 76°F beat direct sun any day. Just remember that we've had our fair share of rain recently, and isolated showers are still in the forecast for later this evening. This means trails could be muddy, uneven, and covered in debris, even if they aren't officially "closed." And let's not forget that general "very warm and muggy" feeling overnight.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Pollen's Pestering Presence:** Moderate pollen levels mean seasonal allergy sufferers will still have a tough time. Don't skip your meds today.
</AlertBox>

So, lace up, but pack your patience and maybe a map of alternate routes. You'll likely need it.
