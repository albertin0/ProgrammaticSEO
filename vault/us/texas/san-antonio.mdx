## Hazards & Head-Ups

### Overcast Doesn't Mean Cool Later

That nice overcast sky might be fooling you into thinking it's a cool October day, but don't be a rookie. While it's a pleasant 25.9°C right now, the heat index is forecast to spike to a brutal 38-40°C (100-104°F) this afternoon. If you're out there when the sun starts peaking through, or later in the day, you're looking at a real battle.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Rising Heat Index!**
If you're heading out after noon, be prepared for rapidly increasing heat and humidity, pushing the "feels like" temperature into dangerous territory. Hydrate aggressively, consider rescheduling, or keep it short.
</AlertBox>

### Afternoon Thunderstorms Rolling In

The nice weather window closes around lunchtime. We've got a 20-30% chance of scattered showers and thunderstorms developing between 1 PM and 4 PM today, with storm chances increasing after that. Nobody wants to be caught on an exposed trail when lightning starts cracking.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Afternoon Thunderstorms Expected!**
Showers and thunderstorms are forecast for this afternoon, especially after 4 PM. If you hear thunder, it's time to bail. Wet trails mean slippery conditions, and lightning means you're a human lightning rod.
</AlertBox>

### Construction Continues to Clog the Greenways

If you're a fan of the linear parks, you already know the drill: orange cones and "Trail Closed" signs are practically permanent fixtures. The **Leon Creek Greenway** is particularly hit, with a significant portion near Loop 1604 closed for TxDOT construction until at least mid-2027, and another section near the Marquis Trailhead blocked by a fallen highway overpass. Parts of the Salado Creek Greenway are also undergoing various repair closures. Don't count on a clear run.

<AlertBox type="info">
**TRAIL TIP: Plan Alternate Routes!**
Check the City of San Antonio's parks and recreation website before you head out, especially for the Leon Creek and Salado Creek Greenways. These aren't minor detours; some sections are entirely impassable for extended periods. Don't be that runner who ends up miles off course, fuming.
</AlertBox>

All in all, get out early if you're determined, but keep it shorter and stay aware of how fast those conditions can change in this city. It's October, but San Antonio still likes to play summer games.
