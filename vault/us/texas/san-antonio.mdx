## The Full Breakdown

Let's rip off the band-aid. The temperature is a lovely 22.5°C (about 72.5°F) with mainly clear skies, which sounds idyllic, right? Almost too good to be true for mid-April in South Texas. And usually, when things sound too good, they are.

Your lungs will be doing alright today, with an AQI of 37, which is firmly in the "Good" category. The dominant pollutant is listed as Ozone/Other, and while PM2.5 levels are "Fair" at around 44, it's not a major red flag for most folks.

However, if you're like me and your sinuses have their own personal calendar for allergen season, you're not entirely off the hook. Pollen is at a moderate level, so don't forget your nasal spray or antihistamines if you plan on pushing it.

### Trail Talk: Don't Go Blind

Speaking of dodging things, let's talk trails. The Salado Creek Greenway has ongoing closures, notably a section from Gembler Road to Houston Street that's expected to be out of commission until winter 2026. Good luck with that. You'll also find intermittent shutdowns near the Tobin Park Trailhead due to TxDOT projects.

The Leon Creek Greenway isn't immune either, with periodic closures under the 1604 overpass north of the Valero Trailhead. And don't even think about the Oxbow Trail along the Museum Reach — it's closed until May 1st. Always check the City of San Antonio Parks and Rec site for current trail conditions and maintenance alerts before you head out, unless you enjoy running into "trail closed" signs and having to reroute mid-sufferfest.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Watch Your Step on City Streets.** Major construction continues on Loop 1604, I-10, and US 281, with various lane and frontage road closures happening, especially on weekends and at night. If you're cycling or running on city streets, be hyper-aware of your surroundings, temporary detours, and distracted drivers navigating the mess. A minor inconvenience for a car can be a major injury for a runner or cyclist.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**My PT Advice:** With all the urban obstacles, consider taking your workout to a less congested spot, or hit the gym if you need uninterrupted miles. If you *must* run outside, pick a familiar, less-impacted neighborhood route away from the Fiesta madness and major construction zones. And seriously, look up those trail closures beforehand. Your knees and sanity will thank you.
</AlertBox>
