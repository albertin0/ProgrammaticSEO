## The Daily Grind in San Antonio: August 23, 2026

Look, I get it. We're runners, we're movers, we want to hit the pavement or the dirt. But some days, Mother Nature (and TxDOT, bless their hearts) is actively working against us. Today is one of those days.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat Advisory IN EFFECT
The National Weather Service has extended the Heat Advisory through today, Sunday, and likely into next week. We're looking at actual temperatures hitting 103-104°F, and the "feels like" temperature will be even worse, easily climbing to 108-110°F. This isn't just about feeling sweaty; we're talking about a serious risk of heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and dehydration. If you *must* be outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and hydrate like it's your job. But honestly? Find an air-conditioned gym or just take a rest day. Your body will thank you.
</AlertBox>

### Air Quality Report: Don't Breathe Too Deeply
Our Air Quality Index (AQI) is sitting at a solid 95 for PM2.5, which puts us squarely in the "Moderate" category. The dominant pollutant is PM2.5, a fine particulate matter. This isn't some fresh breeze, folks. We've got Saharan dust hanging around and some lingering, albeit mostly aloft, smoke from wildfires far away. Combine that with the heat, and you're just putting extra strain on your respiratory system.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### AQI and Pollen: Double Whammy
With moderate AQI *and* moderate pollen levels, sensitive groups (asthmatics, allergy sufferers, basically anyone with lungs that aren't made of steel) should seriously limit prolonged exertion outdoors. Even if you're not usually affected, the combination with this brutal heat can sneak up on you.
</AlertBox>

### Construction & Closures: Your Escape Routes Are Blocked
Still dealing with the never-ending roadwork. The city's website is a minefield of closures. For instance, a portion of the **Leon Creek Greenway at Loop 1604**, specifically north of the Valero Trailhead toward The Rim, is still shut down for TxDOT construction, and won't reopen until mid-2027. That's a long damn time to wait for a clear path. Other sections of the Salado Creek Greenway are also closed for repair work, like the stretch south of the Southside Lions Park entrance until October 9th. And let's not forget the major Loop 1604 and I-10 closures from Friday until Monday morning for bridge construction and that new Diverging Diamond Interchange at Blanco Road. Even if you're not running *on* the highway, getting *to* a trail is going to be a nightmare of traffic and detours.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Pro-Tip: Plan Your Attack (or Retreat)
If you insist on venturing out, check the City of San Antonio Parks & Recreation website for the absolute latest on trail closures *before* you leave. There's nothing more frustrating than getting geared up only to hit a "trail closed" sign. And seriously, carry more water than you think you need. Or, you know, run on a treadmill and blast the AC.
</AlertBox>
