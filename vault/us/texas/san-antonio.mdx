## The Real Talk

Look, I love hitting the pavement or the trails as much as the next sane person, but today is not the day to prove your toughness. This "extreme heat" business isn't a suggestion; it's a warning from people who know what they're talking about. Even if you're not planning a marathon, just being outside for extended periods during the peak heat can be dangerous.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning in Effect!** Do NOT plan strenuous outdoor workouts between noon and 8 PM today. "Feels like" temperatures are projected to hit 112-115°F. This is a life-threatening situation for unacclimated individuals or those pushing too hard. Drink copious amounts of water, stay in air-conditioning, and check on vulnerable neighbors.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, your body will thank you for taking a rest day or moving your workout indoors. There are plenty of gyms in town, or even bodyweight circuits you can do in your living room. Pushing through this kind of heat isn't heroic; it's just dumb, and I say that as someone who's seen plenty of people hauled off trails like the Salado Creek Greenway looking greener than the grass after underestimating San Antonio summers.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch for Trail Conditions!** Due to heavy rainfall earlier this week, expect potential lingering mud, slick spots, and debris on unpaved trails. Even if specific closures from Monday's flash floods are lifted, exercise extreme caution, especially around low-water crossings or creek beds. The City of San Antonio also has ongoing construction on sections like the Leon Creek Greenway near Loop 1604, so be aware of any reroutes or closed-off areas.
</AlertBox>

If you absolutely *must* get out before the worst of the heat hits or after it subsides (and I'm talking *after* 8 PM, not 7:59 PM), hydrate like your life depends on it (because it does), wear light-colored, loose clothing, and listen to your body. That moderate pollen might make your nose run, but the heat will make everything else stop.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Key (Always, but Especially Today).** If you *do* venture outside, carry more water than you think you need. Consider electrolyte drinks to replenish salts lost through sweating. Heat illness can sneak up on you fast in this kind of humidity.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, or better yet, stay *in* there.
