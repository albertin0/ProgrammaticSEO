### Sweat Now, Ache Later? Maybe.

Alright, San Antonio, it's Sunday, March 29th, and while the skies are "mainly clear," don't let that fool you into thinking it's a chill day for a run. The mercury's climbing to 28.1°C (that's 82.6°F for those still stuck in Fahrenheit land), which means your "easy" run is going to feel a lot more like a "why did I do this to myself" grind. We've been dancing with near-record heat lately, and while today isn't hitting the 90s, the trend is clear, and your body needs to adjust.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heat Advisory Jr.:** With temperatures in the low 80s, make sure you're getting enough fluids. Start hydrating *before* you step out, and carry water, especially if you're planning anything longer than a quick loop around your block. Overdoing it in this kind of heat is how you end up sidelined, or worse.
</AlertBox>

Now, let's talk about what you're breathing. The Air Quality Index is currently sitting pretty at 48, which is generally considered good. However, that doesn't account for the "Pollen: Moderate" situation. If your nose is already running a marathon of its own, you know what I mean. Add to that the lovely scent of residual smoke from that mulch fire that's been smoldering over on the Southwest Side near Nelson Road since mid-March. It might not be a huge cloud, but if you're sensitive, you'll definitely notice it. Your lungs aren't going to be thrilled about the combo, especially with the heat.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Road Block Central:** If you planned to run anywhere near downtown, the South Side, or Mission Concepción this morning, change your plans immediately. The Run the Alamo Marathon and Half Marathon has significant road closures in effect from 7 AM to 2 PM. Trying to navigate that chaos is a recipe for frustration and potential injury, weaving through traffic or an unexpected horde of exhausted runners. Stay clear.
</AlertBox>

Beyond the race-day detours, don't forget our city's never-ending construction projects. A portion of the Leon Creek Greenway under the 1604 overpass near the Valero Trailhead is still seeing periodic closures through April due to TXDOT work. And if you were eyeing Lees Creek Park, their loop walking trail has been closed for improvements and, according to the city, officially reopens *tomorrow*, March 31st, but is still considered closed for today. Don't be "that guy" trying to sneak onto a closed trail; it's closed for a reason, usually your safety.
