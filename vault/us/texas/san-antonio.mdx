Yes, it's a beautiful day. You're not going to melt, freeze, or choke on wildfire smoke, which, let's be honest, is a win in itself for San Antonio sometimes. But before you lace up and head out blindly, remember this ain't a free-for-all.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures & Construction Headaches**

Seriously, pick your path carefully. The City of San Antonio and TxDOT are busy folks, and that means chunks of our prime running real estate are often off-limits.
</AlertBox>

Specifically, if you're planning a scenic long run on the **Mission Reach**, know that ongoing "stem-density maintenance" (read: tree and bush removal to manage flood risk) means crews are out there Monday through Friday during the day. Expect temporary trail closures or detours. Don't be that runner who ignores the signage and ends up face-planting into a pile of brush or, worse, causing a construction delay. Those guys don't mess around.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Always Check Before You Trek:**
Before you commit to a long run, do a quick check on the San Antonio Parks and Recreation website or the San Antonio River Authority for the latest trail closure updates. They might not be as timely as we'd like, but it's better than running into a dead end (literally).
</AlertBox>

Other spots getting work done include parts of the Salado Creek Greenway near the Tobin Park Trailhead, Lees Creek Park's loop walking trail until the end of March, and a section of the Leon Creek Greenway under the 1604 overpass. It's a maze out there, so don't assume your usual route is clear.

No major marathons or events are shutting down downtown streets today, which is a nice change of pace after some of the past weekends. So, you won't be dodging cones and race volunteers, but you might be dodging heavy equipment. Choose wisely, San Antonio.
