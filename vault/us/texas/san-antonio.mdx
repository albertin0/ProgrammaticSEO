## Workout Hazards & Local Impact

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Perpetual Construction Blues
San Antonio's infrastructure upgrade never sleeps, and neither does the accompanying chaos. You're going to hit some snags.
</AlertBox>

First up, if you're usually cruising down the **Leon Creek Greenway**, know that parts of it are still a mess. There's an ongoing TxDOT construction project closing a section at Loop 1604, north of the Valero Trailhead, until mid-2027. And don't even think about running near the I-10 connection if you're by the Marquis Trailhead — a fallen highway overpass has that area shut down. Seriously, stay clear. It's not worth the twisted ankle or impromptu detour into traffic.

Then there's the less-than-clear situation at **Classen-Steubing Ranch Park**. The Prickly Pear Path and Sotol Descent trails, plus a bit of the paved loop, were supposedly closed "through October 2026" for construction. Given we're now in November, it *might* be open, but don't count on it. Always check the City Parks website before you head out, or prepare for an impromptu trail running bootcamp if you hit a barrier. Better safe than sorry when it comes to dodging construction equipment. The City of San Antonio's Parks and Facilities updates are crucial here.

Beyond the trails, major roadwork could also affect how you get to your preferred workout spot, or even your run itself if you're a city pavement pounder. The northbound Loop 1604 entrance ramp north of US Highway 90 is closed until tomorrow afternoon for the US 90 Expansion Project. And over on the Northside, the westbound Loop 410 exit ramp to Northwest Military Highway is shut down through the end of 2027 for a massive demolition and rebuild project. This means more cars funneled onto local streets, so keep your head on a swivel if your route takes you anywhere near these choke points.

And just a heads-up for downtown runners: a 24-inch cast-iron water main broke recently beneath the Broadway underpass. While current impacts on foot traffic aren't fully detailed, a major break like that can lead to unexpected soggy patches or uneven surfaces in the vicinity.

Today is a prime example of San Antonio running: beautiful weather, great air, but you gotta navigate the city's constant state of "progress." Pick your path wisely, stay aware, and enjoy those miles while you can.
