Forget about that morning run; today is a day to stay indoors and embrace the air conditioning, unless you enjoy feeling like a slow-cooked tamale.

<LungsJointsScore score={2} />

<BulletList>
*   **Brutal Heat:** We're staring down heat index values that could hit 113°F (45°C) this afternoon, making any outdoor activity a straight-up health risk.
*   **Questionable Air:** The AQI is pushing 95, fueled by PM2.5, Saharan dust, and even some far-off wildfire smoke. Don't be surprised if your lungs feel like a smoker's after a brisk walk.
*   **Trail Treachery:** Several popular greenway trails are still messed up with construction and repairs. Good luck finding a clear path that isn't a detour or outright closed.

Look, San Antonio, I get it. You want to get out there, pound the pavement, or hit the trails. But seriously, today is not the day. The National Weather Service has a Heat Advisory slapped on us until 7 PM, with actual temperatures hovering around 98°F (36.9°C) and the "feels like" temperature skyrocketing to a dangerous 113°F. That's not just uncomfortable; that's heatstroke territory. Your body's not going to cool down effectively, and you'll be risking more than just a bad tan line.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat & Heat Index Warning
With temperatures nearing 104°F and heat index values reaching 113°F, strenuous outdoor activity is highly dangerous. Limit time outside, stay hydrated, and recognize symptoms of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Call 911 for emergencies.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. Our Air Quality Index is currently sitting at a "Moderate" 95, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. We've got a delightful cocktail of Saharan dust, local emissions, and residual smoke from wildfires far away in the Pacific Northwest and Rocky Mountain region contributing to this mess. Don't think for a second that "moderate" means "totally fine," especially if you have any respiratory sensitivities. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality even warns that San Antonio could be reclassified to "severe nonattainment" for ozone next year, so this isn't just a fluke.

Adding insult to injury, we've got moderate pollen levels out there – think grass and weed pollen trying to invade your sinuses. So, while you're struggling to breathe the hazy air in the oven-like heat, your nose and eyes get to have a party too. Fun, right?

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Air Quality & Pollen Alert
AQI is nearing 100 with PM2.5 and ozone concerns. Expect eye and respiratory irritation, especially for sensitive groups. Moderate grass and weed pollen levels will only compound the misery. Consider an N95 mask if you *must* be outside, but seriously, just stay in.
</AlertBox>

If you were planning on hitting up the Leon Creek Greenway for a little peace and quiet, think again. A significant portion of the Leon Creek Greenway at Loop 1604, from north of the Valero Trailhead to The Rim, is still closed for TxDOT construction, and that's not expected to clear up until mid-2027. Same goes for parts of the Salado Creek Greenway, with closures south of the Southside Lions Park entrance until early October for repair work. It's a patchwork of detours and dead ends out there.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Trail Closures & Construction
Avoid portions of the Leon Creek Greenway near Loop 1604 and the Salado Creek Greenway south of Southside Lions Park entrance due to ongoing construction and repair work. Always check the City of San Antonio Parks & Recreation website for the latest closures before heading out.
</AlertBox>

So, do yourself a favor: find an indoor workout, hit the gym, or just curl up with a cold drink and wait for a day that doesn't feel like a sauna designed by Satan himself.
