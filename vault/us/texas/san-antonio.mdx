Today? It’s a classic San Antonio "don't get your hopes up *too* high" kind of day for outdoor workouts.

<LungsJointsScore score={6} />

<BulletList>
*   **Pollen's a nuisance, not a catastrophe.** Moderate levels mean your seasonal allergies might hum a low tune, but it shouldn't send you scrambling for the inhaler unless you're extra sensitive. Still, keep those Zyrtec tabs handy.
*   **Construction is the real MVP of trail obstruction.** Seriously, it feels like every greenway in town is getting a facelift or a full-on demolition. Your usual routes are probably looking more like an obstacle course, and not the fun kind.
*   **Weather's playing nice, mostly.** Overcast and 21.5°C (that's about 71°F for those still stuck in Fahrenheit-land) means no scorching sun, which is a rare gift this time of year. Enjoy it while it lasts.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. You want to hit the trails, shake off the morning cobwebs, maybe pretend you're not going to eat breakfast tacos for lunch. And honestly, the air quality is great (AQI 37? Practically pristine for SA). The temperature is damn near perfect for a long run without melting into the pavement. But here's the kicker, and this is where my PT brain starts screaming at you: the trails are a minefield of closures right now.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heads up on the Greenways:** If your go-to is anywhere near the **Salado Creek Greenway**, especially heading towards the Tobin Park Trailhead, you might hit a dead end. TxDOT is out there doing "construction projects," which usually translates to "you're going to have to find another way around, buddy." It's not just Salado, either. San Pedro Creek Culture Park and sections of the Leon Creek Greenway are also dealing with various forms of "improvements" or "maintenance." This isn't just an inconvenience; unexpected detours onto unfamiliar, possibly uneven, or high-traffic surfaces are how you twist an ankle or get clipped by a car. Plan your route, and for the love of all that is holy, *check for closures before you leave*. The city's Parks & Recreation website usually has updates, though sometimes they're about as fresh as yesterday's pan dulce.
</AlertBox>

Pollen's sitting at a moderate level today. For some of you, that's a non-issue. For others, it means congestion and itchy eyes. If you're in the latter camp, don't pretend you're tough. Pop that antihistamine *before* you head out, or you'll be sniffling through your entire run. There’s no medal for suffering through a preventable allergy attack, just a miserable workout and a runny nose later.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip from your friendly neighborhood PT:** With all the trail work, you might be forced onto pavement or less-than-ideal surfaces. Make sure your shoes are actually doing their job. If they're flatter than a tortilla on a comal, it's time for a new pair. Your knees and shins will thank you, and maybe, just maybe, you'll avoid seeing me in the clinic. Also, keep an eye out for any events or activities that could impact your run. While no major events are explicitly blocking trails today, there's a "San Antonio River Basin Bioblitz" happening. It's unlikely to close trails, but expect more foot traffic than usual in some areas.
</AlertBox>

So, can you work out outside? Yeah, probably. Just don't be surprised when your meticulously planned route turns into a scavenger hunt for open pathways. And remember, sometimes the best workout is the one that *doesn't* end with an ER visit because you ignored the giant "TRAIL CLOSED" sign. Your body is not a construction zone test dummy.
