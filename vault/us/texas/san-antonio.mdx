## Why San Antonio is a Sweaty Mess Today

Look, I get it. We're runners, we're cyclists, we like to move. But Mother Nature (and apparently, half the construction crews in Bexar County) is actively working against us today. If you're eyeing that midday run, just don't. Seriously, don't.

The National Weather Service and local news are all screaming about the heat advisory in effect until late this evening. We're talking heat index values that flirt with 110 degrees. This isn't your grandma's "warm" San Antonio summer day; this is the kind of heat that sneaks up on you, leaving you dehydrated and dizzy before you even realize what's happening. Heat stroke is no joke, and it comes on faster than you think, especially when you're exerting yourself.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** A Heat Advisory is active from noon to 7 PM CDT. Heat index values could reach 108-111°F (42-44°C). Limit strenuous outdoor activities during these hours. Hydration is critical, but it's not a magic bullet against actual heat illness.
</AlertBox>

On the trail front, it's a mess. If your usual loop takes you near **Garza Park**, forget about it. The loop trail, along with some fields, is shut down until January 2027 for the Zarzamora Creek Greenway Trail Project. And that's just one spot. Portions of the Salado Creek Greenway are closed for repairs, and if you head towards Loop 1604, parts of the Leon Creek Greenway are also affected by ongoing TxDOT construction that won't wrap up until mid-2027. Even Classen-Steubing Ranch Park has sections of its trails closed through this month. This isn't just an inconvenience; it means less shade, unexpected detours onto less-safe roads, and generally a less enjoyable (and more frustrating) workout.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Chaos:** Expect ongoing closures and detours on several popular routes, including Garza Park trails (closed until January 2027) and sections of Salado Creek Greenway and Leon Creek Greenway. Always check local park and TxDOT updates before heading out.
</AlertBox>

The good news? No significant wildfire smoke is messing with our lungs today. The AQI is in the "Good" range, and recent forecasts showed no major smoke expected for San Antonio. But seriously, that's like saying the bullet in the chamber isn't rusty. It's still a bullet.
