## Weather: Sunny Now, Stormy Later (and Always Hot)

Right now, it's a relatively pleasant 24.2°C (around 75°F) and clear, making for a deceptive start to your day. But don't be fooled by this brief reprieve. The forecast predicts scattered thunderstorms and a 35% chance of rain later, with temperatures expected to hit a high of 95°F (35°C) and humidity around 59%. Remember that recent days have seen "feels like" temperatures soar to a hazardous 105-108°F in the early to mid-afternoon. A sudden downpour could turn parts of the trails into muddy slip-and-slides, and those lingering high temps are still a real concern for heat-related illness.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Stay hydrated. Seriously, don't just "feel fine" for the first 30 minutes. If you're out there mid-day, carry water or plan your route around accessible fountains. Your body isn't a camel, and this isn't Arizona's dry heat.
</AlertBox>

## Construction Chaos: Your "Scenic" Detour Guide

Here's the real kicker for us runners: San Antonio is a city perpetually under construction, and our greenways are bearing the brunt of it. If your usual route takes you through **Garza Park**, forget about it. The loop trail, along with some fields, is completely shut down until *January 2027* for the Zarzamora Creek Greenway Trail project. So much for your Westside long runs.

But it doesn't stop there. Good portions of the **Leon Creek Greenway** near Loop 1604 are closed until mid-2027 due to TxDOT work. And if you're thinking of hitting the trails at Classen-Steubing Ranch Park, forget the Prickly Pear Path and Sotol Descent trails – they're closed through September. Even the Salado Creek Greenway has multiple closures, including south of Southside Lions Park until October 9th and other sections "until further notice".

This isn't just an inconvenience; it's a safety issue. Detours often lead to less-maintained paths or onto busy streets.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Don't ignore trail closure signs.** They're there for a reason, usually because there's heavy equipment, unstable ground, or literal holes in the trail. Your PR isn't worth a broken ankle or worse.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
Before you head out, check the City of San Antonio Parks and Recreation website for specific trail updates. Don't rely on memory or hope.
</AlertBox>

All in all, today's workout is going to require some planning. Maybe hit the gym or stick to known, unaffected paved routes early in the morning.
