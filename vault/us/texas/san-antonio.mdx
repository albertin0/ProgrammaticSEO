<AlertBox type="danger">
**Overnight Storm Aftermath is Real:** Severe thunderstorms swept through late Sunday into early Monday, bringing damaging winds up to 75 mph, large hail, and 2-4 inches of rain, with isolated spots getting up to 6 inches. This has led to widespread road closures in Bexar County due to flooding and power outages affecting over 17,000 homes. Expect extremely muddy conditions, standing water, and potential debris (fallen branches, trash) on trails and sidewalks. Do NOT attempt to run through flooded areas—it’s not worth the risk of hidden hazards or being swept away. The City of San Antonio explicitly warns to use caution on trails after rain and not to cross trails under water.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ongoing Construction Closures:** Don't expect smooth sailing even on your regular routes. Multiple sections of the Salado Creek Greenway are still impacted by construction, including periodic closures around the Tobin Park Trailhead and a long-term closure from Gembler Road to Houston Street until winter 2026. The Mission Reach trail also has sections closed for erosion repair work from Lone Star Blvd to the first pedestrian bridge. Always check local updates before heading out, as these detours are a constant headache.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Good News for Your Lungs (if you can stay upright):** The air quality is surprisingly good today, with an AQI of 39. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) forecasts San Antonio's PM2.5 AQI to be in the "Good" range, with residual smoke from distant burning activities largely steered over the Gulf. The temperature is a pleasant 18.9°C (around 66°F) and overcast, making for comfortable breathing—if only the ground wasn't trying to actively sabotage your run.
</AlertBox>

Look, the cool air feels inviting after the muggy mess we often get, but the ground conditions are going to be a battle today. If you absolutely *must* get out, stick to paved, well-drained routes you know are clear. Maybe hit a treadmill, or do some strength work indoors. Your ankles, knees, and overall sanity will thank you.
