Forget your outdoor P.R. today, San Antonio, unless your idea of a good time involves dodging actual flash floods. Seriously.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

### Why today is NOT the day:

<BulletList>
  <li>The skies are weeping, and not in a gentle way. We're looking at an 80% chance of rain and scattered thunderstorms, turning your run into a guaranteed soggy slog.</li>
  <li>"Considerable to catastrophic flooding" is happening just west of us, and while the worst might be out by Uvalde and Bandera, San Antonio proper is still under a flash flood watch until Thursday. That means saturated ground and overflowing low-water crossings.</li>
  <li>Moderate pollen levels and an AQI of 64 with ozone as the dominant pollutant means your lungs will be working harder than usual, even if you manage to stay dry. It's not the worst, but it certainly isn't helping.</li>
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We're runners. We're tough. But there's a difference between "toughing it out" and being genuinely foolish. Today leans heavily into the latter. Unless you enjoy navigating muddy quagmires and playing chicken with rising water, keep your workout indoors. I'm a physical therapist, not a hydrologist, but I know a bad idea when I see one.

The San Antonio River Trail, especially sections like the Mission Reach, is likely going to be a mess. The Texas Parks & Wildlife Department explicitly noted the San Antonio River Trail is closed due to muddy conditions and high river levels, with area roads potentially flooded as of yesterday. Even the city's own Parks & Recreation department is warning folks to "use caution on trails before/after rain events" and "Don't cross trails that are under water." This isn't just a suggestion; it's how you end up with a sprained ankle, or worse, needing a high-water rescue.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### Flash Flood Threat is Real
  Multiple rounds of heavy rain are expected through Thursday, leading to a dangerous flash flooding threat. Parts of our region are under a Level 4 "high" risk for excessive rainfall with potential for catastrophic flooding. Avoid low-water crossings. If you see water covering a road or trail, turn around. It's not worth it.
</AlertBox>

On top of the weather, if you usually hit the Leon Creek Greenway, a portion near Loop 1604 is still closed for TxDOT construction until mid-2027. And honestly, with all the rain, any construction zone is going to be even more treacherous. The city also has ongoing transportation construction for hike and bike trails across San Antonio. Stay alert for temporary lane and sidewalk closures, especially if you're venturing anywhere near San Pedro Avenue due to the VIA Rapid Green Line work.
