### Air Quality & Allergies: Breathe Easy (Mostly)

The Air Quality Index is currently a crisp 36, which is firmly in the "Good" category. Your dominant pollutant is "Ozone/Other," but honestly, at that AQI, you're not going to feel it. This isn't one of those days where you finish your run feeling like you smoked a pack of cigarettes at the same time.

However, let's not get too cocky. Pollen is at a "Moderate" level. For us San Antonians, "moderate" means different things to different people. If you're one of the lucky few who can run through a field of ragweed without shedding a single tear, you're probably fine. For the rest of us, especially those with seasonal allergies, moderate can still mean a snotty mess by the time you hit your second mile. Load up on the Zyrtec *before* you head out, not after.

<AlertBox type="info">
If you're prone to seasonal sniffles, consider wearing sunglasses to protect your eyes from airborne allergens. Also, that moderate pollen might feel worse if you're hitting trails like those near the Salado Creek Greenway where there's more natural vegetation.
</AlertBox>

### Hazards: Keep Your Eyes Peeled

I scoured the news for any immediate workout-wrecking hazards over the last 24 hours, and it's surprisingly quiet. No wildfire smoke choking us out, no flash flood warnings turning your favorite underpasses into swimming pools, and no rogue construction projects suddenly closing down half the River Walk. This is good news, because let's be real, a lot of our "scenic routes" have a tendency to become obstacle courses without warning.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Even with no major warnings, always be aware of your surroundings. San Antonio drivers are... well, they're San Antonio drivers. Assume they don't see you, especially if you're out on busy streets near the Medical Center. Stick to dedicated paths where possible.
</AlertBox>

The overcast conditions mean the sun isn't beating down, but don't mistake that for cool weather. 27.5°C is still plenty warm. Hydrate before, during, and after your workout. This isn't the day to try and "train your thirst." If you're doing a long run, carry water, or plan your route around places you can refill. Trying to push through dehydration in this humidity is a one-way ticket to bonk city, population: you.

No major events are blocking access to places like McAllister Park or the Leon Creek Greenway, so your usual stomping grounds should be clear. Go get those miles in while the weather is being relatively cooperative.
