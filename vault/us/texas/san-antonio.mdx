## The Air You Breathe (or Don't)

Look, I get it, "clear" weather at 20.1°C sounds perfect. But don't let that fool you. Our air quality is currently sitting at an AQI of 95, making it "Moderate," with PM2.5 as the main culprit. That's fine if you're just sitting on your porch, but for a solid workout, your lungs are going to be working harder than usual to filter out those fine particulate matters.

Remember that mulch fire on the Southwest Side? Yeah, that thing's been going for a while, and the smoke plumes are still contributing to our less-than-ideal air quality. So if your allergies are flaring up or you feel a tickle in your throat, it's not just "allergies." It's Bexar County's burning trash saying hello. Even the school district was keeping kids indoors last week because of it.

## Trail Closures & Urban Obstacles

If you're planning on hitting some trails, be prepared for a scavenger hunt of detours. The San Antonio River Authority reports ongoing maintenance and closures. For instance, the **San Pedro Creek Culture Park** has a temporary closure on its east bank trail between Cameron St. and West Houston St. Don't even think about running down South Alamo Street expecting a smooth stride; those perpetual construction delays near Hemisfair and La Villita mean brief road closures and a general headache for pedestrians. Apparently, those projects, part of a 2017-2022 Bond Package, were *supposed* to wrap up in Spring 2026. Spoiler alert: they’re not done.

Up north, the City of San Antonio also has portions of the Leon Creek Greenway under the 1604 overpass and sections of the Salado Creek Greenway to Tobin Park Trailhead closed intermittently due to TXDOT construction. Basically, if you see orange cones, assume your path is blocked.

<AlertBox type="danger">
At Confluence Park, a Mexican honey wasp nest was identified on March 25th. The River Authority is working on it, but until it's gone, proceed with extreme caution if you’re running near those trails. Nobody needs that kind of excitement.
</AlertBox>

## Your PT's Two Cents

With the moderate AQI and the lingering smoke, your respiratory system is already under mild stress. If you push it too hard today, you're not just risking a poor performance, you're increasing inflammation in your airways. That's a recipe for a cough that won't quit and potentially setting yourself up for an asthma flare-up if you're predisposed. Pair that with the moderate pollen count, and you've got a perfect storm for a miserable post-run recovery.

On the joint front, 20.1°C is pleasant, so no extreme temperature shock there. But dodging construction, uneven surfaces from torn-up roads, and sudden detours can lead to awkward footing and potential ankle tweaks. Stick to well-maintained surfaces if you can, or at least watch your step on those downtown sidewalks.

<AlertBox type="info">
Consider an early morning run before the day's pollutants fully kick in, or save your intense workouts for indoors today. If you must be outside, keep it easy, and seriously, wear some shades to protect your eyes from any airborne particulate matter.
</AlertBox>
