Look, the skies are clear, the AQI is a beautiful 38, and the dominant pollutant is just "Ozone/Other" – basically, nothing to write home about for most people. That’s a win in my book, especially after some of the hazy crud we've had. However, don't let that fool you into thinking it's a walk in the park. The weather forecast had us eyeing near-record highs for the weekend, and while our current 20.5°C is pleasant, that sun is intense. Those gusty south-southeast winds around 5-10 mph might feel nice, but they're just drying you out faster, and contributing to a persistent wildfire threat in the region. Just because you don't *see* smoke doesn't mean you won't feel the effects of dehydration later.

The real buzzkill for us dedicated pavement pounders and trail blazers? The seemingly endless parade of construction and maintenance. The San Antonio River Authority is doing "stem-density maintenance" on the Mission Reach, for crying out loud. That means crews working Monday through Friday, daytime hours, with "limited trail closures and intermittent work zones." "Limited" is a relative term, my friends. And don't even get me started on the ongoing sagas at Leon Creek Greenway, where a good six miles under the 1604 overpass near the Valero Trailhead are closed through April for TxDOT work. We also have bits of the Salado Creek Greenway to Tobin Park Trailhead intermittently closed. This city is constantly improving, which is great, but it's a nightmare for consistent routing. Always check before you head out, or embrace the adventure of unexpected U-turns.
