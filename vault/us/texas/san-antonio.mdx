Listen up, San Antonio. Today is *not* the day to be cavalier about your outdoor miles. Absolutely not the day to get out there, unless you enjoy wading through floodwaters or navigating closed trails.

Expect 2 to 4 inches of rain, with isolated pockets getting hit with up to 6 inches.
*   The ground is already soaked from recent downpours, which means it won't take much more rain to turn our low-lying areas and creek beds into dangerous torrents. Seriously, "turn around, don't drown" isn't just a catchy phrase; it's how you stay alive out here.

The weather right now might look deceptively clear with a comfortable 18.5°C (65.3°F) and a decent AQI of 30. Good for your lungs, sure, but your joints? Your whole body? Not so much when you're dodging flash floods or tripping over debris on a washed-out path. Pollen is only "Moderate" today, which is probably the least of your concerns.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Imminent Flood Risk!
A Flood Watch is in effect starting tonight, Friday, May 22nd, at 7 PM, through Monday, May 25th, at 7 PM. Forecasters are talking about "life-threatening flooding" in some areas. Do NOT attempt to cross flooded low-water crossings or trails. This isn't a game; people die doing that.
</AlertBox>

Let's talk trails, specifically the popular ones like the Salado Creek Greenway. You might find sections closed for months. The stretch from Gembler Road to Houston Street on the Salado Creek Greenway, for instance, is closed until winter 2026 for maintenance. And that's *before* the new rain hits. The City of San Antonio also warns that water-saturated trails get muddy and impassable, leading to closures for your safety and to prevent trail damage.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Existing Trail Closures & Hazards
Expect existing trail closures and muddy conditions to worsen significantly with the incoming rain. Parts of the Salado Creek Greenway are already impacted by construction, and Camino Coahuilteca is closed due to high water. Also, the north portion of the Brackenridge Park Trail has an electrical issue, meaning no lighting. Stay sharp, even if you find an open path.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your Best Bet?
If you absolutely *must* move your body outdoors, stick to paved roads that you *know* aren't prone to flooding, and turn back at the first sign of standing or moving water. Better yet, hit the gym, do some burpees in your living room, or just accept that sometimes, Mother Nature says "nope." Check the National Weather Service San Antonio page and the City of San Antonio's Parks and Recreation department for the latest closures before you even think about lacing up.
</AlertBox>
