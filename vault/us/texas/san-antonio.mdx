### Air Quality & Allergy Alert

Alright, let's talk about what you'll be breathing. That Air Quality Index of 95, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, is flirting with the "unhealthy for sensitive groups" threshold. If you have asthma, other respiratory issues, or just a general aversion to feeling like you've been sucking on tailpipes, you might want to dial back the intensity. Combining that with the moderate pollen count, it's not exactly a banner day for clear airways. Stick to shorter, less strenuous routes if you insist on being outside, or better yet, hit the gym. Your lungs will thank you.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**AQI (95, PM2.5) is borderline. Consider shorter, lower-intensity workouts, especially if you have respiratory sensitivities or allergies. Moderate pollen isn't helping either.**
</AlertBox>

### Trail Chaos: Construction, Flooding, & Debris

Now for the real kick in the teeth: our trails are a hot mess in a lot of places. Remember that heavy rain and flash flooding we got just a few days ago? It wasn't just a minor inconvenience. It caused widespread closures and even resulted in a fatality along Salado Creek. The San Antonio River Authority is still reporting closures like Camino Coahuilteca and River Front Parkway due to high water, warning everyone to use "extreme caution" on *all* trails and low water crossings due to potential mud and debris.

On top of the lingering flood risks, our city is in a perpetual state of construction, and our greenways are no exception. Parts of the Leon Creek Greenway under the 1604 overpass are seeing periodic closures all month. Sections of the Salado Creek Greenway near the Tobin Park Trailhead are intermittently closed. Even the Museum Reach's Oxbow Trail is blocked off until May 1st for project work. And don't even get me started on the multi-year Loop 1604 North Expansion Project; while it's mostly roads, it creates traffic headaches getting *to* some of our northern trailheads, with the Bulverde Road exit in the westbound lanes closed for months. Trying to navigate these areas could turn your serene run into an infuriating detour or, worse, a safety risk.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Numerous trail sections are CLOSED due to recent flooding, mud, debris, and ongoing construction. Specifically, avoid low water crossings on the Salado Creek Greenway and be aware of closures on Leon Creek Greenway, Culebra Creek Park, and the Museum Reach Oxbow Trail. Disregard barricades at your own risk.**
</AlertBox>
