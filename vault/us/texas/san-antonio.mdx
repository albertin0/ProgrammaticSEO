## The Heat is No Joke, Y'all

Seriously, ditch the outdoor PR attempts. It's October 13th, and we're still battling heat indices that belong in July. The National Weather Service is calling for "feels-like" temperatures well above 100°F this afternoon, possibly touching 105°F. That's a direct route to heat exhaustion or worse. Your body isn't adapted to this kind of prolonged stress in mid-October. Even with "mainly clear" skies, the sun is a brutal companion.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Hazard: Avoid Peak Afternoon Hours
With heat indices forecast to be between 100-105°F, exercising outdoors between 10 AM and 6 PM is extremely risky. Consider moving your workout indoors or rescheduling for much cooler mornings later in the week when a cold front is expected.
</AlertBox>

## Construction: The Never-Ending San Antonio Saga

If you were planning a long run on the **Leon Creek Greenway**, be advised there are significant closures. A portion near Loop 1604, north of the Valero Trailhead, is shut down until mid-2027 due to TxDOT construction. Worse, a section near I-10 by the Marquis Trailhead is closed "until further notice" because of a fallen highway overpass. Seriously? An overpass? Stay clear of that mess.

Other local spots are also impacted:
*   **Classen-Steubing Ranch Park:** Prickly Pear Path and Sotol Descent trails are closed through the end of October 2026.
*   **Elmendorf Lake Park:** Don't even try the parking lot or 19th Street entrance; they're closed through December 2026 for dam repairs.

Road runners and cyclists also need to be wary of the massive Loop 1604 North Expansion project, with multiple segments under construction and impacting traffic and potentially access points through 2028. Similar major work is ongoing on I-35 and US Highway 90. Plan your routes carefully, or better yet, take it to the treadmill today.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail and Road Closures Abound
Check the City of San Antonio Parks and Recreation website and TxDOT's project pages before heading out. Unexpected detours, uneven surfaces, and heavy equipment are real hazards on many of our major routes and greenways.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate, Hydrate, Hydrate (Even Indoors)
Given the extremely high "feels like" temperatures, staying hydrated is crucial whether you're working out or not. If you absolutely must be outside, carry more water than you think you need. Consider electrolyte drinks to replace lost salts.
