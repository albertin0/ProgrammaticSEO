Happy 4th of July, San Antonio. If your idea of "freedom" today involves an outdoor workout, you're going to need a hefty dose of planning and a strong will to navigate the heat, the haze, and the hordes.

### Air Quality & Respiratory Health

That AQI of 95 isn't "Good," folks, it's "Moderate." For most of us, it means a slightly less pleasant run. But if you've got asthma, COPD, or are just plain sensitive to particulates, that PM2.5 level, exacerbated by Saharan dust and potential smoke, is a legitimate concern. Combine that with moderate pollen, and your respiratory system is getting a triple whammy.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Respiratory Risk for Sensitive Groups.** With PM2.5 levels elevated due to Saharan dust and residual smoke, individuals with respiratory conditions (asthma, COPD) should seriously reconsider strenuous outdoor activity. It's not worth an ER visit on the Fourth.
</AlertBox>

### Heat Stroke Isn't a Party Trick

The National Weather Service is predicting typical brutal July heat. An emergency room doctor just warned us about extreme heat being a major Fourth of July danger. Hydrate aggressively, start early, and keep it short. Seriously, that 28.1C might sound okay, but when the heat index is nearing 100, your body works overtime just to exist.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Extreme Heat Risk.** Afternoon temperatures will be in the mid-to-upper 90s (Fahrenheit), with heat index values close to 100 degrees. Hydrate well before, during, and after your workout. Stick to shaded routes and listen to your body – heat stroke can sneak up on you faster than a rogue firecracker.
</AlertBox>

### Navigating the Fourth of July Frenzy

Downtown San Antonio is going to be swamped. The Stars & Stripes 5K kicks off at 8:30 AM, followed by a parade on Houston Street at 10 AM, and then a full-blown music and food festival at Civic Park at Hemisfair. Forget any speedy runs around these areas. Even the River Walk, while lovely, will likely be packed with tourists and celebrants, making a continuous pace almost impossible.

If you're eyeing a longer trail run, know that the west bank of the Mission Reach hike and bike trail from Lone Star Blvd to the first pedestrian bridge is currently closed for erosion repair work until late July. You'll have to use the east bank from Lone Star Blvd. Additionally, portions of the Leon Creek Greenway near Loop 1604 and the Salado Creek Greenway to Tobin Park Trailhead are seeing periodic closures due to TxDOT construction. Don't assume your usual path is open.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip: Plan Your Route & Hydration.** Check park and trail websites for specific closures before you head out. Carry more water than you think you need, especially if you're venturing onto one of the open Greenways like McAllister Park's trails, where shade can be sparse. Consider an early morning indoor session, or embrace the chaos if you dare.
</AlertBox>
