## The Rundown: Don't Get Caught Out There

Look, it's San Antonio in March. The weather's a fickle beast, and today is a prime example of "enjoy it while it lasts." You've got decent conditions for a workout this morning and afternoon, so get after it. The air quality is a sweet 30 AQI, which means your lungs won't be performing a heroic feat just to process oxygen. That "Ozone/Other" dominant pollutant is barely even registering on the scale, so breathe deep – just maybe not too deep if you're sensitive to that moderate pollen count.

Now, for the actual hazards.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Evening Storms are Coming, People!**
The National Weather Service is calling for a line of strong thunderstorms late tonight, hitting San Antonio likely after 9 PM and continuing into early Wednesday morning. While the immediate metro area has a low risk for severe stuff, areas west of us are looking at very large hail, 70+ mph gusts, and even isolated tornadoes. Don't be that person trying to PR in a thunderstorm. Get your miles in early.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Troubles? Always Check Before You Trek.

The good news is we don't have widespread flooding or wildfires messing up *all* the trails today. But that doesn't mean it's completely clear sailing.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Mission Reach Maintenance Continues**
If your usual route takes you along the Mission Reach of the San Antonio River Walk, be aware that there's ongoing stem-density maintenance happening. This means crews are out there Monday through Friday during the day, and you might encounter temporary trail closures or work zones. Don't get bent out of shape, just be ready to reroute or slow down if you see equipment. It's for flood control, which, let's be honest, is probably a good thing considering what's rolling in tonight.
</AlertBox>

And just a heads-up, the San Pedro Creek Culture Park also has some temporary closures on its east bank trail between Cameron Street and other access points. Always a good idea to check the City's Parks & Recreation site for specific trail closures before you head out, because they don't always advertise those small, annoying detours with flashing lights.

So, lace up, hit the pavement or the less-constructed trails, enjoy the cool, cloudy air, but for the love of all that is holy, be done before dinner. Those afternoon thunderstorms that hammered us over the weekend might have been a "mostly quiet Monday" prelude, but Tuesday night is looking less so. And Thursday brings gusty winds and a temperature drop, just to keep things interesting. This is San Antonio, folks, never a dull moment with the weather.
