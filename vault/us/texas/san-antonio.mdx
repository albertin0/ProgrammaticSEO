## The Grim Reality of San Antonio Summer Workouts

Look, I get it. We live in Texas. Summer means heat. But 108-111 degrees *feels like* heat? That's not just "sweaty," that's "your organs are cooking" territory. We're talking about conditions where heatstroke isn't just a possibility, it's practically a guarantee if you push yourself too hard in the afternoon. Early morning, before the sun really starts to beat down, is your only viable window for anything strenuous outside. Even then, you need to be smart.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### Extreme Heat Advisory in Effect!
  The combination of high temperatures (nearing 99°F) and elevated humidity will make it feel like 108-111°F this afternoon. The San Antonio Metropolitan Health District has issued a Level II heat advisory, and honestly, they're not messing around. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity between noon and 7 PM. Seriously. Your body's natural cooling mechanisms just can't keep up when it's this humid and hot.
</AlertBox>

### Your Lungs Aren't Loving It, Even If the AQI Is Good

Even though our Air Quality Index is a stellar 39, meaning PM2.5 isn't clogging your airways today, that's small comfort when you're trying to pull in air that feels like a hot, wet blanket. Your lungs are still working overtime just to keep you cool, and adding intense exercise on top of that is a recipe for disaster. Previous weeks saw some smoke issues from distant wildfires and even agricultural burning earlier in the year, but thankfully, those aren't contributing to the misery today.

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Your Morning Survival Strategy
  If you absolutely *must* get out there, do it as early as possible. Before 8 AM is your best bet. Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate—and I don't mean just a bottle of water. Electrolytes are your friend. Wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, slather on sunscreen, and for the love of all that is holy, listen to your body. If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or unusually fatigued, get inside to AC immediately. This isn't about pushing through; it's about not ending up in the ER.
