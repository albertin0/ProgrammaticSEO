## The Good, The Bad, and The Gritty

Look, the air quality is decent, sitting at a perfectly acceptable AQI of 50, so you can breathe deep without wondering if you're sucking down a lungful of regret. The overcast conditions and mild temps are actually pretty solid for getting those miles in, especially for September in Texas. No extreme heat warnings or anything wild like that to melt your sneakers to the asphalt.

Now, for the "gritty" part. If you're a regular on the greenway trails, you already know the drill: orange cones and "Trail Closed" signs are practically permanent fixtures. The City of San Antonio is working on a lot, which is great for the future, but a pain in the glutes for your weekend plans.

For instance, a major section of the **Leon Creek Greenway** at Loop 1604, specifically north of the Valero Trailhead to The Rim, is shut down for TxDOT construction until roughly mid-2027. That's not a short detour, folks. And don't even get me started on the Salado Creek Greenway; portions near Southside Lions Park, Tobin Park Trailhead, and between Gembler Road to Houston Street are all facing closures or periodic impacts. It’s like a choose-your-own-adventure novel, except every path leads to a "Dead End" sign.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Expect significant congestion and detours if you're trying to access or run on our Greenways. Plan your route carefully and be ready to improvise, unless you enjoy running into construction barriers.
</AlertBox>

And if you thought you'd escape to somewhere like Garza Park, think again. Most of it is closed until January 2027 for trail and park upgrades. The Zarzamora Creek Greenway Trail Project means large equipment and materials, so it's not exactly prime running real estate.

<AlertBox type="info">
Pollen levels are moderate today, primarily Ragweed, Chenopods, and Elm. If you're prone to seasonal allergies, a dose of your preferred antihistamine before you head out might save you from a miserable, sniffly workout.
</AlertBox>

Also, be aware that today is National Public Lands Day, and there are events happening like the Great Springs Trail Race starting at Natural Bridge Caverns. This is fantastic for community spirit, but it does mean certain areas will have increased foot traffic, so factor that into your pacing and patience levels.

## The PT Perspective: Save Your Knees

With all these trail closures, you're likely to find yourself pounding pavement more than you intended. Concrete and asphalt are unforgiving surfaces compared to dirt trails, and that repetitive impact adds up on your joints—knees, hips, and lower back, especially if you’re not used to it. Don't be a hero. Listen to your body, consider shorter runs on hard surfaces, and maybe swap out some mileage for cross-training or a swim if you're constantly rerouted to concrete.

And always, always pay attention to signage. Those closures are there for a reason, usually your safety. Don't be the chucklehead who ignores the barricades and ends up face-planting in a construction trench.
