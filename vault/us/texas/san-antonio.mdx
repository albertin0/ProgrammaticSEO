## The "I'm Not a Fish" Guide to Today's Workout

Today's forecast isn't just "wet"; it's actively hostile to outdoor activity. The NWS has issued a Flood Watch for San Antonio and surrounding counties, warning of potential life-threatening flash flooding. This isn't just about getting a little wet; it's about genuine danger.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### "Turn Around, Don't Drown" is Not Just a Slogan
  Seriously. One foot of moving water can sweep your vehicle away, and even six inches can knock you off your feet. Do not, under any circumstances, attempt to cross flooded roads, low-water crossings, or submerged trails. This goes for driving, running, or cycling. Your workout isn't worth your life.
</AlertBox>

If you had plans for a run along the Leon Creek Greenway, reconsider. While some sections might be clear, the heavy and continuous rainfall means conditions can change in minutes. Many of San Antonio's natural drainage areas, creeks, and low-lying spots are already vulnerable.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ### Watch for Sudden Changes & Hidden Hazards
  Even if it seems like the rain has let up, heavy downpours can cause localized flooding very quickly. Be aware that drains can back up, manholes can pop, and debris can obscure what's beneath the surface of seemingly shallow water. Trails will be slick with mud, especially on inclines and declines.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Indoor Options & Stay Informed
  This is the perfect day for a treadmill run, some strength training at the gym, or an indoor yoga session. If you *must* venture out, stick to known, elevated, and well-lit areas, but seriously, just stay inside. Keep an eye on local news, the National Weather Service San Antonio/Austin, and Bexar County's HALT (High-Water Alert Lifesaving Technology) system for real-time road closures and alerts. Sign up for emergency notifications. Your safety should be your priority.
</AlertBox>
