*   **Perfect Weather, Tricky Trails:** The clear skies and mild 18.5°C (65.3°F) are ideal, but don't let that fool you. Recent downpours can leave trails muddy and full of debris. We saw road closures across Bexar County as recently as May 11th due to heavy rain and wind gusts. Expect saturated ground and potential washouts on unpaved paths. The City of San Antonio Parks & Recreation even warns to use caution on trails before and after rain, and to not cross trails that are under water, as this can lead to erosion and damage.
*   **Construction Chaos Continues:** Our beloved Greenway Trail System is a bit of a minefield right now with ongoing construction. Expect detours and outright closures on sections of the Salado Creek Greenway and Leon Creek Greenway, among others. These aren't minor hiccups; some projects are replacing entire sections of trail or are part of larger TXDOT efforts. Don't be surprised if your favorite segment is a hard stop today.
*   **Moderate Allergens & AQI:** Pollen is moderate, and our Air Quality Index is sitting at a moderate 54, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. It's not a red-alert day, but if you're sensitive to allergens or have respiratory issues, you might feel it on longer or more intense efforts.

### Know Before You Go

**Greenway Trail Headaches:** Seriously, check the City of San Antonio's Parks & Recreation website for closures *before* you head out. They updated their list on May 1st, and it’s extensive. For instance, a portion of the **Salado Creek Greenway** from Gembler Road to Houston Street is closed until winter 2026 for maintenance. You don't want to get three miles into a run only to hit a "trail closed" sign and have to backtrack. The trail within Culebra Creek Park that connects to the Culebra Creek Greenway is also closed for three months, undergoing reconstruction to replace the existing trail with concrete.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch Your Step!** Even on open trails, recent heavy rains may have left behind mud, slippery spots, and hidden debris. Areas near Leon Creek and Salado Creek are especially prone to issues after significant rainfall. If a trail looks questionable, turn around. It's not worth a twisted ankle.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Brackenridge Park Trail Lighting:** If you're an early bird or evening runner on the Brackenridge Trail, be aware that lighting is currently out on the north portion from Josephine Street to just past Highway 281 due to an electrical issue. Repairs are expected by the end of tomorrow (May 14, 2026), but use extra caution in this area if you're running in low light today.
</AlertBox>

### Final Word

Today offers some truly beautiful running weather, but San Antonio is a city perpetually under construction and still recovering from recent wet conditions. Plan your route with an escape hatch in mind, keep an eye on your footing, and listen to your body if pollen or AQI starts to bother you. Don't be that runner who ends up stuck, muddy, or worse.
