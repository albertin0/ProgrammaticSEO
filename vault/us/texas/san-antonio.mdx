## The Daily Rundown: September 3, 2026

Alright, SA, let's talk about hitting the pavement or the dirt today. Your current AQI is a respectable 38, which is great for your respiratory system. That's a win, especially considering the usual summer grind. However, the moderate pollen count means if you're one of the many who sniffle and sneeze their way through a run, you'll still want to grab those antihistamines.

Temperature-wise, we're sitting around 27.3°C (81°F) right now, under mainly clear skies. This isn't the triple-digit inferno we've seen recently, but it's still plenty warm enough to get you sweating buckets if you're not smart about it. The forecast for September generally leans warmer than average, so this heat isn't going anywhere fast.

### Trail Trouble & Construction Chaos

Here's where things get interesting, and by interesting, I mean potentially frustrating. The City of San Antonio's Parks and Recreation department has a *laundry list* of closures right now. Seriously, trying to navigate some of our beloved greenways feels like a game of Frogger.

For instance, if your go-to is **Leon Creek Greenway**, be advised: a significant portion near Loop 1604, running north from the Valero Trailhead toward The Rim, is closed for TxDOT construction until mid-2027. That's not a typo, folks, *mid-2027*. And that's just one example. You'll find sections of the Salado Creek Greenway also closed for maintenance and construction. Even parts of the Riverwalk have been shut down for being "too dangerous" after some, shall we say, "bushwhacking" activity.

These aren't just minor inconveniences; they can force major reroutes onto less-than-ideal sidewalks or, worse, busy roads. As a physical therapist, I can tell you that unexpected surface changes and dodging traffic are recipes for rolled ankles and stress fractures.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures are Widespread!**
Expect detours and blocked paths on many popular greenways, including sections of Leon Creek Greenway, Salado Creek Greenway, and even parts of Classen-Steubing Ranch Park. Always check the city's park closure page before heading out.
</AlertBox>

### Lingering Wetness & Wildfire Watch

Tropical Depression Edouard swung through Texas yesterday, bringing heavy rain and flooding to other parts of the state. While San Antonio itself saw "limited impacts", there were significant flood warnings and reports of excessive rainfall west of the city. This means there's a chance some low-lying trails, especially those near creeks or rivers, might still be muddy, slick, or even have standing water and debris. Remember those August 30 storms that caused widespread damage and two drownings? Don't mess with floodwaters.

On the wildfire front, several blazes are burning across Texas, but thankfully, there are no immediate smoke concerns directly impacting San Antonio's air quality today. The 431-acre River Bluff Fire in Atascosa County, south of us, was contained on Monday evening. So, while the state remains vigilant, your lungs are getting a pass on smoke for now.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Beware of Lingering Floodwaters and Debris!**
Even if the rain has stopped, recent storms and the remnants of Tropical Depression Edouard could mean slick, muddy, or even submerged areas on trails. Never attempt to cross flooded paths or roads. "Turn Around, Don't Drown."
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Key in the Heat**
Despite the clear skies, it's still warm out there. Plan your workouts for early morning or late evening to avoid the peak heat. Carry plenty of water and listen to your body to prevent heat exhaustion.
</AlertBox>
