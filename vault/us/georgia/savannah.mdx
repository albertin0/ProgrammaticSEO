## Sweat Ain't Just a Pretty Glow, It's a Warning Signal

Seriously, look at that temperature. It's March 7th, not July 7th. You know what 32°C feels like down here with our humidity? Oppressive. The Air Quality Index is actually pretty good at 32, with ozone/other as the dominant pollutant, which is a minor win. But good air quality doesn't negate the fact that your body is going to be fighting to cool itself from the moment you step outside. This kind of early-season heat is particularly dangerous because your body hasn't had time to acclimate. You're still wired for cooler weather, and suddenly you're trying to perform in near-summer conditions. It's a recipe for disaster if you're not careful.

A heat advisory has been issued for the Savannah area, effective through late afternoon today. This means a high likelihood of heat-related illnesses for those working or exercising outdoors. Expect heat index values in the upper 90s (Fahrenheit). This isn't just "sweaty," this is "your kidneys might shut down" territory if you push too hard without proper hydration and breaks.

While the pollen count is only moderate today, don't ignore it. When you're huffing and puffing trying to get a workout in this heat, any respiratory irritant can feel amplified. If you've got seasonal allergies, make sure your meds are in full effect before you even think about venturing out.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration Hacks for the Hot & Humid
Don't just drink water. Drink *electrolyte-rich* fluids. Nuun, Skratch Labs, even some good old Gatorade (the real stuff, not the diet crap). Start hydrating *before* you go out, sip continuously *during* your activity, and keep going *after* you're done. And for the love of all that is holy, wear light-colored, loose-fitting, moisture-wicking clothing. Cotton is rotten when it's hot.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Listen to Your Body – It's Not Lying
Dizziness, nausea, excessive sweating (or *not* sweating when you should be), confusion, or a throbbing headache are all signs of heat exhaustion or heatstroke. If you or your running buddy experiences any of these, stop immediately, get to shade or an air-conditioned spot, and cool down. This isn't the day to try to be a hero. There's always tomorrow.
</AlertBox>

My advice? Hit the gym. Or swim. Or do literally anything indoors. If you absolutely *must* run outside, keep it short, stick to shaded areas (like parts of Forsyth Park early in the morning), and go *much* slower than you planned.
