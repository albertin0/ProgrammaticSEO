## Today's Outdoor Rundown

Alright, Chicago, let's talk turkey. The air is clear with an AQI of 33, which is fantastic for your lungs. No smoke or weird stuff clogging the sky. But don't let that fool you into thinking it's a perfect day. That 8.6°C temperature is no joke, especially when you factor in the breezy conditions we've been experiencing. Anyone planning a long run along the Lakefront Trail will feel that wind coming right off the lake. It's the kind of chill that can sneak up on you and make those miles feel a lot longer.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Dress in Layers:** Don't underestimate the cold and the wind. Layers are your best friend today. Think wind-resistant outer shell and moisture-wicking base layers to keep you comfortable. Gloves and a hat might not seem necessary in May, but trust me, you'll be glad you have them.
</AlertBox>

### Construction: The Eternal Chicago Hazard

Speaking of hazards, our city's perpetual state of construction continues to be a headache for anyone trying to get a decent workout in. Mayor Johnson just kicked off the "Build Better Together" initiative, which means more streetscape and resurfacing projects everywhere. While some new bike infrastructure is planned for 2026, including some new paths and bridge work, it also means current disruptions.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Salt Creek Trail Closure:** If you usually hit the Salt Creek Trail near I-294, remember that section is still closed until at least early June due to ongoing Tollway work. Don't assume it's open; you'll just end up frustrated. Plan an alternate route or face a potentially dangerous detour near traffic.
</AlertBox>

Even if you're not on a specific trail with a known closure, keep an eye out. Major expressways like the Stevenson, Dan Ryan, and Eisenhower are all seeing significant lane closures and reconstruction this year, which can spill over into surrounding areas and impact pedestrian and bike routes. Assume there's construction somewhere near your planned route and be ready to adapt.
