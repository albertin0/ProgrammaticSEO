## The Daily Rundown: March 25, 2026

First things first, let's talk about the air you'll be breathing. The AQI is clocking in at a lovely 29, which is practically spa-quality for Chicago. Your lungs are getting a rare treat today, so no excuses about that. However, for those of us who start sneezing just looking at a tree, the pollen level is still "Moderate." It's not peak season yet, but if you're sensitive, you might want to pop an antihistamine before you head out.

Now, about that "nip in the air." It's 1.9°C (that's roughly 35°F for the Fahrenheit-minded among us), and while it's clear, that kind of temperature demands respect. Your muscles and joints aren't going to be happy if you just roll out of bed and sprint. Think layers, proper warm-up, and for the love of all that is holy, stretch *after* you get a little blood flowing, not before. You want to keep moving to generate some internal heat, but don't overdress and risk sweating through everything only to freeze later.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch for Residual Event Clutter in Grant Park!**
The Bank of America Shamrock Shuffle just tore through the city on Sunday, March 22nd. While the official road closures for the main race routes in the Loop are mostly lifted by today, Balbo Drive from Columbus Drive to DuSable Lake Shore Drive was slated to be closed until Wednesday. Expect some lingering cleanup, barriers, or general disarray around Grant Park, especially if you're planning a scenic lakefront run. Don't assume everything is perfectly clear.
</AlertBox>

Beyond the immediate post-race situation, Chicago's never-ending construction saga continues. If you usually hit the **Salt Creek Trail**, be advised that the section under I-294 is still closed for tollway work and won't be reopening until early June. Plan your detours now, don't wait until you're staring at a "Trail Closed" sign in the middle of a long run. Likewise, construction to rebuild the Rand Road bridge over the Des Plaines River started yesterday, March 24th, and will include a multi-use path link to the Des Plaines River Trail. Keep an eye out for potential disruptions or heavy equipment near that area if your route takes you that way.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Layer Up, Buttercup!**
With temperatures hovering around freezing, focus on breathable, moisture-wicking layers. A base layer, a mid-layer for insulation, and a wind-resistant outer shell are your friends. Don't forget gloves, a hat, and consider neck gaiters. Frostbite isn't a badge of honor, it's just poor planning.
</AlertBox>
