Look, I get it. You're itching to get outside. But after Tuesday's near-miss with severe thunderstorms, damaging winds, and hail that had parts of the city under a "moderate" risk, the ground is likely still soaking wet, and low-lying areas could be holding standing water. We had flood watches in effect until 6 AM this morning in counties including Cook and DuPage, so don't assume everything's drained.

You might think the Lakefront Trail is always a safe bet, but after heavy rains, sections can get slick, especially if debris has washed up. If you're out there, watch your footing, especially near underpasses or areas prone to collecting water. It's not just about what's falling from the sky right now, but what *fell* yesterday.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch for Residual Flooding & Slick Surfaces:** Even if it's not actively raining, yesterday's downpour means wet, potentially muddy, and slippery conditions. Urban and low-lying areas, and parts of popular running routes, could still have standing water. Avoid powering through puddles you can't see the bottom of. You don't want to roll an ankle in a hidden pothole.

Always check for local trail closures before you head out, especially after heavy weather, as conditions can change fast.
