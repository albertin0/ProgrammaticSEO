## The Rundown: Breathe Easy, Watch Your Step

Today is one of those rare Chicago days where you don't need to choose between freezing your face off, boiling alive, or coughing up a lung. The temperature is spot on, and the air quality is in the "Good" range, meaning most of us can hit the pavement without a second thought. Even the dominant pollutant, ozone, isn't hitting hazardous levels.

However, "good" doesn't mean "perfect." We're still dealing with moderate pollen, which for some of you means carrying tissues or popping an antihistamine before you head out. Don't be a hero and suffer through it, especially if you're planning a longer session on the Lakefront Trail.

## Local Headaches: Bridges and Detours

Here's the real Chicago special: construction. It's a never-ending saga, and right now, it's impacting our routes. The **Cortland Street Bridge** continues to be a mess, closed to car traffic since last fall, though the city *claims* the pedestrian/bike sidewalk is still open for now. "For now" is always my favorite phrase when dealing with city infrastructure. Expect congestion and be alert if you're in that Near North Side area, especially if you're trying to cross the river.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Bridge Closures & Congestion:** Ongoing repairs on the Cortland Street Bridge, Lake Street Bridge, and Chicago Avenue Bridge are causing significant traffic and potential detours for pedestrians and cyclists. The Illinois Prairie Path also has intermittent closures near Elmhurst due to tollway construction. Always plan your route and be ready for detours.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, if you're running or cycling near these bridges, assume things will be more complicated than they look on Google Maps. The city's official street closures list confirms these ongoing headaches. And for you suburbanites, the Illinois Prairie Path has intermittent pedestrian closures at I-294 and I-290 in Elmhurst because of tollway work. Just another day in the concrete jungle, or the slightly less concrete path.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Allergy Alert:** With moderate pollen, consider taking your allergy meds an hour before your run. A pair of sunglasses can also help shield your eyes from airborne irritants.
</AlertBox>
