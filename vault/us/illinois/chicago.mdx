Look, I get it. The sun is out, the temperature is finally tolerable without feeling like you're running through soup, and your lungs aren't actively trying to escape your chest thanks to a mercifully low AQI of 29. Go for it. Hit the Lakefront Path, do your thing, but keep one eye on the horizon and an ear to the ground.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Afternoon Storms Are Coming, People.** The National Weather Service is calling for scattered showers and thunderstorms across the Chicago area this afternoon and into the evening. Some of these could absolutely get nasty, registering a Level 1 out of 5 Severe Thunderstorm Risk. That means strong winds, maybe some small hail, and enough lightning to make your hair stand on end. Don't push your luck. If it looks dark, it probably is.
</AlertBox>

And speaking of not pushing your luck, if you're planning on a long bike ride or run, especially out on the Bloomingdale Trail (the 606), remember that the city's always tearing something up or building something new. That much-hyped extension of the 606 is slated to kick off construction this year, so expect potential detours or active work zones. It's a fantastic idea, but "construction" rarely means "smooth sailing" for us runners.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Tomorrow is Going to Be a Mess.** Seriously, just stay home if you can. Wednesday (June 17th) brings an "Elevated Flooding Risk" and a "Level 3 of 5 Severe Thunderstorm Risk." We're talking large hail, damaging winds, *tornadoes*, and torrential rain leading to flash flooding. Plus, Lake Michigan beaches will have a "High Swim Risk" for both Wednesday and Thursday. Don't even think about it. We just had a tornado outbreak last week that left 20 confirmed tornadoes across Northern Illinois and Northwest Indiana, and while power's mostly restored, you don't need to be adding to the cleanup.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip:** Download a good weather radar app. Not just some generic forecast, but real-time radar. Check it before you leave, check it halfway through, check it again. Conditions can shift fast around here, especially near the lake. Being prepared means not being soaked, blown over, or worse.
</AlertBox>
