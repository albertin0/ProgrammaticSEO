## Today's Rundown

Today, October 6, 2026, started out clear and mild, which might tempt you to lace up the shoes. And sure, the Air Quality Index is a sweet 29, so your lungs won't be battling any urban smog. But don't let that fool you into a false sense of security.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Flash Flood Warning This Afternoon & Evening
Seriously, this is not a drill. A corridor of torrential rainfall is expected to develop across northeastern Illinois this afternoon and evening. With already saturated soils, flash flooding is a real possibility. Stay off low-lying paths, underpasses, and anywhere water collects. Your PR isn't worth getting swept away or having to call for a rescue.
</AlertBox>

Pollen levels are moderate today, which is a minor annoyance compared to what's coming later. Keep your inhaler handy if you're sensitive, but it's not the primary villain of the day.

## Trail & Road Conditions

The biggest ongoing headache for runners is the never-ending work on the **Lakefront Trail**. It's getting a refresh, which means asphalt patching, seal-coating, and new trail graphics. This started on the south end (around 6300 South/East of DuSable Lake Shore Drive) and is moving north, section by section. So, if your usual route involves long, uninterrupted stretches of the Lakefront Trail, be prepared for active construction zones, signage, and potential closures. Same goes for parts of the Des Plaines River Trail, which has sections fully closed for rehabilitation.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Watch for Construction Debris & Uneven Surfaces
Beyond the official closures, construction often leaves behind small debris, uneven asphalt, or gravel in unexpected spots. Keep your eyes peeled, especially if you're hitting lesser-used sections or early morning runs with low light. Twisted ankles are a real buzzkill.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan B: Indoor Options or Treadmill
Given the high probability of heavy rain and potential flooding this afternoon, consider taking your workout indoors. There's no shame in hitting the gym or the treadmill when Mother Nature decides to turn on the faucet. Or, if you're truly dedicated to the outdoors, get your miles in *early* and stay close to home, with an exit strategy for when the skies open up.
</AlertBox>

Today's not ideal. Don't be a hero; be smart.
