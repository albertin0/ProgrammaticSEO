Alright, Chicago, let's talk about today, May 4th. The good news is the AQI is sitting pretty at a "Good" 33. So your lungs should be mostly happy, provided you don't breathe in too much pollen. Because, yeah, it's "Moderate" out there, so if you're prone to seasonal sneezes, pop that antihistamine before you lace up.

The weather itself is clear and a crisp 8.8°C (that's about 48°F for those still stuck in imperial). Perfect running weather if you ask me – brisk enough to feel alive, not so cold you're risking frostbite just by stepping outside. But don't let that clear sky fool you entirely; a chance of light rain is creeping in later today. Pack a light, water-resistant layer if you're planning an afternoon or evening session.

Now for the real talk: water and concrete. Chicago has had its wettest spring on record, with over 10 inches of rain since March 1st. This isn't just "a few puddles" kind of wet; we're talking about persistent flooding issues, especially along our river systems. The Des Plaines River, for example, along with the Fox and Illinois Rivers, had flood warnings in effect as recently as mid-April, and the city's deep tunnel reservoirs have been working overtime. So, while it's not actively pouring today, don't be surprised if sections of the **Des Plaines River Trail** or other low-lying paths are still a swampy mess or outright impassable.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Trail Conditions Alert: Des Plaines River Trail.** Sections are undergoing renovation to mitigate frequent flooding and may have intermittent or full closures. Expect detours or muddy conditions in areas south of Lawrence Avenue, and don't count on official detours being marked for you.
</AlertBox>

And it's not just the trails getting a facelift. Our city's iconic river bridges are also on the chopping block for much-needed repairs. Expect more closures on downtown bridges like the Northwest South Street Bridge and potentially the Michigan Avenue Bridge in 2026 and beyond. If your usual run involves crossing the Chicago River, check ahead or be prepared for an unexpected detour. Road construction is also rampant across the city and suburbs, though mostly affecting drivers, it can make getting to your preferred workout spot a headache.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Active Flooding & Construction Zones:** While the immediate heavy rain has passed, the ground remains saturated from a record-breaking wet spring. Be cautious of standing water and muddy conditions on unpaved trails. Respect all "Trail Closed" signs, especially near riverbanks; they're there for a reason, not just to annoy you.
</AlertBox>

So, go run, ride, or walk. Just keep your head up, be aware of your surroundings, and maybe stick to the paved Lakefront Trail if you're worried about soggy shoes and unexpected detours today. And for crying out loud, look both ways before crossing those freshly coned-off streets.
