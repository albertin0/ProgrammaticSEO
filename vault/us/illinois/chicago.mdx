### Hazards & Headaches

Now, for the bad news, because it's Chicago, and there's always bad news. While today might be good *weather-wise*, the city's gearing up for a full-on event takeover this weekend, and you, my dedicated runners, are caught in the crossfire.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Lakefront Lockdowns: Chicago Triathlon Closures!** If your usual long run involves DuSable Lake Shore Drive, you need to re-route. The Chicago Triathlon is setting up shop, and major parts of the lakefront and downtown are getting shut down. Expect closures on DuSable Lake Shore Drive, East Balbo Drive, South Columbus Drive, East Randolph Street, and even the Lower Wacker Drive "bat cave" lanes starting as early as 5 AM tomorrow, Saturday, August 22nd, and lasting through Sunday evening. Do NOT plan on an uninterrupted lakefront run this weekend; consider it a concrete jungle gym instead.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Lingering Air Quality Alert:** Despite our currently good AQI, the Illinois EPA issued an **Air Quality Alert that's extended through Saturday** due to elevated ozone and particulate levels. So, even if the numbers look good right now, keep an eye on how you're feeling, especially if you're sensitive. Those "elevated particulate levels" could spike, and nobody wants to feel like they just ran through a campfire.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Chaos Continues:** Oh, Chicago, you never disappoint. The Cortland Street Bridge over the North Branch of the Chicago River is still undergoing its extensive rehabilitation, meaning detours are the new normal around there. And watch your step near the Lake Street Bascule Bridge, where pedestrian access from Lake Street to the 150 N. Riverside Plaza Upper Riverwalk is closed for construction until later this summer. Don't be a hero trying to weave through active construction zones.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Weekend Weather Watch:** While today is mostly clear, forecasts for Saturday and Sunday include scattered showers and thunderstorms with potential for downpours and strong winds. There's even talk of heat index values flirting with 100°F inland on Saturday. If you're planning a long weekend run, stay flexible and be prepared to take it inside if the skies open up or the humidity gets oppressive.
</AlertBox>

So, today's your day, but make it count early. Otherwise, you'll be dodging barricades and probably some weekend warrior triathletes who think they own the road.
