## Today's Chicago Workout Lowdown: Breathe Easy-ish & Watch Your Step

Look, the cool, mainly clear conditions we've got going on are prime for outdoor activity. After that late May heat spike and the accompanying air quality alerts, a little crisp air is a welcome change. You'll see plenty of folks out on the Lakefront Trail, enjoying the breeze off Lake Michigan. And honestly, it's one of the best spots to be when the weather plays nice.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Trust Just Your Eyes for Air Quality!
Your lungs don't lie. Even with a lower AQI reported in some places, the history of "Air Pollution Action Days" for ground-level ozone recently (late May, early June) and AccuWeather's "Poor" rating for today mean you should still be cautious, especially during peak afternoon hours when ozone tends to be higher. If you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, seriously consider a shorter workout, going indoors, or sticking to the early morning. Head over to AirNow.gov or Open Air Chicago to get the hyper-local, real-time data for your specific neighborhood before you commit. This isn't just about feeling a little cough; ground-level ozone causes airway inflammation and can contribute to serious long-term issues.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble & Construction Zones

Beyond the air, there's always some form of urban acupuncture happening in this city. If you're planning a ride out northwest, know that the Bryn Mawr Avenue stretch, connecting the Sauganash Trail to the North Shore Channel Trail, is slated for construction starting this month for new bike lanes. While it's for future safety, expect potential detours or fresh obstacles. Similarly, bike lane construction continues on Grand Avenue, heading eastbound from Damen to Ogden in 2026. Keep your head up and stay aware of signage.

For those venturing further afield, the Salt Creek Trail under I-294, which has been closed for a while, was "tentatively scheduled to complete in early June" with reopening shortly after. That's *early* June, so it might be open, it might still be partially closed. Best to check before you make it your main route, or prepare for an unpleasant detour that many riders have already cursed.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Watch for Wet Spots
The National Weather Service is calling for a "slight chance of showers" this morning. If they roll through, those new bike lanes and fresh asphalt can get slick. Take it easy on the turns and watch for standing water.
</AlertBox>

Don't let the warnings scare you off entirely, but don't be a hero either. Be smart, be aware, and for Pete's sake, check the *local* air quality data for your actual route, not just the general citywide number, before you commit to that 10-miler.
