## The Rundown

Look, after yesterday's drama with those severe thunderstorm warnings rocking Cook, DuPage, and Will counties, you might expect the city to be a disaster zone. But surprise, surprise, Chicago bounces back. We've got clear conditions and a perfectly crisp 60°F, making it feel more like an early fall morning than late June. So, if you've been putting off that run along the Lakefront Path or a quick spin in Lincoln Park, today's your chance to do it without melting into the pavement.

However, don't get too complacent. Just because the rain stopped doesn't mean everything's pristine. Those 60 mph wind gusts and quarter-sized hail from last night could have knocked down some smaller branches, especially on the less-trafficked side trails or in some of the more wooded sections of our parks. Keep an eye out for debris, uneven surfaces, and any standing water that might make for a surprise splash or, worse, a twisted ankle. Nobody wants to end up at a PT office for something entirely avoidable.

### Construction: The Eternal Chicago Hazard

You know it, you love it (or hate it, mostly hate it): construction. It’s always happening. The Illinois Tollway is constantly on the move with lane closures, so if your route takes you near major roadways like the Stevenson or Eisenhower, assume there are cones. More directly for us runners, the **Salt Creek Trail** under I-294 is still undergoing its transformation and isn't expected to fully reopen until late summer. Plan your longer runs accordingly to avoid that detour, unless you're into impromptu route changes.
