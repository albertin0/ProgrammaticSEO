### Weather & Air Quality: Hot and Hazy (But Clear)

It might look clear out there at 21.0°C (about 70°F), but don't let that fool you. The National Weather Service is forecasting daily highs in the low to mid-90s this week, and the *feels like* temperature – that's the heat index – is expected to hit around 100 degrees Fahrenheit, particularly away from the lakefront today and tomorrow. This isn't a drill; people died in the last heat wave we had.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning!
Seriously, Chicago, this isn't the day to push your limits. The combination of heat and humidity is forming what experts are calling a "double whammy" for your system, especially with elevated ozone levels in the air. Reschedule that long run for indoors or later in the week when things cool down. If you *must* be out, hydrate aggressively, wear light clothing, and stick to the shade.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Conditions & Local Hazards

Good news first: the Taste of Chicago street closures in Grant Park officially wrapped up yesterday, July 13th, at 11:59 PM. So, the Loop is mostly clear of festival debris.

However, if you're heading out to some of our beloved suburban trails, keep your head up. The Forest Preserves of Cook County have ongoing construction. Specifically, the **Des Plaines River Trail System** still has water main construction. You'll find closures from Ditka to Herrick Ave, and you'll need to detour to the tan unpaved spur at Fullerton Ave. Don't be that hero who ignores the signs and ends up in a ditch or a work zone.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Trail Construction & Detours
Portions of the Des Plaines River Trail System are still under construction. While the Salt Creek Trail under I-294 *seems* to have reopened as of July 10th (even if signs aren't always updated), assume nothing. Always check local forest preserve alerts before you head out, and if you see caution tape, turn around. It's there for a reason, not just to annoy you.
</AlertBox>

There's also a "Safety Awareness Run" today around 8555 S Green Bay Ave, which could mean increased foot traffic or temporary local disruptions in that specific area.

#### Stay Informed and Safe
Download the Chicago OEMC app for real-time alerts. Check the Open Air Chicago website for localized air quality data, especially given the ozone concerns. And if you're feeling even a little off from the heat, get inside and cool down. Your PR can wait.
