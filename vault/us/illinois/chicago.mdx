The city, as usual, is tearing itself apart, and that means your carefully planned routes are probably going to be interrupted by orange cones and frustrated sighs.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### The Eternal Construction Season Has Begun
Chicago's annual "let's mess up every major road at once" tradition is in full swing. If you're a cyclist or runner who uses major arteries or their adjacent paths, be prepared for diversions and unexpected detours. The Stevenson, Dan Ryan, and I-290/Eisenhower Expressways are all under the knife, with lane closures already in effect. This isn’t just about car traffic; it impacts how you navigate surrounding areas. Assume delays and plan alternative routes, especially if you're trying to get across town to your favorite running spot.
</AlertBox>

Specifically, for those of you who find solace on the trails, here's a heads-up:

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Trail Troubles: Salt Creek and Des Plaines River Trail Sections Closed
If your long run or ride typically takes you along the **Salt Creek Trail** under I-294, you're out of luck for a while. That section is closed for major Tollway work and isn't expected to reopen until early June. So, don't even bother. Turn around. Go home. Find another path. Similarly, the **Des Plaines River Trail (DPRT)** still has some lingering construction from 2025 into this year, particularly south of the shiny new Lawrence Avenue bridge. Always check local forest preserve district websites before you head out, unless you enjoy surprise detours and impromptu cross-country running through mud.
</AlertBox>

And let's not forget our iconic Chicago River bridges. Apparently, they're all crumbling at once, because more closures are slated for 2026. The Lake Street Bridge, for instance, isn't expected to reopen for another two years. This might not directly affect a trail run, but if your urban workout involves navigating downtown, prepare for even more frustrating bottlenecks and re-routes.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Pollen Watch: Not Awful, But Still There
With "Moderate" pollen levels, those of you who normally get hit hard in the spring might feel it today. It's not a lung-buster like high AQI, but a runny nose or itchy eyes can definitely ruin a good rhythm. If you're prone to seasonal allergies, pop an antihistamine *before* you head out, not after you're already sneezing through your run along Lake Shore Drive.
</AlertBox>

So, yeah, it's cool enough, and the air's mostly clear. But you're still in Chicago, which means you'll earn every mile you log today by dodging traffic, construction, and probably a few rogue pigeons.
