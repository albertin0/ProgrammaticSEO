## The Air You Breathe

Let's talk about the air. An AQI of 102 isn't *horrific*, but it's enough to make you think twice about pushing for that new PR. PM2.5 particles are tiny enough to get deep into your lungs and bloodstream, and nobody needs that extra stress on a chilly Friday. If you're feeling any sort of throat irritation or difficulty breathing, cut your workout short. Seriously.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### AQI: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups
With an AQI of 102 and PM2.5 dominating, sensitive individuals (asthmatics, elderly, children) should reduce time spent outdoors. Everyone else: pay attention to your body. Keep outdoor activities shorter and less intense.
</AlertBox>

## Weather Woes & Trail Troubles

The good news? No extreme heat or bone-chilling cold today, sitting around 6.4°C (43.5°F). But that fog? It's a visibility killer, especially near the lake. Be extra cautious if you're out on two wheels or hitting the pavement in the early morning.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Foggy Start
Locally dense fog is expected to impact visibility, particularly in the northern suburbs, early this morning. Dress in bright colors and use lights if you must be out before it clears.
</AlertBox>

And speaking of visibility, watch your step on the Lakefront Trail. The Chicago Park District is still doing repairs, with asphalt patching, seal-coating, and concrete work moving north from the south end. This isn't a minor pothole; we're talking about active construction zones along sections of the trail. You'll see signage, but keep your head up and your phone tucked away. Getting tripped up by an uneven patch of pavement because you weren't paying attention is a one-way ticket to my PT clinic.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Lakefront Trail Construction
Ongoing repairs are affecting the entire 18-mile Lakefront Trail, including asphalt patching and concrete work. Expect uneven surfaces, potential detours, and active construction zones. Stay alert and avoid designated work areas, especially if visibility is poor.
</AlertBox>

So, if you're hitting the pavement, stick to routes you know well, or maybe finally try that indoor cycling class you've been eyeing. Your lungs, joints, and sanity will thank you.
