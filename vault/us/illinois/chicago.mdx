### The Chilly Reality of May Day

Seriously, 4.8°C? It’s May, Chicago, not January. While we've seen worse, this overcast, near-freezing weather means you’ll be bundled up like it's still winter. Those of you with sensitive knees or hips are going to feel every single degree of that chill creeping in. Dynamic warm-ups are non-negotiable today, people. Don't be that hero who pulls a hamstring five minutes into their run because they thought a quick quad stretch was enough.

The air quality, with an AQI of 51 and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, is sitting squarely in the "Moderate" zone. Combine that with "Moderate" pollen levels, and while it's not a red flag for most, anyone with asthma or seasonal allergies might find themselves reaching for an inhaler or tissues more often than they'd like.

### Watch Your Step: Fog & Frost Potential
The National Weather Service issued a heads-up about patchy dense fog this morning, especially outside the immediate urban core, which can reduce visibility to less than half a mile. There's also a chance of patchy frost overnight into early Saturday, so even if you're hitting the pavement later, be mindful of potentially slick spots, especially on less-trafficked side streets or shaded portions of trails.
</AlertBox>

## Trail & Road Hazards

Chicago's a city perpetually under construction, and it seems May 1st is no different. The city’s iconic river bridges are still a mess, with repairs and closures planned for 2026. The Lake Street Bridge, for instance, isn't expected to reopen for another two years. Expect the Northwest South Street Bridge, along with the Michigan and Grand Avenue bridges, to undergo major rehab soon, meaning more detours downtown. If your route involves crossing the Chicago River, double-check your path or just plan on taking the L.

Beyond downtown, if you're venturing out to the suburbs for a longer run or ride, know that the Des Plaines River Trail is still dealing with construction between Lawrence Avenue and Irving Park Road, with intermittent closures anticipated through 2026. The Illinois Prairie Path also has closures on its Main Stem near I-294 and I-290 due to tollway projects. Don't be surprised by unexpected detours or uneven surfaces; assume anything near major waterways or construction zones could be a temporary headache.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Ahead for Events & Construction
With several runs kicking off today, sections of the Lakefront Path, especially around the Museum Campus and Grant Park, will be busy. Be prepared for crowds, slower paces, and potential temporary closures if your route overlaps with these events. On top of that, ongoing construction projects on expressways and bridges mean local roads might see increased traffic and detours. Consider an indoor workout or a less popular route to avoid the logistical hurdles.
</AlertBox>
