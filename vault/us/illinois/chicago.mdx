## The Rundown: Don't Be a Moron, Chicago

Look, the weather's finally not actively trying to kill you, which is a win in my book. We've got clear skies and a temperature that won't give you frostbite or heatstroke. The air quality is firmly in the "Good" category with an AQI of 34 [cite: environmental data]. So, if your biggest complaint is "it's a little chilly," tough it out; that's what layers are for.

<AlertBox type="info">
Remember, even with clear skies, if you're hitting the Lakefront Trail, especially north of Oak Street, keep an eye out for lingering debris. While the specific weather-related closure from back in February might be gone, this city is always doing something to surprise you.
</AlertBox>

Now, for the actual hazards. Chicago is in a perpetual state of "under construction," and this week is no exception. If your route usually takes you anywhere near the **Halsted Street & Chicago Avenue Bridge Project**, prepare for an adventure. They're still in full swing with viaduct removal and demolishing the Chicago Avenue bridge. We're talking noise, we're talking dust – the whole nine yards. Pedestrians on the west side of that bridge? Detoured to the east side. Don't be that person trying to squeeze through closed areas. It’s not worth a twisted ankle or worse. This whole headache is slated to last until the end of 2026, so get used to it.

Downtown isn't much better. The Lake Street Bascule Bridge rehabilitation is causing closures from Michigan to Wabash, and Garland from Haddock to Lake. And if you thought the State/Lake CTA station demolition was done, think again. They've torn down the old one and are now building the new. Expect State Street between Wacker and Randolph to be down to one lane, and Lake Street to see closures during weekday daytime hours. These aren't just minor inconveniences; they’re full-on detours that can add significant time and frustration to your run or ride. Plan your routes accordingly.
