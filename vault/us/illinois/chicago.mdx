## The Chilly Reality: Dress for Actual Winter

Look, the calendar says April, but the mercury says "not quite spring." At 1.8°C, your body is working overtime just to stay warm. That means tighter muscles, stiffer joints, and a higher risk of pulls and strains if you jump straight into a sprint. Layer up, people. Think technical fabrics, not your old cotton college sweatshirt. Frostbite can sneak up on exposed skin, especially fingers, toes, and ears, even in clear conditions. Don't be that person surprised by numb digits.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Skimp on Warm-Up
Your PT, who happens to be me, is telling you: a solid, dynamic warm-up is non-negotiable today. Focus on large muscle groups and gradually increase intensity. Cold muscles are unforgiving.
</AlertBox>

## Breathing Easy? Not Quite.

While the Air Quality Index is a pristine 35, which is fantastic, don't forget about the pollen. It's moderate today, and combined with the cold air, your airways might feel a bit irritated. If you're prone to exercise-induced asthma or just have sensitive lungs, consider an indoor alternative or at least stick to shorter, lower-intensity efforts.

## Navigating the Urban Obstacle Course

Good news for those who dodged the Chi Town Half Marathon yesterday – the crowds are gone. However, the city's perpetual state of improvement (or so they say) continues to throw wrenches in our routes. Headed downtown? Forget seamless access along parts of the Chicago Riverwalk. The ongoing Lake Street Bridge rehabilitation means pedestrian access from Lake Street to the 150 N. Riverside Plaza Upper Riverwalk is still closed until summer 2026. So, if your run usually involves looping through that area, plan your detours now or face unexpected roadblocks.

And for those of you trying to escape the concrete jungle, don't forget about the Salt Creek Trail under I-294. That section's been closed for ages and isn't slated to reopen until early June 2026. The Des Plaines River Trail also has ongoing closures and construction, particularly south of the Lawrence Avenue bridge. It's always something.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Watch Your Step: Construction Zones Ahead
Expect uneven surfaces, reduced pedestrian space, and potential heavy equipment in areas like the Lake Street Bridge. Pay attention to signage and don't assume drivers see you.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Bring Hydration (Warm, if Possible)
Even in cold weather, hydration is crucial. A thermos with warm tea or water can make a huge difference in keeping your core temperature up during or after your run.
</AlertBox>
