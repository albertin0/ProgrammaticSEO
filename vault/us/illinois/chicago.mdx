## The Good News: Breathe Easy (Mostly)

After a few weeks in July where our lungs were taking a beating from distant wildfire smoke, today's Air Quality Index is sitting at a moderate 59, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. Remember mid-July when the Chicago Park District shut down beaches and pools because the air was straight-up hazardous? Yeah, we're not doing that today. The skies are clear, and while "moderate" isn't "pristine," it's certainly not the lung-scorching mess we've seen recently. So, if you've been cooped up, this is your chance to stretch those legs without feeling like you're sucking down campfire fumes.

<AlertBox type="info">
Pollen levels are moderate today, so if you're one of the unfortunate souls who sniffles their way through summer, take your meds. It's not extreme, but it's enough to annoy you if you're sensitive.
</AlertBox>

## The Bad News: Always Under Construction

Chicago's unofficial motto should be "Welcome to Chicago, now under construction." Seriously, it feels like there's always something being torn up, rebuilt, or rerouted. And today's no exception.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch Your Step Around Lake Street Bridge:** Pedestrian access from Lake Street to the 150 N. Riverside Plaza Upper Riverwalk has been closed since January 2026 and is expected to remain that way "later in summer 2026" due to ongoing major rehabilitation of the Lake Street Bascule Bridge. If that's usually part of your Riverwalk loop, find an alternate route. Expect noise and dust from the demolition and construction work on the bridge house and structural steel.
</AlertBox>

Beyond the Lake Street Bridge, general construction around the Loop continues to be a thing. While the specific CTA Loop Elevated weekend closures from earlier this month are over, the State/Lake Station reconstruction is a multi-year project, so anticipate other disruptions down the line.

If you're heading outside the city center, remember that various Forest Preserves of Cook County trails are seeing improvements and new construction throughout 2026. This includes resurfacing in the Tinley Creek region and reconstruction starting at Swallow Cliff. Even the much-anticipated "Big Hill" mountain bike trails at Big Marsh Park were expected to open mid-spring this year. So, while improvements are good, they sometimes mean temporary detours or muddy paths in the short term. Stay vigilant and check local advisories if you're hitting specific trail systems.

All in all, today is a fantastic day to get some miles in, just be aware of your surroundings, especially if you're navigating the concrete jungle.
