## The Rundown: Don't Be a Dummy Today

Look, the temperature's sitting at a nice 14.5°C (that's 58°F for you Celsius-averse folks), and the air quality is actually *good*. That's usually a green light. But dig a little deeper, and it's not all sunshine and PRs out there.

### Weather Whining

Despite the "Clear" condition in our primary data, a recent alert warns of "torrential rainfall and thunderstorms" near the I-88 and I-80 corridors overnight, with "flash flooding (and potentially significant flash flooding)" possible before it tapers off after daybreak. Even if it's clearing up now, that means wet, potentially hazardous conditions on roads and unpaved trails in those areas. And if you're thinking of a lake dip or even just running close to the water, be aware of "rip current warnings" in the forecast. Stick to the paved paths and stay away from the lake's edge, unless you *want* to swim with the fishes.

### Chicago's Never-Ending Construction Saga

This is where your run or ride gets complicated. The **Lakefront Trail** is basically a permanent construction zone these days. The Chicago Park District is deep into "repairs in varying degrees that will be starting in the next few weeks and will continue throughout the next several months" as of September 15th. This means asphalt patching, seal-coating, and edge repairs from 6300 South all the way up to Foster Ave beach. Plus, for you cyclists, "bikes are being rerouted onto the pedestrian side of the lakefront trail between Randolph and a little north of Monroe." And don't even get me started on the "Montrose tunnel is closed again for work on the LFT." Seriously, planning a seamless LFT workout is a full-time job.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Lakefront Trail Users:** Expect detours, narrow paths, and active work zones. Be alert, slow down, and share the path.
</AlertBox>

It's not just the lakefront. Out in the **Forest Preserves of Cook County**, things are equally bumpy. A segment of the Poplar Creek Trail System's Red Paved Loop is closed near Bode East due to IDOT construction. The Thorn Creek Trail System near Glenwood Woods has "intermittent construction activities," so "proceed with caution." And if you're hitting the Tinley Creek Trail, be mindful that "hazardous trees are being removed by contractors." Watch your head, literally.

Downtown isn't spared either. The Lake Street Bascule Bridge is undergoing "major rehabilitation," including "west bridge house demolition." And if you use the 150 N. Riverside Upper Riverwalk access from Lake Street, it's still closed for sidewalk restoration, likely through the end of summer.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Pro Tip:** Always check the Chicago Park District and Forest Preserves websites before heading out. Don't just assume your favorite route is open.
</AlertBox>
