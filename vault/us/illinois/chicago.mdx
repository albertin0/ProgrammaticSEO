Today, October 21st, 2026, Chicago's doing its best impression of a foggy, blustery autumn day. The good news? Your lungs are mostly in the clear, with an AQI of a lovely 29. That means the air itself isn't trying to actively shorten your lifespan. So, that's something.

However, the universe is making up for it with visibility issues and biting winds. The dense fog that's been lingering since yesterday morning, with advisories across Cook County, is still playing peek-a-boo this morning. The National Weather Service warned of patchy dense fog with visibility dropping to near zero in some areas. It should burn off an hour or two after sunrise, but if you're an early bird, seriously, wait it out or stick to a treadmill. Running on the Lakefront Trail when you can't see the person ten feet ahead of you is just asking for a collision, or worse, an unexpected plunge if you get too close to the water's edge.

Then there's the wind. Oh, the wind. A midweek weather alert specifically called out gusty winds and a sharp temperature drop from today through Friday. Even though the thermometer might say 6.1°C, that wind chill is going to make it feel significantly colder. This isn't just about comfort; cold joints are stiff joints, and stiff joints are injured joints. Protect those knees and hips.

No major construction or event-related trail closures are gumming up the works in the city center today, thankfully. The Marathon's long gone, and its associated street closures and Balbo Drive construction are cleared out. So, you won't be rerouted there. But that fog and wind are legitimate hazards for anyone trying to get in an outdoor sweat session.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Foggy Forecast:** If you absolutely *must* run outside during the fog, wear bright, reflective gear and assume drivers cannot see you. Stick to well-lit, familiar paths. Better yet, hit the gym or wait until the fog clears completely, usually by late morning.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Layer Up, Buttercup:** With that wind chill, multiple thin layers are your friend. Think wicking base layer, an insulating mid-layer, and a wind-resistant outer shell. Don't forget gloves, a hat, and something to cover your ears. Your extremities are the first to suffer.
</AlertBox>
