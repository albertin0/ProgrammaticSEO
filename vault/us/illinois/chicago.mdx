### Watch Your Step (and Your Route)

That crisp air and clear sky might tempt you onto some longer trails, but hold your horses. If your usual weekend long run involves the **Des Plaines River Trail**, you need to adjust your expectations. A key portion of this trail, specifically south of the new Lawrence Avenue bridge, remains closed due to ongoing construction that’s been lingering since the beginning of the year. We're talking substantial work, folks. Don't assume "clear" weather means "clear" paths. The Forest Preserves have been working on boardwalks and rerouting to avoid flooding areas, and some of that work is still active.

This isn't just an inconvenience; it can force you onto less safe alternatives or significantly extend your planned route if you have to backtrack. Always check local trail statuses, especially when venturing out on paths that are prone to construction or weather-related issues. The Forest Preserves of Cook County website is usually your best bet for real-time updates on closures, though they sometimes run on "normal business hours" time, so plan ahead.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Des Plaines River Trail Closure:** A significant section south of the Lawrence Avenue bridge is still impacted by ongoing construction. Do NOT plan on a continuous run through this area without checking specific detour information first. Expect the unexpected.
</AlertBox>

Beyond the trails, general city construction is always a factor in Chicago, and you know how it goes. While no specific *running trail* impacting construction was highlighted in the last 24 hours (outside of the ongoing Des Plaines River Trail woes and general Metra delays), expect the usual road work, uneven sidewalks, and unexpected detours, especially if you're hitting urban routes. The Illinois Tollway, for example, is constantly advising drivers to be alert in work zones, and that advice applies to us runners too. A massive $1.3 billion I-80 improvement project in Will County, including the Des Plaines River bridge (different from the trail, but shows the scale of work), means plenty of heavy vehicle traffic and altered road conditions across the broader region.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ozone Alert:** While the AQI is "Moderate" at 58, ozone is the dominant pollutant. Sensitive individuals (asthma, allergies) might experience respiratory discomfort during sustained outdoor activity. Listen to your body.
</AlertBox>
