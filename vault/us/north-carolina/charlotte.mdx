## Charlotte's March Madness: Heat Stroke Edition

I don't care how many miles you've got in your training plan for the Thunder Road Marathon, today is *not* the day to test your limits outdoors. Today's "Mainly Clear" skies might look inviting, but they're a cruel trick. At 37.9 degrees Celsius (that's pushing 100°F for those still on Imperial), you're not just running; you're actively cooking yourself.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### Extreme Heat Advisory
  Running in nearly 38°C heat significantly increases your risk of heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and dehydration. Your body isn't ready for this in late March. This isn't just uncomfortable, it's dangerous. Forget your tempo run; grab an ice pack.
</AlertBox>

### Lung Busters: AQI 127 and PM2.5

The air quality, sitting at 127 AQI, is categorized as "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups," but let's be real, even us seasoned runners pushing a few miles are sensitive to inhaling a bunch of PM2.5. This stuff digs deep into your lungs and can cause immediate symptoms like coughing or shortness of breath, and nobody needs that interrupting their flow on the Little Sugar Creek Greenway. If you're going to insist on moving your body, keep it indoors where the air is filtered and the AC is blasting. Seriously, your lungs will thank you.

### Local Scoop: What's Happening Out There

There are no major trail closures or massive construction projects blocking off your usual routes right now. No unexpected floods on the Toby Creek Greenway after last night, no wildfires sending smoke our way from Gastonia or anything crazy like that. That's the *good* news. The *bad* news is the weather itself is the hazard.

Today, your biggest enemy isn't a poorly placed orange cone or a rogue mountain biker; it's the actual air and temperature. Forget chasing PRs outside. If you *must* exercise, hit the gym, do some strength training, or finally tackle that yoga video you saved. Hydration is key even if you're just sitting on your couch today.
