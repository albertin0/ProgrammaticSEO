Look, I get it, you want to get your miles in. But if you're planning a long run or intense session, especially around midday or this afternoon, you're asking for trouble.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Code Orange Air Quality for Ozone!
The North Carolina Division of Air Quality has issued a Code Orange Air Quality Action Day for Mecklenburg County. This means the air is unhealthy for sensitive groups, including children, the elderly, and anyone with respiratory conditions. Running in this stuff is like giving your lungs a bad workout before you even start moving. Limit prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors, especially during peak ozone hours (late morning through early evening).
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### South End Rail Trail Closure
Still planning to hit the South End Rail Trail? Think again. Significant sections remain closed through July due to construction on the new South End light rail station and track crossing. While detours are marked along Hawkins Street, Dunevant Street, and South Boulevard, they're not ideal for a continuous, uninterrupted workout. Expect delays and less-than-perfect running surfaces. Don't go out there thinking you'll just "power through" the construction zone; it's a legitimate safety hazard with active work.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Beat the Heat (and Ozone)
If you absolutely *must* get outside, make it an early morning affair before the ozone levels spike and the temperatures soar. Stick to shaded routes if possible. Otherwise, hit the gym, find an indoor track, or try some bodyweight circuits at home. Your lungs and joints will thank you. And seriously, hydrate more than you think you need to.
</AlertBox>
