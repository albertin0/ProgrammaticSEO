## Today's Outdoor Workout Hazards

Look, I love hitting the pavement or the trails as much as the next runner, but today is a hard pass. The weather data sent over from some higher power tells us it's a scorching 100.2°F (37.9°C) and "Mainly Clear." Clear, yes, but clear enough to see the heat shimmering off the asphalt, probably. This is not the day to push for a PR, unless "personal record for heatstroke" is your goal.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: 100.2°F (37.9°C)
Seriously, folks, this is dangerous. Heat exhaustion and heatstroke are real, and they can kill you. Your body isn't designed to regulate itself effectively when it's this hot, especially not during strenuous activity. If you absolutely *must* move, keep it indoors where there's AC. This isn't a suggestion; it's a plea from someone who's seen too many people underestimate Charlotte's summer-like days in spring.
</AlertBox>

And as if the furnace blast wasn't enough, we've got an Air Quality Index (AQI) of 127, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. That puts us squarely in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category. So, while you're trying to keep cool, you'll also be inhaling microscopic particles that are great for irritating your lungs and making any existing respiratory issues a whole lot worse. Your lungs are not a filter for the city's crud; treat them with respect.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Unhealthy Air Quality: AQI 127 (PM2.5)
If you have asthma, COPD, or any other respiratory condition, you should absolutely reconsider outdoor exercise today. Even if you don't, prolonged or heavy exertion outside can lead to shortness of breath, coughing, and general discomfort. Don't be a hero; save your lungs for actually breathing.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: South End Construction Continues to Snarl the Rail Trail

If you were hoping to sneak in a run on the **Rail Trail**, think again. Construction on the South End Station is still wreaking havoc, with sections of the Rail Trail from Remount Road to Tremont closed from April through July. It’s a mess of detours, concrete barriers, and general disruption. And let's not forget the ongoing pedestrian bridge construction spanning I-277 that started last fall. So, while the city dreams of a shinier, more connected South End, your run is getting rerouted onto busy streets, which, let's be honest, nobody wants.

Other greenways aren't entirely clear either. The E. John Street entrance to the Four Mile Creek Greenway remains closed due to NCDOT work, and there are still intermittent closures around the McAlpine and Four Mile Creek Greenway trails off Johnston Road for ongoing construction. Always check the Mecklenburg County Park and Rec site before you head out, but today, you're better off staying clear.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Trail Closures & Detours
The Rail Trail through South End is a no-go for much of the spring and summer due to the new station construction. Expect significant detours and plan alternate routes if you insist on being outside.
</AlertBox>
