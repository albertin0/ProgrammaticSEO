## The Deets

Look, the AQI is in the "Good" range, so your lungs aren't going to protest, which is a definite win. Pollen is only moderate, so if you're not super sensitive, a quick antihistamine should have you covered. The big kicker today is the temperature, clocking in at a frosty 2.5°C. "Clear" skies sound nice, but that chill will cut right through you if you're underdressed. Layer up, people.

### Trail Shenanigans

Here's where it gets complicated. The Boise Foothills are still a bit of a minefield if you're not careful. We had some significant rain and gusty winds earlier this week, and while it's clear now, those low and mid-elevation trails are likely still muddy and prone to damage. Ridge to Rivers was practically begging folks to stay off them just last week to prevent lasting damage. So, if you hit something slick and twist an ankle, don't say I didn't warn you.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Alert: Camel's Back Trail & Greenbelt**
A section of the popular Camel's Back Trail is still closed for improvements, with crews adding steps to combat erosion. This work is expected to wrap up sometime this month, weather permitting, so don't be surprised if your usual loop is rerouted. Also, if your run takes you near the Boise River Greenbelt by Wood Duck Island or Barber Park, watch out. Construction for path repairs is happening, with the Barber Park section specifically impacted through mid-May. Expect detours and maybe some heavy machinery.
</AlertBox>

Up at Bogus Basin, they got a fresh 18 inches of snow since Tuesday, so if you're hitting the slopes for a demo day or some turns, enjoy the powder. Just remember, "marked and unmarked trail hazards exist," especially with that fresh snow, so keep your head on a swivel.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip: Stay on Pavement (Mostly)**
Given the cold temps and potential for muddy foothills, today might be a good day to stick to paved routes if you're not heading to the mountains. The Boise River Greenbelt is a good option, *outside* of the noted construction zones. Or hit up those higher-elevation, snow-covered trails if you're geared for it.
</AlertBox>
