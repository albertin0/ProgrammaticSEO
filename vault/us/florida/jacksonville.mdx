### Severe Weather Incoming!
Look, it's not just a sprinkle. There's a "Weather Impact Alert" for Monday, March 16th, from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.. We're talking isolated strong to severe storms, with a line of nastiness moving through in the afternoon. Expect damaging wind gusts of 50-70 mph, which is plenty to knock down trees or send you tumbling on your run. There's also a risk of isolated, brief tornadoes, small hail, and frequent lightning. If you absolutely *must* get out, do it before 8 a.m., and keep an eye on the radar. Better yet, stay home. Your PR can wait. Seriously, don't be that person the fire department has to fish out of a ditch because you wanted to hit your splits.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Drought Conditions & Fire Risk
We're in an extreme drought across the whole region, and it's been bone dry for weeks. While your AQI looks surprisingly decent today (27, "Good"), the underlying conditions mean that any spark could turn into a serious brush fire fast. If you're out hiking on the edges of town, or near any of the drier preserves, be hyper-aware. A stray cigarette butt (don't even get me started on littering, people) or a hot car exhaust on dry grass is all it takes to ruin someone's day, or worse. Keep those trail snacks in your pack, not on the ground.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Emerald Trail Construction

The Emerald Trail is an awesome project, truly. Who doesn't want more pedestrian-friendly routes connecting our neighborhoods? But as you know, awesome things take time (and sometimes, a whole lot of unexpected funding scrambling). The Hogan Street Link, a significant chunk of this urban pathway, is still under construction and isn't slated for full completion until early 2027. A segment near the St. Johns River might be ready by summer 2026, but that's not *today*. This means parts of Hogan Street are narrowed to one lane for traffic, making way for that two-way bike lane. While not a direct trail closure necessarily, it means you need to be extra vigilant if you're navigating that area. Even without the severe weather, construction always adds an element of "watch your ass" to your workout.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Weather Aware
With severe storms rolling through, don't just rely on glancing out the window. Keep your weather alerts on your phone, check reliable local news sources (like First Coast News or Action News Jax), and have a backup plan for your outdoor activities. If you're on a long run or ride, know where you can duck for cover. Spring Breakers, especially, need alternative plans for outdoor fun today. Lightning doesn't care if you're on vacation.
</AlertBox>
