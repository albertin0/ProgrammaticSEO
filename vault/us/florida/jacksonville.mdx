### Main Street Bridge Pedestrian Walkway CLOSED!
If your route takes you across the St. Johns River via the Main Street Bridge, know this: the **southbound pedestrian walkway is closed** as of August 24, 2026, and won't reopen until January 30, 2027. This is for the Riverfront Plaza Main Street Bridge Nub Removal Project. You'll need to find an alternate route for your walk or run. Don't be that person trying to squeeze onto the vehicle lane. Traffic is already reduced to one southbound lane, creating even more chaos. The City has posted detours; actually follow them.
</AlertBox>

## Construction Zone Ahead (Everywhere, Apparently)

It seems like every major artery and scenic route is undergoing some "improvement" today, September 1, 2026. Beyond the Main Street Bridge debacle, be aware of other significant disruptions:

*   **I-95 and MLK Jr. Parkway:** Pedestrian bridge demolition is ongoing, with closures scheduled through September 4th. If you use this area, adjust your plans.
*   **St. Johns Parkway:** Expect daytime closures from September 1st to 3rd. Another one to avoid if you're trying to hit mileage on two feet.
*   **South Beach Park Trail:** Construction is still underway for the South Beach Park Trail and Passive Park Improvement Project. They're aiming for "substantially complete by mid-September," which means today, you're likely navigating equipment and temporary barriers between the South Beach Parkway entrance and Jacksonville Drive.
*   **Emerald Trail:** Various segments of the ambitious Emerald Trail project, including parts of the Hogan Street Link, are still under construction through 2026 and into 2027. Keep an eye on specific segment updates, as detours are a constant companion around here.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Hydration isn't Optional, It's Survival
The forecast says "near/above 90" inland. Coupled with Florida's signature humidity, you're looking at prime dehydration territory. Carry *more* water than you think you need. Pre-hydrate. Post-hydrate. Don't wait until you feel thirsty; by then, you're already behind. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real, and frankly, they're embarrassing to deal with.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Beach Day? Watch for Rip Currents
If your workout involves the ocean, a moderate risk of rip currents is still in effect for area beaches this week. That "elevated rip current risk" isn't just a suggestion, it's a genuine hazard. Even if the water looks calm, those currents can sneak up on you faster than a rogue tourist with a selfie stick. Stick to wading, or at least swim near a lifeguard.
</AlertBox>
