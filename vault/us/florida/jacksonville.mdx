## Today's Hot Mess Express: Weather and Air Quality

Alright, Jax, let's talk about it. The official temperature might be a balmy 24.5°C (that's about 76°F for you Celsius-challenged folks), but don't let that fool you. News reports for today suggest we're looking at heat index values creeping close to 100°F before those afternoon storms roll in. As a physical therapist, I’m telling you now, that kind of "feels like" temperature is prime for heat exhaustion if you push it.

Adding insult to injury, we've got a "Partly Cloudy" sky that's just a front for scattered showers and thunderstorms expected mainly this afternoon and early evening. Some of those could be packing a punch with heavy rain. Nobody needs to be dodging lightning bolts mid-sprint, or worse, slipping on wet pavement and ending up in my office with a sprained ankle.

Then there's the air. Our AQI is sitting at 59, which is officially "Moderate," but the dominant pollutant is PM2.5. Why does that matter? Because fine particles from distant wildfires can drift into Duval County, and by the time you see that hazy glow over the St. Johns River, those readings have been climbing for hours. You might not see the smoke, but your lungs will feel it. Combine that with a moderate pollen level, and if you’ve got any kind of respiratory sensitivity, you’re basically running through a gauntlet of irritants.

### Hidden Heat Hazard

## Navigational Nightmares & River Risks

If you were planning a nice run or bike ride connecting Downtown's Northbank and Southbank, you can forget about a smooth crossing over the Main Street Bridge. Construction started back in August and is going to be messing things up until January 2027. The pedestrian walkway on the southbound side is closed, and traffic's reduced to one lane. Unless you enjoy playing Frogger with angry commuters, find another route. Seriously, don't risk it just to shave a few minutes off your commute. There are other bridges, people!

And for those of you who love a good riverfront jaunt, be aware that minor flooding will continue around high tide along the St. Johns River basin through Monday. That means soggy shoes, potential submerged hazards, and generally a gross experience if you're hitting up any of the low-lying sections of the Riverwalk.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Main Street Bridge Closure
The southbound pedestrian walkway on the Main Street Bridge is CLOSED due to construction until January 2027. Expect delays and find alternate routes for crossing the St. Johns River.
