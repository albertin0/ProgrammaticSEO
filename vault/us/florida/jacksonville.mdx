Look, I get it. The sun's out, and it's a balmy 23 degrees Celsius, heading up to a high of 31C (88F) today. Seems inviting, right? Wrong. Jacksonville is currently battling some seriously crummy air. The Air Quality Index is sitting at a flat-out unhealthy 157, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. That's not just a sniffle waiting to happen; that's inflammation for your airways, especially if you're pushing it.

We've got wildfires still smoldering in surrounding counties, sending smoke plumes right over our heads. Lafayette County, over 100 miles west, and even Brantley County up in Georgia are contributing to this haze. The city's been on top of it, issuing burn bans, but that doesn't clear the air magically. If you're out trying to rack up miles on the Northbank Riverwalk, you'll be breathing in more than just humid air. This kind of air quality is particularly nasty for anyone with asthma, respiratory issues, or if you're over 65, pregnant, or have young kids. Basically, if you're a human, it's not ideal.

And don't even *think* about hitting the beaches for a swim to cool off. Jacksonville's beautiful coastline, from Jax Beach Pier all the way down, is under a **High Rip Current Risk** today. The winds are breezy, around 10-15 knots (about 11-17 mph), which is contributing to some building surf. Those red flags are up for a reason, folks. Stay out of the water unless you're hugging a lifeguard. Actually, just stay out of the water.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Unhealthy Air Quality (AQI 157)
Seriously, take this warning to heart. Minimize outdoor exercise. If you *must* be outside, keep it short and low intensity. Running hard, cycling fast, or any activity that makes you breathe heavy will just pull more of those fine particulate matters deep into your lungs. People with respiratory conditions or sensitive lungs should absolutely stay indoors. Keep your windows closed and run your AC on recirculate.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### High Rip Current Risk
Do NOT go swimming at any local beaches today. The currents are strong, and you could easily get pulled out. If you absolutely have to be near the water, stay shallow and always, always near a lifeguard. This isn't a suggestion; it's a safety imperative.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Pollen & Rising Temps
While the main concern is air quality, don't forget we still have moderate pollen floating around, which can make things even more uncomfortable for allergy sufferers. Combine that with the temperature climbing into the high 80s later, and dehydration becomes a real threat if you ignore the AQI warnings and push it.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Shands Bridge Reopened
Good news for commuters in Clay and St. Johns Counties: the Shands Bridge on State Road 16, which was closed for maintenance, was scheduled to reopen by 4 AM this morning. This means one less traffic headache, but remember, overall road construction, like the nighttime lane closures on I-95, is still happening. Always check your routes before you head out, even for non-running activities.
</AlertBox>
