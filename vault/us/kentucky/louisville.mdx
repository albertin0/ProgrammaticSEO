## Today's Hot Takes & Hazards

It's a beautiful day to hit the pavement or dirt, *if* you're prepared. That 16.8°C is basically perfect running weather, and the air quality is in the "don't even think about it" category for excuses. But let's not pretend it's all sunshine and PRs.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  <h4>Allergy Alert: Pollen is Pumping!</h4>
  Look, spring is here, and so is the pollen. "Moderate" means your sinuses are gonna feel it if you're susceptible. Carry your tissues, pop your meds, and maybe don't plan on stopping to smell *all* the flowers along the Waterfront Park just yet.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble: The Louisville Loop is a Mess (in spots)

If your long run involves the Louisville Loop, you need to pay attention. Parts of the Ohio Valley Section are basically a no-go for pedestrians. Specifically, the stretch between **Mile Markers 10.75 and 13.75** on Camp Ground Road is *unsafe* because, get this, there's no damn pedestrian pathway. Who designed that?

And it gets better: there's a detour between **Mile Markers 4.0 and 6.50** from Northwestern Parkway at 31st Street, all the way to Shawnee Golf Course, thanks to bank erosion repairs near Portland Wharf Park. So, if you were planning on a smooth riverfront cruise, prepare for an unexpected tour through Shawnee Park. Don't be that runner who acts surprised when they hit a closed section. Check the detours.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  <h4>Louisville Loop Danger Zone!</h4>
  Avoid the Louisville Loop between Mile Markers 10.75-13.75 (Camp Ground Road) due to lack of pedestrian path. Also, a significant detour is in place from Portland Wharf Park through Shawnee Park (MM 4.0-6.50) for bank erosion repairs. Seriously, don't ignore these.
</AlertBox>

### Park Purgatory & Potholes

While many trails are drying out, some spots still require caution. We've seen "Caution" notices for **Cherokee Park** as recently as March 25th, so stay aware of potentially muddy or slick sections, especially off the main paved paths. It's still early spring, folks, which means some trails are still recovering from the winter slop. Don't be the person who carves new ruts into a soft trail because you thought your Nikes were a substitute for common sense.

On the road front, construction is still causing headaches. You've got ongoing projects like the **I-65/I-264 ramp work** that was supposed to wrap by the end of March, so overnight/early morning runs near those interstates might still hit some closures. Plus, the usual **pothole patching** crews are out and about on state highways and interstates during non-peak hours, so keep an eye out for unexpected lane shifts if you're logging miles on city streets.

<AlertBox type="info">
  <h4>General Trail Etiquette & Road Savvy</h4>
  If a dirt trail looks wet and squishy, turn around. There are miles of paved paths in our parks. On the roads, assume drivers aren't looking for you. Wear bright colors, obey traffic laws, and watch for construction zones and those sneaky potholes that are still lurking after winter.
</AlertBox>

Get out there, enjoy the good air and temps, but keep your head on a swivel.
