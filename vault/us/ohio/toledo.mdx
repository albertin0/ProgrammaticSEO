## Air Quality & Allergens: Still Kicking Around

Alright, let's talk air. You woke up to a "Mainly Clear" sky and temps around 11.2°C (that's about 52°F for those of you who still cling to Fahrenheit). Nice and crisp. But don't let that fool you into thinking it's pristine out there. Our Air Quality Index is sitting at a nice-sounding 61, which officially puts us in the "acceptable" category. However, the dominant pollutant is still PM2.5, those tiny particles that love to get deep into your lungs.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  The Air Quality Index being "acceptable" with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant isn't a hall pass for everyone. If you've got respiratory issues, or you're just plain sensitive, you might still feel it. Consider easing up on your intensity, especially if you're pushing it along busy roads with car exhaust.
</AlertBox>

And remember that whole chemical leak drama in north Toledo earlier this week at MC Tank Terminal on Progress Ave? While the City of Toledo lifted evacuation orders and preliminary EPA data showed no detection of the released chemical (glycidyl methacrylate) as of Thursday night, some residents and independent testers are still raising eyebrows, with reports of formaldehyde detected near the site. Our AQI *did* spike higher than during the Canadian wildfire smoke earlier this year because of that incident. So, while things are officially "all clear," if you're sensitive or just live in that general area, trust your gut.

<AlertBox type="info">
  Pollen levels are still at "Moderate," so your seasonal sniffles might make an appearance. If you're susceptible, pop those allergy meds before you hit the pavement.
</AlertBox>

## On the Pavement: Watch Your Step (and Your Route)

No major wind or rain alerts today, which is a win in my book. The dense fog advisory from early this morning for parts of NW Ohio and SE Michigan has lifted as of 10 AM EDT. This means better visibility for those morning loops through Ottawa Park or along the University of Toledo campus.

However, if your usual Sunday long run takes you through Rossford, specifically near Rossford High School and the surrounding Colony neighborhood, you're going to need a new plan this morning. The "Racing for Recovery 5K" is happening today, October 25th, starting bright and early. Expect road closures and crowds on Superior Street and the race route.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Avoid Rossford's Colony neighborhood this morning!** The Racing for Recovery 5K will have roads around Rossford High School and Superior Street shut down. Plan an alternate route or cheer from the sidelines.
</AlertBox>

Construction is still the never-ending story around here. ODOT has plenty of projects, including overnight lane restrictions on I-75 near Detroit Avenue and along US 23. While these primarily affect drivers, remember that some construction spills over onto sidewalks or creates uneven surfaces on shoulders. Always keep an eye out, especially if you're trying to sneak in some miles near active work zones like those on Sylvania Ave or Arlington Ave, part of the city's ongoing major roads program.

No specific Metroparks Toledo trail closures have been announced for today, which is a relief. The Beach Ridge Singletrack Trail at Oak Openings always benefits from a dry spell, but no specific updates were available. If you're headed to the Middlegrounds Dog Park, just remember it often closes after rain, but with "mainly clear" conditions, it should be good to go.

Overall, a mostly clear and mild day, but stay smart about the air and definitely check for event detours if you're heading south of the river.
