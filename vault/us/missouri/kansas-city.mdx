## The Rundown

Alright, KC, the weather app might be showing a decent 18°C (64.4°F) and "Mainly Clear" skies, which usually has us lacing up our shoes. But don't be fooled. That lovely sunshine is sitting on top of some seriously waterlogged ground. Seven inches of rain fell in parts of northwest Missouri and northeast Kansas earlier this week, and the National Weather Service issued flood watches for the wider region. Our local trails, especially the unpaved ones and anything near a creek or river, are going to be a disaster. Trying to run on muddy trails damages them and risks an ankle sprain you absolutely don't need. Just ask your PT – I've seen enough of those.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Flooding & Trail Closures are REAL.** The Indian Creek Trail is confirmed CLOSED from Holmes & 99th to Lydia for a water services project. Beyond that specific closure, many other trails are likely closed due to being saturated. Urban Trail Co. reports many area trails, including sections of Blue River Park, are closed due to being "pretty saturated" from recent rains. Turn around, don't drown, and don't bust your butt on slick mud.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. While the current AQI of 54 is technically "Moderate," meaning generally acceptable for most, the forecast for "Poor" air quality later today and for the next few days is a concern, particularly for sensitive groups. PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant points to potential smoke, a familiar adversary from distant wildfires. If you've got asthma, COPD, or just feel a bit scratchy, listen to your lungs.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Air Quality Advisory for Sensitive Groups.** Despite the current "Moderate" AQI reading, expect air quality to potentially worsen to "Poor." Individuals with heart or lung conditions, older adults, and children should limit prolonged outdoor exertion today. Symptoms like difficulty breathing or throat irritation mean it's time to head indoors.
</AlertBox>

## What to Do Instead

Given the soggy ground and iffy air, pivot. Hit the pavement on higher ground if you must run, but stay away from low-lying areas and trail systems. Construction projects are also ramping back up across the metro after the World Cup pause, so watch out for unexpected detours on city streets.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Consider the Treadmill or a Local Gym.** It's not glamorous, but your joints (and your clean shoes) will thank you. If you absolutely need to be outside, stick to known dry pavement, avoid anything resembling a dirt path, and be extra vigilant for construction zones.
</AlertBox>
