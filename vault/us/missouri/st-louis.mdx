## Why Your Lungs Might Be Grumbling (And Your Knees Might Be Too)

Look, 14.1°C (that's about 57 degrees Fahrenheit for those of us who still use real measurements) and mainly clear skies? That's prime running weather, no arguments there. Your joints will thank you for the mild temps. But let's get real about what's actually in that "clear" air and what's blocking your favorite routes.

The AQI pushing 92 with PM2.5? That's not great. PM2.5 is the tiny stuff that gets deep into your lungs. While it's not officially "unhealthy" for *everyone* until it breaks 100, anything in the 51-100 range means "moderate" pollution, and sensitive individuals should absolutely consider modifying their outdoor plans. Couple that with the moderate pollen, and you're essentially signing up for a double whammy on your respiratory system. If you start feeling any throat irritation or shortness of breath, cut your run short. This ain't worth a trip to the urgent care clinic.

### Trail Closures & Construction Headaches

Don't assume your usual route is clear sailing. We've got a smattering of closures to contend with:

<AlertBox type="danger">
The 5-mile trail at Cliff Cave Park is *still* closed due to flooding. Don't waste your gas driving out there expecting to hit those bluff views today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
Buder Park's lower section is partially closed because of water on the roadway, and Dr. Edmund A. Babler Park has rerouted its equestrian trail and has its paved bicycle trail closed due to hazardous surfaces. Don't get caught off guard.
</AlertBox>

On top of that, MoDOT is doing its usual dance across the interstates with various lane closures, some of which are ongoing throughout the day. While these are mostly vehicular, they can impact routes near highway access points for pedestrians and cyclists, so stay aware, especially around I-55, I-270, and I-64. And remember that never-ending construction on Forest Park Parkway for the Glade project means you'll still find some paths closed and detours around the West Pine and Faulkner Drive areas.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Heads up, Dog Owners (and Runners):** While not a "closure," it's always a good reminder that June is "National Dog Bite Awareness Campaign" month. Be mindful of unleashed dogs or aggressive pets, especially on shared paths. Always assume any dog can bite, no matter how friendly it looks.
</AlertBox>

So, yeah, it's a nice temperature out there. Just be smart, check local park websites *before* you leave the house, and maybe stick to an indoor workout or a shorter, less intense outdoor session if your lungs are feeling it.
