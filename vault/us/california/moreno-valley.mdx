## Why today is/isn't the day

<BulletList>
*   That early morning cool-off? A mirage. We're looking at temperatures soaring into the 90s Fahrenheit (32-37°C) today, possibly pushing closer to 98°F (37°C) by afternoon. Your joints, and frankly, your entire cardiovascular system, will thank you for sitting this one out, or at least moving your run to the literal crack of dawn.
*   Air quality is actually decent right now with an AQI of 47, so your lungs get a temporary pass. But with moderate pollen counts, your sinuses might not get the memo. So, while the PM2.5 isn't suffocating you today, don't be surprised if you're still sniffling through your strides.
*   Keep a vigilant eye on the horizon – this statewide heatwave isn't just uncomfortable, it's a giant red flag for wildfire risk. While there aren't any *active* major blazes directly impacting Moreno Valley's air quality *right this second*, the conditions are prime for fires to ignite and spread fast, as we've seen recently with the "Springs Fire" east of us. The Angeles National Forest even elevated its fire danger level yesterday. Smoke can roll in quicker than you can finish a mile, especially if winds pick up.

</BulletList>

## Local Conditions & Hazards

Today, the biggest enemy out on the trails, especially places like the multi-use paths near the Rancho Verde Staging Area or even along sections of the Aqueduct Bike Trail, is the sun. We're talking about direct, relentless sun with those high temperatures. Don't be fooled by the crisp morning air; that's not going to last.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Ahead!
Daytime temperatures are forecasted to hit the low to mid-90s°F (32-37°C), with some predictions nearing 98°F (37°C). This isn't just "warm," it's "pass out on the trail" hot. Heat illness is a serious risk, even for seasoned runners. Limit your time outdoors, especially between 10 AM and 4 PM. If you *must* go, slow your pace significantly.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Wildfire Watch
While our current AQI is good, the extreme heat across California significantly increases wildfire risk. Keep an eye on local news and look for any signs of smoke in the distance. The wind can shift, and what's clear now can become hazy and hazardous for your lungs in a hurry. Previous fires in Riverside County have shown us how quickly things can change.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration is Non-Negotiable
You think you're drinking enough? Double it. Seriously. With these temperatures, dehydration sets in fast. Carry more water than you think you need, especially if you're tackling any of the longer loops around the city.
</AlertBox>
