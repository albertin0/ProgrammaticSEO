### Extreme Heat Warning: Stay Indoors or Exercise with Extreme Caution
With the temperature hovering around 31°C (88°F), heat illness is a serious risk. This isn't your typical pleasant spring day; it's a lingering March scorcher that's been breaking records all week. Avoid peak sun hours (roughly 10 AM - 5 PM) entirely. If you absolutely must be outside, keep it short, keep it slow, and hydrate like your life depends on it – because it kind of does. Expect limited overnight cooling, meaning your body won't get a full break from the heat.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Pollen Alert
Expect some sneezing, sniffling, and possibly itchy eyes. If you have seasonal allergies, this will add to your discomfort. Consider an antihistamine if you're venturing out.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Trail Conditions & Construction
The AQI is good today at 25, so no smog to worry about, which is a small mercy in this heat. However, if you're headed to Sanborn County Park, know that trail construction is ongoing through the end of March, specifically impacting the Costanoan and Ohlone picnic areas. This might mean detours or closed sections. Plan your route carefully and check the Santa Clara County Parks website before you go. The Guadalupe River Trail also saw closures for levee repairs recently, but those were reportedly wrapped up by March 20th.
</AlertBox>

## Hydration is Not a Suggestion
Seriously, folks. Water, electrolytes, more water. Don't be that person collapsing on the Guadalupe River Trail thinking you can power through. This heat, especially coupled with any lingering pollen irritation, makes hydration non-negotiable. Listen to your body. If you feel lightheaded, nauseous, or like a rotisserie chicken, get inside and cool down immediately. Your PR can wait.
