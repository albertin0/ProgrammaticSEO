If you're heading out for a run or ride in San Jose today, you better adjust your expectations and your hydration strategy.

*   **That Heat Ain't Normal:** We're hitting unseasonably high temperatures for mid-March, with the mercury pushing into the low 30s Celsius (upper 80s Fahrenheit). This isn't your breezy spring weather; it's a taste of summer, and it demands respect.
*   **Pollen's Pestering:** Your eyes and nose might be running more than you are, with moderate pollen levels hanging in the air. If you usually suffer, today won't be a reprieve.
*   **Trail Trouble & Road Blocks:** Expect ongoing closures and detours. From permanent trail failures in Alum Rock Park to VTA construction messing with traffic flow on Capitol Expressway, you'll need to be flexible.

## Today's Vibe: Hot & Hazy (for your allergies, anyway)

Look, I'm all for getting after it, but today is not the day for heroics. That 31-degree Celsius heat (that's 88 degrees Fahrenheit for those still clinging to old school measurements) is no joke, especially when it's still officially spring. The National Weather Service is even talking about this "unusual early-season heat wave" possibly breaking records next week. So, consider today a warm-up for what's coming, literally.

Your lungs are probably fine from an air quality perspective – the AQI is sitting pretty at 25, which is excellent. No smoke, no smog choking your stride, thank goodness. But don't get too comfortable. That "Moderate" pollen count is like a tiny, invisible army, ready to ambush your sinuses the moment you step outside. If you're one of the unfortunate souls who gets hit hard by seasonal allergies, you're going to feel it.

## Mind Your Route, Seriously

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** With temperatures this high, especially for mid-March, heat exhaustion and heatstroke are real threats. Limit strenuous outdoor activity between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m. Hydrate *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. And for the love of all that is holy, don't leave kids or pets in a parked car. Not even for "just a minute."
</AlertBox>

Seriously, check your planned route before you head out. Don't just assume your favorite path is clear. Up in **Alum Rock Park**, several trails have been shut down for a while now due to everything from falling rocks to actual mountain lion sightings and trail erosion. The South Rim Trail, Sycamore Switchbacks, and portions of Penitencia Creek Trail are still impacted. So, if your usual loop involves those, pivot. Find an alternative or prepare to turn back. There's no glory in getting lost or injured on a closed trail.

And if you're commuting or planning a longer ride, remember the ongoing VTA Light Rail Extension construction on Capitol Expressway. There are still lane closures and detours to contend with, especially during off-peak and overnight hours. While not directly a "workout hazard" in the trail sense, it can certainly complicate your logistics and add stress to your movement around the city. Don't be that person holding up traffic because you didn't check the road closures.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Key:** Drink more water than you think you need. Consider electrolyte tabs or drinks if you're out for more than an hour in this heat. Light-colored, loose-fitting clothing is your friend.
</AlertBox>

Bottom line: Today is fine for a workout, but you absolutely have to be smarter about it. Embrace the early morning or late evening, pack more water than you think you'll need, and maybe bring some tissues for that pollen. Your joints will thank you for being smart, and your lungs, well, they'll just keep doing their job, mostly unbothered by anything but your own heavy breathing.
