## The Rundown

Alright, San Jose, let's talk brass tacks. If you insist on getting outside today – and I know some of you masochists will – you better be prepared. That 31.0°C is just the air temp, not how hot the asphalt feels on your feet or how quickly you'll be cooking on the Hellyer Park trails. Hydration isn't just a suggestion; it's the only way you'll make it back without passing out. Drink *before* you're thirsty, and for the love of all that is holy, skip the mid-day slog. The City of San Jose's own hot weather safety tips recommend avoiding physical activity between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m. Listen to them, or at least pretend to.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: This Ain't a Pool Day!
With temperatures pushing into the upper 80s (and that 31.0°C feeling even hotter in the direct sun), the risk of heat-related illness is HIGH. This isn't just about feeling a bit sweaty; we're talking heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and ruining your entire week. Seriously, scale back your intensity, shorten your duration, or better yet, take your workout indoors during peak sun hours. If you *must* be outside, seek shade often and wear light, loose clothing.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Troubles & Construction Chaos

Beyond the heat, don't forget our perpetually "under construction" city. The West Little Llagas Creek Trail in Morgan Hill is still shut down for creek work for the rest of the year. And if you were planning a long run through Anderson Lake County Park, forget it – much of that park is closed until 2032 for the dam retrofit. Always check county park websites before you head out, because nothing kills a good run faster than a "TRAIL CLOSED" sign after you've already committed to two miles in.

As for urban routes, construction on San Carlos Street for ADA improvements and traffic signals was still causing headaches as of early this year, with a "spring 2026" completion date. "Spring" can mean a lot of things to a construction crew in San Jose, so keep an eye out for uneven pavement and unexpected detours if you're in that area.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Smoke (Even with Good AQI)
While the overall Air Quality Index is a comfortable 25 (Good) and the dominant pollutant is not smoke, there's an active prescribed burn, RX - Coyote Creek Parkway, in Santa Clara County. Prescribed burns are controlled, but smoke plumes can drift. If you're sensitive, or if you notice any hazy conditions or the smell of smoke, adjust your route or move your workout indoors. Your lungs are not a filter.
</AlertBox>

### Events & Foot Traffic

It's World Cup season, which means FIFA World Cup 26™ festivities are happening throughout the Bay Area until July 1. San Jose is hosting watch parties, notably downtown at San Pedro Square Market. Expect more foot traffic and general hubbub in the downtown core. If you like navigating crowds on your run, go for it. If not, plan your routes to avoid the downtown area, especially around game times.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your PT Pro Tip: Electrolytes aren't just for Hangovers
Don't just chug water today. When you sweat this much, you lose electrolytes. Grab some Nuun, Liquid IV, or even just some good old pickle juice if you're feeling adventurous. Cramps, dizziness, and feeling generally like death are often signs you're low on more than just water. And seriously, check on your elderly neighbors – they're even more vulnerable to this heat than you are.
</AlertBox>
