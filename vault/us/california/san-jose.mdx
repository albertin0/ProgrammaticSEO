## Today's San Jose Rundown

Alright, runners, cyclists, and outdoor enthusiasts, let's talk turkey. The air quality index is a lovely 25 today, which is fantastic for your lungs on paper. You won't be choking on smog, which is a rare treat. However, that's where the good news for unbridled outdoor activity pretty much ends.

The dominant pollutant might be "Ozone/Other," but let's be real, the dominant *hazard* today is the sun trying to cook you alive. With the mercury pushing 31.0°C, your body is going to be working overtime just to keep cool. This isn't the day to push for a PR on the **Guadalupe River Trail** unless you're starting at dawn or waiting until dusk. Even then, hydrate like your life depends on it – because, in these temperatures, it kind of does.

Pollen is hanging out at a "Moderate" level. For some of us, that's just background noise. For others, it means a runny nose, itchy eyes, and generally feeling like you're running through a field of tiny, invisible attackers. If you're a sufferer, take your meds *before* you head out, not after you're already miserable.

### Know Your Local Roadblocks & Hazards

Beyond the atmospheric conditions, you've got to contend with the usual San Jose shenanigans.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning!
Seriously, this heat is no joke. Hydrate *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. Wear light, loose-fitting clothing. Schedule your runs for the early morning or late evening. If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or get a headache, STOP. Find shade, drink water, and cool down. Pushing through heat exhaustion is how you end up in the ER, or worse. Your PR can wait; your health can't.
</AlertBox>

Construction is a perennial joy in this city. While some of the recent levee repairs on the Guadalupe River south of Tasman Drive should be wrapped up by now, you can bet there's always *something* else happening. We've got long-term closures at **Anderson Lake County Park** for the seismic retrofit project, effectively shutting down significant sections like the Serpentine Trail and Lakeview Trail until 2032. So, if that's your usual haunt, find a new one, because that dam isn't getting fixed overnight.

And don't forget the **Alviso Marina County Park**. If you're planning on hitting the Mallard Slough Trail or other eastern sections, be prepared for intermittent closures due to active hauling and construction during weekdays. A quick check of the Santa Clara County Parks website before you head out is always a good idea, or you'll be treated to a surprise detour.
