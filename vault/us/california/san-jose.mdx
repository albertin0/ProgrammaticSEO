Forget a refreshing spring run. Today in San Jose is less "spring" and more "summer's angry cousin."

*   **Heat Dome Wannabe:** At a scorching 31°C (that's 88°F for those still in Fahrenheit land), you'll be sweating through your eyeballs before you hit mile one. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely risky. Your body struggles to cool itself, and dehydration is a real, fast-approaching threat.
*   **Pollen's Mild Annoyance:** Pollen levels are moderate, which means your sniffles might be minor, but combined with the heat, it's just another layer of "ugh" for your respiratory system. Don't expect to set any PRs today if you're sensitive.
*   **Ongoing Trail Annoyances:** While there aren't any *new* massive fire-related closures in the last 24 hours, remember the usual suspects. Always check before you go, because nothing says "fun" like a surprise detour.

Look, the Air Quality Index is a pristine 50 today, and the skies are mainly clear, which is usually fantastic. But that temperature? It's a killer. If you absolutely *must* get your miles in, stick to early morning or late evening. And I mean *early* and *late*. Noon is for lizards, not runners.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning!** 31°C (88°F) is no joke. Running in this kind of heat significantly increases your risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Your internal thermostat can't keep up. Slow your pace dramatically, take frequent breaks in the shade, and hydrate constantly. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get inside immediately and cool down. This isn't about being tough; it's about being smart.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, don't be that person who ends up on the side of the Los Gatos Creek Trail, bright red and gasping, because you thought you could power through. It's not impressive; it's just dumb. There's no medal for heatstroke.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Moderate Pollen Alert:** For those with allergies, "moderate" pollen might still mean some sniffles, sneezing, or itchy eyes. Combine that with the effort of running in the heat, and your lungs might feel a bit more strained than usual. Consider an antihistamine before you head out, and maybe stick to paved paths if tree pollen is your nemesis.
</AlertBox>

Also, just a friendly reminder, our parks technically close a half-hour past sunset until just before sunrise. While some trail entrances might be ungated, parking lots do get locked. Don't get trapped at Guadalupe Oak Grove Park when you're just trying to cool down after that ill-advised evening sprint. Rangers *will* ticket you.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is NOT Optional:** This isn't a suggestion; it's a command. Drink water *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. And I don't mean a tiny sip from a fountain every couple of miles. We're talking serious, dedicated hydration. Electrolytes wouldn't hurt either.
</AlertBox>

Honestly, today might be a good day for a nice, air-conditioned gym, or maybe just a cold brew on the patio. Your body will thank you.
