## The Details: San Jose Weather & Workout Woes

Alright, San Jose, let's cut to the chase. The weather gods decided to fast-forward straight to summer, skipping spring entirely. We're looking at a scorching 31 degrees Celsius (that's pushing 88 degrees Fahrenheit for us old-school folks) and clear skies. While "clear" sounds nice, it means direct sun beating down on you, and that's no joke when temperatures are this far above average for March.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning: Don't Be a Hero**
Listen up: this isn't just "warm," it's dangerously hot, especially for this time of year when your body hasn't acclimated yet. The National Weather Service is even talking about "unprecedented March heat." If you insist on getting out there, keep it short, stay in the shade, and hydrate like your life depends on it – because frankly, it kind of does. Heat stress increases with every day of this nonsense, affecting *everyone*, not just the sensitive types. Early morning or late evening is your only real window.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk & Construction Chaos

Beyond the heat, our local trails have a few ongoing sagas. If you were planning a long trek out at **Anderson Lake County Park**, remember that significant portions are still closed off until *2032* for the dam seismic retrofit. Seriously, that project feels like it's been going on since my first marathon. Don't try to sneak in there; you'll just be wasting your time and potentially getting a citation.

Down in downtown, **St. James Park** is also seeing some changes. There's a big construction project advancing for a new music pavilion, which might impact those historic walking paths. So, while the park will eventually be spruced up, expect some detours and dusty conditions if that's your usual loop.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Local Trail Alert: Anderson Lake Park Still a No-Go**
Avoid Anderson Lake County Park entirely if you're looking for a serious workout. The extensive, long-term closures for the dam retrofit mean many areas are inaccessible. Save yourself the trip and find an open trail.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip: Stay Hydrated, Seriously.**
With these temperatures, your sweat rate is going to be through the roof. Carry more water than you think you need. Electrolytes are your friend. And if you start feeling dizzy, cramping, or just generally crappy, stop. Immediately. It's not worth passing out on a trail and waiting for someone to find you.
</AlertBox>
