## The Heat is On, San Jose

Let's cut right to it: The mercury's climbing to a balmy 31.0°C (87.8°F) today. "Mainly Clear" sounds lovely on paper, but when that sun beats down on the pavement of the Los Gatos Creek Trail or any open stretch, it's going to feel significantly hotter. The City of San Jose has even put out warnings about heat safety, advising folks to limit strenuous activity during the hottest parts of the day. While we might not have an *official* "extreme heat advisory" plastered everywhere for *this exact moment*, 88 degrees is no joke for an outdoor workout, especially as the day wears on and the ground radiates that stored heat. Historically, temperatures exceeding 90°F are considered physically hazardous, and we're dancing close to that line.

### DANGER: Mid-Day Heat is No Joke
With temperatures hitting 31.0°C (87.8°F), heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real risks. This isn't just about discomfort; it can be seriously dangerous. The City of San Jose advises avoiding strenuous activity during the hottest part of the day, typically 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.. If you're out there, bring more water than you think you need, wear light-colored, breathable clothing, and don't be a hero. Cut your run short if you start feeling lightheaded or overly fatigued.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### CAUTION: Guadalupe River Trail Closure
Heads up, Northside runners! The Guadalupe River Trail, specifically the stretch from Trimble Road to the Airport Green Parking Lot, is undergoing sediment removal. This means that section is CLOSED Monday through Friday from 7:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. until September 25, 2026. Don't plan your lunch run through there unless you fancy a detour or a shouting match with a construction worker. Plan your routes accordingly, or hit up another trail like the Coyote Creek Parkway for uninterrupted miles.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Pro Tip: Hydrate, Then Hydrate Some More
Seriously, if you're heading out, even early morning or late evening, pre-hydrate. Start drinking water an hour before you go. Don't rely on getting enough during your activity, especially in this heat. And for longer efforts, consider an electrolyte drink. Water fountains aren't always working, and you don't want to get caught parched.
</AlertBox>
