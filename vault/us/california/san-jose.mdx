## The Heat Is Real, San Jose

Let's be blunt: 31 degrees Celsius in March is not normal, and it's certainly not ideal for a long run or intense workout. While the official heat advisory for the Bay Area may have technically expired early this morning (March 21st) after days of record-shattering temperatures, the heat itself hasn't magically disappeared. We've seen predictions of San Jose hitting 90 degrees Fahrenheit (32°C) for the first time ever in March this week, and your body doesn't care if the advisory is still active or not – 88°F will still lay you out if you're not careful.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** With temperatures hovering around 31.0°C (88°F), heat exhaustion and heatstroke are serious risks. Limit outdoor exposure to early mornings or late evenings, and keep your workouts short and low-intensity. Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate, and then hydrate some more.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk & Construction Woes

If you absolutely *must* get out, be aware of what's happening on the ground. Many areas of **Anderson Lake County Park** remain closed due to the ongoing Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project, with some sections like the Serpentine Trail and boating areas shut down until approximately 2032. So, don't plan on that loop.

Even at popular spots like Máyyan 'Ooyákma – Coyote Ridge Open Space Preserve, weekend hikes require pre-booking through May. The Habitat Protection Area trails (Serpentine Spring, Tule Elk, Bay Checkerspot) are also closed Mondays and Tuesdays for research, though that's not today. Always double-check park websites before you head out, because getting there and finding a "CLOSED" sign is never fun, especially when it's this hot.
