#### EXTREME HEAT HAZARD: 31.0°C (87.8°F)
Seriously, folks, this is not the day for your PR attempts. High temperatures like this dramatically increase your risk of heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and severe dehydration. Avoid exercising between 10 AM and 3 PM. If you *must* be outside, keep it short, stay to the shade, and drink more water than you think you need. Your body's cooling mechanisms are going to be struggling. Even just walking around downtown will feel like a chore.
</AlertBox>

## Construction: The Never-Ending Story

Our fair city is constantly under construction, and today is no exception. Downtown remains a mess, with multiple projects closing lanes and sidewalks, particularly around Market Street and Park Avenue. If your usual route takes you through the heart of it all, plan for detours and watch out for uneven surfaces, heavy equipment, and general chaos.

And for those of you who venture further afield, don't forget the intermittent closures on the Alviso Slough Trail due to ongoing construction for the South San Francisco Bay Shoreline Project. Even Anderson Lake County Park has major areas closed until *2032* for the dam retrofit. Always check county parks alerts before you lace up those shoes expecting a clear path.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### BAY SHORELINE FLOOD ADVISORY
If your workout takes you close to the bay, be advised that a coastal flood advisory is in effect through Saturday due to abnormally high tides. San Jose proper might be inland, but low-lying areas near the shoreline could see minor flooding on paths and parking areas. The Alviso Slough Trail is one place where you might encounter standing water. Don't try to power through flooded sections; you don't know what debris is lurking underneath.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Pro Tips for a Challenging Day
Since the sun's beating down like it's mid-summer, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing. Electrolytes are your friend, not just plain water, if you're out for any length of time. And if you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or get a headache, your body is telling you to stop. Listen to it. Seriously, I've seen enough heat-related injuries to fill a small emergency room. Don't be that person.
</AlertBox>
