Forget about those "perfect running weather" fantasies you had; this is June in San Jose, and the heatwave is making an early, aggressive appearance. We're looking at temperatures that can genuinely put you at risk if you're not smart about it. Expect highs in the upper 80s to low 90s, with some inland spots possibly hitting even higher.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Advisory
A significant early-summer heatwave is impacting California, with temperatures pushing into the upper 90s in some areas. This isn't just "uncomfortable" heat; it's the kind that can lead to heat exhaustion or heat stroke rapidly, especially if you're exercising. Reschedule strenuous activities for early morning or late evening.
</AlertBox>

Your lungs, bless 'em, are getting a bit of a break today. The Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at 25, which is firmly in the "Good" category, with PM2.5 levels being very low. So, you'll be breathing some decent air, even if it feels like someone left the oven door open. Pollen is only moderate, so most folks won't be sniffling their way through their miles.

However, the heat advisory is not to be trifled with. Hydration isn't just a suggestion; it's a lifeline. If you absolutely *must* run that loop around the Guadalupe River Trail, start before the sun decides to wage war, and carry more water than you think you need. Think of it this way: your body's a finely tuned machine, and right now, the cooling system is going to be working overtime. Don't push it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Increased Fire Risk
With the dry conditions and rising temperatures, the risk of brush fires is elevated. Be extra vigilant on trails, especially those near dry vegetation. High fire risk is noted for today and the weekend in San Jose.
</AlertBox>

If you’re thinking about hitting some of the bigger county parks, remember that some long-term construction projects are still ongoing. For instance, the West Little Llagas Creek trail in Morgan Hill is closed in sections for dam work through the end of the year, so plan your routes accordingly if you're heading south. As of now, specific city-wide trail closures for today haven't been announced beyond general maintenance listings from months ago, but always double-check the City of San Jose Parks website before you head out.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Beat the Heat, Not Yourself
If you insist on an outdoor session, aim for before 8 AM or after 7 PM. Wear light-colored, loose-fitting, moisture-wicking gear. Know the signs of heat illness: dizziness, nausea, confusion, rapid pulse. If you feel any of these, stop immediately, get to shade, cool down, and hydrate. San Jose has cooling centers available; check the city's resources if you need one.
</AlertBox>
