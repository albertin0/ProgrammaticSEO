## Don't Be a Hero, It's Hot Out There

Seriously, 88 degrees isn't a joke, especially when you're pushing yourself. We're not talking about some mild spring day here, this is mid-summer temps creeping in early. Your body is going to be working overtime just to keep cool. That means a higher heart rate at a lower perceived effort, and a faster track to dehydration. If you're planning a long haul on the **Guadalupe River Trail**, for instance, you better be starting at dawn or waiting until dusk, and carrying enough water to swim home in.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Extreme Heat Risk
While there's no official "Excessive Heat Warning" in effect for San Jose today, 88°F (31°C) with mainly clear skies is hot enough to cause serious problems for anyone exercising strenuously, especially during peak sun hours. Stick to the shade or take it inside.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Closures, Construction, and Community Chaos

Beyond the heat, there are a few local quirks to consider if you're hitting the trails. Santa Clara County Parks has several ongoing situations that might mess with your usual routes.

For starters, much of **Anderson Lake County Park** is still locked down until around 2032 due to the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. That's a long-term commitment, folks. Don't plan your next ultra-marathon through there anytime soon.

Over at **Alviso Marina County Park**, expect intermittent closures on the Mallard Slough Trail and eastern sections of the Alviso Slough Trail. They're doing some hauling and construction, so your scenic wetland run might be rerouted through a construction zone. Plan ahead or find an alternate route.

And then there's the ongoing drama around the **Coyote Creek Trestle** near Kelley Park. The city is still waffling over whether to demolish it for the Five Wounds Trail expansion, or try to preserve the century-old structure. Officials have already warned that keeping it could lead to future trail closures if the thing decides to fall apart or catch fire again. So, if your route usually involves that piece of history, just know its future, and potentially your future runs, are TBD.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Hydrated, Smarty Pants
It's not just about carrying water; it's about drinking *before* you're thirsty. Electrolyte drinks aren't a bad idea either. And please, wear light-colored, breathable clothing. No one needs to see you looking like a sweaty beet at mile 3.
</AlertBox>

## Other Nuisances & Not-So-Fun Facts

Fire risk for today is moderate, so no immediate smoke advisories. However, we've had our share of vegetation fires recently, so always be aware of your surroundings and report anything suspicious.
