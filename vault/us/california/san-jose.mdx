## The Rundown: Clear Skies, Moderate Air, and Trail Troubles

Look, we've got a gift today with clear skies and a comfortable 9.1°C to kick off your Sunday. No crazy heat, no unexpected downpours. That's a solid start for hitting the trails or getting some miles in on the streets. Your joints will thank you for the crisp air and lack of slipping hazards.

However, let's not get ahead of ourselves. While the weather's playing nice, the air quality is still hovering in that "moderate" category, with PM2.5 as the main culprit. What does "moderate" mean for you? If you're generally healthy, you're probably fine for most activities, but "sensitive groups" – you know who you are, the folks who start coughing just looking at a dusty road – might experience some minor irritation, especially with prolonged exertion. AccuWeather even called today "Poor" for sensitive groups, so keep that in mind. Maybe don't try for that PR on the Los Gatos Creek Trail if your throat feels scratchy.

With AQI in the moderate range and PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant, if you have asthma, other respiratory issues, or are unusually sensitive, consider reducing prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. Pay attention to how you feel.

Now, about those trails. While San Jose boasts an impressive network, you can't just assume smooth sailing everywhere. Construction and maintenance are ongoing, because, well, things break and need fixing. If you're planning on exploring the Midpeninsula Regional Open Space District, specifically around Bear Creek Redwoods Preserve, be aware that several trails like Toad Hollow and Myotis are currently CLOSED due to construction. Don't be that person ignoring the signs; it's for your safety and the crew's.

Further south, if Anderson Lake County Park is your usual haunt, remember that large sections are pretty much off-limits until 2032 because of the ongoing dam retrofit project. And even down by the Alviso Slough Trail, you might run into intermittent closures for hauling and construction. Always, *always* check the park or trail website before you lace up, or you might find yourself doing an impromptu U-turn a mile in.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip: Check Before You Go!**
Seriously, don't just wing it. Check the Santa Clara County Parks website or the specific preserve's page for the latest on trail closures and conditions. A few minutes online can save you a wasted trip and a whole lot of frustration.
</AlertBox>

So, enjoy the crisp air and clear skies, San Jose. Just be smart about where you go and how hard you push it today.
