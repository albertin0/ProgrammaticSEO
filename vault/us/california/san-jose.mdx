## Today's Outdoor Grind: Hydrate or Die (Figuratively)

Look, I get it. The sun's out, it's "Mainly Clear," and you want to get your miles in. But let's not pretend this is ideal running weather. That 31-degree Celsius heat is no joke, even early in the morning or later in the evening. Your body's going to struggle to regulate its temperature, and heat stress is a real, fast-approaching danger.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Heat Exhaustion and Stroke Are Real
Running in nearly 90-degree Fahrenheit weather is asking for trouble. Heat exhaustion can sneak up on you, and a full-blown heat stroke is a medical emergency. Don't be a hero; nobody's impressed by a runner passed out on the Guadalupe River Trail.
</AlertBox>

### Air Quality & Allergy Woes

Our AQI of 55 is technically "Moderate," meaning it's generally acceptable for most people. However, if you're part of the "sensitive groups" club – that includes asthmatics, those with respiratory issues, or even just folks prone to seasonal allergies – today's moderate pollen count combined with the lingering smoke particles could make your workout a lot less enjoyable. Keep an eye on how you feel. If you're wheezing more than usual or your throat feels scratchy, cut it short.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Check Your Trail Before You Go
While there aren't many *new* specific trail closures reported in the last 24 hours that would directly impact your run today, long-term projects like the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project continue to keep parts of Anderson Lake County Park shut down until 2032. Always double-check county and city park websites before you head out, especially if you're venturing somewhere new. The City of San Jose's trail network is extensive, but parts are always under maintenance or development.
</AlertBox>

## My Two Cents: Get It Done Early (or Indoors)

If you absolutely *must* run outside today, aim for sunrise. Otherwise, hit the gym, jump in a pool, or find some air conditioning. This isn't the day to push your limits or try for a PR. Listen to your body, stay ridiculously hydrated, and don't ignore any warning signs. Your health is more important than that Strava segment.
