## Today's Outdoor Rundown: Sweat it Out, But Smart

Look, I get it. The sun is shining, the sky's that quintessential blue, and you're itching to hit those trails. But let's be real, 88 degrees with no cloud cover isn't for setting any personal bests, unless that PB is "most sweat produced." While your lungs are getting a gold-star day with that "Good" AQI, your joints (and every other part of your body) are going to feel that heat if you're not careful.

We had heat advisories slapping parts of the Santa Clara Valley with warnings of mid-90s to low-100s earlier in the week, and while today isn't *quite* that brutal, it's still plenty hot to cause trouble. Forget those lunchtime runs on the Coyote Creek Trail unless you want to spend the afternoon regretting it. This is a morning or late-evening workout kind of day.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Heatstroke Is No Joke.**
It's 88°F and mainly clear. That's prime for heat exhaustion or worse. Drink more water than you think you need. Seriously. If you're not peeing clear, you're not drinking enough. Limit strenuous activity between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. If you feel lightheaded, nauseous, or get a pounding headache, *stop*. Find shade, hydrate, and cool down. Don't be a hero; nobody wants to call the paramedics for your ego.
</AlertBox>

Pollen counts are at a "Moderate" level. For most of us, that's manageable, but if you're one of the sniffly ones, maybe pop an antihistamine before you head out.

### Construction & Trail Etiquette

Speaking of trails, don't wander blindly. Our friends at Valley Water are busy with their Stream Maintenance Program. This means you might run into crews on the Guadalupe River Trail, for example, doing vegetation removal. They'll have flaggers, so follow their directions. This isn't some obstacle course for your enjoyment; it's active work to keep our creeks flowing and reduce fire risk. Don't go trying to sneak through closed sections – those areas are closed for a reason and could be genuinely unsafe.

Also, a heads-up for anyone planning a weekend long run: the Almaden Hills Trail Run Summer Edition is coming up on August 15th at Almaden Quicksilver County Park. Expect elevated foot traffic and pre-dawn parking activity around the Mockingbird lot. If you prefer solitude, maybe hit a different park this weekend.
