## The Air: Don't Breathe Too Deep

Look, I'm all for pushing limits, but not when the air itself is actively fighting your lungs. We've got an AQI of 73 right now, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. However, the real kicker is the ozone. The Air District has slapped us with a Spare the Air Alert because ozone concentrations are forecast to be unhealthy, especially as the day heats up. This isn't just a mild inconvenience; it can cause throat irritation, chest pain, and really mess with anyone who has asthma or other respiratory issues. If you *must* get out, do it before the sun really gets going, like, before 8 AM.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Spare the Air Alert in Effect!** Outdoor exercise should be limited to early morning hours to avoid peak ozone levels. If you've got respiratory issues, or you're a little person or an old person, stay inside. Seriously.
</AlertBox>

## The Heat: Sweat Smarter, Not Harder

It's clear (literally) but it's going to get hot. We're talking high 80s to low 90s. This isn't summer anymore, but the heat advisory is a clear warning that you need to take this seriously. Hydration is key, obviously, but also consider wearing loose, light-colored clothing. That extra mile isn't worth passing out on a street corner near Santa Clara University.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heat Advisory!** High temperatures mean a higher risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Limit strenuous activities to the cooler parts of the day. Carry water, lots of it.
</AlertBox>

## Your Route: Detours and Obstacles

If you're a fan of the Guadalupe River (East) Trail, be aware that from Trimble Road to Airport Parkway, it's closed Monday through Friday from 7:30 AM to 3:00 PM for sediment removal. It reopens after work hours and on weekends, but your lunchtime run is out of luck. There are also ongoing trail closures on the Adobe Creek Loop Trail over in Palo Alto due to flood protection construction, running until the end of October. Always check local park and trail websites before you head out, because nobody likes a surprise dead end or construction detour.

</AlertBox>
