## The Rundown for Your Run

Look, I get it. The sun's out, the sky's mostly clear, and the couch is judging you. But let's be smart about this, especially with the mercury climbing. That 31°C isn't playing around. While the Bay Area might not be under an official "Excessive Heat Warning" right this second, temperatures are on an upward trend for the week. The City of San José itself advises you to limit physical activity during the hottest part of the day, typically between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m.. If you're out there pushing it, your heart rate will be higher, you'll sweat more, and your body will be working overtime just to keep cool. This isn't just about comfort; it's about avoiding heat exhaustion or, worse, heat stroke. As a PT, I've seen enough dehydrated messes to tell you it's not a pretty sight, and recovery takes longer than that PR you're chasing.

### Trail Conditions & Local Gripes

The Air Quality Index is a pristine 25, which means whatever’s burning down south with the Border 6 Fire isn't messing with our air today. Breathe easy on that front. However, if you're planning a long ride or run around Alviso Marina County Park, be aware there have been intermittent closures on parts of the Mallard Slough Trail due to hauling and construction. It's not a full shutdown, but hitting a blocked section when you're in the zone is annoying, and frankly, a safety hazard if you're forced onto less-safe detours. The Guadalupe River Trail, another local favorite, is known for seasonal flooding at low points, but with the current dry conditions, that's thankfully not an issue today.

Your joints, though, are going to feel that heat if you don't take it easy. Running on hot asphalt or concrete radiates heat back at you, making everything feel tougher. Consider hitting some of the shadier trails if you can find them, like those in parts of Almaden Quicksilver County Park (though the Mt Umunhum Ultras event was just there yesterday, so watch out for lingering race debris).

<AlertBox type="info">
### Listen to Your Body, Not Your Watch (Always)
Today, more than ever, prioritize how you feel. If you're dizzy, nauseous, or cramping up, stop. Get into the shade, rehydrate, and cool down. A missed workout is better than a medical emergency. Seriously.
</AlertBox>
