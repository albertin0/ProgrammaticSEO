## Hit the Road Early (or Late)

Look, if you're hitting the pavement or trails today, Tuesday, June 2nd, you'd better be doing it before 9 AM or after 6 PM. We're looking at highs flirting with the 90-degree mark in parts of San Jose, officially hitting the upper 80s, possibly even 94°F according to some forecasts. That's prime heatstroke territory if you're not careful. The City itself warns against midday physical activity, and honestly, so do I. It's not worth passing out on a run.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Dangerously High Temperatures!**
San Jose is heating up. With temperatures in the high 80s to low 90s today, stay hydrated and consider moving your workout to the cooler parts of the day. Avoid strenuous activity between 10 a.m. and 3 p.m. to prevent heat-related illness.
</AlertBox>

## Air Quality & Allergies: Trust Your Gut

Air quality-wise, the official word for San Jose is an AQI of 25, which is firmly in the "Good" category. However, AccuWeather's sitting there with a "Poor" rating for sensitive groups, mentioning PM2.5 at 49 and Ozone at 38. My take? If you've got asthma, allergies, or just generally feel cruddy after a dusty run, listen to your body more than the number on a screen. With moderate pollen in the mix, it's not a free pass for everyone. The dominant pollutant is Ozone/Other, which can also irritate lungs.

## Trail Closures: The Usual Suspects and New Headaches

Now, onto the perpetual pain points: our trails. If you had plans to hit up the **Anderson Lake County Park**, pack it in. Large sections of that park are locked down until *2032* due to the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. Seriously, 2032. Plan a different route, maybe something closer to town that's actually accessible.

And speaking of planning different routes, the ongoing saga of the **Guadalupe River Trail** continues. While no *new* flooding is reported, cyclists who rely on Ewert Road as a bypass for flooded sections near Highway 101 are out of luck. That detour was slated for permanent closure last month (May 1, 2026), making navigation around any future Guadalupe River Trail water crossings a much bigger headache. It's frustrating when what seems like a small road closure can throw off so many commuter and recreational routes.

Further south, if your routes extend that far, the West Little Llagas Creek trail in Morgan Hill is closed from May 2026 through the end of the year for creek widening and deepening. More detours, more frustration for our regional trail users.

Also, keep an eye out for random street repaving projects around town. San Jose is on a mission to pave 205 miles of streets this year, which means unexpected lane closures and temporary parking restrictions could pop up on your regular running loops. Stay alert, don't assume your usual path is clear.
