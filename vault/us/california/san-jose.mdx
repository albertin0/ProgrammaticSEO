Another "spring" day in San Jose that feels suspiciously like August. If you're hitting the pavement, better make it early, or just stay home.

<LungsJointsScore score={4} />

<BulletList>
*   **April Heatwave:** We're clocking in at 31°C (87.8°F) today. That's not "pleasant spring warmth"; that's "hydration is mandatory or you'll be seeing spots" heat. Don't underestimate it.
*   **Pollen's Mild Annoyance:** Pollen levels are moderate. If you're an allergy sufferer, expect some sniffles, but it shouldn't be a showstopper.
*   **Surprisingly Clean Air:** On the upside, our AQI is a respectable 25. So while your body might be cooking, at least your lungs won't be dealing with smog on top of it.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. You want to get your miles in. But today is not the day to push for a PR unless you're a lizard. The heat is the real story here. We just had a heat advisory last month where temperatures hit the low 90s, and we're damn near there again today. Your body doesn't care that it's "only April 8th." It feels 87.8 degrees Fahrenheit, and it's going to demand respect.

Consider your route. Popular trails like the Los Gatos Creek Trail, while beautiful, have sections with minimal shade. If you're out there between 10 AM and 4 PM, you're essentially baking yourself. Plan your run or ride for pre-9 AM or post-5 PM if you absolutely must.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning
With temperatures hitting 31.0°C (87.8°F), the risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke is significantly elevated. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; it's about potentially serious health issues. Slow down, or better yet, take your workout indoors today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Hydrated, Smart Runner
This isn't just about carrying a water bottle; it's about pre-hydrating, hydrating during, and re-hydrating afterward. Electrolytes are your friend. If you're feeling dizzy, lightheaded, or nauseous, STOP. Now. No mile is worth passing out for. Also, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing. Nobody needs to see you in a black long-sleeve today.
</AlertBox>

As for other hazards, the good news is we're not dealing with any major trail flooding right now. Trail conditions for the Guadalupe River, Coyote Creek, and Los Gatos Creek were all reported as below flood level a few days ago. There's also no active construction blocking main routes that just started in the last 24 hours. There's some ongoing expansion planning for the Guadalupe River Trail, but actual construction isn't slated to start until next year. Some Open Space Authority preserves like Coyote Ridge still require pre-booking for weekend hikes and have one-way bike rules on the Arrowhead Trail, but those are ongoing regulations, not sudden closures. No wildfire smoke is impacting our air quality either, despite a small fire active in Calaveras county.

So, it all comes back to the heat. Play it smart.
