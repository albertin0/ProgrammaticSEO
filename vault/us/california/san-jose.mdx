Another day, another dose of San Jose sunshine, which means another day you gotta actually *think* before you lace up those Hokas.

<BulletList>
*   **Heat is No Joke:** At 31&deg;C (that's pushing 88&deg;F for you old-schoolers) and an extreme UV index of 11, the midday sun out here today is actively trying to melt your brain. Don't be a hero.
*   **Shoreline Soggy:** Heads up if your usual route takes you anywhere near the Bay. A Coastal Flood Advisory is in effect from 4 PM today through next Thursday for shoreline areas, meaning some low-lying spots could see minor flooding.
*   **Trail Blockages Remain:** Some of our favorite spots, like Alum Rock Park, still have sections closed from previous storm damage, so double-check before you go thinking you'll hit that specific loop.
</BulletList>

Look, the air quality is pretty damn good today, so your lungs will thank you. But your joints and everything else will be screaming if you tackle a long run in this heat without a plan. That 31&deg;C isn't just a number; it's a warning from the universe that your body is going to work overtime to keep cool. Hydrate early, hydrate often, and then hydrate some more. We're talking serious water intake, not just a sip from your bottle halfway through.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat & UV Risk:** With temperatures nudging into the high 80s to low 90s, especially in the Santa Clara Valley and Eastern Hills, and an extreme UV index, heat exhaustion and sunburn are real threats. Schedule strenuous activities for early morning or late evening. Wear sunscreen, a hat, and light, breathable clothing. If you feel dizzy or overly fatigued, stop immediately. Cal/OSHA is even reminding employers about heat illness prevention for outdoor workers, so you should be paying attention too.
</AlertBox>

Speaking of trails, if you're planning on hitting Alum Rock Park, be aware that the Penitencia Creek Trail is currently closed at the Trestle Bridge. You'll need to use the park road as a detour. The Mineral Springs Trail is also closed due to erosion. These aren't new closures, but they're still in effect, and you don't want to show up only to be rerouted or worse, try to bypass a legitimate hazard. Also, keep an eye on road closures for ongoing construction around the city, like the various lane closures on I-280 or I-680 – they might affect your commute to a trailhead.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Coastal Area Caution:** The Coastal Flood Advisory for our Bay shoreline areas, starting this afternoon, means you might encounter some standing water on trails or roads closer to the Bay. While the Guadalupe River and Coyote Creek trail water levels are currently below flood, localized impacts are possible. Don't be that person who wades through floodwater. Turn around, find an alternate route.
</AlertBox>
