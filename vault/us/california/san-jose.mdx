## Why today is *not* the day for heroics

<BulletList>
*   **The Heat is ON (and it's early):** We're staring down 31°C (a balmy 88°F) and it’s only March. This isn't your typical spring warmth; news outlets were already calling for an "unprecedented heat wave" and "record hot temperatures" for the Bay Area this month, with San Jose hitting the low to mid-90s. It seems that 'unprecedented' is now just 'Tuesday.' This kind of early-season bake-off will absolutely wreck your pacing and put serious strain on your joints and cardiovascular system.
*   **Pollen's Present, Air's Okay:** Pollen is sitting at a "Moderate" level, so if you're one of the sniffly ones, you might notice it. Good news for your actual lungs, though: the Air Quality Index is a pristine 25, meaning your air filter is working overtime and you can breathe easy (if you ignore the pollen, that is).
*   **Construction Continues to Cause Chaos:** Those ambitious downtown bikeway upgrades? Still chugging along. The San Fernando Better Bikeway Project, for example, kicked off this month and will run through next year, so expect detours and disruptions if you're trying to roll through there. And don't even get me started on the ongoing trail closures at parks like Sanborn County Park for "significant trail construction and paving improvements" or the perpetual "Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project" at Anderson Lake County Park, which has large sections closed until 2032.
</BulletList>

### Local Lowdown

Seriously, people, this isn't the weather for pushing intervals on the Guadalupe River Trail around midday. That concrete reflects heat like a pizza oven. Save your speed work for the evening or, better yet, for a day when the air isn't trying to slow-cook you. If you absolutely *must* get out there, be smart.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Hazard!** Today's high temperatures are dangerous. Forecasters warned of "moderate risk of heat-related illness" in our area this week. Avoid strenuous activity between 10 AM and 5 PM. If you're out, hydrate constantly, wear light-colored, loose clothing, and listen to your body. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real, and frankly, not worth a few extra miles. Don't be that person getting carted off the trail.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Zone Ahead:** Whether you're navigating the revamped streets downtown or trying to find a clear path in Sanborn County Park, stay alert for construction equipment, uneven surfaces, and unexpected closures. Always respect signage and detours; those crews are focused, and you don't want to become part of the new infrastructure.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pollen Prep:** With moderate pollen levels, if you're susceptible, consider an antihistamine before you head out. When you get back, shower immediately and change out of your workout clothes to wash off any lingering allergens and prevent bringing them into your home.
</AlertBox>

This isn't a day to bag a Strava segment. It's a day to stay safe, stay cool, and maybe hit the gym's air-conditioned treadmill. Your lungs might be happy with the AQI, but your whole system will be screaming in this heat.
