### The Run Down on San Jose Hazards

Look, let's be real. It's July in San Jose, and the weather's decided to remind us that even if we're not Phoenix, 31°C is plenty hot enough to make you question your life choices mid-run. We've got clear skies, which usually means great visibility, but today it just means the sun's got a direct line to bake your brain. The heat index feeling like 85°F is a solid warning sign for anyone planning more than a quick dog walk. Hydration isn't a suggestion; it's practically a religion right now if you want to avoid ending up a statistic on a Cal/OSHA heat illness report.

On the bright side, your lungs are getting a reprieve with an AQI of 25. That's "Good" territory, meaning any existing crud from distant Canadian wildfires (which were sending smoke our way last week) has mostly cleared out. So, if you manage to avoid heatstroke, at least you'll be breathing easy. Just watch out for that moderate pollen if you're sensitive.

Now for the fun part: trail headaches. If your usual route takes you through the Coyote Valley Open Space Preserve, remember that their equestrian lot is still a no-go zone thanks to ongoing construction. And since today is Tuesday, the Habitat Protection Area, including the Serpentine Spring, Tule Elk, and Bay Checkerspot trails, are shut down for research. So much for that scenic Tuesday morning escape. Plan your routes accordingly, or you'll be staring at a "Trail Closed" sign and wasting precious running time. Anderson Lake County Park also has vast areas closed for the seismic retrofit project until, get this, *2032*. Yeah, don't hold your breath for that one to reopen soon.
