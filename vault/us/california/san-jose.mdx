This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; we're talking about real risks for heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Remember that Bay Area heat advisory from a couple of weeks back, where San Jose was flirting with 103 degrees? Today isn't quite that bad, but it's still warm enough to be smart.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Midday Melt!
With temperatures pushing 31.0°C (87.8°F), any strenuous outdoor activity between 10 AM and 5 PM is asking for trouble. Stick to early mornings or late evenings if you're planning on pushing it. Hydrate *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. And for the love of all that is holy, wear light, breathable clothing. Nobody needs to see you passed out on the Guadalupe River Trail looking like a lobster.
</AlertBox>

Pollen-wise, we're at a "Moderate" level. If you've got seasonal allergies, you know the drill: grab your antihistamines, maybe wear some shades to keep the crud out of your eyes. It won't stop you, but it sure can make that last mile feel like you're running through a field of sneezes.

Now, for your favorite local paths: If you're heading south, be aware that the West Little Llagas Creek Trail in Morgan Hill is undergoing creek widening and deepening. That trail section, from Watsonville Road to Edes Court, is shut down until year-end. Expect more closures later this summer, too. Also, if Anderson Lake County Park is usually your jam, know that large sections are closed for the seismic retrofit of Anderson Dam, and that's going to be going on for years.

And a quick heads-up for anyone thinking of a long weekend run: Calero County Park and Joseph D. Grant County Park have prescribed burn piles scheduled for 2026. Specifically, Joseph D. Grant's south side will be closed from June 23-25 for one of these burns. Plan around it.

### Eyes on the Coyote Creek Trestle!
While not a hazard *today*, the debate continues around the historic Coyote Creek Trestle near Kelley Park. City officials are still weighing options for its preservation versus demolition for the planned Five Wounds Trail expansion. It's a prime example of why we need to pay attention to our local trail infrastructure – what's there today might be gone or changed tomorrow.
</AlertBox>
