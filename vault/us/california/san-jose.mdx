Forget the misty morning runs; today's a clear-cut case of "get it done early or suffer." The mercury is on the climb, and while the air itself is mostly clean, the heat is going to make you work for every stride.

This isn't "record-breaking" heat, but it's enough to mess with your run if you're not smart.
*   **Air quality is solid.** AQI is a beautiful 25, meaning your lungs get a pass today.
*   **Pollen's hanging around.** "Moderate" pollen levels mean some of you might be sniffling or sneezing through your workout.

Look, the sun's out, the sky is clear, and the air *quality* is about as good as it gets around here. That's fantastic news for anyone who usually struggles with the crud we sometimes get. But don't let that fool you into thinking it's a walk in Almaden Lake Park at high noon. Today's battle isn't with particulate matter; it's with the sun beating down on your head.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Midday Burn

With temperatures pushing 31°C (87.8°F) and a heat advisory covering the Bay Area, running between 10 AM and 4 PM is just asking for trouble. We're talking about conditions that can quickly lead to heat exhaustion or worse if you're not careful. This isn't the day to push your long run on the Guadalupe River Trail without an early start or a late finish. That asphalt absorbs and radiates heat like nobody's business, and there's not a whole lot of shade to bail you out. Keep an eye out for any signs of heat illness – dizziness, nausea, excessive sweating, or sudden chills – and get out of the sun immediately.
</AlertBox>

Pollen is "Moderate," which for many of us just means an extra sniffle or two, maybe some itchy eyes. It's not ideal, but it's usually manageable with your preferred allergy meds if you're prone to it. Just don't confuse allergy symptoms with early signs of heat stress. You know your body better than anyone (hopefully).

On the construction front, don't expect smooth sailing everywhere. While the VTA has no major scheduled construction for the I-280/Wolfe Road corridor next week, you might still encounter crews doing maintenance. And if you're navigating urban areas, remember that long-term construction projects, like the one on San Carlos Street, can still create minor detours and annoyances for pedestrians and cyclists. Always be aware of your surroundings and expect the unexpected, especially downtown.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your Game Plan for Today:

*   **Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate.** Start before you go out, carry water, and refuel often. Water fountains on trails might be far apart or non-existent, so plan accordingly.
*   **Time it right.** Seriously, aim for before 9 AM or after 6 PM. Your body will thank you, and you'll actually enjoy the effort instead of just surviving it.
*   **Dress light.** Loose, light-colored, moisture-wicking gear is your best friend. A hat and sunglasses wouldn't hurt either.
*   **Listen to your body.** If you feel off, stop. There's no medal for heatstroke.
</AlertBox>

Don't be a hero. It's a decent day to be active *if* you respect the heat. But pushing it when the sun is blazing is just dumb.
