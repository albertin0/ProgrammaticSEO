## Why today is/isn't the day

<BulletList>
*   **Temperature's a Real Burner:** Don't let the "Mainly Clear" fool you, 31°C (88°F) is hot enough to cook an egg on the pavement by lunchtime. If you're planning a long haul on the Guadalupe River Trail, you better be out there before the sun decides to wage war.
*   **Lingering Whiffs of Trouble:** There was a vegetation fire on Sierra Road just last night. While San Jose Fire got a handle on it quickly and stopped forward progress, smoke can still hang around the east foothills. Your AQI might look green, but local pockets could be funky. Don't be surprised if your lungs feel a little more "crunchy" than usual if you're up that way.
*   **Trail Troubles Persist:** Your favorite long-distance loops might be a no-go. Parts of Anderson Lake County Park, for instance, are basically a construction zone until 2032 thanks to the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. That means boating, fishing, and critical trails like the Serpentine Trail are out. Plan your routes, folks.
</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Heat Exhaustion is No Joke
That 31°C isn't just uncomfortable; it's dangerous. Don't be a hero. Hydrate aggressively *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or like you're running through molasses, stop. Find shade, get some electrolytes, and re-evaluate your life choices. Seriously, that hospital bill isn't worth hitting your Strava segment.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Check for Local Smoke
While the overall AQI is a decent 25, localized smoke from last night's Sierra Road fire could still be an issue, especially if you're running or cycling near the east foothills. Keep an eye (and nose) out. If it smells smoky, find another route or hit the gym. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Know Before You Go: Trail Closures
Don't just head out assuming your usual path is open. Santa Clara County Parks lists ongoing closures, and right now, places like Anderson Lake County Park have significant sections closed for long-term projects. The Mallard Slough Trail and parts of the Alviso Slough Trail also have intermittent construction closures. Nobody wants to hit a "trail closed" sign five miles into their run. Check the county parks website or the City of San José's trail network page before you lace up.
</AlertBox>
