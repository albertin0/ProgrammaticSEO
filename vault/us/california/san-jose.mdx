Look, the air is clean, which is always a win for San Jose. Your AQI is sitting pretty at 25, with "Ozone/Other" as the dominant pollutant, but honestly, that's barely a blip on the radar. Go ahead and fill those lungs; they'll thank you.

But here's where we hit a snag: the weather. We're looking at a cool 12.9°C (that's about 55°F for you Fahrenheit folks) and a "Light Drizzle." The National Weather Service even put out a special weather statement early this morning, so it's not just a passing cloud. A "chance of showers" is hanging around, especially after midnight and into the morning, along with some patchy fog. This isn't just a minor inconvenience; it means those beloved dirt trails you usually fly down are probably slicker than a politician's promise. Asphalt isn't immune either, especially with leaves or debris.

And let's talk about those trail closures. If your go-to is **Anderson Lake County Park**, remember that entire sections, including parts of the Lakeview Trail, are still shut down until around 2032 for the seismic retrofit project on Anderson Dam. Don't be that person who hops the fence because you "know a shortcut." You don't know what you're stepping into, and frankly, it's not worth breaking an ankle (or worse). There are other closures scattered throughout Santa Clara County Parks, so if you're venturing beyond your immediate neighborhood, always, *always* double-check the park's website before you roll out. General City of San Jose trails like those near Lake Cunningham might also have closures at various times, so plan ahead.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch Your Step!** That light drizzle might seem innocent, but it makes paths incredibly slippery, especially on unpaved trails or over bridges. A sprained ankle is a hell of a lot more inconvenient than waiting for a clearer day.
</AlertBox>

Pollen's sitting at a "Moderate" level. For most, this means a bit of a sniffle, maybe some itchy eyes, but it shouldn't be a showstopper. Still, if you're prone to seasonal allergies, pop an antihistamine before you head out, or you'll regret it halfway down the Guadalupe River Trail.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Respect Trail Closures!** Seriously, "Closed" signs aren't suggestions. Long-term projects, especially at places like Anderson Lake County Park, mean the area is actively unsafe. Ignoring these puts you, and potentially emergency responders, at risk.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, no specific wildfire smoke or extreme heat warnings for San Jose today. Traffic-wise, VTA says there are "no scheduled daytime or overnight construction activities" impacting the I-280/Wolfe Road project corridor, which is good if that's part of your route. However, the city is repaving a lot of streets this year, so expect potential localized disruptions on neighborhood roads.
