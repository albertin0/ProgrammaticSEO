## The Rundown: Don't Melt Out There

Look, I get it. The sun is out, the sky's mainly clear, and you've got that itch to get miles in. But let’s be real, running in nearly 90-degree heat isn't exactly peak performance weather. This isn't the day to PR your half marathon or try for that Strava segment record. This is the day for a gentle trot before 9 AM or after 7 PM, if you absolutely *must* be outside.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Heatstroke Isn't a Game, Folks**
With temperatures pushing 31°C (88°F), your body is working overtime just to keep cool. Forget hitting maximum effort. If your run feels harder than it should, it's not you, it's the heat. Slow down, walk, or better yet, head indoors. The National Weather Service even warned of a moderate risk of heat illness for inland areas like San Jose just a few days ago, with Monday potentially hitting 92°F. Today's not much different.
</AlertBox>

### Local Trail Intel

While the air quality is currently "Good" with an AQI of 25, keep a weather eye on the horizon. There was a vegetation fire in East San Jose near Coyote Creek and Tully Road as recently as Friday. While it's reportedly out and not impacting air quality *today*, those dry conditions and elevated fire risk across the interior Bay Area mean things can change fast. Stick to well-trafficked, open areas where you can bail easily if needed.

If you're planning on venturing further south, remember that **Anderson Lake County Park** and its surrounding trails are pretty much a no-go zone until 2032 due to the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. Don't waste your time driving out there only to find everything fenced off.

### **Pro-Tip: Dress for the Heat, Not the PR**
Light-colored, loose-fitting technical fabrics are your friends. A baseball cap or visor can keep the sun off your face, but make sure it's breathable. And for the love of all that is holy, carry water. More than you think you need. Or just fill a hydration vest and pretend you're an ultramarathoner, even if you're only doing two miles.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, listen to your body. It's not worth passing out on a trail somewhere, becoming the next story for the San Jose Fire Department. There will be other, cooler days.
