## Today's Vitals: Sweat, Sun, and Some Sniffles

Let's not sugarcoat it: today's 31 degrees Celsius (that's pushing 88 degrees Fahrenheit for those still using the old scale) is no joke. The good news is our air quality index (AQI) is sitting pretty at a "Good" 25, so at least you won't be choking on smog while you're battling heatstroke. Dominant pollutant is Ozone/Other, which at this low level isn't a major concern for your lungs. The bad news? That sunshine is relentless, and combined with moderate pollen levels, your sinuses might just decide to throw a party you weren't invited to.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Heat Advisory: Seriously, it's hot out there.
Avoid strenuous activity, especially during the peak sun hours of 10 AM to 3 PM. If you absolutely *must* run, hike, or bike, do it before the sun fully commits to scorching the earth or wait until it starts to dip. Drink absurd amounts of water, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothes, and for the love of all that is holy, know the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Dizziness, nausea, or suddenly stopping sweating? Get to shade, get water, and if you don't feel better, get help. This isn't a PR day, it's a survival day.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Conditions & Local Gripes

While no major trail closures directly related to weather popped up in the last 24 hours, there are some ongoing bits to be aware of. The Santa Clara Valley Water District is still doing vegetation removal along the Los Alamitos Creek Trail through early September. You might see flaggers, so pay attention and don't wander into a worksite. It's also worth noting that construction continues on parts of the Three Creeks Trail.

On a less-than-fresh note, residents near the Newby Island Resource Recovery Park (aka the big landfill in North San Jose) just got word that the Bay Area Air District has ordered the operators to address ongoing air quality issues and reduce odors. So, if your usual route takes you anywhere near there, be prepared for more than just the smell of success, or maybe choose a different path today. The Guadalupe River Trail, for instance, offers a longer stretch away from that particular aroma, though it'll still be blazing hot out there.
