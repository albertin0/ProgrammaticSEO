Forget your PRs on the Guadalupe River Trail after 9 AM. Seriously. The sun's going to be beating down, and while that nice breeze from the Bay might offer some temporary relief, it’s not enough to counteract sustained exposure to this kind of heat. You think that asphalt on the trail is cool? Go ahead, touch it. If you can't hold your hand there for seven seconds, your dog definitely shouldn't be on it.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Dangerously High Temperatures Incoming!**
With highs pushing towards 90°F (31°C) and beyond today, heat-related illness is a very real threat. Don't be a hero. Hydrate constantly, wear light-colored, loose clothing, and reschedule your intense workouts for early morning or late evening. If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or get a pounding headache, *stop immediately* and find some shade and water. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; it's about staying safe.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Moderate Pollen Alert**
Tree and grass pollen are at moderate levels today. If you're sensitive, you might experience some sneezing or itchy eyes. Consider taking your allergy meds before heading out, especially if you're hitting spots with a lot of natural foliage.
</AlertBox>

As for other common workout buzzkills, we're mostly in the clear. No active wind advisories today, so you won't be fighting gale-force winds crossing the Mineta San José International Airport. No sudden floods, either; the "once-in-a-lifetime" flooding on Coyote Creek was back in 2017. There are no major marathons or street closures blocking your usual routes right now, though keep an eye on the San Jose Half Marathon planned for later this month if that's your jam.

However, if your usual stomping grounds include some of the county parks, remember those long-term closures. For example, substantial portions of **Anderson Lake County Park** are still shut down until approximately 2032 for the seismic retrofit project on the dam, impacting trails like the Serpentine and Lakeview Trail. Always check county park websites before you head out, because getting halfway through a run only to hit a "TRAIL CLOSED" sign is just plain annoying.

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Workout Smart, Not Hard (in the heat)**
If you absolutely insist on getting your miles in, stick to shaded routes where possible. The trails around Alum Rock Park offer some good tree cover, though even there, the climbs will tax you. Carry more water than you think you need. Or, just hit the gym. There's no shame in AC when it's this hot.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, San Jose. Don't let the heat turn your run into a bad decision.
