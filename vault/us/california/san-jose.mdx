Look, the AQI is practically sparkling today at 25. That's fantastic. Go ahead and breathe deep, assuming you aren't allergic to everything currently in bloom. Because while the air quality is pristine, the mercury is making a strong push for "summer in April" records. We just clawed our way out of a near-week-long heat advisory in March, with San Jose temps hitting the mid-90s. While today isn't *officially* under one, 31 degrees Celsius is no joke, especially if you're planning on pushing serious mileage on the Guadalupe River Trail.

### What to Watch Out For

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning
Seriously, it's hot. This isn't just about feeling sweaty; we're talking about a real risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Stay hydrated like your life depends on it (because it does). Plan your runs or rides for the early morning or late evening. And for the love of all that is holy, do NOT leave kids or pets in a parked car. Not even for a minute.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Construction Zone Ahead
If you usually weave through downtown, expect some detours. Construction is set to kick off this spring on new LS Power transmission projects, including a whopping 12-mile underground line along Monterey Road and another two miles near Los Esteros Road, potentially impacting your usual routes through 2028. Also, keep an eye out around 470 South Market Street; that Gateway Tower project is supposed to get moving "early next year" (which is now), adding more downtown disruption.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Trail & Park Closures
The Santa Clara County Parks system has a few long-term headaches. **Anderson Lake County Park** is still largely closed until around 2032 due to the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. That means no boating, no fishing, and significant trail closures. Also, if you head out to **Alviso Marina County Park**, be aware of "intermittent closures on the Mallard Slough Trail and eastern sections of the Alviso Slough Trail due to hauling/construction". Always check the specific park website before you head out, because nothing ruins a long run like a "TRAIL CLOSED" sign after you've already committed to two miles.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Pollen & Hydration Tips
With moderate pollen levels, those of us who sniffle and sneeze through spring should be prepared. Take your meds before you head out, and maybe save your long-distance efforts for days with less green stuff in the air. For everyone, regardless of allergies: water, water, water. Electrolytes wouldn't hurt either. Your joints, already taking a beating from the pavement, don't need the added stress of dehydration in this heat.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, San Jose. Or at least try not to end up in my PT clinic.
