## The Heat is No Joke

Seriously, I can't stress this enough. This isn't your typical pleasant Bay Area spring day. We've been under heat advisories all week, and the lingering warmth means your body is working overtime to regulate its temperature. Even if the air quality is currently "Good" with an AQI of 25, the heat alone is enough to knock you on your ass if you're not careful. This early-season heat has experts worried about rapidly melting snowpack and an increased risk of wildfires, so while we don't have smoke *today*, the conditions are ripe for it.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### Extreme Heat Risk!
  Today's 31.0°C (87.8°F) is dangerous for strenuous outdoor activity, especially during peak sun hours (10 AM - 5 PM). Symptoms of heat exhaustion include heavy sweating, cold, clammy skin, fast weak pulse, nausea, and muscle cramps. Heat stroke is a medical emergency with hot, red, moist or dry skin, fast strong pulse, and possible unconsciousness. Hydrate *before* you're thirsty, and seriously consider an indoor alternative.
</AlertBox>

## Your Lungs, Your Allergies, Your Problem

That "Moderate" pollen level might sound benign, but coupled with the heat, it can exacerbate respiratory issues. If you're an allergy sufferer, you know the drill. Take your meds, wear sunglasses to protect your eyes, and maybe opt for a shorter, less intense workout. The good news is, no wildfires mean no smoky air for now, which is a small blessing in this hot mess.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ### Moderate Pollen Alert
  Allergy sufferers, be prepared. Histamine blockers and nasal sprays are your friends today. If you're feeling congested or wheezy, take it easy.
</AlertBox>

## Navigating San Jose's Trails and Roads

Watch out for that section of the Los Gatos Creek Trail. "Unsafe conditions" could mean anything from erosion to debris, and a 100-foot detour is more than just a minor inconvenience if you're trying to get a rhythm going. Also, keep in mind that downtown areas, like San Carlos Street, are still dealing with ongoing construction for ADA improvements which can cause traffic and pedestrian detours, though these are more of a nuisance than a direct workout hazard.

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Pro Tip: Hydrate, Hydrate, Hydrate!
  And I don't mean slamming a sugary sports drink after your run. Start hydrating now. Carry plenty of water (more than you think you need), and if you feel any signs of heat stress, stop immediately and find some shade or AC. Your PR can wait; your health can't.
</AlertBox>
