## Heat's On (But Not *Extreme* Heat)

Alright, San Jose, we're not quite in "boiling alive" territory today, but 31°C is plenty warm for a workout. The clear skies aren't doing you any favors either; that sun is going to be intense. While we've seen "Extreme Heat Warnings" earlier this week for other parts of the Bay Area with triple-digit forecasts, today's actual temperature is high enough to warrant caution, especially if you're planning a long run or ride in exposed areas. Hydration is not a suggestion, it's mandatory. Don't be that person collapsing on the side of Los Gatos Creek Trail because you thought a single water bottle would cut it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Mercury
With temperatures around 31°C, heat illness is a real risk, especially mid-day. Schedule your workouts for early mornings or evenings. Stick to shaded routes when possible, and for the love of all that is holy, drink more water than you think you need. Consider electrolytes if you're going for distance or intensity.
</AlertBox>

## Breathing Easy, But Keep an Eye Out

Good news on the air front: our AQI is a beautiful 25, which is firmly in the "Good" category. You can actually breathe deeply without feeling like you're sucking down a campfire. This is a nice reprieve, especially after reports of smoke from the Little Fire near Pleasanton causing air quality advisories elsewhere in the Bay Area earlier this week. San Jose seems to have dodged that bullet for now.

However, "moderate" pollen is still floating around. For those of us with seasonal allergies, this means your nose might run more than you do, and you might sneeze enough to confuse it for a new speed work interval. Pop your meds before you head out, or you'll regret it.

## Navigating the San Jose Obstacle Course

If you're looking for a smooth, uninterrupted long run, you're going to need to do some homework. Construction continues to be the bane of our existence, changing up familiar routes and forcing detours.

The much-anticipated Five Wounds Trail project in East San Jose is currently paused on its southernmost section due to a historic railroad trestle getting in the way. So, don't plan on that connecting through just yet.

Closer to home, Valley Water is busy doing vegetation removal along sections of the Guadalupe River Trail and the Los Alamitos Creek Trail. They say the trails will remain open, but expect flaggers and potential minor delays between 7 AM and 3 PM through early September. Pay attention to their directions; don't be a hero and try to sneak into an active work zone.

And let's not forget the multi-year closures at Anderson Lake County Park. If you're planning on hitting the Serpentine Trail or any areas near the dam, you're out of luck. Large portions, including key trails and facilities, are closed until *2032* due to the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. Plan accordingly and find an alternative route, like Calero or Almaden Quicksilver, which currently have no listed trail closures.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route, Seriously
Before you head out, check the Santa Clara County Parks website for the latest on trail closures, especially around Anderson Lake or if you frequent the Guadalupe River Trail. A little planning saves a lot of frustration and wasted miles.
</AlertBox>

Today's a solid day for outdoor activity if you respect the heat and navigate the inevitable local construction. Just don't expect it to be an entirely smooth ride.
