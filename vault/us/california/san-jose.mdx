## Today's Air & Heat: Listen to Your Lungs, Not Just the App

Look, I get it. The AQI says 25, which *sounds* great. But anyone who stepped outside this morning could tell you there's a distinct campfire aroma wafting through the South Bay. That's thanks to a prescribed burn on Angel Island, about 53 miles north. So, while our local sensors might not be freaking out, your respiratory system might. Don't be a hero; if your throat feels scratchy or you're coughing, cut your session short. Pushing through "moderate" smoke exposure, even if the particulate matter count isn't sky-high, is just asking for trouble down the line.

Then there's the heat. Eighty-eight degrees Fahrenheit is not ideal for hitting your personal best. Your joints will feel the impact of dehydration faster than you think, and your muscles will fatigue quicker. If you're out there, *slow down*. This isn't the day to try and shave seconds off your PR on the Alamden Lake loop. Just enjoy the movement, stay hydrated, and for the love of all that is holy, find some shade.

## Local Hazards & Headaches

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Guadalupe River Trail Closure:** Heads up for anyone planning to hit the Guadalupe River Trail north of Downtown. There's ongoing sediment removal from Trimble Road down to Airport Parkway. That section is CLOSED between 7:30 AM and 3:00 PM, Monday through Friday, through September 25. Don't be that person ignoring the signs; you'll either get ticketed or, worse, get in the way of heavy machinery. Find an alternate route or stick to the southern stretches.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Chaos:** Beyond the trails, San Jose's streets are a perpetual construction zone. Expect lane closures and general disruption on Monterey Road, Market Street, and West Julian Street due to utility work. If your workout involves pounding the pavement through these areas, be aware of traffic, uneven surfaces, and increased noise. It's not just annoying; it's a tripping hazard.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration, Hydration, Hydration:** I'm a broken record, I know, but at 88°F, you need to be constantly sipping water. Don't wait until you're thirsty; by then, you're already behind. Carry more water than you think you'll need, and consider an electrolyte drink if you're out for more than an hour.
</AlertBox>
