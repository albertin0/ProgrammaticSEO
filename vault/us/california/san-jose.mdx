## The Heat Is On, Literally

Alright, folks, let's not pretend it's a mild spring day. The forecast for San Jose today hits a robust 31°C (88°F), and while the air quality is thankfully in the "Good" range with an AQI of 25, that heat is no joke. Forget hitting PRs, focus on not collapsing in a sweaty heap. This kind of temperature significantly increases your risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Save the heroics for a cooler day, or at least one that doesn't feel like a dehydrating oven.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning!
Seriously, 31°C (88°F) is not running weather for most people in the middle of the day. Stick to early mornings or late evenings if you value your health. If you *must* be out, hydrate constantly, wear light, breathable clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, listen to your body. No workout is worth a trip to the ER.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Conditions & Local Gripes

Our trails are a mixed bag, as usual. If you're planning a scenic stroll or run along the **Los Gatos Creek Trail**, be aware there's a partial closure due to "unsafe conditions" affecting a 100-foot section. There's a detour available, but it's always a buzzkill to hit an unexpected roadblock. Meanwhile, over in Sierra Azul Preserve, they're doing wildfire resilience work through May, which is good in the long run, and currently, all trails remain open. So, you're clear there for now. Sanborn County Park still has its share of localized closures, so check their official site before you head up for that punishing climb to Summit Rock (which is closed for peregrine falcons nesting season anyway).

And don't even get me started on the ongoing street construction. Lafayette Street in Santa Clara is undergoing a bikeway project, potentially impacting cyclists, and San Jose is repaving 205 miles of streets this year. So, if your workout involves navigating city streets, be ready to dodge cones and deal with temporary lane closures.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Pro Tip: Check Before You Trek
Seriously, if you're hitting any major trail or specific park, swing by the Santa Clara County Parks website or the specific preserve's page *before* you leave the house. Things change faster than a Bay Area housing market, and nobody likes an unexpected detour a mile into their long run.
</AlertBox>
