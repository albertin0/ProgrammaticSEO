## What's the Deal?

Alright, San Jose, let's cut to the chase: The biggest obstacle to your workout today isn't a surprise fire or a sudden flood. It's the sun, relentlessly trying to turn you into beef jerky. We're looking at a high of 31°C (88°F), but anyone who's actually run here knows those exposed sections of the Guadalupe River Trail or the hills above Almaden Lake can feel like an oven. Cal/OSHA even had to remind employers to protect their workers from this kind of heat, which should tell you something about exercising in it for "fun."

The good news? Your lungs are in the clear. The AQI is fantastic at 25, and the Bay Area Air Quality Management District isn't issuing any air quality alerts for today. So, if you manage to avoid melting, at least you'll be breathing some decent air. Pollen's moderate, so if you're part of the sniffle squad, keep some tissues handy, but it's not a game-changer.

We haven't seen any major event closures or specific trail shutdowns reported for today. The San Jose Half Marathon was back in May, and the CrossFit Games wrapped up last weekend at the SAP Center. There was some roadwork earlier in the week on I-680 near Alum Rock, but generally, local running paths are physically open. The real hazard is that giant fiery ball in the sky.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Risk Midday!** With temperatures hitting nearly 90°F (32°C) on exposed areas, running between 10 AM and 6 PM is genuinely dangerous. You risk heat stroke, severe dehydration, and looking like a tomato.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Stay Hydrated, Seriously.** This isn't a drill. Carry more water than you think you need. Consider electrolyte drinks. Even a seemingly short run can drain you quickly in this heat.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Timing is Everything:** If you absolutely must get out there, embrace the dawn patrol or wait until dusk. Seriously, aim for before 8 AM or after 7 PM. You'll thank me when you're not hallucinating water fountains. Pick shaded routes where possible, like parts of the Los Gatos Creek Trail, but be aware that shade only goes so far when the ambient temp is this high.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Listen to Your Body (and Your PT):** Headaches, dizziness, excessive sweating (or *not* sweating), nausea, or muscle cramps are not "pushing through." They are signs you need to stop, find shade, and cool down immediately. Your PR can wait; your health can't.
</AlertBox>

So, if you're tough enough (or foolish enough) to brave the midday inferno, be smart about it. Otherwise, there's always the treadmill, or you could just accept that some days are for recovery and hydration.
