## Today's San Jose Outdoor Grind

Alright, San Jose, today's shaping up to be a pretty decent day to pound the pavement or hit the trails. We've got clear skies and a comfortable 16.1°C (about 61°F) to kick things off this morning, which is absolutely perfect for an early run or ride. Enjoy it, because the mercury is predicted to climb to a roasting 32°C (89°F) later in the day, meaning anything after lunchtime is going to feel like you're running through a convection oven. Plan your outdoor activities accordingly, unless you *enjoy* that dehydrated, dizzy feeling.

The air quality, for once, isn't a dumpster fire. Our AQI is sitting pretty at a "Good" 25. So your lungs can actually do their job without feeling like you've been chain-smoking at a bonfire. Just a heads-up though, while overall smoke forecasts for the next 48 hours aren't showing anything "significant", we're still in California, and wildfire season is always lurking. Keep an eye on local news just in case the wind shifts some distant smoke our way later in the week.

Now, for the "joints" part of the score: there are a few things to be aware of if you're planning on venturing onto some of our beloved trails.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail Work Ahead!

If your usual loop takes you along the **Guadalupe River Trail** or the **Los Alamitos Creek Trail**, expect some company. Valley Water is doing vegetation removal through early September. The trails are staying open, but they'll have flaggers guiding folks around. Don't be "that guy" who ignores the signs and wanders into a work zone. Pay attention, follow directions, and you'll be fine.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Coyote Valley Open Space Preserve Updates

For those who frequent the **Coyote Valley Open Space Preserve**, know that an accessibility and public access improvement project kicked off back in June. They're trying to keep closures to a minimum, but the equestrian lot is currently closed for trailer parking due to this construction. If you're riding in, you'll still have access, but no haul-ins for now. Also, a quick reminder that the Habitat Protection Area (Serpentine Spring, Tule Elk, and Bay Checkerspot trails) is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays for scientific research. Today's Sunday, so you're clear for now, but keep it in mind for your weekly plans.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Long-Term Closures at Anderson Lake

Planning a big adventure at **Anderson Lake County Park**? Think again. Large sections of the park, including boating, fishing areas, and many specific trails like the Serpentine Trail and parts of the Lakeview Trail, are closed until approximately *2032* due to the seismic retrofit project on the Anderson Dam. Don't make the drive only to find your favorite spots fenced off. Check the Santa Clara County Parks website before you head out anywhere that far afield.
</AlertBox>

So, enjoy the mostly clear day, embrace the cool morning air, but stay vigilant on the trails and check for those pesky local updates. Nobody wants a twisted ankle from ignoring a detour sign or a heatstroke from an ill-timed afternoon run.
