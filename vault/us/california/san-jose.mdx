Today, March 11, 2026, San Jose is serving up a dose of weather that feels more like high summer than early spring. That 31.0°C (87.8°F) "Mainly Clear" forecast isn't a suggestion; it's a warning. We're in the midst of what news outlets are calling an "unprecedented heat wave" for California, with San Jose itself "flirting with 90 degrees" this week. Don't let the calendar fool you into thinking it's still spring training weather.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: It's a Marathon, Not a Sauna!
This isn't just "warm," it's dangerously hot for exercise. Temperatures reaching nearly 90°F in early March are anomalous and pose a significant risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke. Hydrate aggressively *before*, *during*, and *after* any activity. Consider moving your workout indoors or rescheduling for very early morning or late evening, but even then, the ground will radiate heat. If you're out there, stick to shaded routes, listen to your body, and don't be a hero. Your joints and cardiovascular system are already stressed by this kind of heat, even if you feel "fine."
</AlertBox>

On the air quality front, we're in decent shape. The AQI is a perfectly respectable 42, which is "Good," so at least you won't be choking on smog. However, for those of us with sensitive systems, the "Moderate" pollen levels are a reminder that even clear skies can bring some sniffles and eye irritation. Don't forget your antihistamines if you're prone to seasonal allergies.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind Your Allergies: Pollen's Not Playing Nice
While the air is clean otherwise, moderate pollen can still trigger symptoms for many. If you're planning a long run on the Los Gatos Creek Trail, for instance, be prepared for potential sneezing, runny nose, or itchy eyes. It's not enough to stop you, but it's enough to make you miserable if you're not ready.
</AlertBox>

Construction-wise, nothing major is actively blocking entire trail systems today that would impact your average runner. However, Valley Water is performing some levee repair work along the Guadalupe River south of Tasman Drive. While they aim to keep trails open, especially on weekends, weekday work might lead to intermittent closures or detours. Always keep an eye out for signage and heavy machinery if you're hitting those river paths.

For those looking for events, the Cinequest Film & Creativity Festival is happening in Downtown San Jose and Mountain View through March 13. This is more a street and venue affair, so it shouldn't impact your trail runs unless you decide to sprint through a film premiere.

### Stay Hydrated and Be Flexible
Given the unseasonably high temperatures, carrying extra water is non-negotiable. Plan shorter routes or loops so you can refill easily. If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or excessively fatigued, stop immediately, find shade, and cool down. There's no medal for heatstroke.
