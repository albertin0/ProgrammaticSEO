## Your San Jose Workout Reality Check

Look, I get it. The sun's out, the sky's mainly clear, and your running shoes are calling. But seriously, slow your roll. Today in San Jose is less about crushing miles and more about not becoming a heat casualty. The heat isn't just making you sweat; it puts real stress on your cardiovascular system and can contribute to joint issues if you're pushing too hard without proper hydration and recovery.

The current AQI sitting pretty at 25 for Ozone/Other might look good on paper, but if you've been outside, you know the air can still feel heavy. That windblown dust advisory that ran until 9 AM this morning means there could still be some invisible nasties floating around. You don't want to be huffing that into your lungs, especially not when you're already fighting off moderate pollen.

### Trail Talk: Anderson Lake and Beyond

If you're planning a long run, especially out towards the hills, be aware. Many areas of **Anderson Lake County Park** are still shut down until around 2032 due to the Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. This includes major sections like the Serpentine Trail and parts of Lakeview Trail. Don't get caught out there expecting a clear path only to hit a construction barrier. Plan your routes, people! Downtown San Jose also has various construction projects for bikeways and infrastructure that might pop up and impact local rides, even if not explicitly closed for today.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Invisible Air Hazards? Trust Your Gut.**
Despite a "Good" AQI of 25, the recent windblown dust advisory explicitly warned that hazardous ash particles might be present even if not detected by air quality instruments. If you see or smell anything unusual, consider bringing your workout indoors. Better safe than coughing up lung butter later.
</AlertBox>
