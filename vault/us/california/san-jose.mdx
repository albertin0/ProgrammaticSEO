Look, the AQI is fantastic at 25, which is usually a green light to get out there and breathe deep. But don't let that fool you into thinking it's a perfect day for a PR. We're hitting a high of 31°C (88°F), and while "Mainly Clear" sounds inviting, it just means the sun is going to beat down on you with full force. Anyone thinking of tackling the longer loops through Almaden Quicksilver County Park today without proper hydration and planning is asking for trouble. That kind of sustained heat exposure can sneak up on you faster than a cyclist on the Guadalupe River Trail.

There's no news about major trail closures, construction blocking your usual routes around downtown, or any of the usual San Jose chaos. No wildfires choking the air, which is a blessing, especially this time of year. But the biggest hazard today is the one you can't see but will definitely feel: the heat. People underestimate how quickly dehydration sets in, especially when you're exerting yourself. Don't be that person stumbling around Communications Hill wondering why your quads feel like lead and your head is pounding.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Hydration isn't optional, it's mandatory.**
Carry more water than you think you need. Seriously. If you're doing anything over 30 minutes, consider electrolytes. That San Jose sun cooks everything eventually. And for the love of all that is holy, skip the midday run between 10 AM and 4 PM. Your body will thank you.
</AlertBox>

Pollen is still sitting at a moderate level. If you're one of the many Silicon Valley residents who suddenly discovered allergies when you moved here, you'll probably feel it. Don't be surprised by some sneezing or a slightly scratchy throat, especially if you're out near any of the larger green spaces like Kelley Park. It's not enough to stop most people, but it's another mild irritant on a hot day.

The dominant pollutant is listed as "Ozone/Other" but with an AQI of 25, it's practically pristine. So, no issues with your lungs from dirty air today, just from trying to drag them through 88-degree heat.

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Dress for success (and survival).**
Light-colored, moisture-wicking gear is your best friend. A hat and sunglasses aren't just for style points; they help prevent heatstroke and eye strain.
</AlertBox>

Bottom line: The air quality is great, but the heat is a real factor. Play it smart, hydrate like your life depends on it (because it kind of does), and maybe save that long, exposed climb up Mount Hamilton for a cooler day. Or at least go at dawn.
