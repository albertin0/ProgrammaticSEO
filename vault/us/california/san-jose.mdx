## Today's Workout Outlook: Don't Get Caught Slippin'

Alright, San Jose, let's talk about hitting the trails today. The air quality is *chefs kiss* beautiful, an AQI of 25 with "Ozone/Other" as the dominant pollutant means you won't be sucking down wildfire smoke or car exhaust, at least not in any significant amount. That's a win, especially after a couple of months ago when we were dealing with smoke from the Angel Island prescribed burn and then that Cooley Fire up north causing advisories. Seriously, it's nice to breathe easy for a change.

The weather itself, an overcast 14.8°C, is pretty much prime running weather. No brutal sun beating down, just a cool, gray canvas for your suffering, I mean, exercise.

Here's the rub though: we've got a decent chance of light rain rolling in today, increasing significantly tonight. While it might not be a downpour, any moisture on the ground means things get slippery, fast. We've seen what a little rain can do to our trails, remember those Coyote Creek Trail and Guadalupe River Trail closures from flooding a few years back? Even if it's not full-on flood conditions, slick spots are an invitation for twisted ankles and scraped knees.

### Trail Closures & Construction Realities

Beyond the weather, some of our favorite spots have ongoing issues you need to be aware of.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Alum Rock Park Trail Closures:** If you're heading to Alum Rock, know that the Penitencia Creek Trail at the Trestle Bridge is still closed due to "current conditions," and the Mineral Springs Trail is shut down for erosion. Don't try to be a hero and bypass these; rangers will cite you. Stick to the detours or other open sections.
</AlertBox>

The Five Wounds Trail project is still stalled on its southernmost portion thanks to some historic railroad trestle drama. And if you frequent the Guadalupe River Trail near the airport, be mindful of ongoing maintenance – it caused minor delays a couple of months ago, and you might still hit some slow spots. Don't even get me started on Anderson Lake County Park; half of it's been closed for years for that dam project and will be for years more. Plan your routes.
