## The Heat is On, Literally

Look, I've run enough miles in this city to know that even when the official "heat advisory" isn't blaring, 31°C in the afternoon sun is no joke. The National Weather Service was warning us about interior Bay Area heat just last week, and while those alerts expired, the sun didn't pack up its bags and go home. If you're planning on tackling the switchbacks at Almaden Quicksilver Park, make sure you're doing it before 9 AM or after 6 PM. Seriously.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Midday Roast
Temperatures in San Jose can feel significantly hotter on asphalt or exposed trails. Peak heat risk is typically from late morning through late afternoon. Plan your runs accordingly to avoid becoming a human jerky.
</AlertBox>

## Pollen's Annoyance, Not a Showstopper

Moderate pollen isn't going to send you to the ER, but it's enough to make a decent run feel like you're sucking air through a coffee stirrer if you're sensitive. Check your local pollen counts before you head out, and if you're prone to allergies, take an antihistamine. Don't let a runny nose derail your workout if you can help it.

## Trail Talk: Keep an Eye Out

Good news: I haven't seen any flash reports about active wildfires or major new trail closures *today* that should stop your immediate plans in most popular spots. Our AQI is sitting pretty at 25, which means you're breathing some decent air. However, a heads-up for those who plan ahead: Santa Clara County Parks mentions scheduled prescribed fires happening starting June 22nd in some areas, which could mean closures on the south side of *a* park, though they're a little vague on which specific one. Always check the individual park website before you commit to a long drive.

Also, a reminder that Anderson Lake County Park is still largely closed until approximately 2032 due to the dam retrofit project. Don't plan your epic trail adventure there anytime soon; you'll hit a fence. Intermittent closures also continue on sections of the Mallard Slough Trail and eastern Alviso Slough Trail due to ongoing construction related to the South San Francisco Bay Shoreline Project.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate or Dydrate, Your Choice
Seriously, carry more water than you think you need. Even if it's "just" a few miles along Los Gatos Creek Trail, that sun will sneak up on you. Electrolytes aren't a bad idea either.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Listen to Your Body
If you start feeling dizzy, nauseous, or cramping up, stop. Get into the shade, drink water, and cool down. Pushing through heat exhaustion or heat stroke isn't brave, it's stupid. There's always tomorrow for that PR.
</AlertBox>
