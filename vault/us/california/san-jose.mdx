## Today's Vibe: Hot and Hazy (for your sinuses)

So, you've got a basically spotless AQI, which is great. No smoke, no nasty urban haze from exhaust fumes. But that's where the good news ends, folks. We're staring down a main event of 31°C (88°F) under clear skies, and that's just plain stupid-hot for a workout, especially as the day progresses.

### Heat Advisory: Seriously, Stay Indoors

The city's own safety guidelines are pretty clear on this: limit physical activity during the hottest part of the day, typically 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; we're talking about a moderate risk of heat-related illness for anyone sensitive to heat. Children, older adults, and those with underlying conditions are particularly vulnerable. But frankly, even us marathon-hardened maniacs can end up in a world of hurt.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** 31°C (88°F) is dangerous for prolonged outdoor activity. If you MUST run, do it at dawn or dusk, wear light clothing, hydrate constantly, and listen to your body. Seriously, don't be a hero. Your kidneys will thank you.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: Watch for the Cones

While the main weather story is heat, don't think you can escape other urban annoyances. If you're planning on hitting some of the more scenic routes, be aware. For instance, parts of the **Alviso Slough Trail** are experiencing intermittent closures due to ongoing hauling and construction. Nothing worse than getting into your groove only to find a detour sign and a construction crew staring you down.

And if you were eyeing a longer escape to the hills, many areas of Anderson Lake County Park are closed until roughly 2032 because of the seismic retrofit project on the dam. Also, the Ohlone picnic area in Sanborn County Park is closed through the end of April due to significant trail construction and paving improvements.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Construction Alert:** Expect intermittent closures and detours on parts of the Alviso Slough Trail due to ongoing construction. Check Santa Clara County Parks alerts before you head out, especially if your route relies on that area.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Non-Negotiable:** Carry more water than you think you need. Consider electrolyte drinks. Water fountains on trails can be unreliable, especially in remote areas or during closures.
</AlertBox>

My honest advice? This is a treadmill, spin bike, or pool day. If you absolutely have to be outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and for the love of all that is holy, bring water. Lots of it.
