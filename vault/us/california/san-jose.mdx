### Air & Allergens: A Mixed Bag

The overall pollen level is moderate, which for some of you means a sniffle or two, but nothing catastrophic. However, let's talk about the air quality. We're sitting at a "Moderate" AQI with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. While that's technically not "unhealthy," the Bay Area Air Quality Management District (BAAQMD) regularly deals with ozone and particulate matter concerns, especially during warmer months. Couple that with the high temperatures, and you have a recipe for some extra lung stress, especially if you're prone to respiratory issues. The goal isn't just to *survive* your workout; it's to actually benefit from it. Take it easy and listen to your lungs.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### WARNING: Moderate AQI & Potential Ozone
With PM2.5 at moderate levels and the heat pushing ozone formation, consider keeping your workout intensity down. If you're struggling to breathe, it's not "just a tough workout," it's your body telling you to cut it out.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble: Where Not to Wander

Before you lace up and head out, know that some of your favorite local haunts have ongoing issues.

The **Guadalupe River (east) Trail** from Trimble Road to Airport Parkway is closed weekdays from 7:30 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. for sediment removal until October 9th. That means your lunchtime run along the river is a no-go, or at least a major detour. Don't be "that guy" trying to sneak around construction barriers; they're there for a reason.

If you're thinking of a longer adventure, remember that significant portions of **Anderson Lake County Park** are closed until approximately 2032 due to the massive Anderson Dam Seismic Retrofit Project. This includes several trails, boating access, and picnic areas. Don't make the drive out there only to hit a "CLOSED" sign. Check the Santa Clara County Parks website before you go.
