## The Heat is On, Seriously

Look, I love running as much as the next certified lunatic, but 31 degrees Celsius with mainly clear skies isn't "challenging," it's just dumb during peak hours. The forecast for the Bay Area inland is hitting the 90s to 105 degrees Fahrenheit, and while you might be at the lower end of that, it's still dangerously hot. The City of San Jose explicitly tells you to avoid strenuous activity between 10 AM and 3 PM. They're not wrong. Your body works overtime to cool itself, and at these temps, you're just asking for trouble like heat exhaustion or worse. Remember that time you thought "one more mile" was a good idea? Yeah, don't do that today. Stick to the early morning or late evening if you absolutely *must* move. Even then, stay in the shade. That asphalt on the Guadalupe River Trail will cook your soles, and your core temperature will follow.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Hazard:** With temperatures pushing 31°C (88°F), the risk of heat-related illness like heat exhaustion and heatstroke is HIGH, especially during the middle of the day. Stay indoors or restrict outdoor activity to the early morning or late evening. HYDRATE, HYDRATE, HYDRATE.
</AlertBox>

## Local Nuisances & Trail Alerts

Construction. Always construction. VTA's still got light rail improvement work on the Orange Line affecting traffic, and there's utility work on Monterey Road with lane closures. If you're cycling on the road, be extra vigilant. For trail users, Anderson Lake County Park is pretty much a no-go until 2032 due to the dam retrofit project. Also, keep an eye out for intermittent closures on parts of the Mallard Slough Trail and eastern sections of the Alviso Slough Trail; they're doing some hauling and construction out there. It's not a full shutdown, but don't expect a clear path.

<AlertBox type="info">
