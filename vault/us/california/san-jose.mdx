## Today's Vibe: Bake Sale, Not Base Pace

Look, the air is clean, and that's fantastic, especially after some of the hazy days we've seen this month. But clean air won't stop you from cooking if you’re out during peak heat. We’ve seen heat advisories for temperatures in the mid-90s to low 100s just a couple of weeks ago, and while 88°F isn't quite that, it's still hot enough to be dangerous, particularly if you’re pushing it.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Caution!
Today's temperature of 31.0°C (87.8°F) is a serious concern for outdoor activity. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real risks. Don't be a hero. Limit strenuous activity, especially between 10 AM and 4 PM. This isn't just about comfort; it's about your actual well-being.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, those exposed trails around Almaden Quicksilver Park that get ten degrees hotter than the valley floor? They're calling your name, but probably for a very early morning or late evening stroll, not a midday suffer-fest. Even the Los Alamitos Creek Trail, while shaded in some spots, isn't a full escape from this kind of heat.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Beat the Heat, Not Yourself
If you absolutely must get out there:
*   **Go early, go late:** Before 8 AM or after 6 PM is your best bet.
*   **Hydrate aggressively:** Water, electrolytes – more than you think you need. Start hydrating *before* you even step out the door.
*   **Dress light:** Loose-fitting, light-colored clothing is your uniform.
*   **Stick to the shade:** Prioritize routes with tree cover, or just hit the treadmill. There's no shame in admitting the sun is stronger than your ego.
*   **Listen to your body:** Dizziness, nausea, excessive sweating (or *not* sweating) are warning signs. Turn around. Seriously.
</AlertBox>

The trails are here for us to enjoy, but they're not going anywhere. Your body, however, is a fragile, fleshy sack that can overheat. Be smart out there.
