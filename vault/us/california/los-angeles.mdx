## Today's Rundown: Smoke, Sweat, and Still-Closed Trails

Alright, Angelenos, let's talk about what's actually happening out there today, June 23, 2026. Forget the pretty, low overall AQI number of 18 you might see floating around – that's a lie if you live anywhere near the eastern side of the city.

### The Boyle Heights Smoke Show Continues

The big story, and frankly, the biggest gut punch to your lung health, is the goddamn warehouse fire in Boyle Heights. That stubborn blaze has been burning since June 17, and it's still sending plumes of irritating, potentially toxic smoke across central Los Angeles County, the San Gabriel Valley, and the East San Fernando Valley. An air quality advisory for "very unhealthy" fine particle pollution in the Boyle Heights area was extended through at least 12:30 PM today. We're talking about more than just a campfire smell; officials are concerned about toxic chemicals and biohazards from decomposing frozen food. So, if you're smelling it or seeing that haze, *do not* push your outdoor workout, especially if you've got any kind of respiratory issues. This isn't just "unpleasant"; it's legitimately bad for you.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Avoid outdoor exercise in areas affected by Boyle Heights fire smoke.** If you're smelling or seeing smoke, especially in Boyle Heights, East Los Angeles, Montebello, or downtown, stay indoors. This isn't a drill; it's a direct threat to your respiratory health.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Status: Still a Minefield of Closures

On the trail front, it's a mixed bag of "finally open!" and "seriously, still?". The good news: El Capitán State Beach is officially reopening today after 18 months of restoration. If you're up for a drive, enjoy the modernized facilities.

The bad news: Old wounds from the Palisades Fire (Jan 2025) and Eaton Fire (Jan 2025) mean large swaths of the Santa Monica Mountains and Angeles National Forest are still recovering and closed. Places like Temescal Gateway Park, Tuna Canyon Park, and parts of Eaton Canyon Natural Area Park are off-limits, some until December 31, 2027. Don't be that person trying to sneak past closure signs; you're just screwing up the recovery and putting yourself at risk. These areas often have unstable terrain and sometimes even poodle-dog bush, which causes nasty skin reactions.

And for today specifically, if you're planning on hitting Claremont Hills Wilderness Park, be advised that portions of the **Cobal Canyon Trail are closed** due to drainage culvert installation. Expect heavy equipment and find an alternative route.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Numerous trails remain closed!** Always check local park and forest service websites before heading out. Key closures include large sections of Eaton Canyon and the Topanga area trails in the Santa Monica Mountains. Today, specifically, avoid Cobal Canyon Trail in Claremont Hills Wilderness Park due to construction.
</AlertBox>
