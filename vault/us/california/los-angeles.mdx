## Why today is/isn't the day

<BulletList>

*   <AlertBox type="danger">Let's get this straight: a moderate storm system is slamming Southern California today and tomorrow. We're talking 0.5 to 1.5 inches of rain, possibly 3 inches in the foothills. The National Weather Service has issued a Flood Watch for the Eastern Santa Monica Mountains Recreational Area from Wednesday afternoon through Thursday afternoon. If you're even *thinking* about hitting trails near last year's burn scars, or any canyon really, you're asking for trouble. Debris flows are a very real, very dangerous possibility. Your "sunny morning run" could turn into a flash flood scenario faster than you can say "rescue helicopter."</AlertBox>
*   <AlertBox type="warning">Even if you dodge the deluge, your lungs aren't getting a free pass. The Air Quality Index is sitting at a moderate 69, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. While there isn't a major active wildfire pumping smoke directly into the basin right now, the ghost of the Bouquet Fire (which caused air quality issues in Santa Clarita earlier this month) and other recent blazes can contribute to lingering particulate matter. Mix that with a moderate pollen count, and anyone with sensitivities is going to feel it.</AlertBox>
*   <AlertBox type="info">Beyond the immediate weather, don't ignore the lasting impacts of previous events. Trails in Eaton Canyon, for example, are *still* closed through December 2027 thanks to the January 2025 Eaton Fire. That includes sections of the Altadena Crest Trail and the Eaton Canyon Trail itself. Likewise, portions of Angeles Crest Highway (Highway 2) have been under repair for months due to winter storm damage. Always check local park and trail websites for current closures before you even lace up your shoes. This isn't just about avoiding a muddy run; it's about staying out of unstable, dangerous areas.</AlertBox>

## Bottom Line

Look, I love hitting the trails as much as the next masochist, but today's a day for smart choices. The temperature is a pleasant 16.5°C, but that's a red herring. The forecast is for heavy rain and a genuine risk of localized flooding and debris flows. Stick to the treadmill, hit the gym, or get your yoga in indoors. If you absolutely *must* be outside, keep it short, stay on paved, non-flooding urban routes, and for the love of all that is holy, stay far away from canyons and known burn areas. This isn't a suggestion; it's a warning.
