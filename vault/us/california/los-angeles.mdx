## The Good, The Bad, and The Gritty

Look, the immediate weather is fantastic. You've got clear skies, a gentle breeze, and none of that choking wildfire smoke that's been a nightmare in past seasons. Your lungs are getting a break today, especially with the AQI firmly in the "good" category.

| Pollen Type | Level |
| :---------- | :---- |
| Overall     | Moderate |

Now, about that "moderate" pollen. For some of us, that's just a mild annoyance, a sniffle or two. For others, it's a full-blown assault. If you're one of the unfortunate souls who turns into a snot factory at the mere mention of pollen, consider an indoor session or at least hit those allergy meds hard before you head out. Don't be that person wheezing through Griffith Park.

### Hydration is Still Your Best Friend

Even though today isn't a scorcher, let's not forget that Los Angeles *just* crawled out of an "unprecedented" March heatwave. We saw record-shattering temperatures recently, with some areas hitting triple digits, which is just insane for March. There was even a tragic incident yesterday, March 29, where an LA County sheriff's deputy died during a relay race in the Mojave Desert, with extreme heat being a contributing factor. So, while the immediate temperature is mild, your body remembers. Hydrate like it's 100 degrees out, because it very well could be again next week.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip:** Always carry more water than you think you need. Dehydration sneaks up on you faster than a ticket on the 405.
</AlertBox>

### Construction & Trail Troubles Persist

This city is always under construction, and your run is probably going to hit a snag somewhere. Beyond the usual street closures, there are some specific headaches to be aware of if you're hitting the trails or main thoroughfares.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Alert:** Expect ongoing intermittent overnight street closures on **Cahuenga Boulevard East** for pavement rehabilitation through the end of March. Also, a lane on westbound **SR-134** is continuously closed near the I-5 connector for a sewer rehabilitation project until April. Plan your routes accordingly, especially if you're commuting to your workout spot.
</AlertBox>

For those looking to escape the concrete jungle, good luck fully escaping construction. More importantly, check your trail conditions *before* you go. While some previously closed trails in **Topanga State Park**, like Trailer Canyon and East Topanga Fire Road, seem to be open now, other parts of our local wilderness are still a mess from past fires and storms. Much of the **Angeles National Forest** still has closures from incidents going back to last year, with some roads and trails remaining off-limits. Don't be the idiot who jumps a "trail closed" sign and ends up needing a helicopter rescue. It ties up resources and makes you look like a tool.

### Watch Out for Leftovers

While the big "No Kings" protests were largely yesterday and the day before, mainly centered downtown, there's always a chance of residual activity or clean-up impacting your usual running routes in urban areas. Keep an eye on local news for any lingering street closures if you're pounding the pavement near City Hall or Grand Park.

Today's a solid day to get moving, just don't assume clear sailing everywhere. Do your homework, listen to your body, and for god's sake, bring some water.
