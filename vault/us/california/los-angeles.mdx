## Today's Rundown

Let's be honest, the air in Los Angeles today is almost suspiciously clean. With an AQI of a mere 18, you can actually take a deep breath without feeling like you're sucking down car exhaust or wildfire ash. The dominant pollutant is listed as "Ozone/Other" but at this level, who cares? You won't find better conditions for your lungs in this city very often. And at a balmy 16.5°C (that's pushing 62°F for the Fahrenheit crowd) under mainly clear skies, the weather gurus really outdid themselves. This is prime running, hiking, and biking weather.

Now, for your joints and general safety, there are a few things to keep in mind. Yesterday, April 17th, much of the LA area, including the Eastern San Gabriel Mountains and the I-5 Corridor, was under various wind advisories with gusts up to 55 mph. While those advisories expired yesterday afternoon, that kind of wind can leave behind downed branches, loose rocks, and other fun surprises on trails. So, if you're heading for the hills, especially popular spots like sections of the **Altadena Crest Trail** or the **Western Santa Monica Mountains Recreational Area**, maintain situational awareness. Don't be that person who trips over a fresh branch because you're glued to your Strava.

## Long-Term Trail Alerts & Permanent Headaches

This city has some amazing trails, but sometimes it feels like half of them are perpetually closed. And honestly, some closures are for the long haul.

<AlertBox type="danger">
Many trails in the **Angeles National Forest** and **Eaton Canyon** remain closed due to damage from the January 2025 Eaton Fire. These closures are extensive and will last until at least December 31, 2027, to allow for natural recovery. This includes significant portions of the **Gabrielino National Recreation Trail** and sections of Mount Wilson Toll Road. Don't be a hero; respect the barricades. Fines exist, and frankly, you're just messing up the recovery for everyone else if you ignore them.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
Looking ahead, be aware of intermittent weekday closures on the northern segment of **Marshall Canyon Trail** from April to June 2026. This is for pest management (Goldspotted Oak Borer) and tree work. The official start date is the "week of 4/20/2026", so while not today, it's coming up fast. Check the LA County Parks website before you plan your long runs there.
</AlertBox>

So, go enjoy the fantastic air and weather. Just pick your path wisely, keep an eye on the ground, and maybe pack some tissues.
