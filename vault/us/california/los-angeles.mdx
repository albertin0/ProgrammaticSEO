## Today's Rundown: Clear Skies, Hidden Headaches

Look, it's 16.5°C and "Mainly Clear" out there, which sounds like a dream, right? And the Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at a nice, clean 18. Your lungs are probably giving you a thumbs-up. But as always in LA, there's a catch, or three, if you're actually planning to *move* your body.

### Air Quality & Allergens

The good news is the general air quality is decent, but don't get too comfortable. A "Golden Fire" was reported in Los Angeles County early this morning. While the AQI hasn't jumped yet, smoke shifts, and that could change. Keep an eye on it, especially if you're hitting any trails further east. For those of us with seasonal misery, pollen levels are moderate today, so if you usually end up a snotty, wheezing mess after a long run, you've been warned.

### Wind & Trail Warnings

You're probably feeling a bit of a breeze, and it's more than just a refreshing gust. There's a wind advisory for some of our valleys and mountain communities, with gusts potentially hitting 45-50 mph in spots like the Antelope Valley and the Santa Susana mountains. If you're planning a mountain trail run or a hilly bike ride, especially anywhere exposed, prepare to fight for every step or pedal stroke. Gusts like that can be genuinely dangerous, knocking you off balance or sending debris your way.

Speaking of trails, remember those *long-term* closures? They're still very much in effect. The Eaton Canyon area, including the popular Eaton Canyon Trail and Altadena Crest Trail, remains closed until at least December 31, 2027, due to recovery from the January 2025 Eaton Fire. And if you were hoping to drive to some trailheads, Chaney Trail Road is also closed to vehicular traffic until fall 2026. The Angeles National Forest has a "VERY HIGH" fire danger level right now, so respect those closures.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Ongoing Fire Risk:** The Angeles National Forest is under a "VERY HIGH" fire danger. Stick to open, paved areas if you can, and absolutely do not attempt to bypass any trail closures.
</AlertBox>

### Construction Chaos

If you thought the freeways were just for driving, think again. They affect our whole outdoor experience. Caltrans has a host of overnight lane and ramp closures happening *tonight* through the weekend, especially on the I-405 through the Sepulveda Pass for pavement rehabilitation. This means potential construction-related dust and noise if you're running or cycling anywhere near the freeway, and definitely makes getting to open trailheads a headache. Plan your routes to avoid freeway-adjacent paths or expect delays.

Also, a heads-up for long-range planners: sections of the San Gabriel River and Rio Hondo Bike Paths are set to close starting October 1st and will be shut down for *years* due to Whittier Narrows Dam construction. Start figuring out your detours now.

<AlertBox type="info">
**LA Marathon Clarification:** Before you panic about road closures, the 2026 Los Angeles Marathon already happened back in March. Today's date (September 25th) has no marathon-related closures. Breathe easy, downtown runners.
</AlertBox>

So, enjoy the mild weather, but keep your head on a swivel. Check your preferred running apps for real-time wind and air quality updates, respect those long-term trail closures, and pick your routes wisely to avoid construction zones.
