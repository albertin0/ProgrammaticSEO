## The Good, The Bad, and The Wet

Look, the numbers today are pretty solid. Your L.A. lungs usually get a raw deal, but with an AQI of 18, you could practically bottle this air and sell it. The 14.3°C (about 58°F) is practically winter for us, meaning you can push those long runs without feeling like you're baking. But here's the catch, because there's always a catch in this city.

### Coastal Chaos: Don't Be a Hero, Seriously.

If your usual Sunday long run involves the strand down by Santa Monica or a morning spin near Venice Beach, you need to re-route. Immediately. We're looking at "exceptionally high tides" and "significant coastal flooding" hitting the Southern California coastline. This isn't just a little extra water; forecasters are talking about 8-foot tides early next week, and "significant-to-moderate beach erosion" and "isolated damage of structures" are on the table for our county.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Coastal Flood Warning!** Avoid beach trails and low-lying coastal areas from today through mid-week. Those elevated tides could sweep you off your feet or leave you scrambling. Your workout isn't worth becoming a news story.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble: Still Waiting on Mother Nature

For those of you who prefer dirt over asphalt, the Angels National Forest and adjacent areas are still a minefield of closures. The Eaton Canyon area, for example, is still largely shut down until late 2027 thanks to that fire back in '25. That means no Sam Merrill, no Echo Mountain, and parts of the Mount Wilson Toll Road are still off-limits. And if you were eyeing the Pacific Crest Trail near Williamson Rock, forget it – also closed for resource protection. Check your trail apps before you head out, or you'll be driving in circles looking for an open trailhead.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures are Widespread:** Many popular hiking and running trails in the Angeles National Forest and Eaton Canyon remain closed due to fire recovery and rockslides. Respect all signage; fines are hefty, and nature doesn't care about your Strava segment.
</AlertBox>

### Urban Obstacles: The Never-Ending Grind

Out in the concrete jungle, it's business as usual: construction. The Metro Purple Line Extension is still chewing up Wilshire Boulevard in Beverly Hills, so expect sidewalk detours and potential lane reductions if you're navigating that stretch on foot or bike. And down by LAX, roadway construction is causing lane closures on Century Blvd and around the airport's Central Terminal Area through mid-November. If you're running near the airport, just assume everything is under construction.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Plan Your Route (Especially Downtown & Near LAX):** Metro construction on Wilshire Blvd. and ongoing airport renovations mean detours, traffic, and general headaches. Check real-time traffic if your route takes you through these areas.
</AlertBox>

And let's not forget the cultural events. Today is seeing several Día de los Muertos festivals across the city, from Long Beach to San Pedro. While not direct workout hazards, these mean localized road closures and higher pedestrian traffic in those neighborhoods. Good for culture, bad for your mile splits.
