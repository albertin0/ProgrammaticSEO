Today is absolutely NOT the day to push yourself outside unless you're planning an indoor workout, because LA is officially an oven.

## Why today is/isn't the day

<BulletList>
-   **Triple-Digit Heat Wave is Peaking:** Forget that cool morning breeze. Today marks the absolute *peak* of a brutal, extended heat wave. We're talking temperatures soaring into the triple digits across much of the city, with valleys hitting 105 degrees (40.5°C) and downtown areas baking at 100 degrees (37.7°C). Even coastal spots, usually our saving grace, will be sweltering in the mid-90s (around 35°C). The Los Angeles County Department of Public Health has issued an Extreme Heat Warning, and they're not messing around.
-   **Trail Closures & Fire Risk:** This isn't just uncomfortable; it's dangerous. Because of the extreme heat, local trails in Burbank like **Stough Canyon** and **Wildwood Canyon** are officially *closed* today, and through Sunday, October 4th. The Angeles National Forest is also under a "VERY HIGH" fire danger level, which means you should probably steer clear of any remote trails if you don't want to become kindling.
-   **Pollen & Air Quality:** The good news? Our air quality is surprisingly good with an AQI of 18, and the dominant pollutant is just "Ozone/Other." Pollen is at a moderate level, so your lungs aren't fighting a losing battle against allergens. It's just the heat that's going to kill you.

</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning!

Today is not the day for heroics. High temperatures are dangerously hot. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity between 10 AM and 6 PM. If you absolutely MUST be outside, keep it short, stay hydrated, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, listen to your body. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real and can be deadly. Don't be that person.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail Closures & Fire Danger

Many popular hiking spots, including Stough Canyon and Wildwood Canyon, are *closed* due to the heat. Beyond that, the elevated fire danger in areas like the Angeles National Forest means even open trails carry a significant risk. Also, remember that parts of Eaton Canyon and other Angeles National Forest trails have been closed since the 2025 Eaton Fire and remain off-limits until late 2027 for natural recovery and public safety. Check local park websites *before* you head out.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Hydrated and Plan Ahead

Drink water constantly, even if you don't feel thirsty. Consider moving your workout indoors or rescheduling for another day. If you're really itching for some fresh air, hit the pavement *before* 8 AM or *after* 7 PM, but even then, be cautious.

Speaking of fresh air and nature, the "LA Nature Quest" kicks off today at **Griffith Park**, encouraging Angelenos to photograph local flora and fauna. While that sounds lovely, remember Griffith Park is still in Los Angeles and subject to the same scorching conditions. Keep any nature quests to the earliest morning hours.
</AlertBox>
