## Why today isn't the day:

<BulletList>
*   **Your Lungs Will Hate You:** Despite what some general readings might tell you (like that AQI of 18), large swaths of Los Angeles County, particularly central and southern areas, are dealing with *hazardous* air quality until at least 3 p.m. today. That fireworks "spectacular" from last night dumped a load of particle pollution into our already questionable atmosphere, and it's still hanging around. Forget a PR, just breathe.
*   **Trail Traps:** Don't expect your usual Sunday morning stroll through Runyon Canyon Park. Early closures were in effect all weekend, ending today at 5 p.m. Similar restrictions hit Jerome C. Daniel Overlook and Universal City Overlook. Always check park websites before you head out, because nothing says "fun run" like a locked gate.
*   **Beach Bummer:** Thinking of cooling off by the ocean? Some beach water warnings were issued ahead of the Fourth, with officials citing elevated bacteria levels. Best to assume the water's still a petri dish and stick to a cold shower.

## Local Hazards Rundown:

The clear skies and mild 16.5°C (61.7°F) might look inviting, but don't let that fool you. The biggest offender today is the post-fireworks air quality. What we're seeing on July 5th is the lingering effect of yesterday's celebrations. Pollution levels were expected to peak Saturday evening and persist through Sunday afternoon. This isn't just a mild inconvenience; exposure to this particle pollution can trigger chronic coughs, asthma flare-ups, and even heart attacks.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Air Quality Advisory: Hazardous Particle Pollution
The South Coast Air Quality Management District warned of hazardous air quality in many areas of Los Angeles County through 3 p.m. today due to fireworks pollution. This includes downtown Los Angeles and the San Gabriel Valley. Stay indoors, keep windows closed, and avoid strenuous outdoor activity until the advisory lifts.
</AlertBox>

On top of the firework fallout, multiple *new wildfires* were reported in Los Angeles County on July 4th. While containment information is currently unknown for these blazes, any smoke from these fires would only exacerbate the already poor air quality. Keep an eye on local news for updates, especially if you're hitting trails further inland.

If you planned on a scenic bike ride, remember that a section of the Marvin Braude Coastal Bike Trail, specifically near the UCLA Marina Aquatics Center down to the La Ballona Bridge, was closed last night due to Marina del Rey fireworks. While the fireworks are over, expect residual traffic and possibly some lingering pedestrian congestion in that area as things return to normal. Extensive street closures around Marina del Rey for the fireworks meant a lot of people were out and about.
