## The "Perfect" Day is a Lie, Especially in LA

Alright, Los Angeles, it's June 10th, and the weather app probably told you it's "Mainly Clear" with a comfortable 16.5°C (62°F) and an AQI of 18. Sounds delightful, right? A runner's dream. But if you've been around here longer than a minute, you know that "clear" can turn "hazy" faster than a tourist can block a bike lane on the Santa Monica Pier.

### Wildfire Watch: Don't Breathe Easy Yet

Despite that lovely low AQI number, let's get real. Multiple *new* wildfires were reported yesterday across Los Angeles County. We're talking LAC-201277, LAC-200548, LAC-200492, LAC-200443, all just kicking off with unknown containment. Smoke is fickle; it travels. Just because you can't smell it from your perfectly filtered apartment in Santa Monica doesn't mean it won't be in your lungs when you hit a higher elevation trail later. If you can smell or see smoke, even faintly, take your workout indoors. Your lungs are not a smoke filter.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Wildfire Smoke Risk:** New fires in LA County mean conditions can change rapidly. Keep an eye on local news for smoke plume movements, especially if you're headed to the hills or canyon trails. When in doubt, hit the gym.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: Closed Means CLOSED

Speaking of trails, some of your favorites are still a no-go, and for good reason. **Eaton Canyon** and many surrounding trails in the Angeles National Forest are under long-term closure, some extending through December 31, 2027, thanks to the 2025 Eaton Fire. That includes sections of the Altadena Crest Trail and the paved Chaney Trail Road, which is closed to vehicles until Fall 2026. And for those of you who frequent the west side, don't even think about hitting the **Runyon Canyon West Trail** – it's closed indefinitely. Oh, and if Marshall Canyon is your jam, expect intermittent weekday closures through June for oak tree pest management. These aren't suggestions, folks, they're for your safety and the land's recovery. Respect them.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Ignored Trail Closures are Dumb:** Seriously, don't be that person jumping fences or ducking under "CLOSED" signs. These closures are often due to unstable terrain, fire damage, or active maintenance. It's not just about getting a ticket; it's about not getting hurt, or worse, becoming a search and rescue statistic.
</AlertBox>
