And the Air Quality Index is a sweet, sweet 18. That's practically mountain air without having to drive an hour. So, for your lungs, it's a fantastic day to get out there.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Pollen Alert for the Allergy-Prone
While the air quality is excellent, the pollen count is sitting at "Moderate." If you're usually sniffling and sneezing your way through spring, grab your meds before you hit the pavement. Nobody wants to finish a tempo run looking like they just cried through a rom-com.
</AlertBox>

Now, for your joints, and frankly, your sanity, we need to talk about what's *not* open. If your usual grind takes you up into the Angeles National Forest or around Eaton Canyon, you're still going to be out of luck on a lot of popular spots. The 2025 Eaton Fire did a number on those trails, and significant closures are lingering until December 31, 2027, for public safety and land recovery. That means areas like the Altadena Crest Trail and parts of the Eaton Canyon Trail itself are off-limits. Even Chaney Trail Road, which leads into Millard Canyon, is closed to cars until "Spring 2026" for guardrail repairs, though you might be able to hike or bike in.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Persistent Trail Closures Ahead!
Do NOT assume your favorite mountain trails are open, especially in the Angeles National Forest and Eaton Canyon areas. Emergency repairs are still closing sections of SR 2 (Angeles Crest Highway), and many fire-damaged trails remain inaccessible. Always double-check before you head out, or you'll be doing an impromptu parking lot workout.
</AlertBox>

If you're planning a scenic drive and a hike, also note that SR 2 (Angeles Crest Highway) has emergency repairs closing sections from Newcomb's Ranch to Big Pines. Westbound traffic is also reduced near the 405 due to construction. Don't be that person stuck in traffic when you could be out enjoying this glorious weather.

For those sticking to the urban sprawl, you're golden. Griffith Park's main fire roads are wide open, as are most city parks for general recreation. Just remember to check for local construction if your route is heavily street-based, as LA County Public Works has a number of active projects, some ending as early as tomorrow, May 14th, like the Eaton Wash Dam Inlet Works Emergency Restoration Project.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration and Sunscreen: Your LA Essentials
Even on cooler, clear days, the sun here is no joke. Lather up with SPF and keep that water bottle handy. A little common sense goes a long way to avoiding a miserable mid-run bonk or a lobster-red sunburn.
</AlertBox>

In short: great day for a run, but if you're hitting the trails, do your homework first.
