## The Air: Clear... mostly.

Look, I'm usually the first to tell you your lungs are in for a bad time here in the city of smog, but the general AQI for Los Angeles County is sitting pretty at a shockingly low 18. That's "Good" territory, folks. The dominant pollutant listed as "Ozone/Other" really isn't making much of a dent today, so go ahead and take a deep breath – *if* you're not near the problem spots.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Smoke Alert: Santa Monica Mountains & Boyle Heights
Hold your horses, trail runners in the Santa Monica Mountains. The Mountains Recreation and Conservation Authority (MRCA), along with the LA County Fire Department, has a prescribed plot burn scheduled for *today*, June 26, in the Santa Monica Mountains near Santa Maria Road and Dirt Mulholland Road. Expect smoke and temporary trail closures in that immediate vicinity. If you can see or smell smoke, your workout is officially canceled. Seriously, turn around. And for those of you in East Boyle Heights, there's still a lingering smoke advisory from that building fire last week. Stay indoors if you're sensitive.
</AlertBox>

## Your Joints: Mostly Happy, But Watch Your Step

With the mercury hovering around 16.5°C (that's a lovely 61.7°F for the Fahrenheit faithful) and mainly clear conditions, your joints are probably doing a little happy dance. No extreme heat to cook your tendons, no rain to make things slippery. Just about perfect running weather.

However, don't think you can just wander anywhere.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Trail Closures are a Pain in the... everything.
If your plan involved hitting up Marshall Canyon Trail today, think again. Intermittent weekday closures are in effect through June for some Goldspotted Oak Borer pest management. So unless you want to run into a hazmat suit and an angry arborist, find an alternate route. And as a reminder, Eaton Canyon and parts of the Angeles National Forest are *still* closed for recovery from those 2025 fires. It's 2026, people, and those trails are still off-limits. Plan accordingly.
</AlertBox>
