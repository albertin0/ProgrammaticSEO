## Your Lungs

The AQI of 18 might look fantastic on paper, but if you've been living under a rock, the recent Boyle Heights warehouse inferno choked a huge swath of the city with "astronomically high levels of smoke and soot" just last month. While the *acute* smoke from that particular blaze might have dissipated, the fact that we're seeing *new* wildfires pop up in the county means you need to stay vigilant.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Despite today's "Good" AQI, a new wildfire was reported in Los Angeles County yesterday, July 1st. Keep an eye out for any visible smoke plumes, especially if you're heading east or into the hills. If you smell smoke or see ash, cut your workout short and get inside.
</AlertBox>

Pollen is moderate. For most of you, that's just an annoying tickle, but if you're prone to seasonal allergies, make sure your meds are up to date. Nobody wants to blow snot rockets through their entire run.

## Your Joints & Routes

The mild weather is perfect for hitting the pavement or the trails, but you gotta know where you're actually allowed to go. A lot of our beloved trails in the Angeles National Forest are still recovering from the January 2025 Eaton Fire.

<AlertBox type="danger">
Many trails and roads in the Angeles National Forest, including the popular Eaton Canyon Trail and sections of the Mount Wilson Toll Road, remain **closed until December 31, 2027**, due to wildfire damage and recovery efforts. The Williamson Rock area is also closed until the end of this year. Don't be "that person" who ignores the signs and risks injury or impedes recovery.
</AlertBox>

If you were planning a run through Eaton Canyon, find an alternative. Seriously, there's nothing worse than getting stoked for a long run only to be met with a "TRAIL CLOSED" sign. Check the LA County Parks & Recreation website for specific closures before you head out.

<AlertBox type="info">
With the mild temperatures, remember to layer up if you're starting your workout early or finishing late. Coastal areas especially are seeing cooler-than-normal July temps this week. Hydrate, even if it doesn't feel scorchingly hot.
</AlertBox>

Overall, it's a decent day to get out there, just be smart about your route and stay aware of your surroundings, especially with new fire activity in the mix.
