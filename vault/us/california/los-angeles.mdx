Look, I get it. We live in LA. When it rains, it feels like the world is ending, and all your outdoor plans get trashed. But seriously, today's not a day to test your luck. The weather geeks are predicting widespread rain all day, with a decent chance of thunderstorms. That doesn't mean a refreshing sprinkle; it means street flooding is *possible*, and if you're thinking of hitting any dirt trails, you're asking for trouble.

We're looking at a moderate pollen count, so your allergies might be having a moment, but the AQI is surprisingly good at 18. So, your lungs should be happy if they're breathing in *clean* air, not soggy, muddy air with a side of debris.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Flash Flood & Debris Flow Risk!
The National Weather Service is sounding the alarm for potential flooding and dangerous debris flows. This is especially true for areas recently scarred by wildfires. If you usually run trails in places like the Santa Monica Mountains, the Palisades, or Altadena, *do not go there today*. The ground is compromised, and heavy rain can easily trigger mudslides and rockfalls. Seriously, you don't want to be *that* person needing rescue from a washed-out trail.
</AlertBox>

Let's talk about those trail closures, because a lot of you seem to forget these things aren't temporary. For instance, large swathes of the Angeles National Forest, including the popular Sam Merrill Trail, Echo Mountain, and Eaton Canyon trails, are closed until late 2027 due to the aftermath of the Eaton Fire. Today's rain just makes any attempt to skirt these closures even more idiotic. And if you're a cyclist, be aware that sections of the San Gabriel River and Rio Hondo Bike Paths are currently undergoing long-term construction for dam upgrades at Whittier Narrows. Expect detours and potential slowdowns, even if the rain clears up.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Slippery Surfaces Everywhere
Even if you stick to the roads and sidewalks, everything will be slick. Potholes fill up, painted lines become ice rinks, and drivers act like they've never seen rain before. Wear reflective gear, assume every curb is a trip hazard, and shorten your strides. Your joints will thank you later.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### When in Doubt, Stay Out (or Indoors)
If you absolutely *must* get out there, stick to well-lit, familiar, paved routes. Avoid underpasses that might flood, and definitely stay clear of any areas with standing water. Consider a gym workout, an indoor cycling class, or finally tackling that laundry pile. Your safety is more important than hitting your mileage goal today.
</AlertBox>
