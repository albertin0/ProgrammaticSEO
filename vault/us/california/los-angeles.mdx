Look, I love hitting the pavement or the trails as much as the next masochist, but today's an *indoor* day for a lot of you, especially east of the 110. The air quality situation from that Boyle Heights fire is still pretty rough, sending plumes of acrid smoke across the city. The LAFD themselves warned that "the smell of smoke has reached most of the city" and advised everyone to "limit exposure as much as possible." Your lungs aren't going to thank you for pushing it through that haze, regardless of what some generic app might tell you your AQI is. We're also seeing new, uncontained wildfires popping up in Los Angeles County, including one reported early this morning, so things can change fast.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Smoke Alert: Boyle Heights Fire Impact**
A particle pollution advisory is active today due to the ongoing Boyle Heights warehouse fire. If you live in central Los Angeles County, the San Gabriel Valley, or the East San Fernando Valley, or if you can see or smell smoke *anywhere* in the city, **avoid vigorous outdoor physical activity and stay indoors with windows closed.** This isn't just a suggestion; it's a critical health warning, especially for kids, older adults, and anyone with respiratory or heart conditions.
</AlertBox>

Weather-wise, today is actually quite pleasant, with clear skies and temperatures around 16.5°C (about 62°F). Enjoy it from indoors, or in areas completely clear of smoke, because come Tuesday, the heat is cranking up. We're looking at a significant heat wave hitting Southern California, with temperatures soaring into the 90s for downtown LA and potentially hitting triple digits in the Antelope Valley. This isn't just uncomfortable; it elevates fire risk and increases your chance of heat-related illness. A spokesperson for the National Weather Service even explicitly stated, "Do not take your dogs hiking — please" during the upcoming heat. Take that advice seriously, for yourselves and your furry running buddies.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Impending Heat Wave and High Fire Danger**
While today is mild, an extreme heat watch begins Tuesday. Temperatures will climb significantly, reaching the 90s and potentially 100+ degrees in some valleys. Fire danger in the Angeles National Forest is already elevated to "HIGH." Plan your runs for early mornings, carry plenty of water, and seriously reconsider any strenuous outdoor activity from Tuesday onwards until this heat breaks.
</AlertBox>

Finally, if you're looking to hit the trails, remember that some popular spots are still off-limits. For instance, sections of the Marshall Canyon Trail are intermittently closed on weekdays this month for pest management. More severely, trails within Eaton Canyon and the surrounding Angeles National Forest, like portions of the Mount Wilson Toll Road and the Altadena Crest Trail, remain shut down until December 31, 2027, due to recovery efforts from the January 2025 Eaton Fire. Don't be that person who ignores closure signs. They're there for a reason, often involving unstable terrain, ongoing restoration, or even hazardous plants like poodle-dog bush. Always check official park and trail websites before you head out.
