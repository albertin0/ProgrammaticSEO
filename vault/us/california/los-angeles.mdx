<AlertBox type="danger">
### Stay Away From the Beach, Seriously.
If your workout involves the ocean, you need to hit the brakes. The Southern California coast, including Malibu, Long Beach, and San Pedro, is experiencing widespread coastal flooding with "record-breaking tides" that hit as high as 8.02 feet yesterday at the San Pedro tide gauge – the highest since 1923. A Coastal Flood Advisory is in effect through October 29th for much of the area, warning of minor to moderate seawater flooding and significant beach erosion. Don't be that person trying to get a selfie with a flooded parking lot. Stay off those jetties and rock walls; the NWS isn't kidding.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mind the Fire-Related Closures and *New* Blazes
Listen, I know we're always complaining about the same old trails being closed, but some of these closures are still very much in effect. The *entire West Trail at Runyon Canyon* is officially closed indefinitely for habitat rehabilitation after the January 2025 Sunset Fire. Sure, some folks hop the fence, but the signs are clear, and you're just messing with the restoration efforts and risking a fine.

Beyond that, a massive chunk of the Angeles National Forest in the Altadena front country—including sections of the Sam Merrill, Echo Mountain, Mount Lowe, and Eaton Canyon trails—remain closed until December 31, 2027, due to the 2025 Eaton Fire. Don't be a hero; check the Angeles National Forest alerts before you drive up to a locked gate.

And because it's LA, we can't forget there was a *new wildfire reported in Los Angeles County* as recently as last night, October 26, 2026. While the overall AQI is good, localized smoke plumes can pop up fast, especially if you're near a new incident. Keep an eye (and nose) out, particularly if you're hitting trails in the foothills.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Construction & Traffic: The Usual Headache
If you're biking or running near major roadways, expect some overnight construction impacts. Caltrans has overnight lane and ramp closures on SR-91, I-405 near Sepulveda Pass, I-605, and even on State Route 2 in West LA, Hollywood, and Echo Park, stretching through this week. It's mostly traffic management, not trail blocking, but stay aware if you're out late or early on those routes. Also, the Griffith Observatory is closed today (as it is every Monday), but its grounds and park trails are generally open.
</AlertBox>

Enjoy that decent weather, but stick to the open trails and stay far, far away from the coast. Your joints and lungs will thank you.
