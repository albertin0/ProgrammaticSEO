## The Full Rundown

Alright, LA runners, cyclists, and outdoor enthusiasts. Today is one of those days where you really need to be smart, or you'll regret it. The cool morning air in some parts of the city, like near the coast or even in areas of Griffith Park early on, is a total mirage. The National Weather Service has dropped a Heat Advisory on most of Los Angeles County, including the San Fernando Valley, San Gabriel Mountains, and our various inland corridors, until Thursday evening. We're talking low to mid-90s, with some spots flirting with the upper 90s. Running in that kind of heat, especially when combined with elevated humidity, isn't just uncomfortable, it's straight-up dangerous for heat exhaustion and heat stroke, particularly for those of us pushing ourselves.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Afternoon Heat: Not a Game
Seriously, if your workout involves significant effort between noon and 6 PM in any of the inland areas, you're playing with fire. The Los Angeles County Department of Public Health is explicit: avoid vigorous physical activity during the hottest hours. Hydrate aggressively if you absolutely must be out, and know the symptoms of heat illness.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the ongoing saga of the Boyle Heights warehouse fire. That thing started on June 17, and even though the broad AQI is "Good" at 18, the smoke advisory just concluded, and areas near the fire or downwind, like parts of East Los Angeles and the San Gabriel Valley, are still feeling the effects. Winds can shift, and ash can fall. If your lungs are feeling it, or you see/smell smoke, take it indoors. No PR is worth a trip to urgent care for respiratory issues.

### Local Hazards to Watch For

Beyond the heat and the haze, remember those specific closures. If Marshall Canyon is your go-to, remember parts of it are off-limits on weekdays through the end of June due to oak tree pest management. And for our trail runners heading higher, the Angeles National Forest has elevated fire danger and some long-term closures, like the Williamson Rock area and parts of the Pacific Crest Trail, to protect local wildlife.
