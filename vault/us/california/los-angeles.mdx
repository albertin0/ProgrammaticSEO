## The Calm Before the Santa Anas

Enjoy today, seriously. The air quality is sitting pretty at an AQI of 18, which is practically unheard of for LA these days, and the dominant pollutant is just "Ozone/Other" – nothing too nasty to worry about for now. The pollen count is moderate, so if you're usually sneezy, you might feel it, but it's not an extreme day for allergies.

But here’s the rub: a Red Flag Warning is set to kick off tomorrow (Thursday, October 15) at 6 AM and run through Friday, October 16, at 10 PM for the mountain areas of LA County. That means gusty Santa Ana winds, bone-dry humidity, and rising temperatures, creating prime conditions for wildfires to explode. We're talking extreme fire behavior if anything sparks. You’ll also notice temperatures spiking dramatically by Friday, hitting a predicted 35°C (96°F). Keep an eye on your local news; this isn't the time to be playing fast and loose in the foothills.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Impending Fire Danger:** Starting tomorrow, a Red Flag Warning will be in effect for LA County's mountain areas. Avoid anything that could spark a fire and be aware of potential rapid fire spread. This isn't theoretical, folks.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Closures and Urban Obstacles

Even with good weather today, getting your miles in can be a headache thanks to ongoing projects. If your usual route involves the **San Gabriel River Bike Path** or the **Rio Hondo Bike Path**, you’re out of luck on sections of those. They're shut down for years – yes, *years* – due to critical upgrades on the Whittier Narrows Dam, with construction expected to last until 2033. They even closed the connector paths, so detouring is a real pain. You cyclists and runners out there, find alternate routes now or prepare for frustration.

Up in the mountains, portions of the **Angeles National Forest** and trails around **Eaton Canyon** remain under long-term closure from the January 2025 Eaton Fire. Don’t try to sneak in there; the closures are in effect until December 31, 2027, to let the land recover. Chaney Trail Road (paved) is also closed to vehicular traffic until Fall 2026, meaning if you want to access some reopened trails, you might be adding a significant hike just to get to the trailhead.

And for those sticking to urban runs, watch your step. Metro’s D Line Extension work means intermittent lane and sidewalk closures on **Wilshire Boulevard** around the Wilshire/La Cienega area throughout October. Pedestrian detours are in place, but it's not ideal for a smooth workout.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Plan Ahead for Construction:** If you're heading out for a ride or run, especially on the San Gabriel or Rio Hondo Bike Paths, check for detours. These closures are long-term and can disrupt your flow.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Avoid Closed Mountain Trails:** Seriously, respect the closures in the Angeles National Forest and Eaton Canyon. They're there for your safety and to allow fire-damaged areas to heal. Going around them puts you and potential rescuers at risk.
</AlertBox>
