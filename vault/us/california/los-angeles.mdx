## Today's Vitals: A Mixed Bag for Runners

Look, the air quality index is sitting pretty at a cool 18, and the dominant pollutant is just "Ozone/Other," which on a good day here just means you're breathing LA air, not smoke. The 16.5°C "Mainly Clear" conditions right now are actually quite pleasant. If you're out early, before the mercury climbs, you're golden on that front.

However, let's pull back the curtain on what's *really* going on. We just hit the tail end of a predicted record-breaking heat wave that was set to scorch Southern California. While the immediate morning temperature might be a reprieve, the National Weather Service issued a "High Risk for Heat Illness" warning that peaked yesterday, and temperatures are expected to remain "well above normal into next week." Your body doesn't just forget a week of extreme heat overnight. That lingering heat stress is real, and it compounds.

### Trail Closures: Don't Be That Guy Who Ignores the Signs

If your plan was to hit the higher elevations, you need to adjust. Many trails within the Angeles National Forest are currently closed. Specifically, the Mount Baldy area, including popular routes like Mt. Baldy Trail, Devils Backbone Trail, and Icehouse Canyon Trail, are *closed until at least March 20th* for public safety. So much for that summit attempt, champ.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Angeles National Forest - Extensive Trail Closures!**
Several key trails in the Mount Baldy (Mount San Antonio) area are closed until March 20, 2026. This includes popular routes you might think are safe. Beyond that, large swaths of the Eaton Fire burn scar area, including parts of the Mt. Wilson Toll Road, Sam Merrill Trail, and Echo Mountain Trail, remain closed for recovery through December 31, 2027. Ignoring these could land you in trouble or worse. Check the Angeles National Forest and L.A. County Trails websites *before* you head out.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Lingering Heat Risk!**
While the immediate morning temp is mild, Los Angeles has been under a significant heat advisory, with temperatures 20-30 degrees above seasonal norms expected to continue "well into next week." Even if it's 16.5°C now, expect it to heat up considerably later. Hydrate aggressively, and listen to your body.
</AlertBox>
