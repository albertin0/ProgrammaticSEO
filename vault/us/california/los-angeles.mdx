Alright, Angelenos, let's talk brass tacks. You woke up to a pretty damn good day for moving your body outside. We're talking clear skies, a comfortable 11.1°C, and an AQI that’s practically sparkling at 18. If your lungs could give a thumbs-up, they would. This isn't the choking haze we sometimes get, nor the triple-digit furnace that makes running feel like self-punishment. The weather's basically giving you a high-five and telling you to go for it.

However, it wouldn’t be LA without *some* kind of obstacle, would it? While the sky isn’t falling and there aren't any massive wildfires actively raging today (thank the universe for small mercies, unlike those intense days back in January 2025), you absolutely cannot just wing it if you’re heading for the hills.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  Pollen levels are sitting at a "Moderate" right now. If you're one of the unfortunate souls whose nose runs more than you do when the jacarandas are in full bloom, consider grabbing some allergy meds before you head out. Don't let a runny nose turn your personal best into a snot-fest.
</AlertBox>

Here's where it gets real, especially for you trail hounds. The Angeles National Forest and its surrounding areas are still a patchwork of closures from the goddamn Eaton Fire that ripped through in January 2025. I know, I know, it was forever ago, but recovery takes time, and those trails are *still* closed. We're talking large chunks of Eaton Canyon Trail, Altadena Crest Trail, and even some roads leading into popular areas like Chaney Trail Road. These aren't temporary "oops, a branch fell" closures; many are set to last through December 2027 to protect both you and the regenerating wildlands. Seriously, check the county parks website before you load up the hydration pack, unless you enjoy staring at "TRAIL CLOSED" signs. Same goes for the Williamson Rock area, which is shut down through the end of the year for resource protection.

And speaking of closures, if Marshall Canyon is your jam, be aware of intermittent weekday shutdowns through June 2026. They're battling some Goldspotted Oak Borer up there, which sounds like something out of a sci-fi horror flick, but it's serious for our native oaks. So if you're planning a mid-week jaunt, double-check that too.

No major marathons or street-blocking CicLAvia events mucking up the city streets today either (CicLAvia—West LA was Sunday, and the marathon isn't today), so road runners, you're mostly clear. Just dodge the usual aggressive drivers and the occasional rogue scooter.

Overall, it's a stellar day for an outdoor sweat session in Los Angeles. Just be smart about your location and respect those closures. Nobody wants to be *that* person who ignores a sign and ends up needing a rescue or, worse, damaging fragile ecosystems.
