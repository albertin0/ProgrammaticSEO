## The Rundown: Fire, Wind, and Closed Trails

Look, I love a good long run in the canyons as much as the next masochist, but today is not the day to test your luck. The National Weather Service has slapped us with a Red Flag Warning until 10 PM tonight, and it's no joke. Those Santa Ana winds are howling, and the air is drier than my sarcasm. This makes for a perfect storm, literally, for wildfires.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  A Red Flag Warning is in effect for Los Angeles and Ventura County. All MRCA parks and trails within the Santa Monica Mountains National Recreation Area, including popular spots like Temescal Gateway Park and sections of the Backbone Trail, are CLOSED. DO NOT enter closed areas. This is for your safety and to prevent new fire ignitions.
</AlertBox>

On top of the fire threat, we're baking. Temperatures are climbing, especially if you're venturing even a few miles from the coast. A "moderate to high risk for heat illness" is a nice way of saying you're going to feel like you're running in an oven, and your lungs won't thank you for it with the particulate matter floating around. While the AQI from our primary data source is 61, some reports have it as high as 106, which is "Unhealthy" for sensitive groups. Better safe than wheezy, I say.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  A Heat Advisory is in effect. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity during peak heat hours (late morning through late afternoon). Stay hydrated if you must be outside, but honestly, just don't push it today.
</AlertBox>

Road closures are also a thing, not just for planned events, but due to fire-related issues. For example, parts of Mulholland Highway and Encinal Canyon Road in the Santa Monica Mountains are shut down because of the "Bryson Fire." Plus, some Eaton Canyon trails are still closed from last year's fire. So, even if you find an open path, chances are it's not going to be the scenic, fresh-air escape you're hoping for.

<AlertBox type="info">
  Consider an indoor workout today. Seriously. Or stick to highly urbanized areas, but keep your outdoor activity short and gentle. Check official sources like NWS Los Angeles/Oxnard and LA County Public Works for real-time updates before you even think about stepping out.
</AlertBox>
