## Current Workout Hazards

### Construction Chaos: The Never-Ending Story

<AlertBox type="warning">
Expect significant traffic delays and potential pedestrian route disruptions due to ongoing major construction projects across the city.
</AlertBox>

Here's where L.A. decides to remind you it's still L.A. You want to get somewhere? Good luck. Caltrans is going full throttle. If you're trying to get anywhere near **Sepulveda Pass**, know that the 405 Freeway is a mess of overnight lane reductions, ramp closures, and connector shutdowns from 9 p.m. to 6 a.m. all week for some pavement rehabilitation. This ain't just cars; if you bike or try to walk across overpasses in the early morning, be aware of what's happening below you.

Down south, the SR-91 from Cerritos to Gardena is also getting a facelift, with overnight lane, connector, and ramp closures through Friday morning. And for anyone who thought they could get some airport-adjacent strides in, think again. LAX roadway construction on Century and Sepulveda Boulevards means ongoing daytime and nighttime work, with sidewalk closures in the mix. So much for that scenic stroll to baggage claim. Even Wilshire Boulevard is seeing lane reductions and sidewalk impacts due to subway station construction. Factor in the extra time or find an alternative route, unless you enjoy the smell of asphalt and the symphony of jackhammers with your workout.

### Coastal Erosion: The Aftermath of Marie

<AlertBox type="info">
While the extreme surf from Hurricane Marie has passed, coastal areas may still have damaged walkways, eroded beaches, and compromised access points. Check local park alerts before heading to the beach.
</AlertBox>

Remember last week when Hurricane Marie decided to give our coastline a beating? High surf stripped sand from beaches, messed up walkways, and even took out parts of the historic boardwalk on the Long Beach Peninsula. While the immediate threat of huge waves has subsided, some beach access points and coastal trails might still be dealing with the aftermath. Officials were evacuating homes and red-tagging others. So if your plan was a serene beach run, you might find more debris than tranquility. Head down there, but keep your eyes peeled for closures and unstable ground, especially if you're venturing out to places like Malibu where coastal erosion was significant.

Stay smart out there, L.A.
