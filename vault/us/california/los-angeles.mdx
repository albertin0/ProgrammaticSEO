Alright, Los Angeles, today's shaping up to be a pretty decent day to get outside, assuming you're not planning on hitting any of our perpetually-closed trails.

<BulletList>
*   **Air Quality is surprisingly good.** Despite a couple of wildfires still kicking around, the general AQI across most of the basin is sitting pretty low, meaning your lungs get a break.
*   **The weather is prime.** At a cool 16.5°C and "Mainly Clear," it's that rare sweet spot where you won't melt or freeze, making it ideal for joints.
*   **Pollen's a nuisance, not a crisis.** "Moderate" levels mean allergy sufferers should prep, but it's not going to ruin your run unless you're super sensitive.
*   **Wildfire smoke is largely contained**, but keep an eye on localized conditions, especially if you're heading out to the eastern fringes of the county.

Look, I'm not going to lie, after the last week, I half expected to tell you to just stay home and watch reruns. We had that Springs Fire out in Riverside County, which was smoky and fueled by some pretty nasty Santa Ana winds, and then a smaller Crown Fire in our own backyard near Acton. Good news is, fire crews have mostly tamed them, with the Springs Fire about 75% contained by yesterday afternoon and the Crown Fire at 74%. The winds that were fanning those flames have also "dissipated a bit" since Friday, which is always a plus.

But here’s the kicker, always, in this city: our trails. Specifically, if you were eyeing a scenic hike in **Eaton Canyon**, forget about it. Those trails and surrounding areas, still recovering from the January 2025 Eaton Fire, are locked down tighter than a Kardashian's pre-nup, and will be until at least December 31, 2027. Even Chaney Trail Road, a major access point, is closed to cars until "Spring 2026," which, by the way, is *now*. So, unless you're planning a multi-mile trek just to *get* to the trailhead, you're out of luck. The Angeles National Forest also has a smattering of long-term closures due to past fires and resource protection, so don't just blindly head out there either. Seriously, check the Angeles National Forest alerts before you go, unless you enjoy running into "closed for your safety" signs after an hour's drive.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures are Widespread!** Many popular hiking spots in the Angeles National Forest and Eaton Canyon remain closed for rehabilitation or safety. Trying to bypass these closures is not only dangerous due to unstable terrain, but can also lead to fines.
</AlertBox>

For those of you sticking to the urban jungle or the beach paths, you're golden. The air quality index is a happy 18, so your lungs are clear to work as hard as you want. Just remember to dodge the ongoing Metro D Line extension construction if you're trying to sprint down Wilshire Boulevard – that mess is still very much a thing, with phase one wrapping up this month and phase two expected by late spring.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Allergy Alert:** Pollen levels are moderate today. If you're usually affected, grab your meds before you head out, especially if you're planning on pushing serious mileage.
</AlertBox>

So, lace up, hit the pavement, or find an *open* trail. Just don't go wandering into closed areas expecting a heroic adventure. You'll just end up with a twisted ankle and a ticket.
