## The Daily Rundown

Alright, LA, let's talk. Today, August 12, 2026, is serving up some prime running weather, especially for mid-August. We're looking at a glorious 16.5°C (that's about 62°F for the Fahrenheit folks) and "Mainly Clear" skies. The AQI is a beautiful 18, which means your lungs get a vacation from all the usual city grime. "Ozone/Other" is the dominant pollutant, but at that level, it's basically background noise. So, if your allergies aren't flaring from the "Moderate" pollen levels, your respiratory system is getting a gold star today.

However, Los Angeles never gives you a free pass without some strings attached.

### Coastal Chaos & Mountain Mischief

If your workout involves anything near the ocean, ditch that plan for a bit. There were Coastal Flood Advisories yesterday, August 11, for our Southern California coastline, including your favorite Los Angeles County Beaches. Expect "localized flooding in vulnerable shoreline locations," which translates to submerged bike paths, wet parking lots, and generally unpleasant conditions for anything other than a very slow, careful stroll. This minor coastal flooding risk, thanks to those high tides, is hanging around through tonight. Don't be that person who finds out the hard way that seawater and running shoes don't mix.

<AlertBox type="danger">
Coastal flooding is still a concern today, especially around high tide, affecting beaches, parking lots, and low-lying coastal paths. Stay off flooded areas.
</AlertBox>

Head inland to the mountains, and you might run into a different kind of wet mess. Yesterday saw Flash Flood Warnings for parts of LA County, like Lockwood Valley, due to thunderstorms. While that specific warning has expired, the National Weather Service is still talking about chances for isolated thunderstorms through Wednesday night in the "interior mountains and Antelope Valley," potentially bringing heavy showers, strong gusts up to 50 mph, and even isolated lightning strikes that *could* kick off small fires. If you're heading up to the Angeles National Forest, keep an eye on the sky.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Isolated thunderstorms are possible in the interior mountains and Antelope Valley through tonight, bringing a risk of heavy showers, localized strong winds, and potential lightning-sparked fires.
</AlertBox>

### Trail & Road Blocks

Speaking of the Angeles National Forest, remember those old friends, the Eaton Fire Area closures? Yeah, they're still in effect, through December 31, 2027. That means parts of the Altadena Crest Trail and Eaton Canyon Trail are still closed for public safety and environmental recovery. Don't go trying to find a workaround; you'll just be wasting your time and possibly getting yourself into trouble. Chaney Trail Road is also closed to vehicular traffic until Fall 2026.

Closer to the city, our beloved Griffith Park has some vehicular restrictions: Fern Dell Road is closed at Los Feliz Boulevard from 6 a.m. to 8 p.m., and Griffith Observatory parking is still shut off for cars. Foot traffic is usually fine, but be aware if you're driving to a trailhead.

Then there's the delightful symphony of road construction. If your route takes you anywhere near the docks, the Vincent Thomas Bridge on State Route 47 has daytime single-lane closures for pavement repairs (9 a.m. to 2 p.m. westbound) and overnight full closures for deck replacement through Friday morning (10:45 p.m. to 6:45 a.m.). Down south, the Los Angeles County Sanitation District is doing work on Palos Verdes Drive South from Schooner Drive to the landslide area from 4-11 a.m. today and tomorrow, so expect delays there. And let's not forget the never-ending saga around LAX, where construction continues with lane restrictions and closures all month.

<AlertBox type="info">
Many trails in the Angeles National Forest, particularly those impacted by the Eaton Fire (e.g., Eaton Canyon Trail, Altadena Crest Trail), remain closed through 2027. Check official sites before heading out.
</AlertBox>
