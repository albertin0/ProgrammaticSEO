Look, for March 25th, you've got it pretty good in Los Angeles. The mercury is sitting at a comfortable 16.5°C (that's about 61°F for the Fahrenheit crowd), and it's mainly clear out there. The forecast says we'll top out around 24°C (75°F) today, so no excuses about it being too hot or too cold. The AQI is a fantastic 18, so breathe deep – no nasty pollutants messing with your hard work.

But let's be real, it's never *perfect* in this city. While the weather isn't trying to kill you, other factors always are. We've got moderate pollen levels, so if you're one of the many Angelenos who gets hit by the spring sniffles, consider that your pre-run antihistamine reminder. Don't be that person hacking up a lung halfway up the *Baldwin Hills Scenic Overlook* staircase. Nobody needs that soundtrack to their suffering.

Now, for the actual hazards that might trip you up (literally). Those devastating fires from last year and the beginning of this year are still impacting our beloved trails. If you were planning on tackling anything in **Eaton Canyon**, forget about it. The trails and surrounding areas are still closed, and they'll stay that way until at least December 31, 2027, thanks to the January 2025 Eaton Fire damage. This includes popular spots like the Altadena Crest Trail. Even Chaney Trail Road is closed to vehicular traffic until spring 2026, so even getting close is a pain. Don't be a hero; those lands need to recover, and you need to avoid falling debris or unstable ground.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures are NOT a suggestion.** Stick to open trails. Many areas in Eaton Canyon and parts of the Angeles National Forest remain off-limits due to wildfire damage, some until late 2027. Respect the signs; it's for your safety and the ecosystem's recovery.
</AlertBox>

Good news for some of you canyon-lovers: the **Runyon Canyon West Trail** *did* reopen this week after its closure from the January 2026 Sunset Fire. However, the L.A. Parks website apparently still lists it as closed, and "certain areas along the trail remain closed to the public". So, if you're heading there, exercise caution and be prepared for potential detours or unexpected closures within the park. It's not a free-for-all just yet.

Construction is still L.A.'s middle name. The DWP has a major project near the 405 & Rinaldi and along Blucher Ave that just started last week, so expect traffic delays there for the next six months. If your workout involves cycling or running along those streets, plan your routes accordingly. And the Metro D Line Subway construction continues to be a saga, with "UCLA and VA still enormous holes in the ground". While that's more of a traffic headache, remember that construction sites can mean unexpected sidewalk closures or detours for pedestrians and cyclists too.
