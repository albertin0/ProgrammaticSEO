Today, while temperatures are expected to drop a few degrees, an extreme heat warning remains in effect for the Antelope Valley until 8 p.m., and a less severe (but still serious) heat advisory covers the Santa Clarita, San Gabriel, and San Fernando valleys, along with the entire Santa Monica Mountains Recreational Area. If you're in any of those areas, that morning crispness is a distant memory by 9 AM. Your 16.5°C (61.7°F) current temperature is not indicative of the daytime reality, especially if you're not on the immediate coast.

<AlertBox type="danger">

### Extreme Heat Warning & Advisory

If you're heading out to the Antelope Valley, don't. An extreme heat warning means dangerously hot weather. For the valleys and mountains (like the Santa Monica Mountains Recreational Area), we're still under a heat advisory. Drink water like it's your job, stay in the shade, and for Pete's sake, if you feel dizzy or nauseous, stop. Get inside. Call it a day. Your PR isn't worth heatstroke.

</AlertBox>

On top of the heat, we've got flames kicking up. The Walnut fire near DeBell Golf Club and Wildwood Canyon Park in Burbank means local hiking trails are closed indefinitely, and there was the Tasha fire near Santa Clarita. Even if the city-wide AQI is a cheerful 18, that's not what it's like next to an active blaze. If you see smoke, don't run towards it thinking you're getting a unique workout experience. Turn around.

<AlertBox type="warning">

### Fire-Related Trail Closures & Localized Smoke

Due to ongoing brush fires, hiking trails around Wildwood Canyon Park in Burbank are closed. Expect localized smoke impacts in areas near active fires in Burbank and Santa Clarita. Stay clear of these areas for both air quality and safety reasons.

</AlertBox>

Finally, let's not forget the usual LA mess. Metro is doing paving work at the Wilshire Blvd. & La Cienega Blvd. intersection through Monday morning, so expect headaches if you're trying to navigate that part of town. And those long-term closures in places like Eaton Canyon and parts of the Angeles National Forest? Still very much a thing, some going until 2027. Don't be that person ignoring the signs because you think your trail run is more important than public safety or habitat recovery.

### Ongoing Construction & Permanent Trail Closures

Be mindful of construction in areas like Wilshire/La Cienega. Also, remember that many trails in Eaton Canyon and parts of the Angeles National Forest remain closed from older fires. Check local park and forest service websites before you head out, or you'll be driving a long way just to hit a locked gate.
