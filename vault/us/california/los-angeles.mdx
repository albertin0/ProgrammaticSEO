Alright, Angelenos, let's talk turkey. The good news first: your lungs are in for a treat today. The Air Quality Index is a pristine 18, with Ozone/Other as the dominant pollutant. That's a green light for deep breaths, at least on the air quality front.

Temperatures near the coast, like Santa Monica or Venice, are hovering around a pleasant 16.5°C (61.7°F), which is pretty ideal for a run or ride. However, don't let that fool you into thinking the whole basin is a cool breeze. Head inland, especially to the valleys or desert areas, and you're looking at temps that can climb into the high 80s and even 90s, with low deserts pushing past 100°F. We've had heat alerts across Southern California recently, so this isn't exactly groundbreaking news, but it bears repeating: know your route and check *hyper-local* temps.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip:** Your phone's weather app is good, but if you're hitting trails like those near Calabasas or out in the San Gabriels, check the actual forecast for *that specific area*. Coastal temps are often drastically different from inland.
</AlertBox>

Now for the real buzzkill. While the sky might be "mainly clear," the Weather Prediction Center has issued an **Excessive Rainfall Outlook** for the Los Angeles/Oxnard region, valid through Sunday. This means there's a possibility of rainfall exceeding flash flood guidance. Even with recent rains making some waterways "spring-like" again, this outlook suggests that the ground could be saturated or prone to quick runoff. Trails could be slick, and stream crossings could become hazardous. Don't be that person who gets swept away because you underestimated a "trickle."

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Flash Flood Risk:** Despite clear skies, there's an active Excessive Rainfall Outlook. Steer clear of washes, canyons, and low-lying areas. Water can rise fast and furiously, even if it's not raining directly on your head.
</AlertBox>

On the trail front, some old wounds are still keeping paths closed. The **Eaton Canyon** area and parts of the Angeles National Forest that were scorched by the January 2025 Eaton Fire are still off-limits. This includes popular spots like the Altadena Crest Trail and the Eaton Canyon Trail itself, with closures expected until at least December 31, 2027. And if Runyon Canyon is your jam, the West Trail is currently closed. Do your homework before you head out, or you'll be staring at a "Trail Closed" sign after a sweaty drive.

Finally, pollen is sitting at a "Moderate" level today. If you're sensitive, you might still feel it, so bring your meds.

Overall, it's a decent day if you're smart about where and when you move. Stick to well-maintained, open trails, be mindful of any unusual water flow, and don't try to outrun a flash flood. Also, good luck to everyone participating in the Los Angeles Sunset Relays & 5K happening in Griffith Park today – expect some company on those trails!
