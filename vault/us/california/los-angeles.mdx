### Trail & Road Conditions

If you're planning a mountain escape, prepare for disappointment. Our beloved Angeles Crest Highway (State Route 2) is still a mess. Heavy winter storms from last December absolutely clobbered sections of it, and repairs are ongoing. Specifically, you're looking at closures from about 3.3 miles east of Newcomb's Ranch to State Route 138, and a segment from Islip Saddle to Vincent Gap is also still shut down. This severely impacts access to many high-elevation trails in the San Gabriel Mountains. Don't be that person who tries to sneak around barriers; it's closed for a reason, and you're just putting yourself and rescue teams at risk.

And for those of you who love a quick ascent up Runyon Canyon, a heads-up: the West Trail is still closed until further notice. Seriously, LA City Parks has it listed as closed. There are plenty of other spots to get your climb in without tempting fate on a closed trail.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Road & Trail Closures are REAL:** Angeles Crest Highway (SR-2) remains significantly damaged and closed in key sections. Additionally, Runyon Canyon's West Trail is still closed. Check LA County Public Works and Parks & Rec sites for specific updates before you head out. Don't ignore closure signs; they're there for your safety.
</AlertBox>

### Other Critters and Commotion

A mountain lion sighting was reported in Santa Monica on May 29. This is a good reminder that you're sharing the trails with wildlife, especially in areas bordering wilderness. Stay aware, make noise, and know what to do if you encounter one.

Also, be aware of increased traffic around Dodger Stadium and the LA County Fair (ending May 31st) if your route takes you near those areas. We don't need you adding to the gridlock or getting clipped by a distracted driver.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Trail Etiquette & Awareness:** Keep your head on a swivel. Whether it's a mountain lion in the Santa Monica mountains or a new brush fire kicking up, stay informed and aware of your surroundings. And for the love of all that is holy, don't blast your music so loud you can't hear approaching cyclists or fellow runners.
</AlertBox>

So, go get your sweat on, but do it smart. The air and temperature are great for it, but don't let that lull you into complacency about fires, closures, or wildlife.
