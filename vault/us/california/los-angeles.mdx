## Street Closure Chaos

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Avoid Driving and Plan Ahead for Active Transport!** The LA Marathon has taken over, with major street closures from Dodger Stadium all the way through Downtown, Echo Park, Silver Lake, and Hollywood, before wrapping up in Century City. Expect closures on main arteries like Sunset Boulevard and Hollywood Boulevard from early morning until noon or later. If you're not running the race, your usual Sunday morning stroll or ride is severely impacted. Check the official marathon route and local access information before you even think about heading out. Add to that CicLAvia in West LA, shutting down parts of Santa Monica Blvd and Westwood Blvd, and you've got a recipe for urban movement mayhem.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk

For those who prefer dirt paths to asphalt, keep your eyes open. While today's weather is perfect for a hike, several popular spots are still dealing with long-term issues.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Eaton Canyon and Angeles National Forest closures are still in effect.** Due to the January 2025 Eaton Fire, trails and surrounding areas remain closed until December 31, 2027, to allow for natural recovery. This includes sections of the Altadena Crest Trail and Chaney Trail Road. Also, a portion of Marshall Canyon Trail is seeing intermittent weekday closures from April to June 2026 for pest management. Don't just show up; verify your trail's status before you drive all the way out there.
</AlertBox>

## Air Quality & Allergies

The AQI is sitting pretty at 18, which is great. Our dominant pollutant today is Ozone/Other, which at this level isn't a huge concern. However, let's not forget that Los Angeles has a persistent battle with ozone pollution, earning it the top spot nationally for smog for 26 out of the last 27 years. Keep that in mind as temperatures eventually creep up this spring; ozone is no joke for your lungs.

<AlertBox type="info">
Even with a good AQI, if you're hitting those marathon cheer zones or outdoor events, remember you're sharing air with thousands. Moderate pollen means sensitive folks should still bring their meds.
</AlertBox>
