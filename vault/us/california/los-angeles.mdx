## Today's Lowdown: Don't Be a Moron, LA

Let's cut to the chase. The weather gods gave us a break today. The AQI is sitting pretty at a crisp 18, and it's comfortably cool and overcast. For those of us who actually *train* outside, this is a prime window. Get it while it's good, because a "Pineapple Express" storm system is eyeing us for the weekend, bringing rain that could make Halloween less about treats and more about treacherous puddles and slick roads.

### Coastal Chaos & Closed Trails

Now for the buzzkills. If your workout plans involved anything near the ocean, reconsider. Coastal Flood Advisories are still active for Los Angeles County beaches through 2 p.m. today, Friday, October 30. We're talking minor to moderate flooding of low-lying areas like those bike paths and parking lots along the **Santa Monica coastline**. You might also see some "moderate to significant beach erosion". So, unless you've been working on your open-water swimming or enjoy dodging debris, stay off the sand and away from those flooded walkways. Your ankles and your sense of humor will thank you.

And speaking of bad news, those of you looking for a proper climb, remember that trails in Eaton Canyon and surrounding areas, including the Altadena Crest Trail and Eaton Canyon Trail, are still closed until December 31, 2027, thanks to the 2025 Eaton Fire. It’s not just the trails; Chaney Trail Road is also closed to vehicular traffic until Fall 2026, making access a pain even if a trail *was* open. It's a bummer, but safety first, people. We don't need you twisting an ankle on an unstable path.

### Air Quality: A Lingering Shadow

While today's AQI is excellent, let's not get too smug. A new Caltech study, just published yesterday, highlighted that the smoke from the 2025 Eaton Fire exposed millions of Angelenos to dangerously high levels of toxic metals like lead and arsenic. This isn't an *active* issue right now, but it's a stark reminder that even a "good" AQI doesn't tell the whole story, especially when urban fires get involved. Breathe deep today, but remember what's lurking in our city's past.
