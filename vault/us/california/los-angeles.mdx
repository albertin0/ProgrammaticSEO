## Current Conditions: Don't Let the Morning Fool You

You stepped outside this morning and felt that glorious 16.5°C (61.7°F) air and saw mainly clear skies. Perfect, right? You'd think so. Your lungs are getting a break with an AQI of a mere 18, which is practically pristine for LA. No major pollen bombs exploding right now either, just a moderate level to keep your sinuses mildly annoyed.

However, if you've been living under a rock, you know we've been baking. The National Weather Service has extended heat advisories across Southern California, with temperatures expected to climb back into the 82-94°F range for inland coastal areas and downtown LA, and even hitting 100-112°F further inland and in the valleys. While today might be a *brief* reprieve for some, the real heat is still looming, and those advisories are in effect until Tuesday, with an *extreme* heat watch following it up. Plan your outdoor activities for early morning or late evening, or risk becoming a very sweaty statistic.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heat Advisory & Extreme Heat Watch:** Even if it feels mild right now, prepare for significant heat later today and over the weekend. Hydrate relentlessly and don't push it in the afternoon. Heat illness is no joke.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Troubles & Smoke Signals

Alright, let's talk about where you can actually *go*.

Unfortunately, the wildfire situation isn't great. Multiple new wildfires were reported in Los Angeles County just yesterday, July 9th. While the current AQI is good, these things can change on a dime with wind shifts. Smoke can travel, and when it does, your lungs won't be happy. Stay tuned to local news and fire updates.

If Runyon Canyon is your go-to for a quick burn and celebrity spotting (or dog spotting, mostly), be aware that the **West Trail is closed due to a recent fire**. Don't be *that* person who hops a fence – it's closed for a reason, likely for safety and recovery. Stick to the open sections.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Wildfires in LA County:** Several new fires broke out yesterday. While their impact on immediate air quality isn't fully reflected, stay vigilant. Smoke can degrade air quality rapidly, especially in canyon and valley areas.
</AlertBox>

And for those who like to venture further afield, remember that many trails in areas like Eaton Canyon and parts of the Angeles National Forest remain closed from older fires, some well into 2027. Do your homework before you drive all the way out there. Griffith Park also has some persistent closures, specifically the Bee Rock Trail, Burbank Peak Trail to the “Wisdom Tree,” and Eileen Getty Trail.

## Other Annoyances

Construction around LAX (Sepulveda Boulevard) will be ramping up next week with overnight closures, which won't directly impact your trail run but might make getting to your starting point a headache. Also, if you were planning an ocean dip after your workout, think twice. Several LA County beaches, including parts of Will Rogers State Beach and Santa Monica Pier, have elevated bacteria levels. Stick to the shower.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Morning is Your Friend:** If you absolutely have to work out outside, make it an early morning session. The heat advisories become much more relevant as the day progresses. Grab that sunrise run before the city starts to simmer.
</AlertBox>
