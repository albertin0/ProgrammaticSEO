## Hazard Hotspots: Know Before You Go

### Trail Closures are Your Biggest Headache

This is where it gets tricky. While the immediate air is clear, our trails have seen better days, and some of the damage is still very much in play.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Ongoing Fire-Related Trail Closures**
The Eaton Canyon area, for example, along with significant portions of the Angeles National Forest, are *still* dealing with long-term closures from the January 2025 Eaton Fire. We're talking trails like the Altadena Crest Trail, parts of the Eaton Canyon Trail itself, and even Chaney Trail remaining closed until December 31, 2027. Don't try to sneak through, people. It's for public safety and habitat recovery, and frankly, you don't want to get stuck or injured on unstable ground.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Plan Your Route Accordingly**
Before you head out to hit up a mountain trail, do yourself a favor and double-check local park websites or specific trail condition alerts. Expect the unexpected, especially in the canyons and higher elevations of the Angeles National Forest where fire danger remains "VERY HIGH" and rockslides, like the one closing a segment of Rincon-Redbox Road, are a real thing.
</AlertBox>

One small heads-up for tomorrow: if Sycamore Canyon Trail is your jam, know that Southern California Edison is closing it down for pole replacement work on September 13th. So, if you're planning a Sunday long run there, find an alternate.

In short, today’s a winner for pavement pounders and those sticking to open, well-maintained paths. For the trail runners, just be smart about your location and have a backup plan. Your lungs will thank you, and your joints will thank you for not sending them into a closed-off, eroded gully.
