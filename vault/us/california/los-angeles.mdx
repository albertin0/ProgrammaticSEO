## The Air You Breathe (or Don't)

Look, I get it. We live in LA. Perfect air is a myth, like finding parking at Griffith Observatory on a Saturday. Today, we're sitting at an AQI of 79, which officially gets the "Moderate" stamp. The dominant pollutant is PM2.5, which is that microscopic crud that likes to lodge itself deep in your lungs. While it's not "Hazardous" for everyone, don't let the word "moderate" fool you into thinking it's pristine. According to air quality guidelines, moderate air is acceptable, but for some pollutants, there may be a moderate health concern for a very small number of people unusually sensitive to air pollution. If you've got asthma, hit the inhaler *before* you head out, not halfway up your first hill. Even if you don't feel it now, consistent exposure to this stuff isn't doing your long-term lung capacity any favors.

## Overcast Chill Before the Heat Inferno

Right now, it's a comfortable 17.3°C and overcast, which sounds like decent running weather. Enjoy it. Seriously. This little reprieve is a false prophet. Starting Friday, Los Angeles County is bracing for an *extended* heat wave that's going to stick around like a bad ex. Forecasters are calling for "dangerously hot conditions", with triple-digit temperatures expected in places like Pasadena and even Downtown LA by Saturday. If you have any long runs planned for the weekend, reschedule them for dawn or hit the treadmill. This isn't the time to prove how tough you are. This is the time to avoid heatstroke and looking like a melted popsicle.

## Trail Closures: The Never-Ending Story

Still dreaming of that long run through Eaton Canyon? Keep dreaming. Large sections of the Angeles National Forest, including trails in and around Eaton Canyon, and parts of the Altadena Crest Trail, remain closed. This isn't new news, but it's *still* ongoing, dating back to the 2025 Eaton Fire. Some of these closures are locked down until December 31, 2027. Don't be the idiot who hops a fence and then needs a SAR team because you ignored the "AREA CLOSED" signs. Those trails are regenerating, and there are still hazards from erosion and unstable ground. Also, if you're in the western part of the county, the Williamson Rock area is closed until the end of this year for natural resource protection.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Check Before You Trek:** Numerous trails, especially in the Eaton Canyon area and parts of Angeles National Forest, are still closed due to past wildfire damage. Don't assume your favorite route is open. Check local Parks & Rec or US Forest Service alerts before heading out. Also, mosquitoes are still thriving in the damp aftermath of summer storms, so consider repellent if you're hitting trails near water.
</AlertBox>
