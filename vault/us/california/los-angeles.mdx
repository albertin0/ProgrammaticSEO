## The Good News: Air Quality & Weather

Look, let's just get this out of the way: the air quality in Los Angeles right now is fantastic, sitting at a breezy AQI of 18. Pair that with a "mainly clear" sky and a comfortable 14.4°C (around 58°F for those still stuck on Fahrenheit), and you've got yourself some prime conditions for anything from a leisurely walk around the Rose Bowl to a punishing tempo run along the Strand. Your lungs are getting a break today, so enjoy it. Just remember that moderate pollen can still kick your ass if you're susceptible, so take your meds, you know who you are.

## The Usual LA Hassles: Trail Closures & Construction

Alright, now for the part where LA reminds you it's a city of constant *progress* and *regeneration*. If your usual haunt involves dirt, rocks, and switchbacks, you need to pay attention. Several sections of the Angeles National Forest are still locked down tighter than a drum due to the Eaton Fire. We're talking areas around Mount Wilson Toll Road (below the actual trail, mind you) and even Chaney Trail remain closed for repairs, and these aren't short-term fixes; some of these closures extend through the end of 2027. So, if you were planning on tackling the trails above Altadena, find an alternative.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Don't ignore those closure signs.** Seriously. The Mountain Recreation and Conservation Authority (MRCA) has got swaths of the Santa Monica Mountains — like Rogers Road Trail, Temescal Canyon Trail, and other popular spots — still shut down from the Palisades Fire. These aren't suggestions; they're for your safety and to let the land recover. Trying to "explore" closed trails just puts you and the first responders at risk. Don't be that person.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the never-ending symphony of construction. The Metro D Line subway extension is still chugging along, especially around Century City. While great for future commutes, it means some surface-level disruptions for us plebs trying to get a run or bike ride in. Keep an eye out for unexpected detours or narrowed sidewalks in those areas. It's not a major hazard like a wildfire, but tripping over a stray barricade because you're staring at your watch is a classic LA move we can all avoid.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Pro Tip:** Since many mountain trails are a no-go, consider sticking to the beach paths or one of the city's larger parks today. Griffith Park offers plenty of paved and wide dirt fire roads that are generally safe and open. Just remember to share the path with the tourists trying to snap a selfie with the Observatory.
</AlertBox>
