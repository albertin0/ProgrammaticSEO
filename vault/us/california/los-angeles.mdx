The temperature is in that sweet spot where you won't melt or freeze your bits off. Get out there and enjoy it, but don't get complacent.

### Heat Check (Mostly Passed, But Stay Smart)

Just last week, we were facing down an "Extreme Heat Watch" for much of the county, with forecasts pushing into the high 90s and even 100s, especially in the valleys. While today's temps are far more forgiving, hovering around 16.5°C (61.7°F), that extreme heat watch was technically in effect until March 20th for some areas. Consider this a valuable reminder: L.A. weather can flip faster than a pancake on a griddle. Always, *always* check the forecast for your specific location before a long run, and hydrate like it's your job, especially if you're venturing inland.

### Trail Troubles: Still Here From Last Year's Fires

Sadly, the scars from the 2025 wildfires are still impacting some of our favorite spots.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Trail Closures:**
*   **Eaton Canyon Natural Area** and its trails remain closed due to fire damage, and honestly, they're not looking to reopen until at least the end of 2025, possibly later. Don't try to sneak in; it's dangerous, and you're just hindering the recovery.
*   **Runyon Canyon's West Trail** is officially listed as closed "until further notice" due to fire damage. While other parts of Runyon Canyon Park are generally open, attempting the West Trail is a no-go.
*   Up in the **Angeles National Forest**, if you were planning a high-elevation trek, know that popular routes like the **Mt. Baldy Trail, Mt. Baldy Bowl Trail, Devils Backbone Trail, and Three T's Trail** are closed until March 20, 2026, for public safety. Yes, that means today. Stay off 'em.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction Zone Ahead:** If your usual route involves **Agoura Road in Agoura Hills**, be aware that sections will be closed for wildlife crossing construction this coming Monday, March 22nd, from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. Plan your bike ride or run accordingly, or get ready for some detours and potential shuttle rides.
</AlertBox>

So, enjoy the sweet air and pleasant temperatures. Just do your homework, respect the closures, and don't be that person who needs rescuing.
