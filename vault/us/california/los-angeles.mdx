### What to Watch Out For

Look, the LA Marathon was yesterday, Sunday, March 8th, and while the 27,000+ runners have (hopefully) all crossed the finish line by now, you might still encounter some residual weirdness if you're trying to navigate areas that were part of the "Stadium to the Stars" route. I'm talking about places like Santa Monica Boulevard near Century City, where the finish line spectacle was. Road crews are efficient, but a marathon cleanup isn't instantaneous, so don't be surprised by late-reopening lanes or lingering cones, especially early this morning.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Runner's Tip:** Check local traffic apps before you head out, especially if your route typically takes you through neighborhoods like Hollywood, West Hollywood, or Beverly Hills. While major street closures for the marathon should be lifted today, localized impacts might still be present around the finish line.
</AlertBox>

### Know Your Trails

Some of our favorite spots are still locked down, so plan accordingly. Eaton Canyon Natural Area and its trails remain closed due to that nasty Eaton Fire from back in January, and it's not looking like it'll open anytime soon, with closures extended through December 31, 2027. Same goes for significant portions of the Mount Baldy area, which are currently off-limits through March 20th. Don't be "that guy" ignoring the signs; those closures are for public safety and environmental recovery, not just to annoy you.
