## Watch Your Back (and Your Lungs)

While the AQI of 18 looks great on paper, let's not get too comfortable. Los Angeles County is still dealing with active fire situations. A "new wildfire" was reported yesterday, July 12, burning on private land, with no containment information available. On top of that, the Summit Fire, which started a few days ago, is still at 31% containment, having charred over 2,690 acres in Los Angeles County. The Los Angeles County Fire Department even states "conditions favorable for wildfire" remain.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Wildfire Smoke Advisory (Expired but still relevant)
A smoke advisory for the Summit Fire technically ended July 11, but don't let that lull you into a false sense of security. If you're smelling smoke or seeing hazy skies, especially near the San Gabriel Mountains, cut your outdoor workout short. Keep an eye on real-time air quality maps; what's good now can change fast when the winds shift, sending invisible irritants straight into your lungs. You won't see it on the official AQI until it hits hard.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Eaton Canyon is STILL NOT Fully Open (and won't be for a while)
Look, I know you love Eaton Canyon, but thanks to the January 2025 Eaton Fire, many trails and areas are still closed for public safety and environmental recovery, with some closures extending until December 31, 2027. This includes the Eaton Canyon Trail itself and the Altadena Crest Trail. The paved Chaney Trail Road, which leads into Millard Canyon, is also closed to vehicular traffic until Fall 2026. Don't be that person ignoring signs and making a bad situation worse for yourself or the recovering ecosystem. Find another spot.
</AlertBox>

### Other Annoyances

*   **Pollen:** "Moderate" pollen levels, primarily from trees and weeds. If you're one of those unlucky souls whose nose runs more than a faucet this time of year, pop your antihistamines before you head out. Nobody wants to see you snotting your way down the Marvin Braude Bike Trail.
*   **Heat (Coming Soon):** While today is mild, the National Weather Service is warning of a "hot and muggy week expected," with temperatures potentially peaking mid-week. Extreme Heat Watches are likely to become Warnings for most areas by Tuesday through Thursday. Inland valleys, mountains, and deserts could hit 35 to 41°C (95 to 106°F). Plan your long runs accordingly, or face the consequences.
*   **Construction Delays:** Expect ongoing lane reductions and closures on major arteries like the I-5, I-210, I-405 through the Sepulveda Pass, and US-101. Metro construction for the La Cienega station also means intermittent lane reductions. If your commute to your favorite trail involves these, budget extra time.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Pro-Tip for Hot Days
It's going to get hotter this week. If you're going to push it, do it early, finish before 9 AM, or wait until after 6 PM. Hydrate like it's your job, and don't be a hero running without water. Nobody cares how fast you were if you end up with heatstroke on the side of the road.
</AlertBox>
