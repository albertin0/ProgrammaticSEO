Look, March in L.A. usually means we're either bracing for Santa Anas or getting soaked by an atmospheric river, but today, March 29th, is playing it cool. The mercury's sitting at a pleasant 16.5°C (that's 61.7°F for the Fahrenheit folks), and it's mainly clear out there. Your lungs will thank you, with an AQI of 18, meaning the air quality is in the "Good" zone, with "Ozone/Other" as the dominant pollutant – basically, you can breathe without tasting exhaust.

Now, for the downsides, because this is L.A., and there's *always* a catch. We've got moderate pollen levels, which means if you're one of the lucky ones whose sinuses decide to wage war with nature every spring, you'll be feeling it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  Heads up, allergy sufferers! Even with lower grass and weed pollen, moderate tree pollen can still trigger symptoms. Pop that antihistamine before you head out, and maybe stash some tissues in your running belt.
</AlertBox>

While the major road closures from the ASICS Los Angeles Marathon are long gone (that was March 8th, folks, so no need to reroute around Hollywood Boulevard today), some of our beloved trails are still reeling from past disasters. Specifically, the trails within **Eaton Canyon Natural Area and surrounding areas**, including portions of the Altadena Crest Trail and Chaney Trail, remain closed. This isn't some short-term thing; these closures are in effect until at least December 31, 2027, to allow the landscape to recover from the January 2025 Eaton Fire and to ensure public safety. Chaney Trail Road, which feeds into Millard Canyon, is also closed to vehicular traffic until Spring 2026 for guardrail repairs, so even getting to some trailheads is a no-go.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  Do NOT ignore trail closure signs, especially in burn scar areas like Eaton Canyon. The landscape is unstable, and you're not just risking a twisted ankle – you could be in for a much nastier surprise. Respect the closures; they're there for a reason.
</AlertBox>

Past storms earlier this year (February, primarily) brought significant rainfall and some street flooding to areas like Westwood, and while things are dry today, burn scar zones (think Palisades and Franklin canyons) are always prone to mudslides and debris flows, even weeks after a downpour. If you're running in those areas, just be mindful of your surroundings.

  This is a great day for a road run or a bike ride along the beach path. Just keep an eye on that pollen count and remember that even on a beautiful day, L.A.'s burn scars can still be tricky underfoot.
