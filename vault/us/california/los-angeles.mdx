## Today's Vibe: Bake Sale, Not Trail Run

Look, I know you checked your app and saw 16.5°C (that's 62°F for the rest of us) and thought, "Score!" But unless you're literally running *into* the Pacific Ocean from Santa Monica Beach, that temperature isn't what most of L.A. is experiencing. We're in the middle of a serious heat wave, with public health officials practically begging people to stay inside and limit outdoor activities during the hottest parts of the day.

This isn't just about being uncomfortable; it's about genuine risk. We're talking heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and making your joints feel like rusty hinges if you push it. Even experienced trail runners doing events like the Valley Crest Trails race are being warned about "fully exposed course with little to no shade; Hot and dry conditions likely" in mid-July. It's Wednesday, July 15th, and this heat isn't going anywhere until at least Friday.

## Stay Smart, Stay Alive

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat Across Inland Los Angeles County!
Seriously, this isn't a drill. Temperatures could hit 110°F in places like Woodland Hills and the Antelope Valley today and tomorrow. Public health officials are urging everyone to avoid outdoor activity between 10 AM and 3 PM. If you *must* be outside, hydrate like your life depends on it – because it might. Don't wait until you're thirsty.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Wildfire Risk and Trail Closures
The "HIGH" fire danger in the Angeles National Forest means existing trail closures are still in effect, and new ones could pop up. The recent Summit Fire in the Antelope Valley area, though contained, is a stark reminder. Check the Angeles National Forest alerts *before* you head out, especially if you're planning on any high-elevation runs. Multiple trails, like those affected by the Eaton Fire, are still closed for recovery, some until late 2027.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your Best Bet: Early Morning or Indoors
If you absolutely *need* to get a workout in, do it before the sun really starts cooking, ideally before 8 AM, or after sunset. Otherwise, hit the gym, jump in a pool, or find some air conditioning. Your body will thank you.
</AlertBox>
