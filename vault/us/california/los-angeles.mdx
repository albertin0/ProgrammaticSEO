## The Good News: Breathe Easy

Look, let's be real. There are days in Los Angeles when stepping outside feels like choosing between a run and a pack-a-day habit. Today isn't one of them. The AQI is a beautiful 18, which means the air is practically pristine for our smog-choked standards. You've got clear skies and a comfortable 16.5°C (that's about 62°F for the Fahrenheit faithful). This is prime running weather, folks, especially after those ridiculous heat waves we dealt with just last week. No extreme heat warnings, no gusty winds fueling potential infernos today. So, your lungs are definitely getting a break.

## The Bad News: Your Favorite Trails Are Still a Minefield

Now, before you lace up and automatically point yourself towards the foothills, a harsh dose of reality: many of our beloved trails are still closed. Thanks to the January 2025 Eaton Fire, large swathes of **Eaton Canyon** and its surrounding areas, including the Altadena Crest Trail and the main Eaton Canyon Trail, are *still* off-limits until December 31, 2027. That means no waterfall selfies, no casual strolls through that gorgeous landscape. And don't even think about driving up Chaney Trail Road; it's closed to vehicular traffic until Fall 2026.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### Major Trail Closures Alert!
  Seriously, do not just show up. Trails in Eaton Canyon, including the main Eaton Canyon Trail and sections of the Altadena Crest Trail, remain closed due to fire damage through December 2027. Access roads like Chaney Trail Road are also closed to vehicles.
</AlertBox>

It's not just Eaton Canyon either. The Angeles National Forest has a laundry list of long-term closures. Rincon/Shortcut Road is shut down indefinitely, the Williamson Rock area is closed through the end of the year, and a segment of Rincon-Redbox Road is impassable due to a rock slide from late July. Even if you just want to take a scenic drive to access a trailhead, some major forest roads are still closed or have limited access.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ### Angeles National Forest - Proceed with Caution
  While there are no active fire or heat warnings *today*, the Angeles National Forest still has a "Very High" fire danger level, elevated since late July. Always be mindful of dry conditions, even on pleasant days. Furthermore, numerous roads and trails within the Angeles National Forest remain closed due to storm damage and other reasons. Always check the Forest Service website before planning a remote adventure.
</AlertBox>
