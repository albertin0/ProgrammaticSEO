## The May Gray (or Fog, really) is Real

Step outside and you'll notice it: a dense, cool fog has rolled in, dropping temperatures to a brisk 15.1°C (about 59°F). While it feels nice and cool, especially compared to the heat wave baking some of our neighbors further inland, this pea-souper isn't doing any favors for your morning run or bike ride visibility. If you're hitting the streets, assume no one sees you, because they probably don't. Think reflective gear, bright colors, and maybe even a headlamp if you're out before the marine layer decides to burn off (which, let's be real, might not happen until noon).

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Fog Advisory:** Low visibility is a real hazard today. If you're running, cycling, or even walking near traffic, make yourself as visible as humanly possible. Don't be that guy in all black blending into the mist.
</AlertBox>

## Breathing Easy (Mostly)

Good news for your respiratory system: the Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at a low 18, with "Ozone/Other" listed as the dominant pollutant. This is fantastic news, especially after some localized brush and structure fires yesterday. Whatever smoky residue might have been lingering has cleared out, leaving us with some genuinely good air. Your lungs will thank you.

## Trail Trouble Lingers

Now for the bad news, especially for our trail runners and hikers. While our air is clear, the scars from past fire seasons are still very much a reality. Many trails and recreation areas in the Angeles National Forest and parts of the Santa Monica Mountains remain closed, some through late 2026 or even 2027. We're talking about areas around the Eaton Fire and Palisades Fire burn scars, where remediation and recovery are ongoing. For instance, Temescal Gateway Park, a popular spot, still has portions remaining closed.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DO NOT IGNORE TRAIL CLOSURES.** Seriously. These aren't suggestions; they're for your safety. Going off-trail or into closed areas can put you in danger of falling debris, unstable ground, or reigniting embers. It also hinders recovery efforts and can lead to hefty fines. Check the latest updates from Angeles National Forest and Mountains Recreation and Conservation Authority before you plan your route.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Local Construction:** While not directly trail-related, keep an eye out for ongoing construction around town. The Metro D Line extension is wrapping up Phase 1, but work continues on later phases, particularly around Century City/Constellation. Various public works projects are active across the county, too, so expect minor detours or increased traffic in some areas.
</AlertBox>

So, if you're working out outside today, enjoy the good air, but stay vigilant in the fog and stick to open, designated routes. Your ankles, and the local land managers, will appreciate it.
