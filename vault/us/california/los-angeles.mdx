Look, I get it. We live in LA; we're supposed to be outside. But today, July 31st, 2026, Mother Nature is trying to tell you something, and it's not "go hit that PR." While your local weather app might be showing a relatively cool 16.5°C (61.7°F) and an AQI of 18, that's either a very specific, localized coastal reading, or someone's got their wires crossed. The reality is, a dangerous heat wave is building, with an Extreme Heat Warning in effect from 10 AM Friday through Sunday for vast swathes of our county. We're talking 95 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit in the San Fernando and San Gabriel valleys, and even the Santa Monica Mountains Recreational Area. Even coastal areas like Malibu and Palos Verdes Hills are looking at temperatures up to 100 degrees. If you're out running, cycling, or hiking in that kind of heat, you're not getting fitter; you're flirting with heat exhaustion or worse.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Extreme Heat Warning in Effect!** Do NOT underestimate the heat. Inland areas will be dangerously hot (95-110°F) from 10 AM through Sunday. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity between 10 AM and 8 PM. Stay hydrated, seek air conditioning, and check on vulnerable neighbors.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. The South Coast Air Quality Management District has issued an ozone advisory, predicting "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" or even "Unhealthy" AQI levels in interior portions of the South Coast Air Basin, including our valleys, starting today at 10 AM and lasting until Sunday evening. Your current AQI of 18 is likely not what you'll be breathing if you head inland or out during peak smog hours. Ozone is a lung irritant, and strenuous activity only makes it worse.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Ozone Advisory for Inland Areas!** Air quality is expected to reach "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" or worse in the San Fernando, Santa Clarita, and San Gabriel valleys. Monitor real-time AQI at AQMD.gov and limit prolonged outdoor exertion during advisory periods, especially if you have respiratory conditions.
</AlertBox>

To top it off, the Angeles National Forest has elevated its fire danger level to "VERY HIGH" as of July 24th, a direct consequence of this dry, hot weather. This increased risk means trails are already vulnerable, and we're still dealing with the aftermath of past fires. Speaking of which, don't forget that trails in **Eaton Canyon** and surrounding areas, still recovering from the January 2025 Eaton Fire, remain closed until the end of 2027. This includes sections of the Altadena Crest Trail and the Eaton Canyon Trail itself. The West Trail at Runyon Canyon also remains closed. Planning a run there? Think again.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Check Trail Status!** Many trails in the Angeles National Forest and other parks are either under long-term closure for fire recovery (like Eaton Canyon) or facing increased fire danger. Always verify trail accessibility before you head out.
</AlertBox>

If you absolutely *must* get your miles in, hit the treadmill, or aim for a super early morning, very short, low-intensity session *right* along the actual coast, where temperatures might be slightly more forgiving (but still warm) and ozone levels potentially lower. But honestly, today's a day for indoor workouts, swimming, or just chilling. Your lungs and joints will thank you.
