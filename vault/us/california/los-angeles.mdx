Today's the kind of day you rarely get in August where you can actually enjoy a long run without feeling like you're baking inside a convection oven. The mercury's hanging around 16.5°C (that's about 62°F for those still stuck in Fahrenheit land) and the sky is, blessedly, "Mainly Clear." Our Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at a very respectable 18, which means you can fill those lungs without much worry about the usual LA crud. The dominant pollutant is listed as "Ozone/Other," but at this level, honestly, you're probably breathing worse air next to a bus on Wilshire.

However, it's not all sunshine and PRs. While the general air quality is stellar, there's a lingering "Smoke Advisory" still in place for the Boyle Heights area. This is from a warehouse fire back in June that sent a "massive plume of black smoke" across the area. So, if you're planning on a jog around Hollenbeck Park or anywhere east of downtown, be aware that localized conditions might not be as pristine as the city-wide average. Listen to your body – if it smells funky, it probably is.

Now, for my fellow trail fanatics, here's where it gets a little more complicated. While the weather's prime for hitting the dirt, the Angeles National Forest is still playing hard to get in a lot of places. They've got a "VERY HIGH" fire danger level as of late July, which means even if there isn't a new blaze actively raging, conditions are ripe for one. On top of that, there are a laundry list of existing closures for fire recovery, rockslides, and general resource protection, some of which are stretching all the way to 2027. We're talking about areas like Williamson Rock, segments of Rincon-Redbox Road, and parts of the Eaton Fire Area. And don't forget Runyon Canyon – the West Trail is still closed indefinitely. So, before you head out hoping to bag some elevation, double-check the specific trail status. Don't be that person who ignores the signs and ends up needing a rescue.
