## Here's The Rub: Don't Be a Moron.

While the immediate conditions are looking pretty sweet for hitting the pavement or the bike path, this is still LA, and we've got a few things simmering.

### Wildfire Alert (Again, Seriously?)

Yeah, I know. A new wildfire popped up in Los Angeles County yesterday, June 27. It's burning on private land, and the containment status is currently unknown. While our general AQI is good, if you live or plan to work out near any active fire zones, common sense applies: stay away. Keep an eye on local news for specific evacuation or advisory areas. This isn't the first rodeo, so you know the drill.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Pollen's on the Prowl

For all you snot-nosed, itchy-eyed sufferers, pollen is at a **Moderate** level today. That "clear" air might feel crisp, but your sinuses might have other opinions. If you're prone to seasonal sniffles, pre-dose your antihistamines or keep your tissue box handy. Don't be that person coughing up a lung on the *Santa Monica Stairs* and blaming it on your pace.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Trail Closures are a Permanent Fixture

Listen up, hikers. Some of our beloved trails are *still* closed. **Eaton Canyon** and surrounding areas damaged by the January 2025 Eaton Fire remain off-limits until December 31, 2027, to allow for recovery and for public safety. And if you were planning on a weekday jaunt through **Marshall Canyon**, be aware of intermittent closures through the end of June due to oak tree pest management. Don't ignore those "Closed" signs. There are fines, and frankly, you're just screwing up the recovery for everyone else. Also, the Angeles National Forest has elevated its fire danger level to "HIGH" as of June 12, so be extra cautious if you're in that general vicinity.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Wind Advisory (Mountain Edition)

A wind advisory was in effect until 6:00 AM PDT today for parts of Santa Barbara County Southwestern Coast and the Santa Ynez Mountains Western Range, with gusts up to 50 mph. While the immediate city might be calm, if your run or ride takes you into the western San Gabriel Mountains or higher elevations, expect some blustery conditions. That can mean downed branches or debris, so keep your head up.
</AlertBox>
