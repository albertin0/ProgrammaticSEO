## The Rundown: A Breath of Fresh, Not-Too-Hot Air

Alright, Angelenos, let's talk brass tacks. Today, July 3rd, 2026, the air quality is looking fantastic with an AQI of 18 [cite: air_quality data], which means your lungs get a break. Temperature-wise, we're sitting at a comfortable 16.5°C (that's about 62°F for the Fahrenheit folks) and it's mainly clear [cite: weather data]. No heat domes baking *us* alive today, thankfully, unlike what's hitting the East Coast. This is prime running weather, assuming you can navigate the urban jungle and avoid getting hit by a stray sparkler.

### Fire Risks and Trail Closures: Don't Be a Statistic

This Fourth of July weekend is less about freedom and more about fire prevention, apparently. The Angeles National Forest is already at "HIGH" fire danger, and a lot of familiar stomping grounds are straight-up closed. The North Etiwanda Preserve, a popular spot out in Rancho Cucamonga, is completely shut down from Thursday evening through Monday morning to prevent any genius from sparking a wildfire with illegal fireworks. Seriously, people.

Closer to home, if your usual scenic route involves a sunrise trek up Runyon Canyon, tough luck. Runyon Canyon Park is closing early at 5 p.m. from today through Sunday, along with early closures at Jerome C. Daniel Overlook and Universal City Overlook. These closures are for public safety, and frankly, to stop idiots from torching the Hollywood Hills. Don't be that person.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures & Fire Danger:** Popular trails like the North Etiwanda Preserve and Runyon Canyon Park are closed or have early closures this weekend due to extreme fire risk from fireworks. Check local park websites before heading out to avoid wasted trips and potential fines. Also, remember that portions of Eaton Canyon and other Angeles National Forest trails remain closed from the 2025 Eaton Fire until late 2027.
</AlertBox>

### Beach Water Warnings: Maybe Skip the Ocean Swim

So you thought you'd hit the beach after your run? Think again, at least for a dip. The LA County Department of Public Health issued warnings yesterday for elevated bacteria levels at several popular beach spots. We're talking Redondo Beach Pier, Mother's Beach in Marina del Rey, Inner Cabrillo Beach in San Pedro, and the always-popular Santa Monica Pier, among others. Unless you're looking for an impromptu internal cleanse, I'd stick to the sand. The water quality has returned to state standards at Will Rogers State Beach, for what it's worth.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Beach Water Advisory:** Avoid swimming or even prolonged contact with ocean water at several Los Angeles County beaches, including areas around Redondo Beach Pier, Mother's Beach (Marina del Rey), and Santa Monica Pier, due to elevated bacteria levels. Seriously, don't do it.
</AlertBox>

### Boyle Heights Fire Aftermath

Remember that massive Boyle Heights warehouse fire? While the immediate thick smoke appears to have cleared, and our current AQI is good, the South Coast AQMD is still dealing with the fallout. They're approving funds for air purifiers and cleanup efforts are ongoing. Expect potential dust and odors in the general vicinity of Boyle Heights as they remove roughly 85 million pounds of rotting food waste. This isn't great, but it's not the acute "astronomical soot pollution" we saw weeks ago.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Boyle Heights Cleanup:** While overall air quality is good, cleanup from the Boyle Heights warehouse fire is ongoing. Expect localized dust and potential odors near the site. If you're sensitive, consider rerouting your workout if you're usually in that area.
</AlertBox>
