## What's Cooking (and it's not dinner)

Let's cut to the chase: The weather today is brutal. A "mainly clear" sky just means the sun has an unobstructed shot at turning you into a puddle. We're talking about temperatures that warrant an <AlertBox type="danger">Extreme Heat Warning. Hydration isn't enough; avoiding exposure is the play here.</AlertBox> Oceanside isn't built for this kind of sustained heat. Your body can't dissipate heat fast enough, leading to heat exhaustion and, if you push it, full-blown heatstroke.

And while the official news isn't screaming about wildfires *right now*, this kind of dry heat makes conditions ripe for them. We've seen it before in North County, and when the Santa Anas kick up, things go south fast. Always keep an eye on local alerts.

### Air Quality: Don't Breathe Too Deep

Then there's the air. An AQI of 128 is "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups," but honestly, when the dominant pollutant is PM2.5 (fine particulate matter), *everyone* should pay attention. This stuff gets deep into your lungs, especially when you're breathing hard during exercise. Combined with the insane heat, you're looking at a perfect storm for respiratory irritation and potentially exacerbating existing conditions.

<AlertBox type="warning">If you absolutely *must* be outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and listen to your body. Any coughing, shortness of breath, or dizziness means it's time to pack it in and get indoors.</AlertBox>

## Local Impact and Tips

Thinking of your usual run down the San Luis Rey River Trail? Don't. There's not enough shade out there to make it safe in this heat, and you'll be sucking down that unhealthy air for miles. Even a short walk along the harbor or near the Oceanside Pier, while potentially offering a slight sea breeze, won't negate the overall danger. The concrete and asphalt will radiate heat, making everything feel even hotter.
