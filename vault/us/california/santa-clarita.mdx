<AlertBox type="danger">
### Heat Advisory: Seriously, Stay Indoors if You Can
Forget "unseasonably warm" – 36.9°C (nearly 98.4°F) is just plain *hot* for March. We're looking at temperatures that can easily cause heat exhaustion or heatstroke. The National Weather Service was talking about valleys hitting the 90s, and we're seeing it today. If you absolutely *must* be outside, hydrate like your life depends on it (because it might), wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, stick to the shade. This isn't the day to try for a new long run.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Wind Advisory: Watch Your Step (and Your Head)
A wind advisory is active until Saturday afternoon, with gusts up to 40-50 mph across the Santa Clarita Valley, and even higher, up to 65 mph, in some mountain areas like along the 14 Freeway corridor. This means blowing debris – trash cans, loose branches, maybe even your poorly secured gazebo – could be flying around. Running against these gusts is not only exhausting but can also be dangerous if you're not paying attention. Keep an eye out, especially if you're near open areas or exposed ridges.
</AlertBox>

## Trail & Road Updates: Don't Get Stuck

Beyond the weather trying to cook and sandblast you, we've got the usual Santa Clarita joy of perpetual construction.

The **Bouquet Canyon Road Project** is still a thing. That stretch from Plum Canyon Road to David Way is closed for roughly 11 months, with traffic rerouted via Haskell Canyon Road and Copper Hill Drive. If your usual run takes you anywhere near there, assume detours or just pick another spot. While they're building new trails as part of this, the immediate impact is disruption.

Also, don't forget the never-ending **I-5 North County Enhancements Project**. Crews are still doing bridge work and other upgrades through 2026. This means intermittent overnight closures and ramp adjustments, which might not affect your daytime trail run directly, but can certainly make getting to your chosen trailhead a headache. The Santa Clarita River Trail is usually a pretty safe bet, but even there, watch for increased debris from the winds.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Pro Tip: Reschedule or Re-Route
Seriously, consider a treadmill run today, or hit the gym. If you're set on outdoor activity, go early, keep it short, and stick to well-known, paved areas like the paseos, far from major construction zones or super exposed trails where the wind will be brutal. And for goodness sake, carry more water than you think you need.
</AlertBox>
