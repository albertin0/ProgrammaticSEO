<AlertBox type="warning">
### Watch Your Step on the Beach Path!
Recent hurricane swells from Polo and Odalys, compounded by high tides and previous erosion from Hurricane Marie, have left their mark on our south-facing beaches. The Long Beach Peninsula boardwalk has sections that are significantly damaged and unstable. Expect shallow seawater flooding in low-lying areas, and significant beach erosion. Stay alert for debris and unstable ground if you're venturing near the water. This isn't just about wet shoes; it's about structural integrity.
</AlertBox>

### Air Quality & Allergens

The "Moderate" AQI of 53 is primarily due to PM2.5. This isn't from any *current* local inferno, thankfully (no wildfires making our air unbreathable right now, unlike some past incidents). It's more of a general urban haze. If you've got sensitive lungs, you'll feel it. And yes, pollen is out there doing its thing too.

Various projects are underway or just wrapping up, which means disruptions. My advice? If you're hitting the beach path, stick to the higher ground or find an alternate route. Don't be that person who ignores the obvious dangers for the sake of a few extra miles. Your joints (and my PT office) will thank you. For inland runs, the weather's decent, but keep an eye on that moderate air quality. Hydrate, be aware, and for god's sake, don't run with your headphones so loud you can't hear a "road closed" sign.
