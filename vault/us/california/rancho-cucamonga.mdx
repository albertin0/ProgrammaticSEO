You might see "Clear" skies out there and think it's a nice 12.2°C (about 54°F), but that's just a cruel trick. The real story is the wind. It’s not just a nuisance; it’s a legitimate hazard. North winds are whipping through the Inland Empire at 15 to 25 mph, and those 45 mph gusts are no joke. Trying to cycle on the Pacific Electric Trail in this, or even run exposed sections, is asking for trouble. You'll be struggling to maintain balance, and honestly, it's just miserable.

The accompanying Air Quality Alert from the windblown dust means more than just a gritty taste in your mouth. Particle pollution can aggravate conditions like asthma and heart disease, and it's particularly rough on kids, older adults, and anyone with lung issues. Your lungs don't care how "tough" you think you are; they just want clean air. Our moderate AQI of 60 might not sound terrible, but with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and a persistent wind advisory, those particles are circulating directly into your breathing zone.

If you're one of those ambitious types who likes to hit the wilderness, remember that Cucamonga Canyon is off-limits. This isn't a suggestion; it's a formal closure by the San Bernardino National Forest until May 23, 2026. Ignoring these kinds of closures not only puts you at risk but also puts our already stretched first responders in a bind if something goes wrong.

The local area also has some ongoing construction and development, like the Metrolink Rancho Cucamonga Siding Extension Project, which can kick up more dust locally. While these aren't immediate, 24-hour hazards, they contribute to the general air quality issues, especially on windy days like today.

Pollen levels are currently "Moderate," which is just icing on this particularly unappetizing cake of outdoor conditions. So, if you manage to avoid inhaling too much dust, your sinuses still get to battle allergens.
