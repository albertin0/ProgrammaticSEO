## The Rundown: Don't Melt Out There

Seriously, 30.5°C? In *March*? This isn't Palm Springs, people. While the air quality is technically "good" with an AQI of 33, and we're not dealing with any fresh wildfire smoke right now, the sheer heat combined with "mainly clear" skies is your biggest obstacle today.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Extreme Heat Warning: Seriously, Stay Hydrated or Stay Home!**
With temperatures hitting 30.5°C (87°F), heat exhaustion and heatstroke are real threats. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; it's about your body's ability to regulate its core temperature. Stick to early mornings or late evenings if you absolutely *must* exercise outside. Hydrate relentlessly before, during, and after. Wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, listen to your body. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or get a headache, stop immediately. Find shade, drink water, and cool down.
</AlertBox>

### Local Hazards to Watch For

The "Centerville Complete Streets" project is still chugging along, making a mess of things on Fremont Boulevard between Alder Avenue and Thornton Avenue. If you're planning a walk, run, or bike ride through Centerville, expect temporary 24/7 lane closures and pedestrian detours, especially around the Thornton Ave./Fremont Blvd. intersection. They say they've got detours, but "temporary" often means "for-the-next-six-months-and-you'll-figure-it-out." Moderate delays are expected, so plan accordingly, or just find another route.

And for those of you who like to venture towards Vargas Plateau Regional Park, remember that ongoing dispute over Morrison Canyon Road. Some rancher decided to put up a gate back in November, blocking access for hikers and cyclists, even though the city claims it's public land. Don't be surprised if your usual route is still a no-go there. It's frustrating, but better to know before you lace up.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Construction Zone Ahead (and Everywhere Else)**
Beyond the Centerville area, the city's "Concrete Rep & Inter Proj" is also underway, replacing curbs, sidewalks, and driveways across Fremont through the end of the year. This means random closures, detours, and general inconvenience can pop up almost anywhere. Keep an eye out for signs, uneven surfaces, and distracted drivers. Your ankles will thank you.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Pollen's Mild Annoyance**
With moderate pollen levels, if you're prone to seasonal allergies, pop an antihistamine before you head out. Nobody enjoys watery eyes and a runny nose mid-stride.
</AlertBox>

In short: it's hot, pollen's around, and Fremont is perpetually under construction. If you're hitting the pavement, stick to the shade and carry more water than you think you need. Or, just embrace the gym today. Your call, but don't say I didn't warn you.
