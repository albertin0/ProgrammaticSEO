### Weather & Air Quality Rundown

Today's forecast is "Mainly Clear" and hot. The National Weather Service is predicting high pressure to build back into Southern California, drying us out and pushing daytime highs into the mid-90s for inland areas. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's dangerous, especially if you're not acclimated or plan a long, strenuous workout. The good news is that previous tropical moisture has moved out, so rain isn't a concern.

The Air Quality Index is sitting at a moderate 67 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. We've also had a few small wildfires pop up recently, like the Streamview Fire near Streamview Drive and Gayle Street on September 27th, and the FORESTER and Sandia fires earlier in the week. While these are small and haven't drastically spiked the AQI, they're a stark reminder that fire season is a year-round battle, and SDG&E is even talking Public Safety Power Shutoffs. Keep an eye on your local reports if you're near any active incidents.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** With temperatures in the 90s, heat exhaustion and heatstroke are real threats. Schedule your workouts for very early morning or late evening, or better yet, move them indoors. Drink way more water than you think you need.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk & Obstacles

If Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve is your go-to for elevation and views, you're out of luck. The hiking trails and Torrey Pines Lodge are closed for the second phase of infrastructure upgrades, and they'll be shut down until early 2027. Don't even think about trying to sneak in there; state parks aren't playing around.

Over at Mission Trails Regional Park, you'll still find construction impacting access. The Father Junipero Serra Trail has limited weekday access until Spring 2027 due to ongoing sewer pipeline rehabilitation. The vehicle gates are closed 24/7 through late 2028, but parts of the walking/bike path remain open, though likely crowded. Plan your route carefully and be ready for detours.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Coastal Flood Advisory:** Areas along the San Diego coastline are under a Coastal Flood Advisory until Wednesday evening. Expect elevated surf and higher than normal tides causing minor tidal overflow in low-lying beach parking lots and some coastal erosion. If you're running near the ocean, watch your step and don't try to power through standing water.
</AlertBox>
