## Why today is decidedly *not* the day:

<BulletList>
*   It's a full-blown inferno out there with temperatures pushing 33°C (91.4°F) under clear skies. You're not acclimated to this in November.
*   While the air quality isn't *terrible* (AQI 67), that combined with moderate pollen and extreme heat is a recipe for a bad time.

Look, I get it. You want to get your miles in, hit the trails. But today's conditions are borderline dangerous, especially if you're planning anything strenuous.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning!
With the mercury hitting 33.0°C (91.4°F), you're risking heat exhaustion, heat stroke, and severe dehydration. This isn't just "uncomfortable," it's medically significant. Your body is going to be working overtime just to keep cool. Running or intense cycling in this is a terrible idea unless you're literally just crossing the street. If you *must* be outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and hydrate like it's your job. Like, pre-hydrate, during-hydrate, and post-hydrate. And maybe just reconsider entirely. Your PT will thank you for not showing up with heat cramps.
</AlertBox>

### What to Watch For

The air quality around San Diego is sitting at a moderate AQI of 67, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. Mix that with a moderate pollen count, and your lungs are already doing more work than usual. Add the oppressive heat, and suddenly that "moderate" rating feels a lot heavier.

Coastal areas are still a bit of a mess from the king tides that set records just last week. While the peak has passed, don't assume your usual route along the Mission Bay paths or the Silver Strand is perfectly clear or stable. Record water levels mean erosion, debris, and potentially slippery or soft ground. Marin County emergency officials even warned residents not to walk or cycle through flooded areas recently. Best to avoid those low-lying areas near the coast for now.

We've got some localized headaches, too. If you're looking to hit up Tecolote Canyon, be aware that some trail segments in Phase 3 (from Mt. Acadia to Genesee Avenue) are still shut down 24/7 due to the ongoing trunk sewer project. These closures are expected to last well into next summer or even fall 2027. Don't just blindly follow your old Strava segments into a construction zone.

Also, remember it's November 1st, and the city's not exactly quiet. There's the Fall Back Festival in the Gaslamp Quarter from noon to 4 PM, which means crowds and road closures downtown. The Hillcrest Farmers Market and the Gaslamp Artisan Market are also in full swing, closing off streets and drawing people out. Good luck navigating that if you're trying to bike anywhere near those areas.
