Look, I get it. We live in San Diego. We *do* things outside. But today? Today is a strong contender for "stay indoors, hydrate, and pretend you're still working out on the Peloton" kind of day.

The mercury is sitting at a balmy 33.0°C (that’s 91.4°F for those still in the dark ages), and it's "Mainly Clear," which means absolutely no shade to bail you out. The National Weather Service has an Extreme Heat Warning slapped on San Diego County Mountains, Deserts, and the Coachella Valley until August 2nd, with temperatures pushing well into the 90s and even 100s for those inland and desert areas. Even coastal and inland valleys are looking at mid-80s to upper-90s, which is plenty hot enough to ruin your day if you’re pushing it. And don't even think the nights will save you; elevated low temperatures mean limited overnight relief.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning in Effect!** Seriously, folks, this isn't a drill. Heat-related illnesses spike in these conditions. If you absolutely *must* be outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and drink more water than you think you need. Consider moving your workout to an air-conditioned gym or postponing it altogether. Your organs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

Air quality is sitting at a Moderate AQI of 67, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. The good news is we don't have a massive wildfire actively choking us with smoke right now, but that moderate rating combined with the oppressive heat isn't doing your respiratory system any favors. Your lungs are already working overtime just trying to cool you down; don't make them filter extra crud.

And for my fellow ocean-lovers: the surf is up, but not in a good way. A high surf advisory and rip current danger are in effect through this afternoon for San Diego coastal areas, including La Jolla. We're talking 3-6 foot surf with sets up to 7 feet on south-facing beaches. Unless you enjoy fighting currents and potentially getting thrown around, maybe skip the ocean swim and stick to a pool if you're desperate for water time. Minor coastal flooding is even possible during evening high tides.

Don't forget the construction and trail closures. If you were planning on a peaceful jog through parts of **Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve**, think again. Most trails are still impacted by a multi-phase improvement project, with Phase II potentially causing ongoing closures around now. Also, watch out for construction-related closures on the Escondido Creek Trail. If your usual route takes you through downtown, expect some street closures for farmer's markets in Little Italy and Ocean Beach today.
