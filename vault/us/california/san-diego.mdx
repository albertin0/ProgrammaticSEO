## Your San Diego Outdoor Workout Reality Check: October 12, 2026

Alright, San Diego. If you're looking out the window right now and thinking it's a great day for a long run or a beach workout, you might want to rethink your priorities. Yeah, the skies might be "mainly clear" and the sun's beating down, pushing the mercury to a ridiculous 33.0°C (that's 91.4°F for those of you not on the metric system, and way too hot for October), but we're still dealing with the soggy, messy aftermath of Tropical Storm Rachel.

The NWS has been clear: coastal flooding advisories are in effect through tomorrow morning, meaning dangerous high tides are still a factor along our beaches. If you're planning a barefoot run in Coronado or trying to hit the sand in La Jolla, expect significant beach erosion, flooded parking lots, and pathways that are less "scenic stroll" and more "unexpected swim." And those rip currents? They're still strong and ready to pull you out to sea faster than you can say "recovery jog." The San Diego Fire-Rescue Department is literally on "Water Rescue Alert 2" because of all this mess.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Coastal Flood Advisory & Rip Current Warning:** Avoid all beach and coastal path activities. High tides today and tomorrow mean unpredictable and dangerous conditions, including potential for being swept out. The San Diego Fire-Rescue Department is on heightened alert.
</AlertBox>

Inland isn't much better, especially if you planned on hitting any trails near our waterways. Numerous San Diego River crossings and streets, like parts of San Diego Mission Road and Avenida del Rio near Fashion Valley, are still closed due to flooding. Don't be a hero; those "puddles" are likely deeper, faster-moving water than you think. This goes for places like the Tijuana River Valley Regional Park where some trails, including the Cathedral Trail, are explicitly closed because of flooding and road risks. You think your ankles are ready for that? As a PT, I can tell you they are not.

While the local Air Pollution Control District might optimistically say air quality is "GOOD" today, a 67 on the AQI scale isn't exactly fresh mountain air for sensitive individuals. ### Heat & Future Fire Watch

And then there's that heat. 33 degrees Celsius is no joke. Even if it feels "clear," that sun is still baking down, rapidly dehydrating you. If you insist on exercising outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and hydrate aggressively. Your joints might appreciate the warmth, but your core temperature won't.

Looking ahead, we're forecast to swing right into high temperatures and the first Santa Ana winds by the end of the week, which means a heightened wildfire risk. So, if you manage to avoid drowning today, you might be breathing smoke later. San Diego, always keeping it interesting.
