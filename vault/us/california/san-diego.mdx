## Why today isn't the day for heroics

<BulletList>
*   **Scorching Heat Advisory:** An Extreme Heat Warning is active for parts of the county, and frankly, 33 degrees Celsius (91.4°F) in October isn't exactly "fall weather." Expect it to feel even hotter, especially inland, with some areas like El Cajon eyeing triple digits later this weekend.
*   **AQI on the Fringe:** While not in the "danger zone," an AQI of 67 means moderate air quality with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. Combine that with the heat, and your lungs are already working overtime just existing, let alone pushing the pace.
*   **Ever-Present Fire Threat:** It's still fire season, folks. The unusually warm, dry conditions are perfect for ignitions, even if we're not currently inhaling smoke. Keep those eyes peeled and stay off trails that look crispy.
</BulletList>

### The Heat is On (and it's not a good thing)

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### EXTREME HEAT WARNING IN EFFECT!
The National Weather Service has issued an Extreme Heat Warning that started today and runs through next Thursday, impacting our Santa Ana Mountains and foothills, and potentially spilling over into inland and coastal areas. Seriously, 90+ degrees is not the time to push for a PR. Heat stroke is real, and it sneaks up on you faster than you think. Stay hydrated *before*, *during*, and *after* any outdoor activity. If you must get out there, do it at dawn or dusk, and stick to shaded routes.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Moderate Air Quality & Pollen
An AQI of 67 is "moderate," but with PM2.5 as the main culprit, it means there are fine particulate matter floating around. Your lungs don't love that, especially when they're already huffing and puffing from the heat and that "moderate" pollen count. Anyone with asthma or other respiratory issues needs to be extra cautious.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: Mission Trails Regional Park

Heading to Mission Trails Regional Park? Great choice for varied terrain, but remember that river crossing near the Jackson Drive entrance? Still no bridge. While we're not expecting rain today, the water levels can be unpredictable even without recent downpours. A misstep in that heat, soaking wet, is just asking for trouble. Consider alternative routes or be prepared to turn back if it looks sketchy.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Pro-Tip from a Runner with Knees that Feel the Forecast
If you absolutely *have* to get your miles in today, make it short, make it early, and don't be a hero. Listen to your body. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or like you're running through soup, stop. There's always tomorrow. And for the love of all that is holy, bring more water than you think you need.
</AlertBox>
