## Today's Workout Hazards

It's Friday, October 9th, and San Diego is still cooking. While the official "Extreme Heat Warning" for coastal and valley areas might have expired last night, today's 91.4°F (33.0°C) temperatures are no joke. We've seen record-breaking heat all week, and emergency services have been swamped with heat-related 911 calls, many from folks trying to exercise or work outside. Do yourself a favor and find an air-conditioned gym, or at the very least, stick to the shaded, earliest hours if you absolutely *must* be outside.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: Stay Hydrated or Stay Inside!
The mercury is still soaring, making outdoor workouts, especially mid-day, extremely dangerous. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are real threats. This isn't the day to push your limits on the Cowles Mountain trail, no matter how much you love those views. Hydrate constantly, wear light clothing, and seriously consider moving your sweat session indoors.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Coastal Flooding and High Surf: Avoid the Beaches
Thanks to some tropical storm action, our coastline is getting battered. Expect high surf, dangerous rip currents, and localized flooding. Areas like Pacific Beach have already seen berms breached, and the Ocean Beach Pier has sustained damage. Forget your beach run or ocean swim today; it's not worth the risk of getting swept away or caught in rising waters.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Air Quality: Lungs Beware
With an AQI of 67, classified as "Moderate," and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, those with respiratory sensitivities should think twice before prolonged outdoor activity. Even if you're usually fine, the combination of this air quality and the intense heat can be a nasty surprise for your lungs.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan B: Hit the Gym or Embrace the Shade
If you're still determined to get moving, aim for pre-sunrise or post-sunset, and stick to areas with ample shade like some of the tree-lined paths in Balboa Park, though even there, the heat will be a factor. Better yet, find a cool, indoor space. Your body will thank you.
</AlertBox>
