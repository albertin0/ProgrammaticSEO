### Today's Take: Just Stay Inside

Look, I get it. We live in San Diego. We *should* be outside. But today, April 2, 2026, the weather gods are clearly playing a sick joke. That 33°C (91.4°F) isn't just "warm," it's dangerously hot for springtime. You think a little ocean breeze is going to save you? Think again. That heat advisory isn't just for show. We've seen these warnings come out for the deserts and even coastal areas recently, emphasizing just how bad this kind of heat can be.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Danger!
Seriously, that 91.4°F is no joke. Reschedule your runs, hikes, or intense outdoor training for early morning *tomorrow* or hit the gym (if it's not a poorly maintained one like some I've heard about around Mission Valley). Hydration is paramount, and "stay in an air-conditioned room" is not just a suggestion, it's a damn directive when it's this hot. Don't be that person requiring a rescue from some trail because you pushed too hard.

### What's Hitting the Trails (or Not)

Beyond the heat, let's talk about why your usual outdoor routes might be less than ideal. Just a few days ago, on March 30, a brush fire flared up near the Walmart on Town Center Parkway in Santee, close enough to warrant evacuations. While it's reportedly contained, this kind of heat and the "above-normal large fire potential" forecast for Southern California mean you can't be complacent. The air might not be visibly smoky, but lingering particulate matter from any recent fires, coupled with the moderate AQI, is a double-whammy for your lungs.

If you're eyeing a trail run, especially anything rugged, take heed. Reports from the Cleveland National Forest as recent as March 24 indicate significant trail damage. We're talking ruts from previous rains, downed trees you'll have to scramble over, and sections with loose rock due to erosion. And let's not forget perennial closures like parts of Guajome Regional Park's nature trail or several trails in Tijuana River Valley Regional Park. Even supposedly pristine spots like Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve frequently close steeper trails like Broken Hill and Razor Point after rain to prevent erosion, and those steps at Parry Grove? Slippery. So, unless you enjoy a good ankle sprain with your triple-digit workout, maybe pick a paved route or, you know, a treadmill.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Watch Your Step: Trail Conditions Are Sketchy
If you absolutely *must* hit the dirt, be aware that many trails, particularly in areas like Cleveland National Forest, might have hidden ruts, downed trees, and loose rock. Call park offices like Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve before you head out, because they *will* close trails for damage or flooding. Nobody wants to be carried out on a stretcher because they ignored the signs.
</AlertBox>
