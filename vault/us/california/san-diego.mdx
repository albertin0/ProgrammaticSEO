## Today's Local Sweat-or-Suffer Breakdown

### Air Quality & Allergens

The "Moderate" AQI with PM2.5 means your lungs are going to be working overtime trying to filter out fine particulate matter. Think of it as running with a mild cold, but instead of snot, it's microscopic crud. Coupled with a moderate pollen level, anyone with seasonal allergies or asthma is going to have a rough go. Even if you're a granite-lunged beast, why push it?

### Heat & Hydration

That 33.0C (91.4F) is no joke, particularly if you're not staying near the coast, which is usually a bit more forgiving. If you were planning on tackling anything in the San Diego County deserts, you should know there was a wind advisory in effect until 11 AM today with gusts up to 55 mph. That combination of heat and strong, dry winds is a recipe for rapid dehydration and heatstroke, not a PR. Even if the advisory has passed, those desert regions are still cooking.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  If you're out in the inland or desert parts of San Diego County, that 33.0C is officially in "dangerously hot" territory. Seriously, consider indoor alternatives. Heat-related illnesses increase significantly during extreme heat events.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Conditions & Closures

Good luck trying to hit up Waterfront Park downtown today for your usual loop; it's closed for a "special event." So much for that plan. And if you usually frequent the Tijuana River Valley Regional Park, know that parts of it, like the Cathedral Trail, are still closed, and other trails might be iffy. Always check with County Parks if you're heading to lesser-known spots, because "open" doesn't always mean "safe."

<AlertBox type="warning">
  Avoid any deep, strenuous runs in the inland valleys or deserts. Even if you feel fine, that heat combined with active fires potentially impacting air quality across the county is a bad mix. Stick to shaded, flatter routes if you *must* run, and for the love of all that is holy, bring more water than you think you need.
</AlertBox>
