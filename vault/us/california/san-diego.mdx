## The Heat is On (Literally)

Look, I know you love that "sunny San Diego" vibe, but today, that sun is a straight-up oven in a lot of places. While the coast might be a bit milder, if you're thinking of hitting anything east of the 15, you need to seriously reconsider. The deserts are under an Extreme Heat Warning until Thursday, with temperatures up to a scorching 117°F and overnight lows barely dipping below the 80s. Even our mountains, like those around Julian or Palomar, are under a heat advisory, expecting highs in the 90s today and tomorrow. That 33.0°C (91.4°F) reported temperature? That's what you're looking at in our inland valleys, which is just plain too hot for any serious effort, especially mid-day.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Danger: Extreme Heat.** Avoid strenuous outdoor activity between 10 AM and 8 PM, especially in inland valleys, mountains, and *definitely* the deserts. This isn't just about feeling sweaty; heat illness is a real, potentially fatal, threat. Drink fluids like it's your job, and don't be a hero.
</AlertBox>

## Breathing Issues: Smoke and Pollen Combo

Just when you thought the heat was enough, we've got smoke. The Pipeline Fire up on Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton, north of Oceanside, has been burning for a few days, scorching over 1,000 acres. While it's 80% contained now, there are still smoke advisories for communities in North San Diego County like Ramona, Pala, Alpine, Fallbrook, and Valley Center. Our AQI is sitting at 67 ("Moderate"), with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. This means fine particulate matter from that fire is floating around, and it's not good for your lungs. Combine that with a planned CAL FIRE training burn near Phelan today and tomorrow, which will add more smoke to the air, and your breathing is going to suffer if you're in the wrong place.

Then there's the moderate pollen. Not a huge deal on its own, but with PM2.5 coating your lungs, adding allergens into the mix is just asking for a rough run.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Danger: Wildfire Smoke.** If you're in North County, check the hyperlocal air quality before heading out. If you can see or smell smoke, your lungs are telling you to stay inside. Sensitive groups especially should exercise indoors today.
</AlertBox>

For example, the Cathedral Trail in Tijuana River Valley Regional Park is currently closed due to ongoing work. Always check park websites before you head out, especially if you're venturing off the beaten path. Construction happens.
