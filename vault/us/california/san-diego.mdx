## Today's Outdoor Grind: Heatstroke vs. Your PR

Look, I love hitting the trails as much as the next masochist, but today's a real test of judgment, not grit. That 33-degree temperature isn't just "warm," it's a "stay inside and complain about your AC bill" kind of heat. The National Weather Service has issued warnings about this unseasonable heat wave, and for good reason. Your body isn't acclimated to this in early April. Pushing it now is a fast track to dehydration, heat exhaustion, or worse.

### Your Lungs Aren't Fans of This Either

Beyond the obvious heat, we've got a cocktail of air issues. The AQI is moderate, but don't let that fool you into thinking it's pristine. Moderate levels of PM2.5 are the dominant pollutant. And for anyone near the South Bay, around the Tijuana River, there are documented issues with elevated hydrogen sulfide levels, especially during these warmer, stagnant conditions. So, while you're battling the heat, you could also be inhaling some not-so-fresh air.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning:** With temperatures in the 30s Celsius (90s Fahrenheit) across the county, and even higher inland, serious heat-related illness is a very real threat. Avoid strenuous outdoor activity during peak daylight hours. Hydrate constantly, and if you MUST be outside, stick to short, easy efforts in the early morning or late evening. This isn't a day for PRs; it's a day for survival.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**South Bay Air Quality Concern:** If you're running near the Tijuana River Valley or Imperial Beach, be aware of potential localized spikes in hydrogen sulfide due to ongoing sewage contamination. This can exacerbate respiratory issues, especially with the heat. Consider an alternate route away from the immediate area.
</AlertBox>

### Trail & Beach Status

Most major trails like those at Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve are technically open, which is great, but the heat still applies. Just remember their strict "no dogs" policy. However, several trails in the Tijuana River Valley Regional Park – specifically Sherwood, Vireo (west of Hollister St.), and Cathedral Trails – are closed, and you might encounter noise and dust from ongoing work there.

If your idea of a cool-down involves the ocean, note that the waters off Imperial Beach remain closed due to sewage contamination, and advisories are active at several other spots including Glorietta Bay Park Shoreline and Ocean Beach Dog Beach. Stick to dry land, or a pool, for now.
