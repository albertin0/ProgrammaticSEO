### Why today isn't the day

<BulletList>
*   An unseasonably dangerous extreme heat warning is gripping San Diego, with temperatures forecast to hit up to 90°F (32°C) along the coast and well into the 100s inland. This isn't your typical October warmth; it's record-breaking fall heat.
*   Elevated fire weather danger is a very real concern across the county, fueled by this intense heat, low humidity, and weak to moderate easterly winds. This means not only is the risk of wildfires higher, but the air quality could degrade rapidly.
*   Your lungs are already dealing with a double whammy: Moderate air quality (AQI 66, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant) and moderate pollen levels. Adding extreme heat to that mix is just asking for trouble.
</BulletList>

## Today's San Diego Workout Hazards: Sweat, Smoke, and Snot

Look, I love hitting the trails around Mission Trails Regional Park as much as the next runner, but today is a hard pass for anything strenuous outside, especially during peak heat. We're currently sitting at a clear 23.7°C (74.7°F), but don't let that fool you. The National Weather Service is telling us to brace for highs that are 15 to 25 degrees *above* seasonal averages. This isn't a "push through it" situation; this is a "your body will shut down" situation.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning & Elevated Fire Danger
An Excessive Heat Warning is in effect until at least Thursday. Coastal areas are projected to reach the mid-80s to mid-90s, while high-exposure inland zones are set to approach 100°F. This puts you at high risk for heat stroke and exhaustion. On top of that, meteorologists are warning of elevated fire danger due to dry conditions and offshore Santa Ana winds. Any spark could be catastrophic.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Air Quality & Pollen
The Air Quality Index is currently 66, which is in the Moderate range, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. Combine that with moderate pollen levels, and your respiratory system is already under stress. If a fire does kick up, that AQI number will jump, and not in a good way.
</AlertBox>

### Stay Safe Out There (Or In There)
If you absolutely *must* get a workout in, stick to early mornings or late evenings when temperatures are slightly cooler. Hydrate aggressively, far beyond what you think you need. Consider moving your workout indoors to a gym or even just doing some bodyweight stuff at home. Seriously, today is the day for the treadmill or a swim in the ocean (but be aware that ongoing sewage flows through the Tijuana River are making parts of the Pacific unfit for swimmers near the border).
</AlertBox>

Your health isn't worth bragging rights about enduring a scorching run. Live to run another day, when the air isn't trying to cook you alive.
