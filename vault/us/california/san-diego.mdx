### **Extreme Heat Advisory in Effect!**
San Diego County is under a heat advisory through Friday evening, with inland areas potentially reaching 105°F. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous. Heatstroke is a real, potentially fatal, condition. Your body struggles to cool itself efficiently when it's this hot, especially with humidity. That 91.4°F we're seeing locally isn't the whole story; inland zones are far worse, and even near the coast, it's oppressive.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, folks. I've seen too many runners push through "just one more mile" only to end up with heat exhaustion on the side of the road. Your PR can wait. Is that Strava segment worth a trip to urgent care? I'm a PT; trust me, the paperwork is a nightmare.

That "moderate" AQI of 67, with PM2.5 as the main culprit, means tiny particles are floating around, ready to irritate your airways. Pair that with moderate pollen, and anyone with even a hint of asthma or seasonal allergies is going to be miserable. It's not just about feeling it; it's about the microscopic damage you're doing.

And let's not forget the ever-present gift from our neighbors to the south: Tijuana River sewage. The county just approved a huge plan to deal with the decades-long pollution crisis, which includes aerosolized contaminants affecting our coastal air. So, if you were thinking of a picturesque run along Imperial Beach, think again. You're not just getting a sea breeze; you might be getting a dose of *other* airborne particles.

### Where NOT to Push It

Thinking of hitting the iconic trails at **Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve**? That bluff climb in this heat is a guaranteed recipe for a bad time. The exposed trails offer little shade, making the already challenging elevation gain a dehydrating death march. Even a casual stroll could land you in trouble. Stick to the AC, save the views for a cooler, less lung-taxing day.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Coastal Air Quality Concern: Tijuana River Pollution**
Recent news indicates ongoing issues with aerosolized sewage and chemicals from the Tijuana River affecting air quality in San Diego's coastal communities, leading to beach closures. This poses a health risk beyond standard AQI measurements. Exercise caution or choose alternative locations if you're near these areas.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Hydration & Timing Are Key (If You Must)**
If you absolutely *have* to get outside, aim for before sunrise or after sunset. Carry way more water than you think you need. Wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing. And for the love of all that is holy, listen to your body. Any signs of dizziness, nausea, or excessive sweating means it's time to stop. Seriously.
</AlertBox>
