## Today's Hazards: Sweat, Smoke, and Stinging Eyes

Look, I've run enough miles around this city to know when it's a bad idea, and today is one of those days. That "mainly clear" sky is a deception. What it means is that blistering sun is beating down on you with full force, driving temperatures into the low 90s in many parts of San Diego County. You might think the coast is a reprieve, but even there, anything strenuous in this heat is a recipe for trouble.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat and Wildfire Threat

The temperature at 33.0°C (91.4°F) is a serious heat risk. We've had extreme heat warnings for our desert areas just recently, with temperatures hitting over 100°F, and even outside those zones, 90+ degrees is nothing to scoff at. Couple that with an uncontained "Oak Fire" reported in San Diego County on May 12, and you've got a recipe for potential respiratory issues and heightened fire danger. The National Interagency Fire Center even flagged the Southwest for "above normal significant fire potential" this month due to dry conditions. Stay away from any areas with visible smoke, and if you're hitting the dirt, choose somewhere coastal and paved, if you must go out at all. Seriously, don't be a hero.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Air Quality & Pollen

Our Air Quality Index is currently sitting at a "moderate" 67, with PM2.5 being the main culprit. This means sensitive groups, including runners, might already experience some respiratory irritation. Add in "moderate" pollen levels, and your lungs are going to be working overtime even before you factor in the heat or any potential smoke from the Oak Fire. Your sinuses will hate you, and your lungs won't be far behind.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Stay Smart Out There (Or Just Stay In)

If you absolutely *must* get your miles in, pick your timing wisely. Early morning or late evening are your only real options, and even then, hydration is non-negotiable. Plan your route to avoid any known trail closures, like the continued maintenance at Sweetwater Bike Park or the longer-term closures at Guajome Regional Park. And please, tell someone where you're going. Heat stroke comes on faster than you think, especially when you're pushing it.
</AlertBox>
