Look, I get it. The sun is out, it's not scorching, and it's certainly not raining on our parade like it did in other parts of California last year. You look outside and think, "Perfect running weather!" And it *feels* pretty good, I won't lie. But you gotta remember what's actually in that "perfect" air. Moderate AQI means fine particulate matter is floating around, potentially irritating your respiratory system, especially during strenuous activity. It's not a "stay indoors and board up the windows" kind of day, but it's also not pristine mountain air, despite what the postcards might tell you.

For my fellow allergy sufferers, that moderate pollen isn't going to do you any favors. If you're planning a long run through the trails near Florida Canyon or even just around the outer loop of Balboa Park, prep accordingly. Eye drops, nasal spray, whatever your poison – take it. Otherwise, you'll be sniffling your way past the Museum of Us, and nobody wants that.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  The combination of moderate AQI and moderate pollen means it's a double whammy for anyone with respiratory sensitivities or seasonal allergies. Consider shortening your outdoor session or opting for an indoor workout if you're particularly susceptible. Pay attention to how you feel, and don't push through discomfort.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  If you're still determined to get those miles in, think about adjusting your route to areas with less vehicle traffic if PM2.5 is a concern, or staying on paved paths if dusty trails aggravate your allergies. Hydrate well, and maybe save the hill repeats for a day when your lungs aren't already doing extra work.
</AlertBox>

No major fires, no freak floods, no trail closures reported for our area, which is always a win in my book. So, go enjoy it, but don't be a hero. Listen to your body – it's usually smarter than your training plan.
