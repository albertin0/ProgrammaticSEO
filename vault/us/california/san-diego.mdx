## What to Watch Out For

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Extreme Heat & Dehydration Risk**
At 33.0°C (91.4°F), you are squarely in the danger zone for heat exhaustion and heat stroke. San Diego officially considers anything over 93.1°F "extreme heat," and you're within spitting distance. Stay hydrated, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, avoid exercising between 10 AM and 4 PM. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; it's about potentially landing yourself in the ER. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get inside and cool down *immediately*. Outdoor workers and athletes are specifically listed as at-risk groups for heat-related illnesses.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Wildfire Smoke and PM2.5**
The "Moderate" AQI with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant means sensitive individuals – that includes runners pushing their limits – might experience respiratory irritation. There are active wildfires in San Diego County, so localized smoke impacts are entirely possible, even if the overall city reading isn't "Unhealthy" yet. Don't push it if your throat starts to tickle or your chest feels tight.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Navigating Closures & Crowds**
If you're a regular on the trails, check the City and County Parks websites before you head out. Tecolote Canyon is a mess of construction, so your usual route might be a no-go. And good luck getting a peaceful run near Liberty Station with the charity event taking over. Consider hitting up areas like Torrey Pines, which thankfully just reopened their main trails after some construction, giving us a small window of reprieve before Phase 2 kicks off later this summer. However, be mindful of the heat along the exposed coastal trails.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, consider an indoor workout today. Your lungs and joints (and your sanity trying to navigate closures) will thank you.
