## Today's Workout Outlook

Look, the weather gods aren't actively trying to kill you today, which is a win for San Diego in late October. The temperature is a pretty sweet 19.2°C (around 66°F) under an overcast sky, meaning no brutal sun exposure to drain you halfway through your Torrey Pines climb. That's a definite plus for pushing those longer distances without feeling like you're running on the surface of the sun.

However, let's not pretend it's all sunshine and PRs. We've got that pesky "moderate" air quality, largely thanks to PM2.5 particles. If you've got any kind of respiratory sensitivity—asthma, bronchitis, or just a general aversion to coughing up a lung—take it easy. This isn't the day to try and set a new personal best on your speed work if you're one of those folks. The air might not look visibly hazy, but those microscopic particles are still there, doing their thing.

Then there's the "moderate" pollen count. For half of us, that means nothing. For the other half, it means spending half your run thinking you're getting sick, when really it's just your body's immune system overreacting to plant bits. If you're planning a long loop around **Mission Trails Regional Park**, especially on some of the dusty singletrack trails, consider popping an antihistamine an hour before you head out. Trust me, snot rockets aren't a good look, and they definitely won't improve your splits.

<AlertBox type="warning">

### Watch Your Lungs, Watch Your Nose

With AQI at a moderate 62 and pollen also moderate, anyone with allergies or respiratory issues should proceed with caution. Listen to your body and don't push through discomfort. It's not worth it.

</AlertBox>

No major fires, road closures, or gale-force winds have been reported locally in the last 24 hours, which is good news for once. The overcast skies do mean things might be a bit damp underfoot, especially in shaded areas or on less-trafficked trails. A bit of light rain is even in the forecast for tonight, so if you're an evening runner, expect potentially slicker conditions later on. Choose your footwear wisely, especially if you're tackling any technical terrain or heading up the slick rock areas at Cowles Mountain. Nobody wants a twisted ankle before the holiday season.

<AlertBox type="info">

### Hydrate Smart, Stay Aware

Even with overcast skies and cooler temps, don't skimp on hydration. And if you're out on any trails, be mindful of potentially damp or slippery surfaces, especially if you're trail running in shoes with worn treads.

</AlertBox>

So, can you go outside? Yeah, probably. Should you go for an all-out effort? Only if your lungs and sinuses are made of steel. The rest of us should probably just enjoy a steady pace and appreciate not melting in the sun.
