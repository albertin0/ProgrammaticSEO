While the coastal temperature of 18.1°C (a pleasant 64.6°F) might make you think it’s prime running weather, don't let that fool you. San Diego County is under the shadow of an active wildfire, and the air quality reflects it. PM2.5 particles are small enough to get deep into your lungs, and even "moderate" levels can irritate airways, especially if you're pushing the pace. Combine that with a moderate pollen count, and anyone with allergies or asthma is in for a rough go.

<AlertBox type="danger">
The Mtz/sdu//border 7 Fire that started yesterday in San Diego County is uncontained. Smoke can travel, and even if you don't see flames, the particulate matter will impact air quality. If you start to smell smoke or feel any respiratory irritation, cut your workout short and head indoors. This isn't the day to try and PR.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, folks. Between the smoke-induced PM2.5 floating around and the moderate pollen, your respiratory system is already working overtime. Layering on high-intensity exercise is just asking for trouble. If you absolutely *must* get out, consider a shorter, low-intensity walk. And for the love of all that is holy, check for fire updates before you head anywhere near brushy areas. Wildfire season is here, early or not.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Multiple trail systems are experiencing closures. Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve and Del Mar Mesa Preserve trails remain closed due to the Sorrento Fire. And with a new fire active, expect more potential closures. Always respect posted signs and avoid closed areas. Not only is it dangerous, but you could also interfere with emergency response.
</AlertBox>

Even if you're sticking to the pavement, you're not entirely in the clear. Today is jam-packed with events. If you were thinking of a leisurely jog around the Embarcadero, prepare to navigate around crowds heading to The Rady Shell for the Jazz Festival. Heading north? The Switchfoot BRO-AM in Encinitas is a massive beach event that will cause gridlock and pedestrian chaos. Sometimes, the biggest hazard isn't the environment, but the sheer volume of other people.

<AlertBox type="info">
If you're determined to get outside, stick to known, open areas, and maybe adjust your expectations. A casual stroll is probably a better choice than an all-out sprint. Hydrate extra, even though it's not scorching hot, and if you have any respiratory sensitivities, consider an indoor alternative today.
</AlertBox>
