### Beach Bum Warning: High Surf & Rip Currents
Heads up, coastal runners! The National Weather Service has issued a High Surf Advisory through Tuesday night. We're talking surf generally 5-7 feet, with some sets hitting 8 feet, especially on those south and southwest-facing beaches. Strong rip currents are a serious hazard, so if your run takes you near the water's edge, stay vigilant. Don't be that person who ignores the warnings and needs a lifeguard to ruin their morning. It's not worth it.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Troubles & Construction Zones

Even if you're not a beach person, you're not entirely in the clear. Construction is the never-ending story in San Diego, and it's impacting a few key spots:

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Mission Trails Regional Park: Pipeline Pains
The Father Junipero Serra Trail within Mission Trails Regional Park is still an active construction zone for sewer rehabilitation. While the trail generally remains open for walkers, hikers, and cyclists, expect temporary lane closures and vehicle gates closed 24/7 through late 2028. That means no vehicular access for some trailheads like Oak Grove Loop, Climbers Loop, and Grinding Rocks. Expect noise, dust, and maybe even some odors. Keep your eyes peeled for signs, and be patient with the crews working.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Torrey Pines Trails Are Currently Open (Mostly)
Good news for fans of those iconic ocean views: Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve's main trails, upper parking lots, and park roads are currently fully accessible after Phase 1 of construction wrapped up. So, get those glorious climbs in while you can, because Phase 2 is slated to kick off in August or September.
</AlertBox>

Overall, it's a decent day to get out there, but you need to be smart about *where* you go. Skip the risky beach runs by the water and be prepared for detours if you're hitting the trails where active construction is happening. Don't let a nice cool day trick you into complacency.
