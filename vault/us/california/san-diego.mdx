You want to work out outside today? Don't be an idiot. Seriously, stay indoors.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
*   San Diego is under a Heat Advisory, with temperatures soaring well into the 90s and even pushing 100°F in inland valleys. Your body isn't built for this kind of torture.
*   Air Quality Index is sitting at a moderate 67, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. Couple that with moderate pollen levels, and your lungs are going to feel like you're sucking on a tailpipe during allergy season.
*   Forget those epic climbs; several popular trails are *closed* for the entire month of August specifically because of this kind of dangerous heat.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We live in San Diego. The sunshine usually calls to us like a siren song. But today, that siren is luring you straight into a heat stroke. The National Weather Service issued a Heat Advisory for a reason, folks – high temperatures are expected to cause heat illnesses. We're talking mid-90s to 103°F in places like the inland valleys. Your trusty weather app might say "Mainly Clear," but it's clearly not factoring in how much your kidneys will regret a long run right now.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning!
An official Heat Advisory is in effect until Sunday evening, warning of dangerous temperatures in the mid-90s to 103°F in San Diego's inland areas. Outdoor recreation during the daytime is *discouraged*. This isn't a suggestion; it's a plea to avoid heat exhaustion or worse.
</AlertBox>

Your joints? They're already working overtime trying to regulate your internal temperature. Adding impact from running or heavy lifting outside will just stress them further, increasing inflammation. And let's not pretend you're getting enough quality work in when you're constantly battling dehydration and lightheadedness.

The county isn't messing around either. They've already shut down trails at places like El Capitan Preserve, Hellhole Canyon, and Mount Gower for the entire month of August, specifically due to the extreme heat. That should tell you everything you need to know about the risk level. If the trails themselves are taking a vacation, so should you, or at least your outdoor activities.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Moderate Air Quality & Pollen Combo
With an AQI of 67 and moderate pollen, even without the scorching heat, your respiratory system is facing a double whammy. If you've got any kind of lung issue or seasonal allergies, you're going to feel this one.
</AlertBox>

Stick to the treadmill, hit an air-conditioned gym, or at least save your outdoor adventures for the very early morning or late evening, though honestly, even then, the ground will be radiating heat for hours. And if you absolutely, positively *must* be outside, please hydrate like it's your job. We're talking water, electrolytes, and more water. It's not about being tough; it's about being smart.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Hydration is NOT Optional
If you ignore all warnings and decide to brave the heat, carry more water than you think you need. Aim for 2-4 liters for anything over an hour, and consider electrolyte drinks. Wear loose, light-colored clothing. And for the love of all that is holy, know the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke.
</AlertBox>
