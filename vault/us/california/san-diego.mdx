### Air Quality Report

Alright, let's talk about the air you'll be sucking down. The Air Quality Index is currently sitting at a "Moderate" 91, with PM2.5 being your dominant pollutant. For anyone with even a hint of a sensitive respiratory system, or if you're planning a sustained effort, this isn't ideal. "Moderate" means there's a real chance for sensitive groups to experience minor to moderate symptoms if you're out there huffing and puffing for too long. This isn't fire smoke, thankfully, but it's still particulate matter that your lungs don't need to be dealing with. If you start feeling any throat irritation or breathing difficulties, consider that your body telling you to pack it in.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**AQI Warning: PM2.5 is Present!** With an AQI of 91, sensitive individuals (asthmatics, older adults, children) should seriously limit prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. Everyone else should reduce it too. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

### Weather Report: Scorcher Alert

Today is not just warm; it's straight-up hot. We're looking at a blazing 33°C (91.4°F) under mainly clear skies. Forget the "June Gloom," March 27th, 2026, is serving up an early summer furnace. There are still residual heat advisories and warnings floating around the county from earlier this week, with temperatures expected to be 20 to 30 degrees above normal for this time of year in some areas. This kind of heat, especially when you're not fully acclimated, is a recipe for disaster. We're talking heat exhaustion, heat stroke – the whole miserable package. Don't be that person wheeled into the ER because you "had to get your miles in."

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Danger!** Temperatures at 33°C (91.4°F) are dangerous for outdoor exercise. Hydration is crucial, and if you *must* be outside, keep it short, stay in the shade, and go during the coolest parts of the day (which, let's be real, are not midday today). Listen to your body – it's not being dramatic.
</AlertBox>

### Local Hazards & Annoyances

Beyond the air and the oven-like temps, San Diego's usual state of perpetual construction is throwing some wrenches in your plans.

If you were planning on a breezy run along the **Mission Beach boardwalk** near Belmont Park, think again. Construction on the temporary lifeguard station has closed the Ventura Place roundabout and a section of the boardwalk on weekdays. They've put up a temporary walkway across the sand, but it's hardly ideal for hitting your stride.

Further south, if you frequent the **Tijuana River Valley Regional Park**, be advised that Sherwood Trail, Vireo Trail (west of Hollister St.), and Cathedral Trail are all closed for ongoing work. Expect noise and dust if you're in the vicinity. And don't forget the storm drain upgrades tearing up parts of Fifth Avenue in Hillcrest, limiting access and parking there. Similarly, University Avenue in City Heights is still recovering from a massive street improvement project that has disrupted sidewalks for ages.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction Navigation:** Check local park and city websites before heading out. The Mission Beach boardwalk and Ventura Place roundabout are impacted by construction, and several trails in Tijuana River Valley Regional Park are closed. Plan alternate routes if these are your usual stomping grounds.
</AlertBox>
