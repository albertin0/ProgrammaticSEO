## Today's Workout Outlook: Mostly Green Light, But Watch Your Step

Looks like another classic San Diego day for an outdoor workout. We've got mild temperatures and partly cloudy skies, which is usually a runner's dream. But don't just blindly hit your usual spot, especially if you're a north county coastal enthusiast.

### Air Quality & Allergies: Breathe Easy, Mostly
The air quality is registering at a moderate 52, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. It's not terrible, but if you're sensitive, you might notice it on longer efforts. Pollen is also hanging out in the moderate range. So, for those with allergies or respiratory sensitivities, consider sticking to shorter, lower-intensity workouts, or time your run for earlier in the day when the air tends to be a bit crisper.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Torrey Pines Trail Closures Incoming!
This is a big one for anyone who loves the Torrey Pines cliffs. Starting Monday, September 21, the historic Torrey Pines Lodge and its surrounding hiking trails will be *closed* for extensive modernization. This isn't a quick fix, folks – expect this to last until early 2027. You can still hit Torrey Pines State Beach via the South Beach and North Beach parking lots, but those iconic sandstone trails? Fuhgeddaboudit for a while. Find a new hill to climb this weekend before the gates go up.
</AlertBox>

### Coastal Concerns: The Ocean's Got Attitude
Heads up for those planning beach runs or coastal trail adventures. Southern California is experiencing some pretty significant coastal changes right now due to a marine heatwave and a strengthening El Niño. We're talking huge waves, coastal flooding, and noticeable beach erosion. There's a "coastally trapped Kelvin wave" that was expected to hit San Diego around, well, *today*. While it might not look like a monster surf wave, it contributes to higher sea levels and more erosion.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Stay Clear of Eroded Coastal Bluffs
Given the ongoing coastal erosion and higher sea levels, those stunning bluffs at places like Sunset Cliffs and, yes, even parts of Torrey Pines (away from the immediate construction closure, mind you) are even less stable than usual. Keep a significant distance from cliff edges and be aware of your surroundings. The ocean doesn't care about your Strava segment.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Local Construction Watch
While no major *workout-specific* construction closures were reported today (like trails being blocked), several road maintenance projects were underway yesterday in the 4S Ranch area on streets like Deer Trail Drive and Fox Valley Drive. Also, there's ongoing construction impacting bus stops downtown (11th Ave & Broadway) today, and long-term projects affecting routes near the airport. If you're commuting to your workout, factor in potential traffic or transit delays.
</AlertBox>

Overall, it's a decent day to get out there, but know your route and respect the ocean. And seriously, if Torrey Pines is your jam, start exploring alternatives this weekend.
