## The Rundown: Don't Be a Heatstroke Hero

It's May 30th, and Mother Nature apparently thinks we’re auditioning for a spot on the sun. With the mercury hovering around 91°F (33°C) and "mainly clear" skies, you’re looking at prime conditions for overheating if you’re not careful. This isn't just "a bit warm"; we've got heat advisories for the inland valleys and a full-blown Extreme Heat Warning for our desert friends out near Anza-Borrego. Forget that lunchtime 10k in the canyons. Stick to early mornings or late evenings, or better yet, find some air conditioning. Seriously, your body will thank you.

### Air Quality & Allergy Report

The good news is our air isn't trying to actively kill your lungs today. The AQI is a respectable 28, which is "Good." So, if you manage to avoid spontaneous combustion from the heat, at least your respiratory system won't be under attack from pollutants.

Pollen is at a moderate level, so if you're one of the many San Diegans who turn into a snot factory this time of year, pop your meds before you head out. It's not a severe hazard, but it's enough to make a trail run less enjoyable if you're sensitive.

### Trail Updates: Mission Trails is Still a Construction Zone

If your usual loop involves Mission Trails Regional Park, remember that the Father Junipero Serra Trail is still undergoing extensive pipeline construction until late 2028. That means vehicle gates are closed 24/7, and while you can usually walk, run, or bike through, expect temporary lane closures and detours. It's not exactly the serene nature experience you're probably looking for, so be prepared for some heavy equipment and potential dust.

### Extreme Heat Warning for Inland/Desert Areas
Today's 91°F in many parts of San Diego County is dangerous. The deserts are under an Extreme Heat Warning with temps hitting well over 100°F. If you're out there between 10 AM and 6 PM, you're asking for trouble. Heat exhaustion and stroke are real, and they come on fast. Stay hydrated, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, don't leave kids or pets in cars.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Elevated Wildfire Risk
The combination of high temperatures and dry brush means the fire danger is real. The National Interagency Fire Center has noted an "above normal significant fire potential" for the Southwest. Be extra cautious with anything that could spark a flame, and if you see smoke, report it immediately.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### E-Bike Safety Reminder
With the recent advisory on e-bike injuries, especially among younger riders, stay aware on shared paths. These things can go faster than you expect, and not everyone on them knows the rules of the road (or trail). Give 'em space.
</AlertBox>
