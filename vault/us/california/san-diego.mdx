## The Rundown: Don't Be a Hero, San Diego

Look, I love hitting the trails as much as the next runner, but today is not the day for heroics. We're staring down the barrel of a serious heatwave, and while 33°C might feel "normal" for some parts of the country, it's hot for us, especially with the humidity and those warm overnight lows.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Advisory: It's Going to Get Worse
This 33°C you're feeling? It's just the appetizer. An "Extreme Heat Watch" is in effect for San Diego County, with inland valleys expected to hit triple digits, potentially up to 42°C (108°F), and even coastal communities will be near 32°C (90°F) for the next several days. This isn't just a warm day; it's a *prolonged* thermal swell that significantly raises the risk of heat-related illnesses. That lack of overnight cooling means your body won't fully recover. Consider moving your workout indoors or rescheduling for much later in the evening, after the sun sets, if you absolutely must. Seriously, hydrate, hydrate, hydrate, and don't assume the marine layer will save you from this.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Coastal Run? Watch for Flooding!
If your usual route takes you along the beach or boardwalk, particularly south-facing spots near Mission Bay, be aware. A coastal flood advisory is active until 5 p.m. today. Elevated surf and high tides from Hurricane Polo could mean unexpected water on pathways and in low-lying areas. Best to stick to higher ground or just avoid it entirely for today.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Moderate AQI & Pollen
An AQI of 67 is "moderate," which sounds okay, but with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, anyone with respiratory issues, or even just sensitive lungs, might feel it. Couple that with moderate pollen, and you've got a recipe for some uncomfortable breathing. If you're running, keep your pace easy and listen to your body. Allergies and irritation can make exertion feel a lot harder than it is.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, folks. I've seen too many people push it in conditions like these and end up worse for wear. There's always tomorrow, or at least a cooler, less watery, less lung-taxing day. Stay safe out there, or better yet, stay *in* today.
