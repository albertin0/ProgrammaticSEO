### Today's Brutal Reality Check

Let's cut to the chase: today is an oven. That 33°C feels even hotter when you're out there pounding pavement or hitting the trails. This isn't just "a bit warm," this is "unseasonably dangerous heat" that's shattering records for March. The National Weather Service has been screaming about heat advisories for pretty much everyone, from the coast to the valleys and deserts.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Extreme Heat Warning: Seriously, Stay Indoors.**
  Hydration is a joke if you're actively trying to dehydrate yourself in this kind of heat. If you absolutely *must* be outside, keep it short, stick to shaded areas, and go at dawn. Any strenuous activity during peak heat hours (which is basically all day today) is a recipe for heatstroke, exhaustion, and a very uncomfortable trip to urgent care. This isn't hero time, it's hydrate-and-chill time.
</AlertBox>

Your lungs also get a double whammy today. The AQI is pushing into moderate territory with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. Mix that with moderate pollen, and anyone with allergies or asthma is going to be wheezing harder than a rusty accordion. If you're planning on any intense cardio, especially in areas like Mission Trails Regional Park where you're exposed to the elements, you're asking for trouble.

### Local Lowdown & Hazards

Beyond the obvious atmospheric assault, there are a few other things to keep in mind if you're still stubbornly set on outdoor activity.

**Trail Talk:** While Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve trails reportedly reopened earlier this month after their utility upgrades, the dry conditions mean any unpaved trails are going to kick up more dust than usual. And remember that elevated fire risk. Don't be the person who accidentally sparks something because you thought a run was more important than not starting a wildfire.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **I-805 Construction May Impact South Bay Runs.**
  If you're usually logging miles near the I-805 in the South Bay, be aware that the massive rehabilitation project is causing weekend closures and ramp shutdowns throughout 2026. This could mean unexpected detours or more traffic on alternate routes, making your run less enjoyable and potentially less safe. Plan your route carefully if you're near this corridor.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  **General Wisdom (that you'll probably ignore, but I have to say it):**
  - Drink way more water than you think you need, and don't wait until you're thirsty.
  - Wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing.
  - Listen to your body. That "push through it" mentality is how I see people in my clinic with heat-related injuries.
  - Consider moving your workout indoors to a gym or even just doing some bodyweight stuff in your air-conditioned living room. Your health (and my patience) will thank you.
</AlertBox>
