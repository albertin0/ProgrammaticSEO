## The Rundown

Alright, San Diego, it's 19.7°C (a breezy 67.5°F) with clear skies, which sounds idyllic, right? Almost too good to be true for an August morning. Well, it is. The temperate weather is a nice break, but the air quality is still hovering in the "moderate" zone at an AQI of 67, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. This means that while it feels good, those microscopic particles are still hanging around, irritating your respiratory system if you're out there really sucking wind. Add a "moderate" pollen count on top of that, and your lungs might be staging a quiet protest even if you can't see the air.

If your plan was to get some serious mileage in on a remote trail, you need to adjust. San Diego County has a standing August closure on several popular, more challenging hiking spots. El Capitan County Preserve, Mt. Gower Preserve, and Hellhole Canyon Preserve are all locked down until September to protect people from the notorious August heat and to allow for essential maintenance. Trust me, as a PT, I've seen too many people underestimate those trails; a closure isn't just bureaucracy, it's a literal lifesaver.

And for those of you who commute or train near the urban core, don't forget the perpetually shifting landscape of construction. The Harbor Drive Trunk Sewer Project is still chugging along downtown, meaning you'll hit single-lane reductions and potentially full southbound lane closures for vehicles and bikes between Park Boulevard and Beardsley Street. If you usually run or bike along there, be prepared for frustration and close calls. Over in University City, the Pure Water Project is doing night work, which includes messing with bike lanes on major roads like Genesee and Nobel. Don't be that person surprised by a sudden detour in the dark.
