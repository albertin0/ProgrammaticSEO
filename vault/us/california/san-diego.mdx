## Current Hazards & Why You Should Probably Stay Indoors

Let's cut right to it: that 33.0°C (91.4°F) temperature you're seeing? That's not "a nice warm day." That's "stay hydrated or end up in urgent care" territory. San Diego might be known for its mild weather, but today is not it. We've seen heat advisories come and go this month, and this is prime conditions for heat exhaustion or worse. Your body isn't going to acclimate fast enough, especially if you're pushing it.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: 33.0°C (91.4°F) is no joke.
Limit outdoor exertion to the absolute bare minimum. If you *must* be out, hydrate relentlessly and listen to your body. Heat stroke is a real, potentially fatal, risk.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. The AQI is sitting at a moderate 62, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. While the Henderson Fire near Camp Pendleton is 100% contained as of yesterday, those fine particulate remnants can still irritate your respiratory system. Combine that with the moderate pollen count, and anyone with allergies or asthma is in for a fun time. You'll be gasping, not just from the heat, but from the air quality.

And just when you think you've dodged the worst, look to the sky later. A strong fall storm system is heading our way, with showers possible by dinnertime and "low-topped thunderstorms" expected late tonight and into early Thursday morning. Gusty winds up to 60 mph are also a possibility. So, that nice clear sky right now? It's a temporary illusion. If you're out running on trails like those around **Mission Trails Regional Park**, expect slick surfaces, reduced visibility, and rapidly changing conditions. Those drainage crossings will turn into ankle-breakers, and nobody wants to be out there when lightning starts cracking.
