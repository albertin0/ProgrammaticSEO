## Today's Local Rundown: San Diego

Look, the weather might be playing nice with this overcast cool front, but don't let it lull you into a false sense of security. We've had some wild swings this month. Just yesterday, the region was experiencing a heat wave that pushed SDG&E to record demand and caused nearly 15,000 power outages across the county. While *today* isn't scorching, remember that we're heading into fall with a "strong" El Niño expected to bring more rain, potential flooding, and mudslides. Prepare your routes, people, because your favorite trails might look like new rivers soon.

That means if you're planning a Sunday long run or hike, today is your *last day* before the closure. ### A Word on AQI and Pollen

With an AQI of 56 and moderate pollen, it's not the cleanest air you'll breathe, but it's far from the worst. The San Diego County Air Pollution Control District actually forecasts "GOOD to MODERATE" air quality for today. If you have asthma or severe allergies, maybe keep your distance from busy roadways or areas with a lot of natural brush that's currently blooming. Otherwise, for most of us, it's just a bit of an irritant, not a showstopper. Hydrate more than usual and maybe save that hill sprint session for a clearer day.

</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">

### El Niño Prep: Think Ahead, San Diego

While today is mellow, the talk of a "strong" El Niño this fall and winter isn't just meteorologist chatter; it means legitimate hazards for our outdoor activities. The city has already started installing sand berms along city beaches and Mission Bay shoreline to mitigate flooding. Heavy rains can quickly overwhelm our stormwater systems, leading to urban flooding, mudslides, and coastal erosion. This isn't just about wet shoes; it's about structural integrity of trails and roads. Keep an eye on weather alerts as the season progresses. If the ground is saturated, those canyon trails you love might become unstable.

</AlertBox>
