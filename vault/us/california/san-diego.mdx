## The Heat is No Joke, San Diego

Look, I love our city's "winter" as much as the next runner who scoffs at actual cold weather, but 33.0°C in March? That's not a balmy spring day; that's a summer heat wave trying to sneak in early and ruin your PR. We've been in this unusual heat streak for a while now, with forecasts consistently showing temperatures 20 to 30 degrees above normal for this time of year. While some of the official "extreme heat warnings" might have technically expired over the weekend, don't let that lull you into a false sense of security. This heat is still dangerous, especially if you're pushing yourself.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Hazard:** With temperatures pushing into the 90s, the risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke is critically high. Hydrate constantly and consider rescheduling your outdoor workout for another day.
</AlertBox>

Even the best trails, like those winding through **Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve**, offer precious little shade. Tackling those climbs in this kind of heat isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely hazardous. Your joints might be screaming from the pavement, but it's your core temperature you need to worry about most right now.

## Breathe Easy? Not So Much.

Adding insult to injury, our air quality isn't exactly sparkling. Today's Air Quality Index is 67, with PM2.5 being the main culprit. That puts us in the "Moderate" category. For those of us who spend a lot of time outside, or if you have any respiratory sensitivities, this means your lungs are working overtime.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Air Quality Alert:** With a Moderate AQI and PM2.5 dominance, sensitive groups (asthmatics, those with heart/lung conditions, older adults, and children) should seriously limit prolonged outdoor exertion. Check for localized updates, especially if you're in an area prone to inversions.
</AlertBox>

While there aren't any massive wildfires actively raging *in* San Diego right now that contribute to this PM2.5, the prolonged dry conditions fueled by this unseasonable heat wave do elevate the general wildfire risk across Southern California. It's a reminder that even when the smoke isn't visible, things are drying out quickly.
