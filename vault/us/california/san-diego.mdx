## Today's Outdoor Grind: Proceed with Extreme Caution

Look, I love a good long run through Balboa Park as much as the next masochist, but today isn't the day to push it. That 33.0°C (91.4°F) air temp, even with a "mainly clear" sky, is brutal. Your body is going to be working overtime just to regulate its temperature, so any strenuous activity is a one-way ticket to bonk city or, worse, heatstroke. The heat advisory from NWS San Diego is no joke, warning of high temperatures 95-100 degrees expected for the valleys and foothills, with "widespread moderate HeatRisk" across the county. This isn't just about feeling sweaty; it's about genuine health risks.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat Risk!
Do NOT ignore the heat advisory. High temperatures combined with elevated low temperatures mean your body won't recover easily. Stick to indoor workouts or wait until the evening, but even then, expect minimal relief. Drink fluids even if you don't feel thirsty, and wear light, loose clothing. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get inside immediately and cool down. Your PR can wait.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the air. AQI at 67 isn't red alert, but with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant, your lungs are still doing extra filtering. While the Thorn Fire is thankfully fully contained, the Border 8 Fire, which just popped up yesterday, could be contributing to that particulate matter. If you're out running on trails, especially those closer to the backcountry, pay attention to any smoky smells or visible haze.

If your idea of a workout is hitting the beach, think again. That high surf advisory means dangerous conditions, particularly at south-facing spots like those along Coronado or Torrey Pines. Those rip currents aren't playing around. Save the ocean dip for another day when you won't get dragged out to sea.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### WARNING: Dangerous Beach Conditions
High surf and powerful rip currents are present along San Diego's coast. Inexperienced swimmers and surfers should stay out of the water. Even experienced individuals need to exercise extreme caution. Don't risk it for a casual swim.
</AlertBox>

And a quick heads-up if you're a bike commuter or sharing paths: UC San Diego just dropped a study showing an "exponential increase" in e-bike injuries, especially for kids under 14, with more severe outcomes than regular bikes. Watch out for each other on shared pathways.

<AlertBox type="info">
### PSA: E-Bike Safety
If you're on a shared path in places like Mission Bay or along the coast, be extra vigilant. E-bike riders, especially younger ones, are experiencing more moderate-to-severe injuries. Keep your head up, be predictable, and share the space.
</AlertBox>
