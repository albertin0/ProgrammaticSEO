## The Heat is No Joke

Let's be blunt: 91.4°F is dangerously hot for any significant outdoor workout. While the *official* extreme heat warning for the deep deserts might have technically expired on Monday, we're still seeing temperatures well above seasonal norms for inland areas like Escondido and Ramona. Even if you're sticking to the coast, that marine layer isn't going to save you entirely from this kind of heat. If you absolutely *must* get outside, hit it before the sun fully commits to its assault – think pre-dawn or deep into the evening. And even then, keep it short, keep it light, and for the love of all that is holy, drink more water than you think you need. Heat exhaustion is sneaky, and heat stroke is a 911 call you don't want to make.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Extreme Heat Risk!** Temperatures at 91.4°F significantly increase the risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Avoid peak sun hours (10 AM - 4 PM) entirely.
</AlertBox>

## Breathe It In (Or Maybe Don't)

Your nose isn't lying; it's moderate pollen season. Pair that with a moderate AQI, and anyone with asthma, allergies, or other respiratory sensitivities is going to feel it. While San Diego isn't directly battling wildfires today, those prescribed burns up in San Luis Obispo County *can* kick up some smoke depending on wind patterns, potentially impacting our air quality, especially inland. If you find yourself struggling to catch your breath, or your throat feels scratchy, don't ignore it. It's not just "toughing it out"—it's putting yourself at risk.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Moderate Air Quality & Pollen!** With a moderate AQI and pollen count, sensitive groups should limit prolonged outdoor exertion. Watch for smoke in the air, especially if you're inland.
</AlertBox>

## Navigating the Urban Maze (and Trails)

Finding a clear path today might be trickier than usual. For instance, if your go-to is the **Sweetwater Bike Park**, tough luck – it's closed for annual maintenance until May 20th. Several trails in Tijuana River Valley Regional Park (Sherwood, Vireo, and Cathedral) are also currently closed for repairs. Beyond specific park closures, there's the perennial joy of San Diego construction. Caltrans is undertaking a massive I-805 overhaul, which means "weekend closures and ramp shutdowns throughout the corridor beginning in 2026". While not necessarily blocking *trails*, it can make getting to your preferred starting point a nightmare, and some areas are getting new bike paths, which always means temporary disruptions. Also, keep an eye out for events; today alone sees the Hanohano Outrigger Canoe Club Crystal Pier Race and the Abrazo 5K/10K. While fun for participants, these can definitely cause congestion or temporary closures on pathways and roads.
