## Today's Rundown: Sweat, Smoke, and Swells

Look, I love hitting the pavement as much as the next masochist, but today isn't exactly prime time for crushing those PRs, especially if you're venturing beyond the immediate coast. We're staring down the barrel of an Extreme Heat Warning that's got the National Weather Service telling everyone from Coachella Valley to the San Diego County Deserts to expect a blistering 112 to 117 degrees. Even our western valleys are looking at mid-90s, with foothills pushing 105. So unless you're a lizard, take it easy out there.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning & Park Closures:** Don't even think about hitting the backcountry trails today. El Capitan Preserve, Hellhole Canyon, and Mt. Gower Preserve are all *closed for the entire month of August* due to dangerous heat. Seriously, leave them alone. CAL FIRE is already busy.
</AlertBox>

On the air front, our AQI is hovering at a "Moderate" 61, with PM2.5 being the main culprit. That's fine for most of us iron lungs, but the forecast explicitly warns of periods dipping into "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups." Why? We've got the Grade Fire burning near Lake Henshaw, kicking smoke into areas like Santa Ysabel and Warner Springs. If you're in those parts, or just generally prone to coughing up a lung after a run, maybe stick to the treadmill.

### Coastal Chaos & Construction Commutes

Thinking of a cool ocean breeze? Think again. A Coastal Flood Advisory and High Surf Advisory are in effect until Saturday evening. We're talking large swells up to nine feet and high tides that are begging to swallow low-lying beach parking lots and boardwalks. Rip currents are also a real threat. If you're heading to Pacific Beach or Ocean Beach for a run, keep an eye on the water, but maybe consider a different route altogether.

And for those of you who navigate the concrete jungle, watch out for fresh disruptions. Starting tomorrow night (August 2), the Pure Water Construction project is kicking off night work on Genesee Avenue, affecting bike lanes and street parking from Clairemont up through University City. That means more dodging cones and less smooth sailing on your commutes.

As for my fellow trail runners, **Mission Trails Regional Park** continues its saga of pipeline construction. The Father Junipero Serra Trail is closed to vehicles 24/7 through late 2028, and weekday access (7 AM - 4 PM) for us pedestrians and cyclists is limited between Gate 1 (Visitor Center) and Gate 10 (Old Mission Dam) until Spring 2027. Don't be that person ignoring the signs and holding up crews. Plan your route, or better yet, run elsewhere.
