### Today's Take: Don't Be a Hero, Hydrate!

Look, I love hitting the trails as much as the next runner, but 33&deg;C is not "ideal" running weather, no matter what your Strava feed says. We're on the cusp of a serious heatwave that's already making its presence felt. The National Weather Service is even putting out "Extreme Heat Warnings" for our desert neighbors and "Moderate Heat Risk" advisories for inland valleys. Even if you're sticking to the coast, that marine layer isn't going to save you from this kind of temperature.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Heat is Your Biggest Enemy Today.** With temperatures already high and climbing, heat-related illnesses increase significantly. Seriously, don't mess around. If you're out there, go early, go short, and drink *more* water than you think you need. Or better yet, save the long runs for when the sun goes down.
</AlertBox>

While our air quality is currently sitting at a "Good" AQI of 48 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, don't get complacent. The "elevated fire risk" isn't just a boilerplate warning. Small brush fires can kick up fast in these conditions. Keep an eye on local news if you're planning any long-distance, remote trail excursions, especially in areas like Cleveland National Forest, where trail conditions already report downed trees and overgrown spots.

If your usual route takes you anywhere near the **Tijuana River Valley Regional Park**, be aware that several trails—Sherwood, Vireo (west of Hollister St.), and Cathedral—are currently closed. And frankly, until they get that whole cross-border sewage situation under control, you might want to consider alternative routes anyway. That airborne hydrogen sulfide isn't exactly a performance enhancer.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Trail Closures in Tijuana River Valley Regional Park!** Specifically, Sherwood Trail, Vireo Trail (west of Hollister St.), and Cathedral Trail are closed. Find an alternate route to avoid any unpleasant surprises (or smells).

Pollen levels are moderate, so if you're one of the sniffly ones, take your meds before you head out. But honestly, the heat and potential fire risk are the bigger fish to fry today.

Stay safe out there, San Diego. Don't be a statistic.
