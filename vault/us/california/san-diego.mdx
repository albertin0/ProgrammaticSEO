Today? Unless your definition of "working out" involves sitting in an air-conditioned room, you might want to rethink that long run.

<LungsJointsScore score={4} />

<BulletList>
*   **You're baking, not burning calories.** That 33°C isn't just warm, it's actively trying to turn your internal organs into slow-roasted BBQ. High temps mean your body works overtime to cool itself, which saps energy and makes even easy efforts feel like climbing Cowles Mountain with a weighted vest.
*   **Air's just okay, at best.** With an AQI of 67 and PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant, your lungs are getting a moderate dose of stuff they don't want, on top of trying to manage the heat. Don't add more stress than necessary.
*   **Pollen's still out to get you.** "Moderate" pollen might sound benign, but combine it with the heat and mediocre air quality, and suddenly your sinuses decide they're hosting a snot party. Not ideal for sustained breathing.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We live in San Diego. We *expect* to run outside year-round. But today's a prime example of why you sometimes just need to put your ego aside and listen to your body – and the actual environmental conditions. That 33°C (that's about 91°F for those still in denial) is no joke. The City of San Diego has been practically begging people to avoid Open Space trails during extreme heat, and that advice isn't just for tourists who think flip-flops are appropriate hiking footwear.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning (Still Relevant, Folks!)
While some of the official "Extreme Heat Warnings" from earlier in the week might have technically expired for coastal areas, the current 33°C temperature makes the risk of heat-related illness *very real* for intense exercise. Your body is going to struggle to thermoregulate, leading to potential dehydration, heat exhaustion, or even heat stroke. This is especially true if you're hitting those inland trails where it always feels ten degrees hotter anyway.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, if you *must* be outside, keep it short, keep it light, and stick to the early morning hours, which, let's be honest, have already passed. Or find some shade – good luck with that. And don't even think about pushing the pace. This isn't the day to PR your usual loop around **Los Peñasquitos Canyon**. Speaking of which, remember that the Rancho Peñasquitos Adobe Loop has been closed since June due to fire risk and remains so. Don't be that person who ignores the signs and risks needing a rescue because you thought you were invincible. Heat-related rescues happen, and they tie up resources.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate Air Quality + Pollen = Double Whammy for Lungs
An AQI of 67, with PM2.5 as the main culprit, is not going to make your respiratory system happy, especially when you're sucking down air at a high rate. Add in moderate pollen, and if you have any seasonal allergies, you're setting yourself up for a miserable post-workout cough and sniffles. Keep your inhaler handy if you need it.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration isn't a suggestion, it's a command.
If you're out there, you better be pounding water like it's your job. And I mean *before*, *during*, and *after*. Water fountains are great, but bring your own bottle and a backup. Better yet, stay inside and rehydrate with some electrolytes from your fridge. Your joints will thank you for not turning them into creaky sandpaper.
</AlertBox>
