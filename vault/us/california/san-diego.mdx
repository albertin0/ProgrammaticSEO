## Today's Workout Outlook: Don't Be a Heat Casualty Wannabe

Alright, San Diegans, it's August 15th, and if you're looking at the clear skies and that comfortable 19.7°C, you're probably itching to hit a trail. And for the most part, you'd be right to. The Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at 36, which is "Good," meaning your lungs won't be doing any extra work filtering junk today. That's a huge win compared to what other parts of the state deal with.

But here's the deal, fresh off the presses: while *today* might not be scorching, the lingering effects of our summer heatwaves are still impacting access to some of our most challenging and, frankly, most dangerous trails. The San Diego River Gorge and Three Sisters trails, including the infamous Cedar Creek Falls, are *closed for the entire month of August*. This isn't some arbitrary decision; it's because forest officials have been pulling people off those trails left and right with heat-related illnesses – we're talking 11 medical rescues and 18 assists just since early July. These "reverse-grade" trails mean the toughest part of the hike, the uphill climb, comes when you're already exhausted and sun-baked, and people keep running out of water. Don't be that person. Seriously.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Immediate Hazard: Trail Closures!** The San Diego River Gorge and Three Sisters trails (including Cedar Creek Falls) are CLOSED through August due to extreme heat and numerous heat-related rescues. Do NOT attempt to hike these trails. Other county parks like El Capitan Preserve, Hellhole Canyon Preserve, and Mount Gower Preserve are also closed for the month.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, we're not dealing with any local wildfire smoke today, which is a relief. And while there were severe thunderstorm warnings and coastal flood advisories earlier in the week for other parts of Southern California, San Diego proper seems to be in the clear for any immediate high winds or flooding impacting your run.

Just because the big stuff isn't happening *right now* doesn't mean you can ignore the basics. Moderate pollen is still in the air, so if you're an allergy sufferer, pop that antihistamine before you head out.
