Look, I get it. We live in San Diego, we *want* to be outside. But unless you're a lizard, 33°C is asking for trouble. We've been under heat advisories for a while now, with temperatures soaring 20-30 degrees above normal for this time of year, hitting the 90s in coastal areas and even triple digits inland. Running your usual loops around Mission Trails Regional Park, or even a casual stroll down by the Embarcadero, could easily land you with heat exhaustion or worse. Health officials are already begging people to take it easy. This isn't just about feeling sweaty; it's about legitimate health risks when your body can't regulate its temperature.

On top of that, you're breathing air that's got enough particulate matter to be noticeable, and the moderate pollen count means your allergies might kick into overdrive. It's a triple threat to your respiratory system and overall comfort.

**Extreme Heat Warning!** 33°C (91.4°F) is dangerous for outdoor activity. Hydration is critical, but honestly, it's better to move your workout indoors or postpone it. Heat-related illnesses are no joke.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Moderate Air Quality & Pollen:** While not critical on their own, combined with the extreme heat, these can exacerbate respiratory issues and allergies. If you *must* be outside, monitor your symptoms closely.

You might also hit some detours if you're out and about. There's plenty of infrastructure work going on across the city, from the San Diego International Airport expansion to projects like the Riverwalk San Diego in Mission Valley. And if you're anywhere near the University of San Diego, watch out for road work on Camino San Diego Road starting April 1st, which means traffic pattern changes and limited parking. These aren't necessarily workout stoppers, but they add to the general urban obstacle course. Torrey Pines trails, which were closed for improvements, are theoretically open now, but still, consider that intense sun exposure.

So, what's a runner to do? Get on that treadmill, hit a spin class, or find a nice, air-conditioned gym. If you absolutely *have* to get outside, make it an early morning walk or a late evening cool-down, but even then, be smart. Drink more water than you think you need, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, listen to your body. It's not worth passing out on a trail just to hit your daily steps.
