## Today's Vitals: Not Exactly Prime Running Weather

Look, I love our city's trails as much as the next masochist, but today isn't a banner day for pushing limits. We've got 21.6°C (about 71°F) and partly cloudy skies, which sounds delightful until you remember the air itself isn't exactly pristine. The Air Quality Index is hovering at a "Moderate" 64, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. For sensitive groups, AccuWeather is even calling it "Poor," so if you've got asthma or dodgy lungs, maybe stick to an indoor session today. You don't want to be huffing and puffing wildfire remnants or whatever else is floating around.

#### Trail Closures Due to Fire and Heat!
Seriously, don't even think about it. Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve is still under post-fire closures. If you're looking for that waterfall selfie, tough luck. Even worse, popular spots like Three Sisters Falls and the San Diego River Gorge are *closed for the entire month of August* because people keep getting rescued from heat exhaustion. Don't ignore these warnings. They're there because people actually get into trouble.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Inland Heat Advisory
While coastal temperatures are mild, our inland valleys are under a Heat Advisory today through Monday. If you live or plan to run anywhere east of the 15, prepare for significantly hotter conditions than what you see by the beach. Hydration is key, and seriously, consider adjusting your plans to early morning or late evening.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Gyms Are Still Open, Folks
If the air quality, heat, or closed trails are cramping your style, remember the local gyms are always an option. I know, "the dreadmill," but at least it won't land you a ticket or a helicopter ride. There was even EMS activity at the 24 Hour Fitness on Balboa Ave earlier this week, so if you *do* push it too hard indoors, at least you're probably closer to help.
</AlertBox>

In short, it's one of those San Diego days where a casual stroll is fine, but anything approaching "workout" territory requires some serious risk assessment. Don't be a hero. Your body will thank you.
