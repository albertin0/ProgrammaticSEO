## Heat, Floods, and Questionable Air: What You Need to Know

Today is a triple threat of bad ideas for outdoor exercise. The heat alone is enough to send you indoors. San Diego officially considers extreme heat days when temperatures exceed 93.1 degrees, and we're looking at that and then some. Staying hydrated is non-negotiable, but better yet, stay in an air-conditioned space. Remember that just a couple of months ago, popular spots like the San Diego River Gorge and Three Sisters trails were fully closed due to excessive heat because emergency services were pulling people out left and right.

Then, there's Tropical Storm Rachel. This isn't just a little drizzle. We're talking flash flood warnings and significant rainfall expected right over San Diego County. This means rapid runoff, slick surfaces, and dangerous conditions on trails and roads. Don't be the person who needs rescuing because you thought you could "power through" a flooded trail. River crossings, like those around the San Diego River between Avenida de Rio and Hazard Center, are already closed due to flooding.

### Mission Trails and Other Local Impact

Even our usually reliable spots aren't immune. In **Mission Trails Regional Park**, the Father Junipero Serra Trail has ongoing pipeline construction. While usually open for pedestrians and cyclists, given the current tropical storm conditions, you can bet parts of it will be a messy, potentially flooded, and hazardous proposition. Vehicle gates are already closed there 24/7 through late 2028 anyway, so don't plan on driving in. County parks frequently close trails during and after storms for public safety due to flood and mudslide risks. Expect similar situations across the board.
