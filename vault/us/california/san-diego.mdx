<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning (Basically)
While the official "extreme heat warning" for San Diego County deserts, mountains, and inland valleys expired recently on August 29th, don't let that lull you into a false sense of security. Temperatures are still brutally high, sitting at a scorching 33.0°C (91.4°F) today. Even coastal areas, which often catch a break, are feeling the burn. Stay out of the sun during peak hours, and if you must be out, think short, slow, and *lots* of water. Seriously, I'm a PT, and I see too many of you pushing it in this stuff. Don't be that person ending up in the ER.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Choke on the Air or the Allergens
With a moderate AQI of 67 and PM2.5 leading the charge, your lungs are already working overtime to filter out crud. Combine that with moderate pollen, and you've got a recipe for an uncomfortable, potentially unhealthy, workout. If you've got asthma or any respiratory issues, consider moving your sweat session indoors. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk

The good news is we aren't hearing about widespread, new wildfire smoke choking the county *today*. The "Sorrento Fire" near San Diego, for example, was mostly contained back in mid-August. However, the dry conditions that fueled those fires haven't magically disappeared.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Watch Your Step (and Your Map)
While no major, new trail closures popped up overnight, some of our favorite spots always need a check. The City of San Diego advises calling the Park Ranger Office for up-to-date status on trails that might be closed due to damage or flooding at places like **Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve**. Flooding isn't today's issue, but damage from past events or ongoing maintenance is always a possibility. Lakeside River Trail also has ongoing improvements that might limit pedestrian access. A quick call can save you a wasted trip. Oh, and if you're near the Tijuana River Valley, remember that high temps can sometimes kick up those lovely hydrogen sulfide emissions. Probably best to avoid heavy breathing down there anyway.
</AlertBox>

Honestly, today is a day for an air-conditioned gym, a swim, or some low-key stretching at home. Your body is already under stress just existing in this heat. Pushing a hard workout on top of it is just asking for trouble.
