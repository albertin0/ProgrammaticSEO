If you're thinking about a long run or a strenuous hike today, August 31st, just don't. Seriously, stay inside.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
  - **Relentless Heat:** That 33.0°C (81.4°F) on the coast is a *lie* for anyone venturing inland. We've been under extreme heat warnings all week, with inland valleys hitting triple digits and mountains not far behind. Don't mess with that.
  - **Closed Trails (for good reason):** Many popular hiking spots, including the notoriously challenging Cedar Creek Falls and Three Sisters Falls trails, were shut down for the entire month of August due to a ridiculous number of heat-related rescues. Today's the last day of August, and those conditions haven't magically vanished overnight. Same goes for El Capitan, Hellhole Canyon, and Mt. Gower Preserves.
  - **Moderate AQI (and potentially smoke):** While not terrible, an AQI of 67 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant isn't doing your lungs any favors, especially when you're trying to push yourself in this heat. There was a 95% contained Sorrento Fire earlier in the month, so while localized smoke is probably gone, it's a reminder of how quickly air quality can change.
</BulletList>

### Today's Rundown

That "Mainly Clear" sky isn't the innocent blue you're hoping for; it just means there's nothing shielding you from the sun's oppressive gaze. Your 33.0°C (81.4°F) is coastal cool. Drive ten miles east, and you're sweating through 90s and potentially pushing towards 100°F (37.8°C). Don't be that person we see airlifted off Cowles Mountain in Mission Trails because you thought "it's just a little warm." It's not. It's dangerously hot, and the ground is still radiating heat from days of baking.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Extreme Heat Hazard:** We're still dealing with the tail end of an intense heatwave. Inland areas are facing dangerously hot conditions. Heat-related illnesses are no joke and rescues have been frequent. Stick to indoor workouts or wait until the sun is completely down and temperatures have actually dropped.
</AlertBox>

Even if your go-to trails aren't explicitly closed, the reason others *are* closed should be enough to make you rethink your plans. Those steep climbs on the San Diego River Gorge trails? They become death traps when your body is struggling to cool itself.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Trail Work at Torrey Pines:** If you're heading north, remember that Phase 2 of construction at Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve is slated to begin this month or next. You might hit unexpected detours or closed sections. Always check ahead before you go, or better yet, just admire the bluffs from your air-conditioned car today.
</AlertBox>
