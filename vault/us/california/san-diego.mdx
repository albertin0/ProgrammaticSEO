### Today's Rundown

Alright, San Diego, if you're planning on getting those steps in today, you need to be smart about it. The good news is the general Air Quality Index (AQI) is holding at 67, which is "Moderate," with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. The San Diego County Air Pollution Control District agrees, forecasting "GOOD to MODERATE" air quality today. However, that’s a broad stroke, and reality is a bit more nuanced.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Inland Heat Dome
If your usual route takes you anywhere near the San Diego County Mountains or further east into the deserts, turn around. There's an Extreme Heat Warning for our desert areas and a Heat Advisory for the mountains, with forecasted highs well into the 90s and even hitting 110 degrees in some spots through Friday. Overnight lows aren't offering much relief either, staying in the 80s and 90s. This isn't just "a bit warm" – this is "heatstroke waiting to happen." Stick to coastal areas or hit the gym if you value your organs. "Moderate to Major HeatRisk" is expected.
</AlertBox>

Speaking of coastal, if you're thinking of a beach run or swim, be aware. While the city itself is clear and a pleasant 24°C (76°F), we're looking at significant surf. The National Weather Service has issued warnings for high surf and strong rip currents, especially along south-facing beaches, which will continue through today. Those waves combined with astronomical high tides could even bring minor coastal flooding in low-lying areas. So, keep an eye on the tide charts and don't get swept away trying to impress anyone.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Watch Your Breath
A wildfire near Brengle Terrace Park in Vista yesterday forced evacuations, and locals reported "a huge cloud of smoke." While the fire might be under control, residual smoke can linger and impact air quality, especially in the North County inland areas. Additionally, CAL FIRE is starting up their "Gore Ranch Vegetation Project" today and tomorrow, conducting prescribed burns to reduce brush. Expect to see and smell smoke in those areas, potentially affecting your morning run around places like Black Mountain Open Space Park or even extending closer to the coast depending on wind shifts. If you're sensitive, or if you see visible smoke, maybe opt for an indoor workout.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Conditions & Construction

For those of you who hit the trails, know that the popular San Diego River Gorge and Three Sisters hiking areas (including Cedar Creek Falls) in the Cleveland National Forest were closed for much of August due to extreme heat and numerous heat-related rescues. While the immediate closure period might be over, the ongoing heat warnings for our backcountry mean these trails remain incredibly risky. Don't push it.

Good news for I-5 commuters: the full southbound I-5 closure from SR-52 to Mission Bay Drive, which was in effect for roadwork, officially wrapped up at 5 AM this morning. So, your commute *should* be smoother, but always check traffic. However, other various park construction projects are ongoing across the county, like playground upgrades at Dos Picos County Park or William Heise County Park, which might mean localized noise or temporary access changes. Just be aware if you're headed to any county park.

#### Pro-Tip: Hydrate Smarter, Not Harder
Seriously, folks. With these temperatures, especially if you're even *considering* being active outdoors, you need more than just water. Electrolytes are your friend. Grab a Nuun tab, some liquid IV, or even just a pinch of salt in your water bottle. Your muscles (and your brain) will thank you.
</AlertBox>
