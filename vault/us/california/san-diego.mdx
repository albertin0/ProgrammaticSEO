Forget your PRs today, San Diego. It's a furnace out there, and your lungs (and brain) are going to hate you for pushing it.

<BulletList>
*   **Extreme Heat Warning:** We're talking 33°C (that's 91°F for the Fahrenheit crowd) and forecasts pushing into the 90s for coastal areas, even triple digits inland. This isn't just "warm," it's "National Weather Service issued an extreme heat warning" hot. Record-breaking for March, apparently.
*   **Moderate Air Quality (PM2.5) & Gusty Winds:** The AQI is sitting at 67 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, which isn't terrible on its own, but combine it with a weak offshore flow bringing 25-40 mph wind gusts through the coastal areas today, and you've got a recipe for dehydration and increased heat stress. The wind will just dry you out faster.
*   **Moderate Pollen Count:** Another layer of "meh" for your respiratory system. If you're sensitive, you'll feel it on top of everything else.

### Don't Be a Hero, Seriously.

Look, I love pounding the pavement around Mission Bay as much as the next runner, but today is not the day for that 10-miler or pushing any serious speed work. This heat is no joke, and we're only in March. Forecasters are telling everyone to limit strenuous activities to early morning or evening. Guess what, folks? It's morning, and it's already roasting. It's going to get worse.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Hazard:** Temperatures are 20-30 degrees above normal. Heat-related illnesses are a high risk. Stay hydrated and avoid peak sun hours. Your body will thank you, your ego will recover.
</AlertBox>

If you *must* get out, consider it a forced recovery day. Keep it short, keep it slow, and stick to the shadiest routes you can find – maybe a quick loop through Balboa Park before the sun truly beats down. Even then, know your limits. That "moderate" AQI combined with the heat and wind means your lungs are working overtime.

We've got ongoing construction in the South Bay along I-805, with weekend closures expected this year for road rehabilitation. While not a direct trail block, it's a reminder that our infrastructure is always under construction, and your routes might change. Keep an eye on local alerts if you're venturing further south.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is Key (and I mean *more* than usual):** You’re going to sweat a bucket in this heat, especially with the wind. Drink plenty of fluids *before*, *during*, and *after* any outdoor activity. And no, that post-run beer doesn't count as hydration.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Wind Gusts:** The 25-40 mph wind gusts, while sometimes offering a momentary "cooling" sensation, actually accelerate dehydration in high temperatures. Don't let the breeze fool you into thinking it's cooler than it is.
</AlertBox>

The good news? Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve, which was partially closed until February 2026 for upgrades, should be back to full access now. But even there, those exposed trails will feel like an oven today. Maybe plan that scenic cliffside run for a day when it's not trying to set a March heat record.

Stay safe out there. Or better yet, stay *in* and hit the gym.
