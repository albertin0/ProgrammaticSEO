## The "Run" Down

Alright, let's talk real. That 91-degree heat isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous. I don't care how many miles you've logged on the Bayshore Bikeway, pushing a long run in this kind of heat is asking for trouble. Heat stroke is real, and it sneaks up on you faster than you think. Your body struggles to cool itself when it's this hot, especially without much of a breeze. Even if you're "used to it," your performance will tank, and your recovery will suffer. You're not a hero; you're just risking a trip to urgent care for dehydration.

### Air Quality & Pollen Punch

The "Moderate" AQI of 67, with fine particulate matter (PM2.5) being the main issue, combined with moderate pollen levels, means your respiratory system is already working harder than usual. Those tiny PM2.5 particles can get deep into your lungs, and if you're out there huffing and puffing, you're just sucking in more of them. For those with asthma or seasonal allergies, this combo is going to make you feel like you're breathing through a wet sock. Don't be surprised if you're wheezing your way up the climbs in Mission Trails Regional Park today. Save the epic trail adventures for a day when the air isn't trying to actively inflame your airways.

### Hydration isn't a Suggestion

If you absolutely, positively *must* be outside, like maybe you're being chased by a particularly grumpy sea lion, you need to be smart. Hydrate relentlessly, and I mean *before*, *during*, and *after*. Water isn't enough in this heat; you need electrolytes. Those fancy sports drinks aren't just for show. Stick to shaded routes if you can find them – good luck with that on most of our popular running paths – and consider breaking up your workout. A couple of shorter bursts are safer than one long slog.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning!
Heatstroke and heat exhaustion are serious risks today. Symptoms include dizziness, nausea, confusion, rapid pulse, and hot, dry skin. If you feel any of these, STOP immediately, get to shade, cool down, and hydrate. Don't mess around with this.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Early Bird (or Late Night Owl) Gets the Worm
Seriously, if you're going to run, do it at dawn or dusk. Even then, the pavement will be radiating heat from the day. It's still going to be hot, but you might avoid the worst of the sun's direct assault.
</AlertBox>
