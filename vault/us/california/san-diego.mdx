Today is *not* the day to push yourself outside, folks. Unless your idea of a good time involves inhaling smoke and risking heatstroke, maybe hit the treadmill.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
  <li>The Thorn Fire is actively burning in southeast San Diego County, uncontained at over 1,200 acres, with gusty winds challenging firefighters. This isn't just a concern for those near Buckthorn Trail; smoke spreads, making that "Mainly Clear" sky a liar.</li>
  <li>An Extreme Heat Warning is in effect until 8 PM PDT tonight for most of our inland valleys and mountains, where temperatures could hit the low 90s to 100 degrees. If you're not within spitting distance of the ocean, it's dangerously hot.</li>
  <li>Our AQI is sitting at 67 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. That's "Moderate," sure, but "Moderate" with active wildfires means your lungs are working harder than they should be, especially if you're sensitive.</li>
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We live in San Diego; the urge to be outside is practically in our DNA. But sometimes, Mother Nature throws a curveball, or in this case, a raging wildfire and a heat dome. As of this morning, the Thorn Fire, among others like the Independence Fire and Haverford 2 Fire, is tearing through parts of San Diego County. If you were planning a long run or hike anywhere near Buckthorn Trail or out in the eastern county, think again. Those areas are actively threatened, and even if you're not *in* the fire zone, the air quality is going to be suspect. CAL FIRE explicitly mentions "gusty winds" making containment tough, which means that smoke isn't staying put.

Pair that with an Extreme Heat Warning that covers San Diego County Valleys, Mountains, and points east, and you've got a recipe for disaster. While it might feel decent at 19.5°C (67°F) along the immediate coast, step a few miles inland and you're looking at temperatures in the low 90s, potentially topping 100. Your body simply can't cool itself effectively in those conditions, and heat-related illness is no joke. Don't be that person paramedics have to scrape off the trail at Mission Trails Regional Park because you thought you were invincible.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Extreme Heat & Wildfire Smoke are NOT a drill.** Do not underestimate the combined effects of these two hazards. Heat exhaustion and heatstroke are serious. Smoke inhalation exacerbates respiratory issues.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Check local park and trail status BEFORE you leave.** While Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve trails are currently open after recent construction, other areas like Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve might have unannounced closures or existing damage. Call ahead!
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Early mornings are your best bet, but not a pass.** If you absolutely *must* get out, stick to coastal areas *very* early, stay hydrated, and keep an eye on how the air feels. If it smells like a campfire, turn around.
</AlertBox>
