## What's Happening Out There

Look, I've run enough miles in this city to tell you when it's just not worth it, and today is one of those days where you need to be smart, not stubborn. That 91°F might feel like a "typical" San Diego summer day to some, but don't let the marine layer mirage fool you. We've been under a serious heat advisory for San Diego County Mountains and extreme heat warnings for the deserts, with some spots hitting nearly 117°F in recent days. While you might not be running in Borrego Springs, that kind of heat lingers and impacts the entire region. The NWS explicitly warns about significant increases in heat-related illnesses during these periods.

Trying to conquer Cowles Mountain in Mission Trails Regional Park today is a recipe for disaster. The exposed trails offer no reprieve, and pushing yourself when your body is already struggling to regulate its temperature is just asking for trouble. We're also seeing moderate PM2.5 levels, which means there's fine particulate matter floating around. Combine that with the heat, and your lungs are working overtime. For anyone with respiratory issues or just, you know, a desire to breathe easily, this isn't ideal.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat is No Joke.** The current temperatures, especially further inland, are dangerously high. Reschedule strenuous outdoor activities for much cooler times (early morning, late evening) or move indoors. Know the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke, and don't hesitate to call 911 if you or someone else is in distress. Stay hydrated, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that is holy, don't leave kids or pets in cars.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch Your Lungs.** With an AQI of 67 and PM2.5 as the main culprit, those with sensitive respiratory systems might experience irritation. This is compounded by the heat, making breathing heavier. Consider shortening your workout or choosing a low-impact indoor activity.
