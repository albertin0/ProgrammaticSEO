This isn't the day to push it, San Diego; the heat is trying to kill you.

<LungsJointsScore score={4} />

<BulletList>
*   **Extreme Heat Warning is ON:** Forget the pleasant-sounding 27.9°C you might see reported; the National Weather Service slapped an *Extreme Heat Warning* on the coasts and valleys through tonight, with temperatures easily soaring into the 90s and triple digits inland. We're talking 100+ degrees in some areas, compounded by nasty humidity from Tropical Storm Marie, making it feel even worse. Overnight lows won't offer much relief, so don't even *think* about that late-night long run.
*   **Air Quality is a Mixed Bag:** While the AQI is technically a "Moderate" 60, some reports are flagging today as "Poor" for sensitive groups due to elevated PM2.5 and nitrogen dioxide. Pair that with moderate pollen, and your lungs will be working overtime.
*   **Coastal Concerns & Nighttime Trail Closures:** High surf and coastal flooding were concerns just yesterday, especially around Mission Beach. Even if the immediate coastal flooding risk is easing, exercise caution near the water. Also, if you’re an early bird or night owl on two wheels, know that the **Rose Canyon Bike Path has nighttime closures (9 p.m. to 5 a.m., Sunday through Thursday) for the next couple of weeks for landscaping work.**
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We live in San Diego; we expect perfect weather year-round. But today is a hard pass for peak outdoor performance, especially if you're planning anything strenuous or high-mileage around **Mission Trails Regional Park** or similar inland spots. Seriously, that heat warning isn't just for show. Your body is going to struggle to regulate its temperature, and that puts undue stress on everything, including your heart and kidneys. You're increasing your risk of heat exhaustion or worse, and then I get to see you in my clinic, which, while great for my business, sucks for your training cycle.

If you *must* get out there, keep it short, keep it slow, and stick to the absolute coolest, shadiest parts of your neighborhood. Consider taking it indoors for a change. There's always tomorrow, assuming the weather decides to be less aggressively Californian.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat is No Joke:** Temperatures are expected to be dangerously high, potentially 100+ degrees in many areas, with high humidity. Hydrate excessively *before* you go out, wear light, loose clothing, and listen to your body. Any signs of dizziness, nausea, or excessive sweating mean you need to stop *immediately* and find shade/water. This isn't about being tough; it's about not ending up in the ER.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Air Quality & Pollen Combo:** For those with asthma or other respiratory issues, the "Moderate" AQI with some "Poor" pollutant readings and moderate pollen could trigger symptoms. Be extra vigilant with your inhalers or allergy meds.
</AlertBox>
