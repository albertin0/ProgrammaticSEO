## Today's Vitals: Cooked, Not Clear

Let's cut right to it: The mercury's pushing a frankly absurd 33.0°C (that's 91.4°F for those of you clinging to Fahrenheit) and it's mainly clear out there. You don't need a medical degree to know that's not ideal for pounding the pavement or hitting the trails. This kind of heat is how you earn yourself a one-way ticket to heat exhaustion, or worse, heat stroke. And yes, "outdoor workers and athletes" are specifically called out as at-risk groups for heat-related illness.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: You're Not a Lizard!
Seriously, 91 degrees in November is dangerous. Your body struggles to cool itself, and dehydration sets in faster than you can say "where's the water fountain?" Avoid peak sun hours (which is pretty much all day when it's this hot), drink copious amounts of water, and watch for signs of heat illness like dizziness, nausea, or confusion. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; it's about staying safe.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Obstacles & Open Paths

Good news, there aren't any massive wildfires actively choking our skies right now. The Henderson Fire from mid-October near Camp Pendleton, which briefly shut down I-5, is long contained, and the county even lifted burn permit suspensions yesterday. So, at least you won't be running through smoke, which is... something.

However, if you're a regular on the Tecolote Canyon trails, you're still out of luck. Sections of those hiking trails are closed 24/7 until summer 2027 due to ongoing trunk sewer improvement and golf course water connection projects. This means heavy construction vehicle traffic, deep excavations, and around-the-clock work. Don't be "that guy" who ignores the closure signs; it's dangerous and you're just making more work for the crews.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Tecolote Canyon Trails: Closed for the Long Haul
Heads up, the hiking trails from Mount Acadia Boulevard to Balboa Avenue in Tecolote Canyon are closed 24/7 until summer of 2027 due to sewer construction. All trails within the golf course area are also out. Find an alternative route!
</AlertBox>

Other spots like Mission Trails Regional Park seem mostly clear, with the Father Junipero Serra Trail open, though the Kumeyaay Campground and Visitor Center are still closed. And no, despite some recent Halloween storms, we're not dealing with active flooding or major trail erosion today, though city and county crews are preparing for potential El Niño impacts in low-lying areas like Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve. Beach hazards like rip currents that were in effect through Monday have now expired.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Smart Choices for a Scorcher
If you absolutely must get your workout in outdoors, do it at the crack of dawn or well after sunset. Seriously. Consider indoor alternatives like a gym with A/C. Hydrate aggressively, wear light, loose clothing, and don't push yourself if you start feeling off. This isn't the day to try for a PR.
</AlertBox>
