Today is a day to seriously question your life choices if you're planning any strenuous outdoor activity after 9 AM. This isn't your typical San Diego March.

<LungsJointsScore score={4} />

<BulletList>

*   **Scorching, Unseasonal Heat:** We're talking mid-90s Fahrenheit in many coastal and inland valley areas, pushing triple digits further east. This is a full-on summer heatwave hitting us in March. Your 33.0°C (91.4°F) data for the city is no joke.
*   **Moderate Air Quality:** With an AQI of 86 and PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, your lungs are already working harder. Add that to the heat, and you're just asking for trouble.
*   **Moderate Pollen Count:** Another layer of fun for your respiratory system, especially if you're prone to seasonal allergies. San Diego's even been flagged as a top "Allergy Capital" for 2026.

</BulletList>

Look, I love pushing limits as much as the next runner, but today is not the day to PR your half-marathon split on the **Mission Trails Regional Park** paths. The National Weather Service has issued a Heat Advisory for our mountains until Friday evening, and an Extreme Heat Warning for the deserts through Saturday. While the immediate coast *might* catch a slight breeze, the rest of San Diego County is just baking. Expect temperatures 20 to 30 degrees above average.

<AlertBox type="danger">

**Extreme Heat Risk:** With temperatures hitting the 90s (and higher inland), heat-related illnesses are a serious threat. Dehydration, heat exhaustion, and heat stroke aren't just for summer, folks. Limit strenuous activities to the very early morning or late evening, if at all.

</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">

**Air Quality & Allergies:** The moderate AQI and pollen are a double-whammy for your lungs. If you feel any irritation, tightness, or excessive coughing, cut your workout short.

</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">

**Hydration is Non-Negotiable:** Carry more water than you think you need. Seriously. And don't wait until you're thirsty.

</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">

**Trail Check for Construction:** While Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve was expected to resume full access by March 1st after major renovations, always double-check their official site before heading out. There's also ongoing Pure Water construction in University City around Genesee and Nobel that could impact local routes with dust or detours.

</AlertBox>
