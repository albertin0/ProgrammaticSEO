### The Heat is On

Let's be clear: 33 degrees Celsius is not ideal running weather. We've seen heat advisories plastered across the county this week, and today is no different. Even if you're used to San Diego's sunny disposition, this kind of heat can sneak up on you faster than a runner with negative splits. If you absolutely *must* run outside, get it done before 8 AM or after 7 PM. And I'm not kidding about staying hydrated; chugging water like it's your job is the bare minimum. Forget PRs today; survival is the goal.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning!** With temperatures soaring to 33.0°C (91.4°F), heat exhaustion and heatstroke are serious risks. Limit outdoor activity, especially during peak sun hours (10 AM - 6 PM). Drink copious amounts of water, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, and listen to your body.
</AlertBox>

### What's in the Air?

Our Air Quality Index is currently sitting at a "Moderate" 67, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. "Moderate" sounds nice, but it still means there are fine particles floating around that can irritate your lungs, especially if you're pushing hard. Add to that the fact that we've got the Warnock Fire and Main Fire burning in San Diego County as recently as yesterday. While some reports suggest smoke might be pushed inland, it's a fickle beast. The Mateo Fire to our north also prompted a smoke advisory recently, affecting air quality in "some areas" of San Diego County. This means localized hot spots of nasty air are entirely possible. If you start coughing or your chest feels tight, turn around. Your training run isn't worth an ER visit.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Wildfire Smoke & Moderate AQI:** Active fires in San Diego County mean smoke can drift, impacting air quality locally. An AQI of 67 (PM2.5) is moderate, but if you're sensitive or pushing intense workouts, consider alternatives to outdoor exercise.
</AlertBox>

### Mind the Construction, Especially on Mission Trails

If your favorite long run takes you through Mission Trails Regional Park, specifically along Father Junipero Serra Trail, be aware of the ongoing pipeline construction. It's 75% complete, but that still means heavy equipment, noise, and dust through late 2028. The trail itself remains open for us walkers, hikers, and cyclists, but vehicle access is closed 24/7, and occasional pedestrian closures can happen without much notice. Keep an eye on local park alerts before you head out, and if you're listening to music, keep one ear open for heavy machinery or unexpected reroutes.
