## Today's Vitals: Don't Be a Hero

Seriously, San Diego, it's August 20th, and Mother Nature is cranky. The National Weather Service has an Extreme Heat Warning active for our desert areas until Friday evening, with inland valleys also baking under a Heat Advisory. We're talking temperatures that can easily hit 105°F (40.5°C) in spots, and even along the coast, it's pushing uncomfortable. If you're planning on tackling anything more strenuous than a short, shady walk, you're playing Russian roulette with heat exhaustion or worse.

And for those who like to hit the dirt, forget it. Several major trails are *closed* for the entire month of August precisely because of this kind of heat and the resulting parade of heat-related rescues. This includes popular spots like El Capitan County Preserve, Hellhole Canyon Preserve, Mt. Gower County Preserve, the San Diego River Gorge, and Three Sisters trails. If you ignore these closures and need a rescue, you're not just risking your own skin, you're tying up resources that could be fighting a real emergency.

Speaking of emergencies, yesterday saw the Dehesa 7 Fire kick off in East County. While crews have thankfully halted its forward spread at 94 acres, a smoke advisory is still in effect for communities like Alpine, Lakeside, Santee, and El Cajon. Our overall AQI is sitting at 67 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, meaning you're breathing in fine particulate matter that's no friend to your lungs, especially if you're working out hard. Your planned run through Mission Trails Regional Park might just turn into a hazy, lung-burning slog.

Roads aren't exactly clear sailing either. Caltrans has overnight electrical work closing the northbound SR-75 connector to southbound I-5, and the westbound I-8 connector to southbound I-15 is shut down 24/7 through 5 AM today for emergency work. Plan your carbo-loading drive accordingly, or just stay home. Also, expect limited access around Waterfront Park through August 24th due to event setup and breakdown.
