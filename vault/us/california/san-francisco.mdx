## The Daily Grind: What to Know Before You Go

Look, San Francisco weather is basically a moody teenager. One minute it's "Mainly Clear" and 14.4°C, the next you're running through a low-hanging fog that feels like a cold, wet blanket. Today, however, we seem to have caught it on a good day. That 14.4°C is damn near perfect for a run up Bernal Heights or a solid loop around Golden Gate Park. No surprise heatstroke, no frozen fingers. Just good, honest running weather.

Now, about those "hazards." Wildfires are always a lurking monster this time of year, but thankfully, there are no major local blazes spewing smoke directly into the Bay Area right now. That's a win. The air quality reflects it, staying in the "Good" range. We're not getting any crazy wind advisories either, so no risk of getting blown off the Golden Gate Bridge – which, let's be honest, is always a low-key concern when you're out there.

<AlertBox type="info">
Remember, even on a "good" day, layers are your friend in this city. Start cool, end warm. Or vice-versa, depending on which way the wind shifts by noon. And for the love of all that is holy, hydrate. It's not scorching, but you're still working.
</AlertBox>

Construction is the eternal hazard in this town, and it seems the persistent work around the **Presidio Tunnel Tops** area continues. While the main trails are generally open, keep an eye out for detour signs, unexpected barriers, or equipment that might have spilled onto a path. Don't assume your usual route is perfectly clear. A quick mental check before you hit that familiar turn could save you a twisted ankle or an abrupt stop.

The good news? No major road closures reported today that would mess with your usual running routes through the city. No last-minute street fairs blocking JFK Drive or random protests shutting down Market Street for your commute run. Just you, the pavement, and maybe a few tourists who haven't quite figured out how crosswalks work.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Even with good air quality, those with sensitive lungs should still be mindful of ozone, especially if you're hitting the trails later in the afternoon when ground-level ozone can sometimes peak. It's usually not a huge deal at an AQI of 53, but listen to your body.
</AlertBox>

Finally, that moderate pollen count. For most, it's just a nuisance. For some, it means itchy eyes and a runny nose that makes breathing harder than it needs to be during a tough mile. If you're in the latter group, consider an antihistamine *before* you head out, not after you're already sneezing your way through Fort Mason.
