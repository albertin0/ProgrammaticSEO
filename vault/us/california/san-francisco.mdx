### Lungs: The Inevitable SF Allergy Tax

Look, it's spring. You live in a city with a ridiculous amount of plant life (and wind to spread its reproductive bits). Today's pollen is sitting at a solid "Moderate," with the usual suspects like Ash, Alder, and Cedar/Juniper doing their best to make your sinuses their personal playground. If you're one of the chosen few who actually enjoys breathing through both nostrils, consider some pre-emptive allergy meds.

On the air quality front, we're clocking in at an AQI of 54, with PM2.5 as the main culprit. That's "Moderate" territory. It means the air is acceptable for most, but if you've got asthma or are just generally sensitive to airborne crud, you might notice it. Don't go trying to set any PRs if you feel like you're sucking air through a coffee stirrer.

### Joints: Clear Skies, Clear Paths (Mostly)

The good news for your knees and ankles? The weather's ideal for a run or walk. A balmy 14.4°C and mostly clear skies mean no slippery streets from unexpected rain, no battling gale-force winds (just the usual San Francisco breeze, which is practically a gentle caress by comparison), and no dodging rogue hailstones.

However, "mostly clear paths" is key here. If your go-to is the **Old Springs Trail near Tennessee Valley**, forget about it for a while. It's shut down until early August for some much-needed boardwalk replacement and general upkeep. Don't be that person who ignores the signs; they put them up for a reason, and it's rarely because they just *love* designing signage. There are plenty of surrounding trails, so find an alternative route.

Also, if you typically park or start your runs around **Upper Fort Mason**, be aware that roads and parking areas are still experiencing closures from utility and seismic retrofit projects, ongoing since last fall and continuing through *this* fall. Expect some detours and potentially longer walks to your usual trailhead.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Coyote Watch in the Presidio:** It's prime coyote pupping season (April through October) in the Presidio Trust Trails. Keep an eye on your pets and small children, and give any coyotes you spot plenty of space. They're just trying to raise their families, but let's keep interactions minimal.
</AlertBox>

So, lace up, breathe as best you can, and enjoy the temperate weather. Just don't expect *all* your usual haunts to be wide open, and certainly don't ignore those itchy eyes if you're prone to allergies.
