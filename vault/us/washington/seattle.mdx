### Air Quality & Allergens

The air quality index is a beautiful 26. A 26! That's practically mountain air, without the mountain. You've got an "Ozone/Other" dominant pollutant, but at this level, it's more like a suggestion than a threat. Your lungs can relax today; no need to start a GoFundMe for a new pair of them.

Now, pollen. Yeah, it's "Moderate." For some of you, that means a slight tickle; for others, it means looking like you just watched *The Notebook* for the tenth time. Tree pollen (birch, alder, cedar) and grass pollen are the usual suspects this time of year in our neck of the woods. Just be aware, especially if you're planning a longer run through, say, the more wooded sections of Discovery Park. A few sneezes won't kill you, but a full-blown asthma attack might put a damper on your PR attempts.

### Hazard Check

I did a quick dive into the local chatter for the last 24 hours, and honestly, we've got one major head-scratcher and a few ongoing annoyances.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Bear Attack on Mount Si!** Yes, you read that right. Two teens were injured in a bear encounter on Mount Si yesterday (June 16), leading to *all Mount Si trails being closed* while the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife works to locate the bear. So, if your plans included elevation gain near North Bend, pivot. Immediately.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Burke-Gilman Trail and Arboretum Waterfront Trail Construction.** The Burke-Gilman is a perennial work-in-progress, and updates from earlier this month indicate ongoing issues, especially through the U-District and where the "Missing Link" is still being designed. Expect detours, impatient cyclists, and general disruption. Additionally, the Washington Park Arboretum's popular Waterfront Trail began Phase 1 construction this month, meaning a portion of that floating walkway is closed. Always keep your head on a swivel.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**World Cup Crowds are Here!** With Seattle hosting FIFA World Cup matches from June 15 to July 6, expect increased foot traffic and general congestion across the city, especially near downtown, the Seattle Center, and public transportation hubs. Plan your runs accordingly and be prepared for more people in our parks and on our shared paths.
</AlertBox>

No extreme winds or heat advisories, which is frankly a miracle for June. The partial demolition at Gas Works Park's iconic towers has been ongoing since April, addressing safety hazards, but earlier path closures from that work are mostly cleared up now, so you should be good for those skyline views, just don't try to climb anything.

So go on, get your miles in. Just bring a light jacket for after your run, because it's still Seattle, and 57°F can feel chilly once you stop moving.
