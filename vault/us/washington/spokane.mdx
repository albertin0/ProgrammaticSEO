### Weather & Air Quality Breakdown

Today's sitting at a brisk 4.7°C (40.5°F) under an overcast sky, with a "feels like" temperature closer to 2°C (36°F). We've got southwest winds kicking around at 5 to 15 mph, with gusts reaching up to 25 mph. That's enough to add a real bite to the air. There's a near 100% chance of rain this morning, potentially dropping up to half an inch, then tapering off to a chance of rain or snow overnight. So, yeah, plan for damp, if not outright wet, conditions.

The good news? Our Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at 30, which is "Good," with Ozone being the dominant pollutant. No smoke, no weird industrial haze—just good, clean (if chilly) Spokane air.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Skimp on Layers, Folks!
With temperatures hovering just above freezing and that wind, hypothermia is a real concern if you're not dressed right. Think wicking layers, a wind-resistant outer shell, and maybe some gloves and a hat, even if you think you're "tough." Those trails get cold, especially near the river.
</AlertBox>

### Local Hazards: Construction Chaos Continues

Alright, here's the real kicker for many of us: construction. It seems like the entire city is getting an upgrade right now, and our trails are no exception. The Children of the Sun river crossing, a crucial connector to the Centennial Trail, is slated for completion this year, and that means ongoing work around those areas. There are also major improvements happening at Camp Sekani & John H. Shields Park extending into 2026.

Out in Spokane Valley, you can expect disruptions from the Pines Road/BNSF Grade Separation project, which is a beast of a project running through spring 2027, and Sprague Avenue Preservation starting up this spring. Even getting out to the Sunset Highway will involve navigating new construction for a nearly two-mile protected path expected to begin this spring. The City of Spokane even releases weekly obstruction notices, so expect detours and closed sections.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Watch Your Step (and Your Head)
Active construction zones mean uneven surfaces, equipment, and potential detours. Pay extra attention to signage, especially if you're running on or near the Centennial Trail around Spokane Community College or where new connections are planned. Don't assume your usual route is clear. Be ready to reroute or hit the gym instead.
</AlertBox>
