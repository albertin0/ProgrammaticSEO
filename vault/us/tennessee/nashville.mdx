Seriously, a Moderate Flood Watch is on for Davidson County all day and into tonight. This means flash flooding is a real possibility. Do *not* run through standing water. You can't see what's underneath, and getting swept away or stepping into a hidden hazard isn't worth a few miles. Avoid areas known to flood.

If you're still determined to get out there, be smart about it. The weather forecast shows scattered thunderstorms and a good chance of rain. That means wet, slippery surfaces are a guarantee. The John Seigenthaler Pedestrian Bridge, for example, might be open, but it'll be slick, and surrounding downtown streets are still recovering from CMA Fest closures. Even if the "road closed" signs are gone, watch for leftover debris or unexpected traffic patterns.

And speaking of closures, if you're a fan of the Whites Creek Greenway, remember that a section is temporarily closed due to long-term TDOT road upgrades on Clarksville Highway. Plan your route accordingly, or explore other options to avoid unexpected detours.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Watch Your Step!
Wet leaves, slick bridges like the John Seigenthaler Pedestrian Bridge, and standing water increase your risk of falls. Reduce your pace and wear appropriate footwear. Visibility will also be reduced under overcast, rainy conditions.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Check Before You Go
Even with the rain, it's muggy out. Hydrate often, even if you don't feel like you're sweating buckets. And always, *always* let someone know your route if you're heading out solo. The flood watch makes this even more critical.
</AlertBox>
