### Lungs: Prepare for a Fight
The AQI is sitting at 116 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, and let's not gloss over that Code Orange for ozone. If you've got any kind of respiratory issues – asthma, allergies, or just a nagging cough from that summer crud – you need to seriously reconsider outdoor exertion, especially during peak ozone hours later this afternoon. Even if you're a seasoned runner, prolonged exposure today could leave you feeling like you ran through a haze of stale exhaust fumes. Which, honestly, isn't far from the truth with this stagnant air.

### Joints & Body: It's a Sauna Out There
Look, 28.3°C is warm, but the *heat index* is the real killer, projected to hit up to 108°F (42.2°C). This isn't just "uncomfortable"; it's a "Minor Heat Advisory" level, meaning heat exhaustion and heat stroke are legitimate risks. Hydration won't just be a suggestion; it'll be a survival strategy if you insist on being outside during the hottest parts of the day, typically 10 AM to 4 PM. Your joints might feel okay initially, but your core temperature will climb fast, putting immense strain on your entire system.
