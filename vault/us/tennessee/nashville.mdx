### Weather Outlook: Overcast, Cooler, with Afternoon Rain Chances

After a rough Saturday of heat, gusty winds, and the threat of severe storms and flash flooding, today brings a welcome cool-down. The mercury is hovering around 20.9°C (that's just under 70°F for you Fahrenheit fundamentalists), under an overcast sky. This makes for some prime running weather, especially if you get out early. However, the National Weather Service still expects showers and thunderstorms to become "likely" this afternoon, particularly in Middle Tennessee. So, plan your run for the morning or be prepared to cut it short. Don't be that person caught in a downpour miles from home.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heads up for afternoon storms!** While not expected to be severe like yesterday's threat, a 70% chance of showers and thunderstorms could mean sudden downpours. Keep an eye on the sky and local radar if you're venturing out later in the day. Yesterday's atmosphere was "loaded with moisture," leading to a flash flood warning for Davidson County; while that's lifted, heavy rain can still cause localized flooding, especially in low-lying areas or underpasses.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Talk: Shelby Bottoms Greenway & Centennial Park

If you're eyeing a long run, know before you go. The **Shelby Bottoms Greenway** is a perennial favorite, but sections are currently affected by closures. The boardwalk near the observation deck is still closed for repairs. Also, the Bendelow Lane Bridge in Shelby Park is out of commission for another four months while it gets replaced. This means you'll need to adjust your usual routes through there. It's a bummer, I know, but better a detour than a face-plant on a busted boardwalk.

Over at **Centennial Park**, phase three renovations are underway. Specifically, the trail near the Bandshell has been closed for maintenance and repairs for the last few days and will likely remain so through at least tomorrow. Always check Nashville.gov's Parks and Recreation closures page before heading out to avoid surprises. Between that and the constant city-wide infrastructure projects Mayor Dunaway keeps talking about, expect some detours and construction noise around town.
