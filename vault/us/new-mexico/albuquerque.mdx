You might look outside and see "Mainly Clear" skies and think, "Perfect running weather!" Not so fast, sunshine. That's not just "warm," that's "stay in the shade and rehydrate constantly" territory.

## Hazards to Watch For

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat — Don't Be a Dummy!
Today's heat is no joke. The region has been flirting with heat advisories, and while Albuquerque might not be hitting 105°F like some other spots in New Mexico have recently, 95°F is plenty hot enough to cause problems. If you're out between 10 AM and 6 PM, you're actively seeking trouble. Your body can only do so much to cool itself, and when the ambient temperature is this high, you're fighting a losing battle.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Wildfire Smoke — Keep an Eye on the Horizon
We've got an elevated fire danger around the Bosque, with increased patrols and sensors deployed to catch anything sparking up. And let's not forget the McCauley Springs Fire burning up in the Santa Fe National Forest near Jemez Springs. While the fire itself is contained at 43% as of yesterday, smoke *may be visible from Albuquerque*. Even if the overall AQI is good, localized smoke plumes can pop up and mess with your lungs. Don't assume clear skies mean clear air for your whole run, especially if you're hitting the trails along the Rio Grande.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate Like Your Life Depends on It (Because It Does)
Seriously, carry more water than you think you need. Pre-hydrate, carry a bottle, and refill whenever you can. Plan your routes around water fountains if you're not carrying a pack. If you're feeling lightheaded, dizzy, or just generally miserable, stop. Find shade. Cool down. No workout is worth a trip to Presbyterian.
</AlertBox>

Look, I love running these trails as much as the next lunatic, but know your limits. Get your miles in early, stay hydrated, and keep an eye on that sky. We don't need any more heroes collapsing on the bike path.
