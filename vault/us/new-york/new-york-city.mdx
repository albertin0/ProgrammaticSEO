## Your Daily Dose of NYC Reality: Construction Never Sleeps

While the skies are clear and the air is clean, this is still New York City, which means one thing: *construction*. It's practically our official city sport.

### East River Greenway: Still a Maze

If you're planning a scenic waterfront run, be advised that the **East River Greenway** is still a hot mess in several sections. The portion south of Stanton Street in John V. Lindsay East River Park remains completely closed as part of the East Side Coastal Resiliency Project. That's a long-term closure, people, since September 2025, and it ain't reopening fully until late 2027. You can access bits of it via the new Delancey Street and Corlears Hook pedestrian bridges, but don't expect a smooth, continuous flow.

Further north, you'll still find detours around 74th Street, which means you're popping off and back onto the path, often navigating crowded sidewalks or less-than-ideal street crossings. It’s not just an inconvenience; it's a safety concern when you're suddenly sharing space with distracted pedestrians or delivery bikes on a narrowed path. Your joints will feel it from the constant changes in surface and rhythm.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Watch Your Step:** The Manhattan Tunnel Project also means the Hudson River Park segment of the Empire State Trail between W. 29th and W. 30th Streets has narrowed bikeways and walkways, plus potential construction truck crossings. Keep your head on a swivel.
</AlertBox>

### Ruppert Park: New Obstacles

Over on the Upper East Side, the $8.8 million renovation of **Ruppert Park** just kicked off yesterday, September 14th. Phase 1 of this project is expected to last until summer 2027, so your local green space might be looking more like a construction site for a while. While it's getting a dog run and exercise area eventually, for now, it's a no-go for uninterrupted workouts.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Pro Tip:** With these ongoing closures and detours, consider exploring some less-traveled routes or hitting up a track if you need consistent pacing. Randall's Island is often a good escape from mainland chaos, accessible via several footbridges from Manhattan, Queens, and the Bronx.
</AlertBox>
