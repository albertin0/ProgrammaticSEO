<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality Advisory for Sensitive Lungs
While we're not under a full "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" advisory *today*, an AQI of 78 with PM2.5 means if you've got asthma, other respiratory issues, or just want to avoid feeling cruddy, consider dialing back the intensity or opting for an indoor session. NYC has been in and out of those advisories recently, with ozone levels hitting the "unhealthy" range just a few days ago, so stay vigilant.
</AlertBox>

Then there's the perpetual urban obstacle course: construction. Seriously, sometimes it feels like the entire city is one giant excavation site. If your go-to route involves the Lower East Side, specifically around **Corlears Hook Park** and the East River Greenway, be prepared for some detours and potential dust. The Parallel Conveyance Project is still going strong, with ongoing daytime road closures of Jackson Street between Water Street and South Street, which means heavy machinery and deep excavation are part of the scenery. You might think you're getting a clear path along the water, but the city's always got a surprise in store.

Even if you're on the FDR Drive's adjacent paths, watch out for overnight closures for median reconstruction between Montgomery Street and East 15 Street, though those are usually late-night and early-morning deals. Just remember, construction crews rarely prioritize your PRs.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Construction Chaos on the Lower East Side
Expect active construction, road closures, and potential for dust and noise around Water Street, Jackson Street, and Corlears Hook Park. Jackson Street between Water Street and South Street is seeing ongoing daytime closures. Plan your runs accordingly or face an unplanned agility workout.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration and Awareness: Your Best Training Partners
Despite the moderate temperatures, always carry water, especially with that elevated AQI. Keep your eyes peeled for unexpected detours, uneven surfaces, and construction debris. Your body will thank you, and your PT won't have to hear about another twisted ankle.
</AlertBox>

So, if you're heading out, be smart. Hydrate, pick your routes wisely, and maybe save that lung-busting interval session for a day when the air isn't quite so... particulate-rich.
