### Hazards to Dodge

While the immediate weather is clear, we're still dealing with the aftermath of some pretty gnarly rain and thunderstorms that hit NYC just days ago. That means trails could be unexpectedly wet, muddy, or even have some debris. Be extra vigilant if you're hitting spots like the unpaved sections of the **Central Park** bridle path or even just a quick sprint down a tree-lined street; roots covered by wet leaves are ankle-breakers.

Speaking of Central Park, our urban oasis is a patchwork of closures. The East 110th Street Playground and West 85th Street Playground are both closed for restoration. Bethesda Fountain is also off for restoration, and some benches and paths on the Concert Ground are temporarily closed. If you're a fisherman (or just like watching them), the catch-and-release program at the Harlem Meer is suspended due to harmful algal blooms. And, of course, the Central Park Drives north of East 90th Street are getting repaved weeknights, which means cyclists and pedestrians need to be extra cautious if you're out late.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Expect slick surfaces, residual puddles, and potential debris on trails and sidewalks today due to heavy rainfall earlier this week. Don't go flying around blind corners.
</AlertBox>

The never-ending saga of construction continues to reshape our running routes. If you're planning a bike ride or run along the **Hudson River Greenway**, be advised that between West 29th and West 30th streets, the path is narrowed and detoured for the Gateway Tunnel Project. Watch out for construction vehicles. And if you're using the **Brooklyn Bridge** tonight or early tomorrow, remember the bikeway and a Manhattan-bound lane are closed from 9 PM to 5 AM until September 3rd for repairs. Cyclists will be directed to a shared promenade or advised to use the Manhattan Bridge instead.

<AlertBox type="danger">
Significant construction impacts are active on the Hudson River Greenway and Brooklyn Bridge. Expect detours, narrowed paths, and be alert for heavy machinery, especially during off-peak hours.
</AlertBox>

Finally, if your workout involves a dip to cool down, think twice. As of yesterday, five NYC beaches in Brooklyn, Queens, and the Bronx were under "Do Not Swim" advisories due to elevated bacteria levels. Stick to the pool, or at least check the latest advisories before you take the plunge.
