### Air & Allergies

The good news? We're not choking on Canadian wildfire smoke today, at least not like we were a few months back. Your lungs aren't fighting a losing battle against a "Very Unhealthy" AQI. Our Air Quality Index is sitting at a "Moderate" 54, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. For most people, that's perfectly fine. If you're part of the sensitive crew (asthma, heart issues, or just generally delicate), an AQI in the 51-100 range might mean you feel it a bit, so keep an eye on how you're feeling and don't push it too hard.

Pollen is also at a moderate level, so if your nose usually runs like a hydrant in spring, you might get a little sniffle, but probably not a full-blown attack.

### Trail Talk & Street Smarts

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Hudson River Greenway Headaches:** Cyclists, take note! The Hudson River Greenway bike path is still temporarily re-routed around W 29th and W 30th Streets due to that never-ending Gateway Tunnel Project construction. Do *not* try to sneak onto the pedestrian walkway; you're just asking for a collision or a scolding. Stay on the marked detour. Ongoing landscape and esplanade design work between W 29th and W 44th Streets also means things can be a bit messy.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Central Park Closures & Construction:** Our beloved Central Park is a perpetual construction zone, and today is no different. The North Meadow Ballfields and Heckscher Ballfields are both closed through Sunday, September 13th, for scheduled maintenance. If you were planning a pickup game or just a sprint across the grass, find another patch. The English Garden at Conservatory Garden is also scheduled to close early at 2:00 PM today if a postponed event is moved due to inclement weather from yesterday. The drives north of East 90th Street are still undergoing weeknight milling and repaving (9:00 PM - 4:00 AM), so if you're a night owl, watch out for equipment.
</AlertBox>

On the street front, the city's "Open Streets" initiative continues with permanent improvements near schools, like the first gated Open Street in Tribeca on Clarkson Street. These are generally good for pedestrians, but keep an eye out for new traffic patterns if you're running or cycling through neighborhoods like Tribeca, the Bronx (Beaumont Avenue), or Manhattan (Avenue B).

Bottom line: It's not a bad day to be out, but keep your head on a swivel. New York City never sleeps, and neither does its construction or event schedule.
