## Your Body Isn't a Toaster, Don't Bake It

Let's be real, New York. It's August 7th, and the city is a giant, humid oven. The National Weather Service isn't messing around with this Heat Advisory, warning us that heat index values are going to feel like the upper 90s to a scorching 104 degrees Fahrenheit. This isn't just a "drink more water" situation; this is a "stay inside or severely modify your workout" warning. Your heart is already working overtime just to keep you cool, so adding a five-mile tempo run on top of that is just asking for trouble. Heatstroke isn't bragging rights, it's an ambulance ride.

Then there's the air. We're sitting at a moderate AQI of 72, with PM2.5 as the main culprit. That's not terrible, but it's not exactly crisp mountain air either. Combine moderate particulates with extreme heat and humidity, and every breath feels thicker, every effort more taxing. If you've got any kind of respiratory issues, or even if you don't, your lungs are going to feel this.

## Navigating the Urban Obstacle Course

For those absolutely committed to moving outdoors, consider your route carefully. Today is one of those glorious Saturdays when Manhattan throws open its "Summer Streets" from 7 AM to 3 PM, closing a whopping 20 miles of roadway from the Brooklyn Bridge all the way north to Inwood (Broadway and Dyckman Street) to vehicle traffic. If you're going to brave the heat, this is your best bet for uninterrupted movement. Less dodging cabs, more room to sweat. But even on these car-free stretches, the heat and air quality remain.
