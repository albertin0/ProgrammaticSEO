## Watch Your Step, NYC

It might look clear out there right now, but this isn't the day to zone out on your run or ride. The mild temperatures are a nice change, sure, but those winds are no joke. Running over the Brooklyn Bridge, for instance, you'll be fighting a battle against the elements that could quickly turn a good workout into a miserable slog.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Wind Advisory:** Strong southwest winds with gusts up to 29 mph are expected. Cyclists, be extra cautious for sudden crosswinds. Runners, brace yourselves, especially on open stretches or bridges. Don't be that person who gets blown into traffic.
</AlertBox>

Afternoon brings the rain, which means all those usual city hazards – potholes, metal plates, manhole covers – will become slick death traps. If your route takes you anywhere near the FDR Drive, expect damp conditions that demand your full attention.

### Don't Forget the Dust and Grime

While the AQI is in the moderate zone (61 for NYC), and pollen is at a moderate level, it's still enough to irritate sensitive lungs. You're not going to spontaneously combust, but if you're someone who feels it when the air isn't perfectly crisp, today might be better for an indoor spin class than an outdoor sprint.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Layer Up and Stay Visible:** With temperatures fluctuating and showers expected, dressing in layers is key. Clear conditions early don't mean clear all day. If you're out later, wear reflective gear; visibility drops fast once the rain starts.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Construction Zones:** There are ongoing street closures for construction around the city. While not directly on major running *trails*, closures on streets like 16th between 5th and 6th, or 54th between 1st and 2nd, mean more detours and potential congestion for getting to your workout spots. Stay alert for unexpected diversions.

No major races are shutting down your favorite spots today, so no need to reroute around a half-marathon, thankfully. Just the usual urban obstacles, magnified by wind and impending rain. Be smart, New York.
