## Today's Vibe: Sunny with a Side of Side-Eye

So, the sun's out, the temperature is a decent 19.8°C (that's 67.6°F for you old-schoolers), and it *looks* like a great day to pound the pavement. But don't let the clear skies fool you. The Air Quality Index is sitting at a moderate 61, with ozone as the dominant pollutant. The New York State Department of Environmental Conservation even slapped an Air Quality Health Advisory on us. If you've got asthma, active kids, or just lungs that aren't forged from steel and spite, consider taking it easy. "Reduce prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors," they say. Translation: your PR attempt might just make you cough up a lung.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality Advisory for Sensitive Groups
With a Moderate AQI and an active advisory, those with lung conditions, children, and even generally active adults should consider scaling back the intensity and duration of their outdoor workouts.
</AlertBox>

### Post-Rain Puddle Patrol

Yesterday, the city got a good soaking with heavy rain and potential flash flooding. While today is clear, some of our less-than-stellar drainage spots – I'm looking at you, underpasses and certain street corners – might still be holding onto some gnarly puddles. Remember, standing water means unknown depths, hidden debris, and generally disgusting street sludge. Don't risk a twisted ankle or an impromptu, unsanitary foot bath. Be vigilant, especially if your route takes you through any known low spots.

## Local Lane Lamentations

If you were planning a nice long run or bike ride along the **Hudson River Greenway** today, be aware that parts of it are currently rerouted. The Gateway Tunnel Project construction means the bike path is temporarily diverted at West 29th and West 30th streets. And just for kicks, Pier 51 is completely closed for playground reconstruction starting *today*. Seriously, try to make it work, folks.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Midtown 5k Alert
The "Manhattan Midtown 5k" is happening today at Pier 84 on West 44th Street. If your usual mid-day run takes you through that slice of Manhattan, expect some increased foot traffic and potential minor detours. Plan accordingly or hit a different borough.
</AlertBox>

And because this is New York, there's always construction. Various bridge rehabilitation projects, like those on the Springfield Boulevard Bridge and Cross Island Parkway, continue to cause lane closures. The BQE near the Brooklyn Bridge exit also has lane restrictions for deck replacement. While these are mainly traffic-related, if your route involves these areas, give yourself extra time or find an alternate path.
