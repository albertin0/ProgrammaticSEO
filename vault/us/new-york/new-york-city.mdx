Today, October 15, 2026, New York City is serving up some prime running weather. The crisp 13.4°C (that's about 56°F for the Fahrenheit folks) and clear skies are pretty ideal for hitting the pavement or the trails. Your lungs will be happy with an AQI of 45, which is firmly in the "good" category. This is a welcome change from recent days, where smoke from Canadian wildfires pushed our air quality into "unhealthy for sensitive groups" territory just yesterday. Today, those fine particulates have mostly cleared out.

However, don't just blindly sprint out the door. The moderate pollen count is nothing to sneeze at (pun intended, obviously). If you're one of the many who get hit hard by seasonal allergies, a moderate rating can still mean a runny nose, itchy eyes, and generally feeling miserable on your run. Pop those antihistamines before you lace up.

Now, for your joints and general sanity: expect some navigational gymnastics if your route takes you through certain green spaces. The NYC Parks department has ongoing construction impacting several popular spots. For instance, sections of **East River Park** south of Stanton Street are temporarily closed due to the East Side Coastal Resiliency Project, and parts of The Battery are also closed for their own coastal resilience work. Even along the Hudson River Park segment of the Empire State Trail, you'll find a small detour between W. 29th and W. 30th Streets due to the "Manhattan Tunnel Project". So, don't count on a clear run through these areas; be prepared for detours or to choose an alternate path.

And if you're planning a tranquil morning jog in **Central Park**, be aware that the TCS New York City Marathon Kids Kickoff is happening today, featuring timed and competitive races for the younger generation. While inspiring, this means certain pathways could be crowded. If you're looking for an actual race, the "191 Run" is a decentralized event starting today, giving you plenty of time to log miles on your own terms.

### Pollen Alert for Allergy Sufferers
Today's moderate pollen count means allergy symptoms are a real possibility. Consider taking your allergy medication before heading out, and perhaps opt for shaded routes if pollen tends to linger in open, sunny areas.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route, Avoid Surprises
Before heading out, check the NYC Parks website for specific closures or event impacts, especially if you're planning to run in East River Park, The Battery, or Central Park. Construction and events can change your planned route significantly.
</AlertBox>

### Layer Up, It's Crisp Out There
With temperatures in the low teens Celsius, it's definitely cool. Dress in layers, especially if you're starting early, to stay comfortable as you warm up. A light jacket or long-sleeve shirt might be wise, even if you plan to shed it a few miles in.
