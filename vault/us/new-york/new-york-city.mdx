## Your Lungs & the Concrete Jungle

So, the air's clocking in at an AQI of 53, with "Ozone/Other" as the main culprit, and pollen is sitting at a "Moderate" level. That's not terrible, but it's not pristine either. What's "other" mean for us? Well, on Sunday, a pretty gnarly warehouse fire over in Belleville, NJ, threw a massive plume of smoke right over Manhattan and Jersey City, and let's not forget the wind-fueled blaze in Queens on May 3rd. While the immediate visible smoke might have cleared, these things leave behind particulates. Add in those "warm winds" that spiked fire risk just a couple of days ago, and you get the picture – the air isn't exactly mountain fresh. If you're prone to seasonal allergies or have any respiratory issues, today's the day to err on the side of caution. Consider that easier pace, or maybe stick to an indoor session if you feel any tightness.

### The Construction Gauntlet

Look, it's New York. Something's always under construction. But right now, if you're planning on logging miles along the East River, be warned: that greenway is a minefield of detours and closures. Construction operations in East River Park are shutting down sections 24/7. We're talking about the permanent closure of the greenway between Montgomery Street and Stuyvesant Cove Park. You'll be rerouted, potentially onto streets with more traffic, and some of these operations are loud and disruptive. It's not just a minor inconvenience; it's a legitimate safety concern to be mindful of cyclists, pedestrians, and actual active construction zones.

**Watch Your Step (and Your Head)!**
Ongoing, 24/7 construction in East River Park means significant greenway closures and detours. Expect noise, potential traffic reroutes, and keep an eye out for heavy machinery and uneven surfaces. Stay alert and follow all signage.

Elsewhere, Riverside Park South is seeing reconstruction on the West 69th Street Transfer Bridge. While this might not shut down an entire running path, it's another reminder that our precious outdoor spaces are constantly being "improved," often at the expense of our immediate workout plans.
