### Fog and Footing

The early bird might get the worm, but today, the early runner gets dense fog and potentially damp, slippery surfaces until at least mid-morning. This isn't just about looking cool in a misty photo; it's about not face-planting on the path. Reduced visibility means you need to be hyper-aware of your surroundings, especially if you're dodging tourists or potholes on the Hudson River Greenway.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch Your Step & Be Seen:** If you're out before 9 AM, assume surfaces are damp and slick. Wear bright colors, maybe even reflective gear, and carry a light. Seriously, drivers and cyclists might not see you until it's too late.
</AlertBox>

### What's In The Air?

We’re not talking about spring breezes. Yesterday, a wildfire in New Jersey meant an Air Quality Health Advisory for the metro area, with PM2.5 levels that were no joke for sensitive groups. Today, the official AQI clocks in at a "Moderate" 57, with PM2.5 still the dominant pollutant. That means tiny particles getting deep into your lungs. Add in a moderate pollen count, and if you've got asthma or allergies, you're probably feeling it. Don't push hard if your breathing feels off, no matter what the number says. Your body's internal alarm system is usually pretty accurate.

### Running Routes: Expect the Unexpected

Forget your usual Central Park loop for the next week or so. The United Airlines NYC Half Marathon construction embargo is wreaking havoc on some of our favorite routes. This isn't just a "detour here or there" situation; we're talking about significant sections of Brooklyn's Washington Avenue, Flatbush Avenue, and parts of Central Park's East and West Drives being under strict permit suspensions.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Central Park & Brooklyn Runners Beware:** Check the NYC DOT's construction embargo map before you head out. Don't assume your usual path is open or safe. You could be forced onto unfamiliar, crowded, or even unsafer streets if you don't plan ahead. East River Park's northern end is also still fully closed for flood protection construction, a project expected to continue through 2026.
</AlertBox>

Today's not the day to blindly hit the pavement. Plan smart, stay safe, and maybe just hit the treadmill.
