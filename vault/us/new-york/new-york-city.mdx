## Why today isn't the day to push it (or even step outside much):

<BulletList>
*   **This isn't just hot, it's *dangerous* hot.** We're in the middle of a multi-day heat wave with "feels-like" temperatures hitting 105 to 115 degrees Fahrenheit. This isn't the kind of heat you "power through." Your body simply can't regulate itself safely.
*   **Your lungs are already working overtime.** While our current AQI is sitting at a "moderate" 66 for PM2.5, the city's been under an Air Quality Alert for ground-level ozone, and it was expected to exceed 100 (unhealthy for sensitive groups) just yesterday. With this stagnant, scorching air, ozone pollution is a real and present danger, especially for anyone exercising outdoors.
*   **Holiday weekend doesn't mean hazard-free.** While the Port Authority is suspending non-essential bridge and tunnel construction for the 4th of July weekend, which is *some* relief, there are still ongoing detours like the one on the Hudson River Park segment of the Empire State Trail between W. 29th and W. 30th Street. And if you think about running near the water for the fireworks tomorrow, expect increased security, which might affect your usual routes.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. The sun's out, and you want to feel that burn. But the only burn you'll be feeling today is a heat-related illness. New York City tied its record high temperature of 100 degrees yesterday in Central Park, and the Bronx hit 107 degrees. We're looking at more of the same today and through Saturday.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: Seriously, Stay Inside
An Extreme Heat Warning is in effect through Saturday, with "feels-like" temperatures that are deadly. Heat stroke and heat exhaustion are not a joke. Cooling centers are open across the city; find one if you don't have AC. This is the leading cause of weather-related fatalities in the US.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality Alert for Ozone
Even if your app says "moderate" AQI right now, an Air Quality Health Advisory was just extended for ozone in the NYC Metro area yesterday, with levels expected to exceed 100. This ground-level ozone is formed when car exhaust bakes under strong UV rays on hot days. Exercising vigorously in this can cause shortness of breath, chest pain, and coughing.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration is Non-Negotiable
If you absolutely *must* be outside for short periods, drink water constantly. Like, more than you think you need. Mayor Mamdani is even urging New Yorkers to set thermostats to 78 degrees to conserve energy amidst the strain on the grid. This heat is serious for everyone, especially children, older adults, and those with underlying conditions.
</AlertBox>

Today is a day for air conditioning, hydration, and maybe some indoor bodyweight exercises. Your marathon training can wait. Your life can't.
