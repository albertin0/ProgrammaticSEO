## The Lowdown for Your Lungs & Legs

Good news on the air front: our Air Quality Index is sitting pretty at a "Good" 28 today. No need to worry about choking on smog while you're trying to hit your stride, which is a rare treat these days. The dominant pollutant is Ozone/Other, but at this level, it's nothing to write home about.

<AlertBox type="info">
Remember, even on good AQI days, if you have asthma or other respiratory conditions, listen to your body, especially with moderate pollen around.
</AlertBox>

| Overall     | Moderate |

Moderate pollen is the usual spring-in-NYC story. If you're one of the lucky ones who can run through a field of dandelions without turning into a snot-nosed mess, you're probably fine. For the rest of us, maybe pop an antihistamine before you head out, especially if you're hitting any of the more treed areas like parts of Central Park.

## Weather Woes: Wet & Wild (Yesterday's Wild, Today's Wet)

It's a cool 15.6°C (60°F) and overcast out there. Sounds pleasant enough, right? Except the rain is definitely back on the forecast for today. Yesterday's severe thunderstorms brought significant wind gusts and heavy rain to the boroughs, causing flooding across Brooklyn and Queens, even closing parts of the Long Island Expressway. While today's rain might not be as intense, expect slick surfaces and potential hazards from standing water.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Yesterday's torrential rain means there's a good chance of lingering puddles and localized flooding, particularly in low-lying areas. Don't be a hero; avoid running through deep water where you can't see what's underneath.
</AlertBox>

## Trail Talk: Obstacles Abound

If your usual route takes you along the waterfront, prepare for some navigation. The Hudson River Park segment of the Empire State Trail between W. 29th and W. 30th Street is still impacted by the Manhattan Tunnel Project, meaning narrowed paths and construction trucks to watch out for. Over on the East Side, large sections of East River Park, particularly south of Stanton Street and the east end of Corlears Hook Park, remain closed due to the East Side Coastal Resiliency Project. They're even doing night work in the northern section, which is just delightful. Check NYC Parks advisories before you commit to a long run down there.

<AlertBox type="danger">
With the combination of ongoing construction and wet conditions, trails and park paths could be extra slippery or have unforeseen obstacles. Stay alert and consider sticking to familiar, well-maintained routes.
</AlertBox>
