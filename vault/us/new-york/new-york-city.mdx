Today is Friday, July 17, 2026, and the smoke from those Canadian wildfires is still lingering over the city, meaning our air quality is less than ideal for any strenuous outdoor activity. Yesterday, the AQI for the NYC Metro area hit "Unhealthy" levels, with some reports even touching 162. While the provided AQI for *right now* is 114, putting us in the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category, the forecasts from yesterday warned that smoke would continue to affect air quality through tonight and could build back into the area later this afternoon and evening.

This isn't just a sniffle-inducing pollen situation; we're talking about fine particulate matter (PM2.5) that can get deep into your lungs. Even if you don't consider yourself "sensitive," prolonged exposure when the AQI is over 100 can still cause issues.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**WILDFIRE SMOKE WARNING: LIMIT OUTDOOR ACTIVITY!**
The Air Quality Index (AQI) is 114 (Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups), with expectations for smoke to build up again later today. This means everyone should limit strenuous and prolonged outdoor activities. If you have asthma, heart/lung disease, or are an older adult or child, seriously consider moving your workout indoors.
</AlertBox>

If you absolutely *must* be outside, keep your activity light and short. Consider wearing a well-fitting KN95 or N95 mask to filter out some of that particulate matter. Hydrate like it's your job, and pay attention to how your body feels. Any coughing, wheezing, shortness of breath, or chest pain? Get inside. Seriously.

As for other hazards, there's always construction in this city. A small detour is currently in effect for the Hudson River Park section of the Empire State Trail between W. 29th and W. 30th Street due to the Manhattan Tunnel Project, with bikeways and walkways narrowed. Just another headache on a hazy day.

Stay safe out there, or better yet, stay *in* there. Hit the gym, find an indoor track, or just tackle that mountain of laundry. Your lungs will thank you.
