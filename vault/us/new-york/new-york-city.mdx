## Air Quality: Still Not Breathing Easy

You might look outside and see "Clear" skies this morning at 71°F (21.7°C), but don't let that fool you. Our air quality is still taking a hit thanks to those persistent wildfires up in Canada and Minnesota. The Air Quality Index is currently sitting at 132, which is "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups". That means if you've got asthma, heart conditions, or are just generally not a fan of coughing up a lung, you should probably be thinking twice about pushing hard outdoors.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Danger: Don't Be a Hero, Seriously.**
Wildfire smoke contains fine particulate matter (PM2.5) that can irritate your eyes, nose, and throat, and worsen conditions like asthma and heart disease. Even if it's not a full-blown orange haze like we saw on Thursday when the AQI hit 265, "lingering effects" are still a concern. Officials recommend limiting strenuous outdoor activity and even wearing an N95 mask if you absolutely have to be outside for an extended period.
</AlertBox>

## Weather Woes: Watch Your Step

Yesterday was a mess with severe thunderstorms and flash flooding across the city. While conditions are expected to improve today, it doesn't mean the streets are suddenly pristine. Roads like the BQE and Clearview Expressway saw closures, and though they've reopened, residual delays and localized problems can pop up if the drainage isn't quite caught up. You're going to encounter wet, potentially slippery patches, especially in those charming low-lying areas that always seem to turn into impromptu swimming pools. Take it easy on those turns, folks.

## Gridlock and Detours: The Urban Gauntlet

As if the air and wet pavement weren't enough, it's Sunday, July 19th, and that means one thing: World Cup chaos. Today is officially a **Gridlock Alert Day** because of a match, and the city is implementing major street closures in Midtown. Running through Manhattan, particularly near 42nd Street, 6th Avenue, or the Port Authority area, is going to be a nightmare of restricted access, bus-only corridors, and general pedestrian mayhem. You'll spend more time dodging tourists and construction barriers than actually running.

And speaking of construction, if you were planning a nice long run along the Hudson River Park segment of the Empire State Trail between W. 29th and W. 30th Streets, know that the bikeway is narrowed to 12 feet and the walkway to 8 feet due to ongoing work. Just another charming obstacle for your Sunday funday.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Caution: Event Congestion Ahead.**
With the World Cup in town, major thoroughfares in Midtown are subject to extensive closures and restrictions. This will impact not just driving, but also pedestrian and cycling routes. Plan alternative routes and expect significant delays if you're venturing into these areas.
</AlertBox>
