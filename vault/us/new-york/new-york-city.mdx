Look, the weather gods threw a fit yesterday, and while the severe thunderstorms and even that bizarre tornado watch for parts of the metro area have theoretically passed as of early this morning, the aftermath is still very real. We're talking widespread rainfall totals that could have hit 1 to 1.5 inches across the city, with localized pockets getting up to 2.5 to 3.5 inches. That means low-lying areas and poor drainage spots are likely still holding onto some serious puddles, if not full-on localized flooding.

This isn't about avoiding a little splash. Flash flooding was a concern, and while the immediate danger has subsided, lingering dampness on trails, sidewalks, and underpasses can turn a routine jog into a twisted ankle waiting to happen. Consider anywhere that had "widespread nuisance ponding" yesterday. Your balance and proprioception are going to be working overtime, and not in a good, strengthening way. Your ankles and knees are probably already bracing for impact just thinking about it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Watch Your Step!** Yesterday's deluge, which included heavy rainfall and a flood alert through early Wednesday morning, means trails, park paths, and even city sidewalks in low-lying areas could still be wet, muddy, or have standing water. This is prime time for slips, trips, and sprained ankles.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, the wildfire smoke event that had us all choking a few days ago seems to have mostly cleared out, with the AQI firmly in the moderate zone today. So at least you won't be actively damaging your respiratory system with every breath, just dealing with the usual city grime and a side of moderate pollen.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Allergy Alert:** With moderate pollen levels, if you're prone to seasonal allergies, you might experience some extra congestion or watery eyes. Consider an antihistamine or sticking to an indoor workout to save yourself the misery.
</AlertBox>

For those of you who insist on getting out there, be smart. That ongoing Gateway Tunnel Project work on the Hudson River Park Greenway, specifically between W. 29th and W. 30th Streets, still means detours, narrowed paths, and construction vehicle crossings. Don't be that person who ignores the barriers and ends up dodging a truck or, worse, falling into a trench. Pay attention to signage. And if you were planning on hitting up one of the NYC Parks recreation centers, double-check before you go. Many are still operating with reduced hours or closures due to summer camps or ongoing maintenance, like the Asser Levy Recreation Center pool closure through mid-September.
