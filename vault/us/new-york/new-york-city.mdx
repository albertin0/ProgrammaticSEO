## Today's Rundown: Navigating the Urban Obstacle Course

Alright, New York, if you're lacing up your shoes this Thursday, April 2nd, prepare for some classic Big Apple "character building." We've got a decent AQI, which is always a small victory, sitting pretty at 41, with Ozone/Other as the dominant pollutant. Breathe deep, your lungs will be mostly fine.

But let's talk about where you're actually *going* to run. The city's perpetual facelift is in full swing, and it's impacting some of our most beloved paths. The Department of Transportation is kicking off construction on the Manhattan-side entrance to the **Brooklyn Bridge** this month, aiming to give cyclists and pedestrians separate entrances. Sounds good in theory for the future, but for today and the foreseeable future, that means navigating a construction zone. Cyclists trying to cut through pedestrian crowds might find new obstacles. Runners, watch your step on Centre Street/Park Row.

### East River Greenway and Hudson River Park Woes

It's not just the Brooklyn Bridge getting a workout. The East Side Coastal Resiliency (ESCR) project continues to mess with the **East River Greenway**, particularly between Montgomery Street and Stuyvesant Cove Park. There are closures and detours, some even involving night work and partial FDR Drive lane closures. If you're usually cruising along the East River, double-check your route before heading out, or mentally prepare for an impromptu detour through the grid.

And for those of you on the west side, the Empire State Trail in **Hudson River Park** between W. 29th Street and W. 30th Street has a small detour due to the Manhattan Tunnel Project. It's not a huge stretch, but it's enough to throw off your rhythm if you're not expecting it.
