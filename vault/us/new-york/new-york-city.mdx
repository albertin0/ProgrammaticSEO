<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Watch Your Head and Your Step, Seriously.

Just because the sky is clear today doesn't mean the city got its act together after the winds on Monday. NYC Parks warned us about strong gusts bringing down trees, loosening facades, and scaffolding across the city. These hazards don't just magically disappear with a calendar flip. If you're running anywhere near construction sites, or even just tree-lined streets in, say, **Central Park**, keep your eyes peeled for loose branches or anything that looks like it's about to take flight or fall. It's not just a minor inconvenience; it can be genuinely dangerous. Stay clear of scaffolding and anything that looks remotely unstable.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Your Lungs Aren't a Filter, So Don't Treat Them Like One.

An AQI of 85 with PM2.5 as the primary culprit isn't ideal for a hard effort, even if it's considered "moderate." Fine particles are nasty business, linked to heart and lung disease and contributing to asthma incidents. If you're doing intervals or a tempo run, especially along busy roads like the West Side Highway or the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway where local emissions are higher, consider toning it down. It might not feel immediately bad, but cumulative exposure adds up. Listen to your body – if your throat feels scratchy or you're breathing harder than usual, take it inside. Don't be a hero.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### General Wisdom (The Stuff You Already Know, But I'll Say Anyway)

Hydrate. Always. Even if it's cool. And for God's sake, if you feel any chest pain or severe shortness of breath, stop. Get help. Your PR isn't worth a trip to the ER. This city's got enough ambulances on the road already. Don't add to the traffic.
</AlertBox>
