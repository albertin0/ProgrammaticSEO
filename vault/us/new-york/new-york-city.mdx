### Heads Up, NYC Runners: Air Quality & Half Marathon Havoc!
The AQI is currently 106 with Ozone being the dominant pollutant, pushing us into the "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" category. For anyone with asthma, COPD, or other breathing issues, this is a clear signal to modify your outdoor activity. Even if you consider yourself healthy, prolonged or strenuous exercise could lead to irritation.

Compounding the lung-burner air, the United Airlines NYC Half Marathon has initiated a major construction embargo starting *today* and lasting until March 15th. This isn't just a few cones; we're talking about significant sections of iconic running spots like Central Park's East and West Drives being affected. Don't assume your go-to route through the park is open. Plan for serious detours or consider indoor alternatives.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Get Frosty
While 2.4°C might not sound like Arctic conditions, an "overcast" sky means less sun to warm you up, and the "feels like" temperature hovers around -2°C. This is cold enough to make exposed skin numb surprisingly quickly. Hydration is still crucial even when it's cold, but consider a warm drink instead of ice water before your run. Remember, muscles are less pliable in the cold, so a dynamic warm-up is non-negotiable if you want to avoid strains.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your Best Bet Today?
If you're still determined to log miles, consider an indoor track, a gym workout, or even just some stair repeats in your building. If you insist on hitting the pavement outside, layer up, wear a mask for the poor air quality, and for the love of all that's holy, check live maps for Central Park and other embargoed areas before you leave. You don't want to add a bonus half-mile of backtracking to your run just because you hit a barrier.
</AlertBox>
