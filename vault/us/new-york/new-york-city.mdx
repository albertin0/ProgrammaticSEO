Look, I get it. We're runners. We like to pretend a little drizzle is "character-building." But today isn't a drizzle day, it's a "don't be an idiot" day. The city is under a *Flood Watch* until tomorrow morning. That means the National Weather Service thinks there's a significant risk of flash flooding, especially in those low-lying urban areas and underpasses you usually zip through. Think about running down the West Side Highway path and suddenly hitting a small lake, or worse, trying to cross a street near a flooded sewer. That's not a challenge; that's a trip to urgent care or, for the truly unlucky, an unexpected swimming lesson in questionable urban runoff.

And those thunderstorms? They're not just about getting wet. We're talking damaging winds that can take down trees and power lines. You don't want to be anywhere near that, especially not on some exposed stretch like the Manhattan Bridge bike path where gusts can really whip through. Save the bridge selfies for a clear day.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Flash Flood Warning & Severe Storms Expected
Seriously, stay indoors today if you can. NYC Emergency Management and the National Weather Service are warning about heavy rainfall, widespread flash flooding, damaging winds (up to 70 mph), and a slight risk of tornadoes. Streets can flood rapidly, making running treacherous and downright dangerous. Lightning is also a significant threat.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, literally, the temperature is decent, sitting around 21°C (70°F). But that "clear" condition mentioned in some forecasts? It's a bit of a tease, because the rain and storms are definitely moving in.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality & Pollen Still a Factor
Even if you brave the storms, the air quality is still in the "Moderate" zone with PM2.5, and pollen is moderate. If you have asthma or allergies, today's conditions are a double whammy for your lungs.
</AlertBox>

And let's not forget the usual construction nonsense. If you were planning a long run along the east side, forget about the northern stretch of East River Park, from Houston Street upwards. That glorious running track? Still closed off as part of the East Side Coastal Resiliency Project and will be for the rest of the year. So if you head out there, you'll just be rerouted onto already congested streets.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your Best Bet: Indoor Workouts
This is a prime day for the treadmill, hitting the gym, or tackling that bodyweight circuit you've been putting off. Don't risk injury or worse just to prove a point to yourself. There will be other, safer days to run outside.
</AlertBox>
