## Today's NYC Running Rundown

Alright, listen up, because nobody wants a sidelined spring season. Today's forecast for March 28, 2026, is a real mixed bag. The good news? No heavy rain or oppressive smog. The bad news? It's cold enough to make a polar bear reconsider its choices, and the city's still a giant construction zone.

First, let's talk about the chill. We're hovering around freezing, and with those "breezy conditions," especially "North winds around 15 mph," you're going to feel it in your bones. This isn't just about being uncomfortable; it's about actual risk. Hypothermia is no joke, and those cold gusts can numb your extremities fast. Think layers, people. Technical fabrics, wind-resistant outer shells, hats, gloves, and maybe even a face covering are non-negotiable if you're planning on anything longer than a sprint to the bodega. Your lungs are going to hate that cold air, so ease into it.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Watch Out for Wind Chill & Frostbite**
Even with the sun, the near-freezing temperatures and persistent "breezy conditions" create a significant wind chill factor. Exposed skin can get frostbitten faster than you think. Cover up!
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, the air itself isn't actively trying to kill your lungs today. The AQI is a solid 35, which is "Good" by all metrics, with "Ozone/Other" as the dominant pollutant – not the usual nasty particulates. So breathe deep... if you can manage through the chill. Pollen is "Moderate" though, so if you're prone to seasonal sniffles, pop an antihistamine before you head out.

Now for the real pain in the ass for us dedicated pavement pounders: construction. The city never stops, and neither do the closures. If your usual route takes you along the East River Greenway, particularly north of Houston Street, forget about it. The "northern section of East River Park — basically everything above Houston Street to 15th Street" is still completely shut down due to the East Side Coastal Resiliency project. We're talking "closure of the Greenway, 24 hours/day, 7 days/week." You’re not getting through. You'll hit a wall, literally, and have to detour onto crowded streets or cut your run short. Plan for alternate routes like the Hudson River Greenway, but even there, always check for local street closures and events. The NYC DOT's weekend advisories are packed with various street closures for crane operations and inspections across Manhattan.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **East River Park: Still a No-Go Zone (North of Houston St.)**
Do not attempt to run the East River Greenway north of Houston Street. The East Side Coastal Resiliency project has that section, including the track and esplanade, completely closed until at least the end of 2027. Find another path.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### **Dress Like an Onion, Not a Sausage**
Layers are your best friend today. Start with a moisture-wicking base, add insulating layers, and top with a wind-resistant outer shell. You can always shed a layer, but you can't conjure one out of thin air if you underdress.
</AlertBox>

Overall, today’s a day for the tough, well-prepared, and navigationally savvy. It's not a write-off, but it demands respect.
