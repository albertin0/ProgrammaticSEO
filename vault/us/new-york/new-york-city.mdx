Your body will thank you for proper layering and a dynamic warm-up that lasts longer than a New York minute.

Yes, it’s still March, but don't let that fool you—pollen is already making its move with moderate levels. If you’re one of those unlucky souls whose nose starts running at the mere mention of spring, pop an antihistamine before you head out, unless you enjoy the snot-rocket aesthetic.

### Mind the Freezing Temps!
At -0.3°C, exposed skin is asking for trouble. Think layers: base, mid, and a wind-resistant outer shell. Don't forget insulated gloves, a hat, and thick socks. Your fingers and toes will be the first to complain if you cheap out on gear.
</AlertBox>

Now, for the real fun: navigating the city's perpetual state of "under construction." The NYC DOT's weekend advisory is a veritable novel of street closures, especially in Manhattan. We're talking crane operations, building inspections, and material hoisting shutting down streets all over. While many were scheduled through March 29, meaning some might be clearing up, others are ongoing for weeks or even months. You think you're getting a clear path down, say, East 3rd Street or West 13th? Think again. These aren't just minor detours; they can force you into traffic or onto rough sidewalks, testing both your patience and your ankles.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Construction Chaos Ahead
Expect major disruptions, especially in Manhattan. Multiple street closures for crane work and building projects mean your usual routes are likely blocked or narrowed. Keep your head on a swivel for unexpected traffic reroutes and less-than-ideal running surfaces. The Hudson River Park Greenway is still experiencing narrowed pathways and potential construction vehicle crossings between W. 29th and W. 30th Streets due to the Manhattan Tunnel Project. This isn't a short-term thing either; this particular detour is expected to last about six months.
</AlertBox>

So, if you’re heading out, know what you’re getting into. Bundle up, stay vigilant, and maybe embrace the spirit of urban parkour as you navigate around the latest structural adjustments to our fair city. And for the love of all that is holy, look up the specific DOT advisories for your area *before* you lace up. Otherwise, you'll spend more time rerouting than actually running.
