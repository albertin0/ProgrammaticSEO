### Weather Whiplash is a Real Injury

Seriously, what in the actual heck is going on with the weather? 29.3 degrees Celsius in early March is less "spring preview" and more "hellscape prequel." Your body's internal thermostat, especially after a Rochester winter (even a mild one by our standards), is going to be utterly confused. You might *feel* fine for a bit, but heat exhaustion and heat stroke are no joke, and they sneak up on you faster than a pothole on Culver Road after a thaw. Stay hydrated, and if you're hitting the pavement, acknowledge that this isn't normal and scale back your efforts significantly. Your heart and joints will thank you for not pushing it like it's a perfect 70-degree day in May. The City even has an "Extreme Heat Plan" for when the heat index is above 90°F for two days, and while we're not quite there, this is still dangerously warm for the season.

### Breathe It In (Or Don't)

With an AQI of 59, we're in "Moderate" territory. For most of us, it's probably fine for a short, easy jaunt. But if you have any respiratory issues – asthma, COPD, or just a chronic case of Rochester sinus crud – you might notice it. And don't forget that moderate pollen. Combine that with the heat, and you've got a recipe for wheezing and sneezing that'll make your run feel like a death march.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### EXTREME UNSEASONAL HEAT ALERT!
Running or cycling in 29.3°C (84.7°F) weather in early March is highly dangerous. This is not normal. Reduce your intensity, wear light clothing, and hydrate constantly. Better yet, move your workout indoors. Your body is not acclimated to this.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble Ahead

If you thought you'd escape the heat on our glorious trails, think again. The Erie Canalway Trail between Lyell Avenue and Buffalo Road is still under a "complete closure" for a restoration project that began back in December. So much for a nice long stretch there; expect detours. And don't forget the I-490 overpass repair in Perinton, which could mean "temporary impacts" and flaggers near the canal trail there through the fall. Even around the Genesee River Trail, near Flint Street and South Plymouth Avenue, ongoing brownfield cleanup projects mean increased construction traffic, even if the trail itself has reopened in parts. Keep your head on a swivel if you're in those areas.

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Trail & Road Construction Warnings
Portions of the Erie Canalway Trail (between Lyell Ave and Buffalo Road) are *completely closed* for restoration. Expect delays and potential impacts near the I-490 overpass in Perinton and increased construction traffic around the Genesee River Trail (Flint St/S Plymouth Ave area). Plan alternate routes.
</AlertBox>
