## The Full Baltimore Breakdown

Look, I get it. We love our city, we love our runs through Druid Hill Park or along the Inner Harbor. But today, Mother Nature, along with City Hall, is telling you to take a chill pill.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Code Red Extreme Heat is NO JOKE!
The City Health Commissioner, Dr. Michelle Taylor, didn't issue this Code Red for fun. The heat index is projected to exceed 105 degrees Fahrenheit, and that's a direct threat to your well-being. Heat-related deaths have already occurred in Baltimore this year. If you absolutely *must* be outside, keep it short, stick to the shade, and drink more water than you think you need. Better yet, find a cooling center or just stay home.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Breathe That In: Poor Air Quality Alert
As if the heat wasn't enough, the Air Quality Index (AQI) is sitting at a "Poor" level, specifically unhealthy for sensitive groups. Tiny particulate matter (PM2.5) is floating around, irritating lungs and potentially aggravating heart and lung conditions. This isn't the day for deep breathing exercises on the Gwynns Falls Trail.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Mind Your Surroundings (and Your Knees)
While not directly impacting *all* workout routes, be aware of ongoing roadwork. Downtown has lane closures on Guilford Avenue, Fayette Street, and Charles Street all week. There's also overnight paving kicking off on MD 140 (Reisterstown Road) and major work on the Baltimore Beltway. Even if you're out before crews, uneven surfaces and shifting traffic patterns are an ankle sprain waiting to happen. For the evening, expect high winds (gusts up to 50-70 MPH near the Bay) and tidal flooding later today. If you're near the water, be extra cautious after 5 PM.
</AlertBox>
