Shorter days and reduced visibility mean you're harder to see, and let's be honest, drivers around here aren't always winning awards for attentiveness. If you're hitting the streets, especially near busy intersections or areas like the approaches to **Lake Montebello** or around the Inner Harbor, assume no one sees you. Make eye contact. Wear bright colors. Don't be a statistic.

However, recent days have seen much worse conditions, with "Code Red" wildfire smoke alerts impacting the region as recently as yesterday, though today's official reading is moderate. While some forecasts might still lean towards 'poor' air quality for today, the current reading is firmly in the moderate zone. Still, if you've got existing respiratory issues, you might feel it on longer, more strenuous runs.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ### Moderate Irritants: Know Your Limits

Keep an eye on any developing haze; we're not out of the woods entirely on air quality just because the number is "moderate" today.
</AlertBox>

## Construction & Road Updates

Forget the Baltimore Running Festival street closures from last week (October 17th) – those are long gone, so you won't be running into that particular mess today. However, this city is a perpetual construction zone. Baltimore County has ongoing roadway improvement projects on I-695 and I-70 that were scheduled through mid-October, with some paving work potentially continuing overnight today, impacting drivers more than runners. Closer to home, the Baltimore City Department of Transportation (DOT) has advised of general weekend road closures (Oct 23-25) and temporary lane closures on the southbound Jones Falls Expressway/I-83. If your route takes you anywhere near these arteries, expect potential detours or less-than-ideal conditions. Many "Streets of Baltimore" projects are also active, including roadway improvements and cycleway extensions on streets like Harford Road and in neighborhoods like Johnston Square. Always check local DOT maps before you head out if you're running on city streets.

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Plan Your Route Smart
  With ongoing road work, urban running routes might have unexpected detours. Check the city's DOT site or stick to known, dedicated paths like the B&A Trail (if you're heading out of the city) if you want to avoid unexpected obstacles and focus solely on your workout.
</AlertBox>
