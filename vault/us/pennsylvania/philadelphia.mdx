### Weathering the Heat (or Not)

Let's be real, the "Mainly Clear" 13.0°C from some automated reports is a pipe dream. The *actual* forecast for today, June 4, 2026, is the start of a heat wave, with highs pushing near 90 degrees. While they're saying the humidity will be mercifully low, don't mistake "dry heat" for "safe to crush a 10K at noon." Your body still has to work overtime to cool itself. Physicians are already warning against exercising outdoors in these conditions during the peak heating of the day. If you insist on hitting the pavement or the Schuylkill River Trail, do it at dawn or dusk. And for the love of all that is holy, hydrate like your life depends on it, because it kind of does. Don't wait until you're thirsty.

### Storm Clouds on the Horizon?

Compounding the heat is a conflicting weather picture. While some forecasts show sunny conditions with low rain chances, the National Weather Service is indicating "Areas of Severe Thunderstorms and Excessive Rainfall Thursday." This isn't a drizzle; this is potentially dangerous weather. Keep an eye on the sky and your weather apps. If you hear thunder, it's time to bail. No PR is worth getting struck by lightning or caught in a flash flood.

### Local Hazards to Watch For

The good news: the notorious sinkhole on the Schuylkill River Trail between Race Street and JFK Boulevard was repaired back in December 2025. So that specific section is open. However, we've had at least five water main breaks reported across the city in the last 24 hours. These can turn sidewalks and streets into slippery, uneven messes, which is great for no one's knees or ankles. Be extra cautious on city streets, particularly around North Philadelphia and areas that have seen recent breaks.
