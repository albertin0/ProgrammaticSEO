## Today's Rundown: Breathe Easy? Maybe. Run Fast? Possibly.

Look, after a brutal stretch of heat and humidity that saw heat index values pushing into the upper 90s and even over 105 degrees in some areas, today's cooler temperature is a welcome relief. But that doesn't mean it's a completely clear shot for your workout. We're sitting at an Air Quality Index of 86, which is still in the "Moderate" zone. That means if you're prone to respiratory issues – think asthma, bronchitis, or just generally having lungs that aren't thrilled with city air – you might feel it. And yeah, moderate pollen isn't doing anyone any favors either.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Air Quality Alert: Keep an Eye (and Lung) Out.** With an AQI of 86 and ozone as the dominant pollutant, those of you with sensitive lungs should consider scaling back your intensity, especially if you're planning a longer session on a heavily trafficked route like parts of Kelly Drive. Wildfire smoke has also been an issue for Pennsylvania's air quality lately, so even if it's not the main culprit today, the overall air quality isn't stellar.
</AlertBox>

Weather-wise, while the morning looks "Mainly Clear," the forecast hints at "scattered thunderstorms" later today as a cold front approaches. Yesterday had its own bout of severe weather, so stay alert for changing conditions. No one wants to be caught in a downpour or worse during their tempo run, so if you see dark clouds rolling in, cut your losses and head inside.

### Upcoming Trail Changes: Fox Chase Lorimer Trail

Heads up for those who frequent Northeast Philly's trails: the Pine Road Bridge over the Fox Chase Lorimer Trail is slated for a full closure starting August 17th, running through Summer 2027. While the trail itself *should* remain open with "a few temporary closures during construction activities," that's still a headache for planning. Plan your routes accordingly in the coming weeks.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip: Hydration is Always Key.** Even with cooler temps, your body is still working hard. Don't skimp on the water. And if you're hitting the trails, tell someone where you're going and when you expect to be back. It's just smart running.
</AlertBox>
