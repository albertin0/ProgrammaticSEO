## The Heat Is On (And It's Nasty)

Forget the 13°C you might have seen floating around – that's some kind of cosmic joke. The actual temperature here in Philadelphia this morning is already 74°F (23°C), and it's climbing to a projected high of 91°F (33°C). But wait, there's more! Factor in the delightful humidity, and it’s going to *feel* like the mercury is pushing right up to 100°F (37.8°C) later today.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Hazard:** Temperatures this high, especially with the "feels like" factor, dramatically increase your risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. If you absolutely *must* be outside, avoid the peak heat hours, which are roughly 11 AM to 4 PM. Stay hydrated, wear light clothing, and for the love of all that's holy, listen to your body. Or just, you know, stay inside.
</AlertBox>

## Thunderstorms, Heavy Rain, and Lingering Flooding Risks

Yesterday brought torrential rain to the region, and while the flash flood warnings from Thursday night officially expired at midnight, the ground is likely still saturated. Today, the National Weather Service is forecasting "Areas of Severe Thunderstorms and Excessive Rainfall." "Scattered thunderstorms" are expected throughout the day, which can mean localized heavy rain and gusty winds popping up without much warning.

Remember that flash flooding from earlier this month shut down parts of Kelly Drive? With more rain on the way, those low-lying areas, and any underpasses or creek crossings, are going to be dicey. Getting caught in a severe storm out on the Schuylkill River Trail or in Fairmount Park isn't just inconvenient, it's dangerous with lightning, slippery surfaces, and potential debris.

## Air & Obstacles

On the air quality front, we're sitting at a "Moderate" AQI of 55, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. For most folks, this is "acceptable," but if you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, you might feel some irritation with prolonged exposure. Couple that with a "Moderate" pollen count, and your lungs aren't getting any favors.

Speaking of obstacles, the FIFA Fan Festival at Lemon Hill in East Fairmount Park is still in full swing until July 19th. This means Sedgely Drive and Poplar Street (at 30th Street) are shut down to traffic, and Waterworks Drive is closed daily from 10 AM. Don't count on parking or smooth sailing around that whole area if you were planning a run or ride there. There's also plenty of I-95 related construction impacting surface streets in Northeast Philadelphia, like Richmond Street and Castor Avenue, so watch out for lane closures and detours if your route takes you through there.

Seriously, find a treadmill, hit the gym, or get your sprints in before the sun really gets going and the storms roll in. Your body will thank you.
