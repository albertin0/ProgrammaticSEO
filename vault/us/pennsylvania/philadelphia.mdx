## Today's Rundown: What to Watch Out For

Alright, Philly, let's break down what's actually happening out there. The air is "Mainly Clear" and the thermometer is sitting at a comfortable 13°C (55°F) according to the latest data. That's fantastic news for the joints; no pavement-baking heat or bone-chilling cold to contend with.

However, your lungs might still feel a bit of a pinch. We're looking at a **Moderate Air Quality Index (AQI) of 81**, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant. For those with asthma or other respiratory sensitivities, this means taking it easy or opting for an indoor session might be a smart move. On top of that, pollen levels are also moderate. So, if you're prone to seasonal allergies, grab those tissues before you head out the door.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Moderate AQI and Pollen Alert
With an AQI of 81 (PM2.5) and moderate pollen counts, sensitive individuals might experience respiratory irritation. If you feel it, cut your workout short or consider moving it indoors.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Trouble & Water Woes

While the main arteries like Kelly Drive seem clear for your morning strides, don't get too complacent, especially if you're hitting the longer stretches of the Schuylkill River Trail (SRT). There's been ongoing construction on various parts of the SRT. For instance, a section in the Conshohocken/Spring Mill area was undergoing widening and repaving and was expected to conclude work by the "first or second week of August," which means some lingering work might still be a factor. Farther out, a two-mile stretch near Birdsboro remains closed indefinitely. Always check local signage before you commit to a long haul, because nobody likes an unexpected detour a few miles in.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Schuylkill River Trail (SRT) Check
While central sections of the SRT are generally fine, be aware of potential localized closures or construction, especially on segments outside of the immediate city center, like those near Conshohocken or Birdsboro.
</AlertBox>

And speaking of the Schuylkill, if your workout takes you near the water, keep in mind a recent report found "alarmingly high" fecal contamination in the Schuylkill, Delaware, Pennypack Creek, and Frankford Creek. I know we're runners, not swimmers, but it's a good reminder to avoid contact with the water and definitely don't let your dog take a dip.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Water Quality Hazard: Stay Out of the Rivers!
A recent study indicates "alarmingly high" fecal contamination in Philadelphia's waterways, including the Schuylkill River. Keep yourself and your pets out of the water to avoid potential health risks.
</AlertBox>
