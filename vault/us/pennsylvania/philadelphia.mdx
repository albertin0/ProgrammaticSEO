## The Rundown: What's Hitting You Today

Look, it's September, and the weather's finally deciding to be a grown-up and not a sweaty toddler. We've got a cool 13°C (that's about 55°F for you Celsius-challenged folks) and clear skies. Perfect running weather, right? Mostly.

The AQI is sitting at 55, which the official sources call "moderate" for PM2.5. What does that actually mean for your lungs? If you're generally healthy, you'll probably just feel a bit of extra crud in your throat if you're really pushing it. If you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, you know your body better than some government website, so use your head. Maybe keep that tempo run to Kelly Drive instead of hitting the Schuylkill River Trail where you might be sucking in more exhaust from I-76. The good news is, no major wildfire smoke plumes are choking us out like they did a few years back.

Pollen's still kicking around at a moderate level, mostly from weeds. If you're one of those people who looks like they've gone twelve rounds with a beehive after a run in the fall, pop your meds before you head out. It's not awful, but it's enough to make you regret forgetting your Claritin.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Bridge Work on South Street
Heads up if your route takes you near the South Street Bridge. There's some ongoing maintenance, mostly underneath, but traffic patterns are wonky and you might encounter some unexpected detours or slowdowns if you're trying to cross it on foot or bike. Keep an eye out for construction vehicles and don't assume drivers see you.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration Isn't Just for Summer
Just because it's not 90 degrees doesn't mean you can skip the water. That cool, clear air can still dehydrate you, especially on longer efforts. Stick a water bottle in your pocket or plan a route with a water fountain.
</AlertBox>

Overall, today's a solid day for logging some miles. Just be smart about where you're running, and listen to your body, especially if you're sensitive to that "moderate" air quality. Don't be a hero; nobody needs another "I pushed through it" story that ends in a trip to urgent care.
