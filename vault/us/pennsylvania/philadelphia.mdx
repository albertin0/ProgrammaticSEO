## Why today is/isn't the day

<BulletList>
*   **Tree Pollen is High:** If your sinuses are already staging a coup, today's heavy tree pollen count means you'll be running with a built-in booger factory. Thanks, spring.
*   **Slick City Streets & Trails:** Expect rain showers throughout the day, possibly moderate, along with some fog developing tonight. That means anything from greasy asphalt to treacherous mud on the trails. Your joints will thank you for taking it easy, or staying inside.
*   **Schuylkill River Trail Construction:** Still dealing with ongoing construction on parts of the Schuylkill River Trail, especially near Pottstown and areas with AT&T work. Expect detours and intermittent closures. Don't be a hero and try to sneak through a work zone.
</BulletList>

Look, the temperature's actually pretty mild, hovering in the low 40s to upper 40s Fahrenheit (around 6-9°C) with cloudy skies, which is decent. But the air itself, between the rain and the pollen, is basically a giant snot rag today.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heads up for those with allergies or asthma:** Today's high tree pollen is no joke. Pair that with "Moderate" air quality (AQI 55, driven by PM2.5), and your lungs might feel like you're running through a cottonwood snowstorm. Consider an indoor workout if you're sensitive. PM2.5, those tiny airborne particles, can really dig deep into your lungs and mess with your system. We've seen Code Orange alerts for this stuff when conditions are similar, like light winds and fog.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, if you *must* get out there, avoid the Schuylkill River Trail sections around Norristown, Conshohocken, and particularly the Industrial Highway in Pottstown, where the Keim Street Bridge construction is a persistent headache. You'll likely hit detours or outright closures that will just kill your flow.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Watch your step on *all* surfaces.** The combination of rain and potential fog means reduced visibility and slippery conditions. Roads, sidewalks, and especially trails like those in Wissahickon Valley Park, will be slick. Rocks and roots in places like Forbidden Drive will be extra hazardous. A sprained ankle or a nasty fall isn't worth bragging rights.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration isn't just for sunny days.** Even with the cooler temps and rain, your body still needs water, especially if your allergies are acting up. Also, dress in layers and make sure you're visible if you're out near traffic. Drivers already struggle in the rain and fog.
</AlertBox>
