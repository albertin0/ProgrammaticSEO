## The Rundown: Breathe Easy? Kinda.

Alright, folks, it’s May 12th, and honestly, the weather gods aren't actively trying to kill us, which is a win in my book. With a comfortable 55°F (13°C) and clear skies, you might actually enjoy that tempo run. Seriously, soak it in before summer hits and every run feels like slogging through a hot, wet towel.

However, don't get too complacent. The air quality is sitting at a "Moderate" 55 AQI, with PM2.5 being the main culprit. That's not terrible, but it's enough to irritate sensitive lungs, especially if you're pushing hard. Pair that with moderate pollen levels, and your seasonal allergies might decide today's the day to throw a party in your sinuses. My advice? If you're prone to asthma or allergies, pre-dose your meds. Don't be a hero.

## On the Pavement and Trails: Watch Your Step

### Construction Chaos

Philly is always under construction, and today is no different. While the major work on the Schuylkill River Trail extensions and the MLK Drive Bridge was largely wrapped up by 2025, there's always something else brewing.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Expect localized construction on sidewalks and streets. The Philadelphia Water Department has several "green stormwater infrastructure" projects kicking off in May 2026, especially in Tacony, Port Richmond, and Mayfair. If your route takes you through these neighborhoods, be prepared for detours or uneven surfaces. Don't trip over a newly dug trench trying to beat your PR.
</AlertBox>

Specifically, the Tacony Street Sidepath project in Northeast Philly is slated for completion this year, so you might still encounter active work zones if you're out that way. And let's not forget the perpetual I-95 reconstruction, which, while mostly affecting drivers, can still lead to unexpected lane shifts or closures impacting pedestrian access near major interchanges.

### Know Your Neighborhood

Today, for instance, the City is holding "Kensington Service Connections/Site Clean-ups" in various Kensington locations. If you're running or walking through the 800 block of East Allegheny Avenue, or near Kensington Avenue at Clearfield or Orleans Streets, just be aware. You might encounter more foot traffic, temporary obstructions, or cleaning crews. It's not a full-blown trail closure, but it means you need to be more alert than usual.

<AlertBox type="info">
Remember, even on a generally good weather day, conditions can change. Always carry your phone, tell someone your route, and for the love of all that is holy, look where you’re going. Those fresh potholes don't care about your Strava segment.
</AlertBox>
