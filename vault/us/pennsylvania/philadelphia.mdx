## Why today is decidedly *not* the day for PRs

<BulletList>
*   **It’s still brutally hot.** Yesterday hit a record-breaking 97 degrees. The National Weather Service says the heat and humidity are sticking around through today, with highs in the low to mid-90s and heat index values making it feel like the mid-to-upper 90s. This is a major risk for anyone active outside. Your body overheats quicker than you think.
*   **The air isn't doing you any favors.** We just had a Code Orange Air Quality Alert for ozone yesterday across Southeastern PA, including our city. While the AQI technically registers "Moderate" today, the context of that ozone alert, combined with the continuing high heat, means lung irritation is a real possibility, especially if you've got any respiratory issues.
*   **Pollen is just a moderate annoyance.** Not the main killer today, but it's another layer on top of everything else.
</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Seriously, Stay Indoors if You Can.
The combination of continued high heat and humidity, with lingering air quality concerns, makes strenuous outdoor exercise genuinely dangerous. Doctors were urging caution along Kelly Drive yesterday, and that advice holds true today. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are no joke. Hydrate like it's your job if you *must* be outside, and keep those workouts short and easy.
</AlertBox>

### What to Watch Out For on the Trails

If you're still determined to get some miles in, heed these warnings. The Schuylkill River Trail, especially sections north toward Norristown and Conshohocken, has ongoing construction. Expect detours and intermittent closures due to Keim Street Bridge construction in Pottstown, which will impact the trail through 2026. There's also a year-long detour on the Chester Valley Trail where it meets the Schuylkill River Trail in Norristown. Don't assume your usual route is clear or safe.

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Consider Shorter, Cooler Options
If you absolutely can't skip your run, head out at dawn or dusk, when temperatures are (slightly) lower. Stick to shaded areas. And maybe, just maybe, hit the treadmill or the weights instead of tempting fate with this heat. Your body will appreciate it more than you think.
</AlertBox>
