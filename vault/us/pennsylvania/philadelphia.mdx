### Joints & Trails: Proceed with Caution (and Frustration)

The biggest headache today, beyond your usual city traffic, is still the **FIFA Fan Festival** operations over at Lemon Hill in East Fairmount Park. This thing is ongoing until mid-July, and it's locking down huge chunks of what used to be prime running real estate.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Major Road Closures & Restricted Access around Lemon Hill / Benjamin Franklin Parkway:** If your route usually takes you through East Fairmount Park, particularly around Lemon Hill, Sedgely Drive, or Poplar Street (near 30th Street), expect significant detours and restricted access. Parking in the surrounding neighborhoods is also heavily restricted, requiring permits. Plan your loops elsewhere, or be prepared for an obstacle course. Kelly Drive is *mostly* open, but don't assume your usual turn-offs are clear.
</AlertBox>

And speaking of frustrating closures, that Schuylkill Crossing project, linking the Grays Ferry Crescent to Bartram's Mile, is still under construction. Don't count on that connection opening up anytime soon; they're still working on joining sections and pouring concrete. So if you were hoping for a seamless long run along that section of the Schuylkill River Trail, adjust your expectations.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Schuylkill Crossing Construction:** The new swing bridge south of the Grays Ferry Avenue Bridge remains an active construction zone, meaning that section of the Schuylkill River Trail is inaccessible. Find an alternate route or prepare for an out-and-back.
</AlertBox>

For those of you hitting the Wissahickon Valley Park, you're mostly in the clear from major events, but remember that ground-nesting wasps are an "occasional hazard" in the park. Keep your eyes peeled and listen to your body – literally. Also, Friends of the Wissahickon are piloting a Japanese knotweed control program in Spring 2026 at the Upper Valley Green Lot, so expect some activity in that area.

<AlertBox type="info">
**PT Tip: Listen to Your Body on Moderate Air Quality Days.** Even if you're not typically sensitive, a moderate AQI and pollen count can subtly increase respiratory effort. If you feel any unusual tightness, shortness of breath, or excessive fatigue, dial back the intensity or cut your run short. Pushing through isn't worth feeling like garbage tomorrow.
</AlertBox>

Overall, the weather is fantastic, but Philly's still got its usual urban challenges. Be smart, stay aware, and you'll still get a solid workout in.
