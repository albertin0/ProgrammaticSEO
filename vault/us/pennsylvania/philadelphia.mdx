## The Air You Breathe (or Don't)

Your lungs are getting a slight reprieve today, with an Air Quality Index (AQI) of 55, which is squarely in the "Moderate" zone, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. But don't get too comfortable. This time last week, we were under Code Red and even Code Purple alerts, with the city's air being "very unhealthy" due to dense Canadian wildfire smoke. Health officials were practically begging people to stay inside and put off "marathon training plans."

Adding to that, a massive fire ripped through South Philadelphia yesterday, sending heavy smoke into the air and closing streets. While the immediate danger from that blaze might be contained, "smoky conditions" were reported for residents nearby, and particulate matter hangs around longer than your good intentions. So, even with a "Moderate" AQI, sensitive groups should probably still keep their long, intense outdoor sessions brief or indoors.

"Moderate" pollen is just par for the course in July. For most of you iron lungs out there, it's probably not a deal-breaker, but if you're usually sniffling and wheezing after a run through Fairmount Park, grab your antihistamines. It's not *bad*, but it's not *zero*, either.

## Weather Whiplash & Water Woes

Right now, it's 13.0°C and "Mainly Clear"—a deceptively pleasant start to the day. But don't you dare step out thinking it'll last. The National Weather Service has issued a Flash Flood Watch for Philadelphia County, effective from 6 AM today through 2 AM tomorrow. We're looking at severe thunderstorms expected to roll in this afternoon and evening, bringing "damaging straight-line winds, blinding downpours," and "frequent lightning." The worst of it is expected between 2 PM and 8 PM. Seriously, folks, we've had enough microbursts and downed trees this month.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**IMMINENT THREAT: Severe Storms & Flash Flooding**
Heavy thunderstorms, damaging winds, and flash flooding are expected throughout Tuesday, especially from 2 PM to 8 PM. Avoid outdoor activities, especially on low-lying roads and trails, including sections of the Schuylkill River Trail, which are prone to flooding. "Turn Around, Don't Drown" is not just a catchy slogan; it's how you stay alive.
</AlertBox>

Speaking of water, the Schuylkill River's water quality was rated "Green" (suitable for contact) as of yesterday, with lower bacteria levels. But with all this rain coming down, run-off will definitely impact that, so probably best to just admire the river, not swim in it, for the next few days. High flows and poor visibility are no joke.

## Mind the Obstacles

If you're heading out to the trails along the river, be aware that the Falls Bridge has overnight *vehicle* closures from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. through Friday for inspection work. Good news for runners and cyclists, one sidewalk remains open. However, in Northeast Philadelphia, I-95 overhead bridge construction means daytime lane restrictions on Venango Street, Castor Avenue, Richmond Street, and Wheatsheaf Lane from 6 AM to 5 PM, Tuesday through Friday. Expect delays and intermittent traffic stops if you're driving through there.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip: Charge Your Devices**
City officials are urging residents to treat today like a day when the power might go out due to the severe storms. Charge your phones and portable batteries in advance. Don't be that person caught on the SRT with a dead phone and a sudden downpour.
</AlertBox>
