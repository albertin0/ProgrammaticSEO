### Air Quality: Don't Breathe Too Deep

Seriously, that AQI isn't a suggestion; it's a warning label. With PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and the seasonal advisory for ozone and wildfire smoke in full swing, your lungs are going to feel it. If you're susceptible to respiratory issues, or honestly, even if you're not, today is a prime candidate for an indoor workout. That 13.3°C (56°F) and clear sky might *look* inviting, but it's a trap. The air quality for Las Vegas is specifically rated "Poor" by AccuWeather for today, April 27th, meaning it's unhealthy for sensitive groups.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Danger: Lung-Buster Air!** An AQI of 155 means "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups." Limit prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors. If you feel any symptoms like coughing, shortness of breath, or throat irritation, get inside immediately.
</AlertBox>

### Wind & Weather: Not the Worst, But Not Helping

The winds for the Las Vegas Valley today are forecast to be a breezy 5 to 15 mph from the southwest. That's not enough to knock you over, but it *can* stir up more of that fine particulate matter that's already making our air quality less than ideal. Temperatures are mild and pleasant, ranging from the mid-50s to mid-70s. So, your joints will be happy, but your lungs? Not so much.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Heads Up: Road Construction Around Harry Reid Airport!** If you're heading out to hit the trails near the Airport Connector, like some routes that branch off towards the Wetlands Park area, expect lane restrictions on the southbound Airport Connector and the ramp to eastbound I-215. This closure is continuous from Sunday night, April 26, until 5 a.m. Thursday, April 30. Plan your routes accordingly, or just avoid that mess entirely.

### Trail Talk: Red Rock Canyon

If you're still determined to get some miles in, and you're thinking about Red Rock Canyon, be aware of the air quality. While the scenic loop and main hiking trails are open, the air will still be the same air, just with a prettier view. Remember, the northern areas of the Spring Mountains, north of Red Rock Canyon, still have long-standing closures from the Carpenter 1 fire. So, stick to the well-trafficked and clearly open areas.
