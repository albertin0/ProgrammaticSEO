<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat is a Real Danger.** Tucson already hit 100 degrees on March 19th, a record for the earliest triple-digit day. We're still in the thick of this unseasonably hot stretch. Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are no joke. Drink way more water than you think you need, wear light-colored, loose clothing, and *do not* leave kids or pets in your car, not even for a minute. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get to shade and cool down immediately, or call 911.

Alright, let's talk about hitting the pavement or dirt today. Given the current Extreme Heat Warning still active for Pima County through today, this isn't the weekend to set a new personal best in the middle of the day. The forecast suggests highs pushing into the high 90s, possibly even 99°F (37°C), following a week where we've already seen 100°F. Your body simply isn't ready for this kind of furnace in March. Stick to dawn patrol or twilight trots if you absolutely must be outside.

For those of you who swear by **The Chuck Huckelberry Loop**, listen up. There's still a section south of Sunset Road, near the Santa Cruz River and I-10, that's closed through the end of 2025 for new pathway construction. Don't get caught off guard trying to navigate a construction zone on your long run. Also, the **22nd Street Bridge** project means the Aviation Bikeway is closed both north and south of the bridge, starting March 3rd. This isn't just a minor detour; it's a significant disruption for anyone planning to use that corridor. Plan your routes, folks, or you'll be adding unintended miles on sketchy detours.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Local Trail & Bikeway Disruptions:** The 22nd Street Bridge project has shut down sections of the Aviation Bikeway. Additionally, Maeveen Behan Desert Sanctuary Trails Park has intermittent closures through March 2026 due to irrigation line replacement for Arthur Pack Park. Don't assume your usual path is open; check local alerts before you head out, or you'll be turning around frustrated.
</AlertBox>

Air quality, at an AQI of 50, is generally good, so your lungs get a bit of a break there, thank goodness. We're not currently dealing with local wildfire smoke, unlike some past years. But that moderate pollen level? It's enough to make a decent workout feel like you're sucking air through a dryer sheet if you're sensitive. So, if you insist on being out there, hydrate like your life depends on it (because today, it might), take it easy, and maybe stick to the shadier, less exposed areas. Save the heroics for a day when Mother Nature isn't actively trying to bake you.
