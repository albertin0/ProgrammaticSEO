It's July in Phoenix, so unless your idea of a "workout" is meticulously monitoring your core temperature from inside an air-conditioned gym, stay the hell inside. Seriously.

We're talking "don't leave your dog on the pavement" hot, but applied to your own body. A hiker was found dead on South Mountain just this past weekend, and police are still investigating if heat was a factor. That should tell you everything you need to know.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning: Deadly Conditions
The National Weather Service has issued an Extreme Heat Warning, effective through at least August 2nd. Temperatures are not just hot, they are *dangerously* hot, with afternoon highs expected to remain well above 110°F (43°C), possibly touching 120°F (49°C) in some areas. Heat-related illnesses like heat exhaustion and heat stroke are a real, present, and potentially fatal risk. Do NOT attempt strenuous outdoor activity during the daytime.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Mandatory Trail Closures & Strong Discouragement
The City of Phoenix has implemented its Trail Heat Safety Program. This means trails on Camelback Mountain, Piestewa Peak, and large portions of South Mountain are CLOSED from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Furthermore, the city *strongly discourages* hiking on **all** Phoenix trails during these hours. Listen to them. They're trying to prevent you from becoming another statistic.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### If You *Must* Move (Indoors, Preferably)
If you absolutely can't skip your workout, keep it indoors. Hit the treadmill, find a spin class, or dive into a pool. For those stubborn enough to venture out before dawn or after dusk (and we know who you are), prioritize hydration like your life depends on it (because it does). Drink water constantly, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, and immediately head inside if you feel *any* symptoms of heat illness. Know the signs: confusion, dizziness, nausea, headache, rapid pulse. This isn't a game.
</AlertBox>
