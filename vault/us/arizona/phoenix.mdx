Look, I get it. You live in Phoenix; you think you're built different. But 101 degrees is not a joke, especially not when the sun's high. The City of Phoenix isn't closing down parts of Camelback Mountain, like the Echo Canyon Trail, between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. because they enjoy spoiling your fun. They do it because people *die* out there from heat exposure. This isn't just about feeling a little sweaty; this is about heatstroke, dehydration, and making some poor first responder haul your cooked carcass off a mountain.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning: Stay Indoors, Especially Mid-Day!
Temperatures pushing past 100°F are dangerous. Limit outdoor activity to early morning or late evening, if at all. Hydrate excessively, and for Pete's sake, wear appropriate light-colored clothing. Ignoring this is just asking for trouble. Seriously, a casual stroll can turn into a critical incident fast.
</AlertBox>

And if the heat isn't enough to scare you, how about a good old-fashioned monsoon-style downpour? We've got a Flood Watch slapped on Phoenix Metro and parts of Maricopa County starting this evening and lasting through Sunday night. That means those perfectly dry washes you planned to run through? Yeah, they can turn into raging torrents in minutes. Yesterday saw flash flood warnings and water rescues, so this isn't some distant threat. If you see dark clouds, or if it starts drizzling, get out of low-lying areas and avoid crossing any flooded roads. "Turn around, don't drown" isn't just a catchy slogan; it's how you stay alive.
