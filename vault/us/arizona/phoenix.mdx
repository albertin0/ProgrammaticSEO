### Extreme Heat Warning & Trail Closures IN EFFECT
An Extreme Heat Warning is active for the Phoenix metro area until at least Tuesday. Afternoon highs will easily exceed 105°F. This isn't a suggestion; it's a life-or-death scenario if you're out pushing it during the middle of the day.

**Crucially, hiking trails are CLOSED.** That means if you planned to hit Camelback Mountain's Echo or Cholla Trails, or Piestewa Peak, forget about it between 8 AM and 5 PM. Ignoring these closures puts both you and our already stretched emergency services at serious risk. These rules are in place because people die out there when they don't listen.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Ozone High Pollution Advisory
Beyond the heat, we've got an Ozone High Pollution Advisory. For those with respiratory issues, kids, or older adults, this means avoiding outdoor activity altogether. Even if you're a healthy runner, you should reduce the intensity and duration of any outdoor movement. You don't want to be huffing this stuff deeper into your lungs than necessary.
</AlertBox>

### What to Do (Or Not Do)

Today is not the day to find out how tough you are by running 10 miles at noon. Your body takes months to acclimate to this kind of heat, and we're getting it early this year.

*   **Early Bird Gets the (Slightly Less Hot) Worm:** If you absolutely *must* get outside, aim for before sunrise. We're talking 4 AM if you want to avoid the worst of it, but even then, temps are climbing fast. Hydrate *before* you even step out.
*   **Indoor Alternatives are Your Friend:** This is the perfect day for the gym, a spin class, or literally anything with air conditioning. There are plenty of options in town, so no excuses for heatstroke.
*   **Road Closures to Navigate:** As if the heat wasn't enough, ADOT has several freeway restrictions and closures this weekend, including sections of I-10, SR 51, and Loop 101. If you're driving to any remaining open park areas, plan for delays and use alternate routes. This might not directly impact your workout *safety* but it will impact your sanity.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration is Non-Negotiable
You should be drinking water constantly today, even if you're indoors. If you venture out for any reason, carry more water than you think you need. Dehydration leads to heat exhaustion, which can quickly turn into heat stroke. Know the signs: headaches, dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating, or conversely, warm, dry skin if it's progressed to heat stroke. If you feel any of these, stop immediately, find shade, and cool down. Call 911 if it's serious.
</AlertBox>

Look, I get it. We live here for the outdoor life. But sometimes, the desert demands respect, and today is one of those days. Listen to the warnings, stay safe, and save the heroics for a day that doesn't feel like the surface of the sun.
