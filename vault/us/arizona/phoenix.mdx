### Heat Stroke Isn't a Medal, It's a Hospital Visit

Let's not mince words: it's a scorcher out there. The National Weather Service has issued an Extreme Heat Warning for our neck of the woods, active until Tuesday evening, with daytime temperatures soaring between 110 to 118°F (43 to 48°C). This isn't just "uncomfortable," folks; this is "major to extreme heat risk." We're in a record-breaking stretch, and the worst part? There's virtually no relief when the sun goes down, meaning your body can't recover.

Seriously, Maricopa County has already confirmed 40 heat-related deaths this year as of August 1st. Don't become a statistic because you *really* wanted to get your miles in.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning in Effect!** Do NOT engage in strenuous outdoor activity between 8 AM and 8 PM. Reschedule your workouts to indoor, air-conditioned environments, or postpone them entirely. This isn't a suggestion; it's a life-saving directive.
</AlertBox>

### Trail Closures and Air You Can Practically Chew

Those early morning runs aren't much better. The City of Phoenix has enacted its Trail Heat Safety Program, which means popular spots like the trails around Papago Park are restricted during the peak heat hours. They do this for a reason – mountain rescues in this heat are no joke for first responders.

And your lungs? While the overall AQI is moderate at 66 with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, we're under an Ozone High Pollution Advisory. The Arizona Department of Environmental Quality anticipates peak ozone levels to be in the upper moderate range, pushing close to "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" through Monday. So, you'll be breathing in more than just hot air. Add a moderate pollen count on top of that, and anyone with allergies or asthma is in for a rough time.

### Your PT Advice: Stay Indoors, Hydrate Smart

I'm a physical therapist, and my advice today is simple: stay inside. Go to a gym, hit a treadmill, or do some bodyweight work in your air-conditioned living room. If you choose to ignore all warnings and step foot outside for a workout, you better be starting before sunrise.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration is NOT a joke.** If you *do* brave the outdoors, drink water *before*, *during*, and *after* your activity. We're talking more than you think you need. Wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing. Protect your head. Tell someone where you're going and when you expect to be back. And for the love of all that is holy, know the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke.
</AlertBox>

Oh, and if you're driving to one of those early morning spots, plan for extra time. ADOT has freeway closures and restrictions all over the place this weekend, including sections of Loop 101, Loop 202, and SR 51 for various improvement projects. Just Phoenix being Phoenix.
