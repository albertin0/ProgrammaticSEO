For most people, you might not notice it, but if you're sensitive, or if you're really pushing your cardio, those fine particulates can irritate your respiratory system. Couple that with a moderate pollen count, and if you suffer from seasonal allergies, prepare for some extra sneezing and congestion. It’s enough to make you consider that treadmill.

<AlertBox type="warning">

## Smart Moves for a Sweltering City

Look, I get it. You want to move. But today is not the day for heroics on the trails. If you absolutely *must* get out, do it before the sun really hits its stride, or wait until well after it's gone down. And even then, with 100+ degree days, the pavement radiates heat for hours.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Phoenix Pro Tips for Surviving Today:**
*   **Hydrate, Hydrate, Hydrate:** And then hydrate some more. Water isn't enough; electrolytes are your friend.
*   **Early Bird or Night Owl:** Plan your runs for the absolute coolest parts of the day – before 6 AM or after 8 PM.
*   **Stick to Flat & Shaded:** Forget the mountain trails (they're probably closed anyway during the day). Opt for shorter, flatter routes in shaded parks, if you can find them. The Arizona Canal path might offer some relief *early*, but it's still largely exposed.
*   **Listen to Your Body:** If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or excessively fatigued, stop immediately. Heat stroke is a medical emergency. Don't risk it.
</AlertBox>
