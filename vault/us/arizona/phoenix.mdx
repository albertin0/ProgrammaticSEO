Today is *not* the day to push yourself outside unless you're actively trying to end up in an ER or become another heat-related statistic.

<LungsJointsScore score={2} />

<BulletList>
*   **Extreme, Dangerous Heat:** We're staring down 38.7°C (101.7°F) with forecasts indicating highs around 101-102°F. Even if the official "Extreme Heat Warning" from earlier this week theoretically expired, the conditions are still absolutely brutal and pose a major health risk.
*   **Trail Closures:** Don't even *think* about hiking Camelback Mountain, Piestewa Peak, or South Mountain's restricted trails during the daylight hours. The city's Trail Heat Safety Program shuts these down specifically to prevent heat-related emergencies and mountain rescues when it's this hot. These aren't suggestions; they're for your safety and to keep our first responders from risking their own lives to get your heat-stroked self off a trail.
*   **Moderate Air Quality:** The AQI is clocking in at 55, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. While not "unhealthy" for everyone, sensitive groups will feel it, and combined with the heat, it's just another reason to stay indoors.
*   **Moderate Pollen:** Ragweed, Mulberry, and Grass are all hanging out at moderate levels. If your allergies are already acting up from the air quality, this certainly won't help your lungs or sinuses.
</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: Extreme Heat Conditions!
Seriously, Phoenix, it's over 100°F out there right now, and it's only going to get worse during peak sun. This isn't just "uncomfortable," it's life-threatening. The risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke is sky-high. Heat has contributed to at least 136 deaths in Maricopa County this year, with hundreds more under investigation. Your outdoor workout can wait. If you *must* be outside, keep it to the absolute earliest hours, stick to shaded areas, and hydrate like your life depends on it (because it does).
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality and Pollen Advisory
With an AQI of 55 and moderate PM2.5, folks with respiratory issues, asthma, or other lung conditions should limit prolonged outdoor exertion. Throw in moderate pollen levels, and you're asking for trouble. Keep your inhaler handy if you absolutely cannot avoid being outside, but frankly, today is a prime day for indoor cardio.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Smart Choices for a Scorcher
If you're still determined to move your body, consider hitting a gym, an air-conditioned mall for walking laps, or getting in some laps at an indoor pool. If you insist on outdoor activity, aim for before 7 AM or after 7 PM. Even then, listen to your body, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing, and carry way more water than you think you need. Make sure someone knows your route and when you expect to be back. And remember: if you feel dizzy, nauseous, or stop sweating, get inside immediately and cool down. This isn't a game.
</AlertBox>
