### AQI: Pay Attention, Sensitive Breathers
The AQI at 55, driven by PM2.5, is on the higher end of "Moderate." While some sources say air quality is "good", other reports indicate "Fair" conditions where sensitive groups may experience minor to moderate symptoms. If you have asthma, COPD, or other respiratory sensitivities, today is *not* the day to push for a PR on the **Camelback Mountain** summit. Keep your workout indoors or choose a less strenuous activity if you insist on being outside. Breathing in elevated PM2.5, even if it's not wildfire smoke, isn't doing your long-term lung health any favors.
</AlertBox>

But let's talk about that ASU incident. A college athlete in the 80s, collapsed with heat stroke. That tells you that heat isn't just a concern when the NWS drops an "Extreme Heat Warning."

The official temperature is 28.7°C (around 83.7°F) with overcast skies. That's still plenty warm to cause problems if you're not properly hydrated or you're pushing too hard. That overcast cover isn't a force field against heat illness.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Your Body Isn't a Machine (Unless it's a Malfunctioning One)
Even with the clouds, 83.7°F is warm, especially if you're engaging in high-intensity exercise. Remember Matthew Mayich. Drink more water than you think you need, especially before you head out. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or suddenly get goosebumps, stop. Find shade, cool down, and don't be a hero. Your ego isn't worth an ER visit or, worse, requiring a mountain rescue team to haul your stubborn self off a trail.
</AlertBox>
