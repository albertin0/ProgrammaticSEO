<BulletList>
*   **Heat is still a major player:** While the morning started overcast and relatively mild at 83°F, the forecast high for Sunday is still projected to hit 103°F. ## Don't Let "Overcast" Be Your Downfall, Especially on the Trails

Look, I get it. Overcast days in Phoenix in June feel like a gift from the heavens. You might think, "Oh, it's cooler, I can tackle that summit on South Mountain now!" WRONG. The ambient temperature is still going to climb to 103°F later today. Just because the sun is hiding doesn't mean the heat isn't there, radiating off every rock and patch of asphalt.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### The "No Official Warning" Trap
The National Weather Service isn't issuing an "Extreme Heat Warning" for Phoenix today, which means popular spots like Echo Canyon and Cholla trails on Camelback, Piestewa Peak, and some trails on South Mountain (Holbert, Mormon, Hau'pal Loop) aren't under mandatory 8 AM-5 PM closures. *However*, this is a technicality. One hundred three degrees Fahrenheit is still dangerously hot for sustained outdoor activity. Don't be a hero.
</AlertBox>

Phoenix fire officials have repeatedly warned about the uptick in mountain rescues, emphasizing that hikers often underestimate the heat and terrain. They've installed all sorts of safety measures – electronic gates, signage, ice machines – but people still push it. Don't be one of those statistics.

With the AQI at a moderate 67 and PM2.5 as the main culprit, those of you with any kind of respiratory sensitivity should really take it easy. Add moderate pollen to the mix, and your lungs are already working harder before you even start sweating. Breezy conditions, with gusts up to 25-30 mph, aren't just annoying; they can kick up dust and allergens, making that "moderate" AQI feel a lot worse.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Run Early, Run Smart (or Just Don't)
If you absolutely *must* get out there, do it at sunrise, hydrate like your life depends on it (because it does), and listen to your body. Seriously, this isn't a badge of honor. A treadmill or the gym's A/C is always an option. If you're planning a long run, stick to routes with shade or water access, like parts of the Arizona Canal Path, but even then, be cautious. That freeway construction on Loop 101 isn't affecting trails directly, but it means you'll hit traffic getting to your trailhead if you don't plan.
</AlertBox>
