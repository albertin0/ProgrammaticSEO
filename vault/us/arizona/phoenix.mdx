Today is an objectively terrible day to be outside for any significant length of time, unless your goal is heatstroke.

<LungsJointsScore score={3} />

<BulletList>
*   Heat is the main event today, folks. We're looking at near 39°C (that's 102°F for those not on the metric system yet), which is firmly in "stay indoors" territory. Your body simply cannot cool itself efficiently when the ambient temperature is higher than your skin temperature.
*   AQI is sitting at 55, which is "Moderate." It's not *terrible*, but with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, you'll still be breathing in fine particulate matter. Combine that with gasping for air in the heat, and your lungs won't be thrilled.
*   Pollen is moderate, another layer of "meh" for your respiratory system.
</BulletList>

Look, I love hitting the trails around Camelback Mountain as much as the next masochist, but today is not the day to try for that PR on Echo Canyon. Trying to push yourself outside right now is just asking for trouble.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Extreme Heat Warning!
Seriously, I'm not kidding. The National Weather Service issued an Excessive Heat Warning for the Phoenix area, effective from this morning through Friday evening. Daily temperatures are expected to soar, hitting up to 109 degrees Fahrenheit (42.8°C) in some areas. Working out during these hours dramatically increases your risk of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. People have died from this, people. This isn't a joke or a challenge to conquer. Stay hydrated, but more importantly, stay *inside*. If you must be outside, keep it to extremely short bursts, early morning, and stick to shaded areas, which are few and far between on most Phoenix trails.
</AlertBox>

The Maricopa County Department of Public Health is even warning people to avoid outdoor activities between 10 AM and 6 PM. That's basically prime running hours for anyone not waking up at 4 AM. Even then, the heat is already building.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Air Quality Still Not Great
While an AQI of 55 isn't "red alert," it's still "Moderate" with PM2.5 being the primary offender. This means people who are unusually sensitive to air pollution should consider reducing prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors. Given the heat, everyone's "sensitivity" goes up because your respiratory system is already working overtime. Do yourself a favor and hit the treadmill if you absolutely *have* to get a workout in.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate, Hydrate, Hydrate (and Electrolytes!)
If you insist on being outdoors for even short periods, proper hydration is non-negotiable. Don't just drink water; you need electrolytes to replace what you're sweating out. And for the love of all that is holy, wear light-colored, loose-fitting clothing. Even better, just don't go out.
</AlertBox>
