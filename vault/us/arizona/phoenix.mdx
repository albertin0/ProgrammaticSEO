Look, we survived the earliest *ever* March 100-degree days just last week, which even forced trail closures on spots like Camelback Mountain. That's wild for March, and frankly, a bit of an early warning for the summer to come. While we're not hitting those extreme warning levels today, don't get complacent.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Don't Be a Desert Dummy: Hydration is King!
Even at 80 degrees, the desert sun (even through clouds) will sap you dry. If you plan on tackling anything more challenging than your mailbox, like the trails on South Mountain, carry way more water than you think you need. Pre-hydrate, during-hydrate, post-hydrate. This isn't a suggestion; it's survival.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### Mind Your Air, Literally
An AQI of 65 isn't shutting down the city, but with PM2.5 being the primary pollutant and all that recent blowing dust, anyone with respiratory issues (or even just an aversion to gritty lungs) should consider a mask, especially if you're really pushing the pace. This time of year, with wildfire season ramping up (state officials just rolled out new AI cameras to spot fires, so you know it's on their mind), air quality can turn on a dime.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Stick to the Cooler Hours
The City of Phoenix has a trail safety program for a reason, closing popular routes when it gets too hot. Today might be "cooler" but 80°F is still plenty warm for heat exhaustion if you're out mid-day. Get your run in before 8 AM or after 6 PM. Your body hasn't fully acclimated to this kind of heat yet, no matter how tough you think you are.
</AlertBox>

So, yeah, you *can* go outside today. Just don't be shocked if you're coughing up a dust bunny and feeling a little crispy by the time you're done.
