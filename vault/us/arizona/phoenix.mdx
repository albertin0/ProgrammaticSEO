Today is absolutely *not* the day to push your limits outdoors unless you've got a death wish or a really good air-conditioned treadmill. Seriously, stay inside.

<BulletList>
*   **Extreme Heat Warning:** The National Weather Service has an Extreme Heat Warning in effect for the entire Phoenix Metro area until 8 PM MST tonight. Afternoon temperatures are expected to climb to a brutal 104 to 110 degrees Fahrenheit, posing a major heat risk. This isn't "hot for Phoenix," this is "dangerously hot" even for us desert dwellers.
*   **Trail Closures in Effect:** Don't even think about hitting the mountain trails during the day. Popular spots like the Echo Canyon and Cholla trails on Camelback Mountain, along with the Piestewa Peak Summit trail and several routes on South Mountain, are officially closed from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. due to the extreme heat. These closures are there to protect you and, frankly, the first responders who'd have to haul your dehydrated carcass off the mountain.
*   **Moderate Pollen:** While your lungs might not be battling bad air quality today (AQI is good at 47), moderate pollen levels mean your allergies might still try to kick you when you're already down from the heat. Don't add sneezing fits to your list of heat-related miseries.
</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
### DANGER: EXTREME HEAT IS A KILLER
This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; this is about severe heat exhaustion and potentially fatal heat stroke. Temperatures in the 100s, especially at 104-110°F, can cause your core body temperature to rise rapidly. Your body can't cool itself effectively in this kind of dry heat, no matter how much water you drink. Reschedule any strenuous outdoor activity to another day, or seriously, hit the gym.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
### If You Absolutely MUST Go Outside (Don't Say I Didn't Warn You)
If you're one of those stubborn types, or your job forces you outside, here's the drill:
*   **Early Bird Gets the Worm (and avoids heatstroke):** Get your activity done before 8 AM. Seriously, set that alarm.
*   **Hydrate Relentlessly:** Drink water *before*, *during*, and *after* any outdoor exposure. We're talking more than you think you need. Avoid sugary drinks, alcohol, and caffeine.
*   **Dress the Part:** Loose-fitting, light-colored, moisture-wicking clothing and a wide-brimmed hat are non-negotiable.
*   **Know the Signs:** Dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating, a weak or rapid pulse, and muscle cramps are signs of heat exhaustion. If you stop sweating but your skin is warm and dry, or you feel confused, that's heat stroke—call 911 immediately. And don't forget your pets; leave them home.
</AlertBox>
