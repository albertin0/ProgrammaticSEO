Look, I'm usually the first one to tell you to just stay home and foam roll, but today isn't one of those days. We're sitting at a comfortable 19.7°C (that's about 67.5°F for you Celsius-averse folks) and the sky is *mainly clear*. What is this, paradise? No, it's Phoenix, so don't get too comfortable, but soak it up while you can.

The air quality is actually good, with an AQI of 38. That's miles better than the "unhealthy for sensitive groups" we saw earlier this year in July, especially around downtown and the west side, when dust clouds were practically a local landmark. Today, the dominant pollutant is Ozone/Other, but at this level, your lungs won't be staging a protest halfway up Piestewa Peak.

Now, about that pollen. It's still in the "Moderate" range. If you're sensitive, you might feel it, but for most of us, it's just background noise. Don't go rolling through a field of ragweed, but your usual run should be fine.

<AlertBox type="warning">
Listen up, because this is important: Just yesterday, October 12, the Valley saw flash floods that required rescues. And on October 10, we had scattered showers, dust storms, and wind gusts up to 45 mph. While the rain seems to have largely cleared today, it means that trails, especially those low-lying paths and underpasses along washes, could still be muddy, slick, or have debris. Don't be that person who ignores obvious signs of a washout. Exercise caution, particularly if you're hitting spots like the trails around South Mountain Park and Preserve where washes frequently cross paths.
</AlertBox>

Speaking of trails, the good news is that with temperatures firmly in the 60s, you won't be dealing with any extreme heat-related trail closures. The city's Trail Heat Safety Program, which shuts down popular routes on Camelback Mountain and parts of South Mountain when it hits 105°F+, is thankfully not in effect today. We've had enough of those this year already.

<AlertBox type="info">
Remember, even on a perfect day, the sun here is no joke. Hydrate before you go, carry water, and tell someone where you're headed, especially if you're tackling any of our mountain trails.
</AlertBox>

Finally, if you're driving to your workout spot, be aware that ADOT just started the third leg of the I-10 widening project between Loop 202 and the Gila River yesterday, October 12. While the major *weekend* freeway closures on Loop 101, Loop 202, and SR 51 from Oct 9-12 are now officially over, ongoing construction means you might still encounter general traffic delays. Plan your commute accordingly.
