## Phoenix, You're Too Hot for Your Own Good

Seriously, Phoenix, it’s May. This unseasonably brutal heat is already here, and it's not messing around. We've been getting these Extreme Heat Warnings for a while now, and today is no different. Trying to push it on the trails right now is less about fitness and more about tempting fate. Your body hasn't fully acclimatized to this kind of consistent oven-like punishment.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Extreme Heat Warning in Effect!** Do not, I repeat, **DO NOT** attempt strenuous outdoor activity between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. today. The National Weather Service has issued a Major Heat Risk, and that's not a suggestion, it's a dire warning. We saw trail closures at places like Camelback Mountain and parts of South Mountain earlier this year for far less heat than this. Assume similar risks apply *everywhere* unless you're literally just walking to your air-conditioned car.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
  **Ozone Pollution Advisory.** That "Moderate" AQI with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant might sound okay, but with the specific ozone advisory, it means your lungs are going to be working overtime. If you have asthma, COPD, or just enjoy breathing without a wheeze, keep your sweat session indoors.
</AlertBox>

## What Not to Do, You Idiots

Look, if you insist on moving your body outside (and I'm judging you), make it short, make it early, and make it flat. Forget trying to conquer Piestewa Peak right now; those trails are practically a death trap in this heat, and the city actually restricts them during these warnings for good reason. Hydrate like your life depends on it (because it does), and wear clothes so light they practically evaporate. And for the love of all that is holy, tell someone where you're going and when you expect to be back. The last thing our overstressed fire rescue crews need is another heat-related emergency to pull you off a mountain.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **Early Bird Catches the... Less Horrible Conditions.** If you absolutely must get out there, aim for before 6 AM. The slight chance of isolated dry thunderstorms later today, though minimal, means lightning is always a consideration. Also, keep an eye on those road construction projects. While ADOT says no full freeway closures for Memorial Day weekend, there are still existing work zones on the Loop 202 and Loop 101 that can cause delays and frustration even if you're just trying to get to a different running spot.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, find a treadmill. Or a pool. A really, *really* cold pool.
