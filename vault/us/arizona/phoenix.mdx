<AlertBox type="danger">
  ### DANGER: Early Heat Wave Incoming!
  Seriously, I cannot stress this enough: what's coming is not normal for March. Doctors at Valleywise Health are already warning that our bodies aren't ready for this kind of early heat, especially if you're not a lifer here. The National Weather Service is forecasting widespread minor heat risks through the weekend and moderate risks next week, with temperatures likely hitting the 90s, and then *surpassing 100 degrees* as early as March 17. This could be the earliest triple-digit day on record for Phoenix.
</AlertBox>

When that heat hits, trails like those on Camelback Mountain (Echo Canyon and Cholla, specifically) and Piestewa Peak Summit will be under their seasonal heat restrictions, closing from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Extreme Heat Warning days. Don't be "that person" who ignores the signs and puts our first responders at risk. They've got enough to do without hauling your heat-stroked self off a mountain because you thought you were invincible.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ### Construction Ahead
  It's Phoenix, so construction is basically another season. While no major workout-specific closures for *today* were flagged in the news, ADOT has several freeway projects planned for 2026, including work on Loop 303, Loop 101, and the I-10/Loop 101 interchange. A pavement improvement project on State Route 51 is also kicking off this weekend (March 13-16), so keep an eye on road closures if your route takes you near that area. Always check your commute before you head out.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  ### Smart Runner Tips for the Coming Heat
  *   **Hydrate now, and then hydrate more.** Start pre-hydrating for the weekend.
  *   **Shift your schedule.** Once temperatures climb, get your runs in at dawn or dusk, not midday.
  *   **Know the signs.** Headaches, dizziness, nausea? Get to shade, drink water, and cool down immediately. Don't be a hero.
  *   **Tell someone your route.** Especially if you're hitting trails.
  *   **Check trail closures.** Phoenix Parks and Rec is serious about heat safety. Don't mess around with closed trails.
</AlertBox>

Enjoy this little reprieve, Phoenix. It's not going to last.
