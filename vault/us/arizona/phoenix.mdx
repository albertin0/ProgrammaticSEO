## Today's Outdoor Conditions: Rain, Floods, and Questionable Air

Alright, Phoenix, let's cut to the chase. Monday, September 28, 2026, is not your day to set any records on the trails. Unless, of course, your record is for "most waterlogged run before calling emergency services."

We've got a **Weather Impact Alert** in effect for widespread rain and thunderstorms through today and into Tuesday. This isn't just a sprinkle; we're talking about strong thunderstorms capable of hail, gusty winds, lightning, and significant flash flooding. The National Weather Service is saying we're at a "moderate risk" for excessive rainfall, meaning low water crossings and washes are going to be absolutely churning. Multiple rounds of storms are expected to "train" over the same areas, which means if one wave doesn't flood you out, the next one will.

<AlertBox type="danger">
  **Flash Flood Warning!** Seriously, stay away from washes, low-lying areas, and any trails that cross them. This is not a drill. What looks like a trickle can become a raging torrent in minutes, and we're seeing "moderate risk" for excessive rainfall today.
</AlertBox>

On the bright side, the temperature is actually pretty pleasant, hovering around 25.6°C (78°F) with overcast skies. That's a nice change from the usual September inferno, but it's a direct result of the storm system. Don't let the cooler air lull you into a false sense of security.

As for the air, your lungs aren't getting a gold star either. While the AQI is listed as 54, which is "Moderate," the dominant pollutant is PM2.5. This fine particulate matter can mess with your lungs and bloodstream, especially if you're sensitive. Plus, AccuWeather is still labeling today's air quality as "Poor" and "unhealthy for sensitive groups." So, if you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, seriously consider the treadmill.

### Trail Closures & Other Annoyances

Good news, for once: since we're not dealing with extreme heat today, those typical heat-related trail closures for places like Camelback Mountain and South Mountain are likely *not* in effect. However, the rain and flooding could effectively close trails regardless. Even Piestewa Peak, which usually extends its Summit Trail hours until 9 p.m. this time of year, is not going to be a fun climb when it's slick, muddy, and covered in lightning.
