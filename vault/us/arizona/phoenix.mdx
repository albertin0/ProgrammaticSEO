## Current Conditions & Hazards

### Weather Whiplash (But Don't Get Complacent)

Look, I know we've been roasting. Just a few days ago, Phoenix was hitting unprecedented triple-digit temperatures for March, shattering records and prompting "Extreme Heat Warnings" across the Valley from March 19-22, with some warnings extending through Sunday, March 23. Our current 25.3°C (77.5°F) and overcast conditions feel downright pleasant in comparison, but let’s not pretend we're out of the woods. This is still Phoenix in late March, and conditions can change faster than a jackrabbit on the I-10. Always hydrate like you're training for Badwater, and tell someone where you're going.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  Even with the current cooler temp, remember the ground absorbed days of record-breaking heat. Dehydration is still a major risk, and many popular trails were *just* under mandatory closure. Check official city sources before heading out, especially to spots like **Camelback Mountain** or **Piestewa Peak**. The City of Phoenix proactively closes trails like Echo Canyon and Cholla on Camelback, and the Piestewa Peak Summit trail from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. during extreme heat warnings for a reason.
</AlertBox>

### Air Quality & Pollen Report

Today’s AQI of 51 is "Moderate." This means that while it's generally acceptable for most, those with respiratory sensitivities or unusual sensitivity to air pollution should probably dial back their intensity. PM2.5 is the dominant pollutant, which means tiny particles are floating around, ready to mess with your lungs. The Phoenix-Mesa area consistently ranks poorly for both short-term particle pollution and ozone, so this isn't exactly surprising.

<AlertBox type="info">
  **AQI 51 (Moderate):** If you're sensitive, consider indoor alternatives or shorten your outdoor time. Stick to less strenuous routes.
</AlertBox>

Pollen is also moderate, so if you're prone to seasonal allergies, you know the drill: take your meds *before* you head out, not after you're already a snotty mess halfway down the Grand Canal path.

### Construction & Trail Access

ADOT has multiple freeway projects underway in the Valley this year, and while they might not directly close your favorite running path, they can certainly impact your commute to trailheads. For example, the I-10 through the Wild Horse Pass Corridor has been seeing overnight lane restrictions from March 22-28, which could affect early morning or late evening access. Plus, other improvements are happening on Loop 303 and the I-10/Loop 101 interchange. Always budget extra time.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  Ongoing ADOT construction projects on major freeways like I-10 and Loop 303 can cause unexpected delays and detours when trying to reach your workout spot. Check traffic apps before you roll out.
</AlertBox>

No active wildfires are reported directly impacting Phoenix air quality today, which is a small blessing given our increasingly year-round fire season. Just remember, we're still in the desert; stay vigilant.
