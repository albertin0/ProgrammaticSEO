## Today's Vitals: Don't Be a Hero, Be Hydrated (Indoors)

Let's not sugarcoat it. Phoenix in late July is an oven, and today, July 23rd, is no exception. The "clear" skies might look inviting, but they're just giving that sun an open shot at baking you. At 33.0°C (around 91°F) already, and the mercury set to hit 108°F by the afternoon, your body is going to be fighting an uphill battle against heat exhaustion and stroke. And if the heat wasn't enough, we've got a lovely blanket of ground-level ozone thanks to vehicle emissions cooking in the sun. This stuff is seriously bad for your lungs, especially if you're out there huffing and puffing.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### **Extreme Heat Warning & Trail Closures**
An Extreme Heat Warning is active today from 10 AM, lasting through Monday, July 27th. This isn't just a suggestion; it triggers specific closures. If you were planning to conquer Echo Canyon or Cholla Trail on Camelback Mountain, or the Summit Trail on Piestewa Peak, forget about it between 8 AM and 5 PM. These closures are for your own good, and for the safety of the first responders who'd otherwise be scraping you off the rocks. Seriously, respect the closures.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### **Ozone High Pollution Advisory**
Maricopa County is under an Ozone HPA for today, July 23rd. Ground-level ozone is a nasty lung irritant that forms when pollutants react with sunlight. This means reduced outdoor activity is recommended for *everyone*, but particularly for kids, the elderly, and anyone with respiratory issues. Keep your runs short, or better yet, move 'em inside. And maybe hold off on using that leaf blower; it just contributes to the mess.
</AlertBox>

### **Hydration & Timing are NOT Enough Today**
You know the drill: early morning, late evening, copious amounts of water. But today, with highs forecast to push 108°F, even those tactics are risky. Overnight lows aren't dipping much below the upper 80s, offering little recovery. Consider shifting your workout entirely indoors or to a shaded, air-conditioned environment. Your PT (that's me) will thank you, and so will your lungs and joints. And if you absolutely *must* be outside, keep it short, low-intensity, and stay in your own neighborhood. Don't go wandering off onto trails that are closed or otherwise asking for trouble.
