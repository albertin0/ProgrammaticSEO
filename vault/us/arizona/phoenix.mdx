## Current Environmental Rundown

It's clear skies out there, but that doesn't mean it's all sunshine and daisies for your workout. Your body is going to be working harder than usual if you're hitting the trails or pounding the pavement today.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Wildfire Smoke Potential
The Hazen Fire, burning in Maricopa County, was reported as 10% contained and covering 1049 acres as of yesterday. While it's not directly in central Phoenix, winds can shift and carry smoke and particulates. If you smell smoke or see haze, cut your outdoor activity short, especially if you have asthma or other respiratory issues. Check local updates before heading out, particularly near the Buckeye area where the fire originated.
</AlertBox>

## Heat is Still a Factor (Even If Not "Extreme")

Today's current temperature might be a pleasant 20.8°C (69.4°F), but don't let that fool you. The National Weather Service expects Phoenix to hit highs near 92°F later today. While this isn't in the "Extreme Heat Warning" territory that triggers automatic trail closures on places like Camelback Mountain or Piestewa Peak, it's still hot enough to cause trouble.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydration & Timing are Key
Even when it's "only" in the low 90s, Phoenix heat is no joke. Hydrate religiously, and I mean *before*, *during*, and *after* your workout. Stick to early mornings or late evenings if you insist on any strenuous outdoor activity. Midday is for AC, my friends, or at least a shady walk around your neighborhood, not tackling the South Mountain trails.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
### Ongoing Construction Disruptions
If your route takes you anywhere near the I-10 and Loop 101 interchange in the West Valley, be aware that a multi-year construction project is underway. While the specific weekend freeway closures from May 17-18 are over, ongoing work means you'll likely encounter heavy equipment, potential detours, and general road chaos. Stick to dedicated running paths or sidewalks away from major freeway work zones.
</AlertBox>

In short, today isn't the day for heroics, especially on those more exposed trails. Be smart, be safe, and remember, there’s always tomorrow for that PR.
