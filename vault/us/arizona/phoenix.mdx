If you're looking to crush a midday workout, today isn't your friend. If you're smart about your timing, though, you can still get out there.

Look, it's September in Phoenix. If you haven't learned to respect the sun by now, I don't know what to tell you. While we might not be under an official Extreme Heat Warning *today*, with the National Weather Service forecasting a high of 99°F, and even hotter temps expected by the weekend, this isn't the time to push your limits during peak sun. This entire month is projected to see highs between 96°F and 108°F, so consider this your baseline for the next few weeks.

The city's Trail Heat Safety Program usually kicks in when the NWS issues an Extreme Heat Warning, restricting popular trails like those on Camelback Mountain (Echo Canyon and Cholla) and specific routes on South Mountain. While those formal closures might not be active *right now*, the spirit of the rule still applies. Heading out to tackle the Cholla Trail at noon is just asking for a bad time and potentially putting first responders at risk, even if the thermometer isn't quite hitting that 105°F mark that triggers the official "Stay Off The Mountain" signs.

Air quality is moderate at an AQI of 51, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. For most of us, that's manageable, but if you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, you'll feel it. Don't plan any personal bests when your lungs are already working harder. Also, for those of us who get hit hard, the moderate pollen count isn't doing any favors either. You can try to run along Tempe Town Lake, but don't expect a breeze that clears out those allergens.

<AlertBox type="warning">
  The temperature is still dangerously high for prolonged mid-day activity. Plan your outdoor workouts for pre-sunrise or well after sunset. Seriously, it’s not worth the risk.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  Hydrate, hydrate, hydrate. And then hydrate some more. Carry more water than you think you'll need. Phoenix isn't messing around with heat, even when it's "just" in the high 90s.
</AlertBox>

No new major wildfire smoke impacting Phoenix directly right now, which is a small blessing, and ADOT isn't reporting any *planned* freeway closures for Labor Day weekend that would mess with major routes to trailheads, though general traffic will be heavier. Just watch out for ongoing overnight lane restrictions around Sky Harbor Boulevard if you're commuting to a very early morning flight or late-night run in that vicinity.
