If you're considering an outdoor workout in Phoenix today, July 8th, I'm going to assume you're either new here or actively trying to make a trip to the ER. Don't do it.

<LungsJointsScore score={2} />

<BulletList>
*   **Extreme Heat Warning in full effect.** Maricopa County is under an "extreme heat" warning through Thursday, with forecast highs easily hitting 111 to 116 degrees Fahrenheit (43.9-46.7°C). Your "maintaining fitness" goals are not worth heat stroke.
*   **Trail closures are active.** City of Phoenix has already shut down popular spots like Camelback Mountain's Echo and Cholla Trails, along with the Piestewa Peak Summit Trail and several at South Mountain Park and Preserve, from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. This isn't a suggestion; it's a rule to keep you and the first responders from boiling alive.
*   **Air quality is just 'meh.'** On top of the oven-like conditions, we've got moderate AQI and an Ozone High Pollution Advisory hanging over the Valley. Your lungs don't need that extra stress trying to suck in hot, ozone-laced air.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We runners are a stubborn bunch. We think we can "acclimatize" or "just do a short one." But today, the desert is actively trying to kill you. The 38.7°C (101.7°F) reported temperature is just the *start* of the heat climb for the day. This isn't just "warm," this is "asphalt-will-melt-your-shoes" hot.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Seriously, Stay Inside
An Extreme Heat Warning means there's a significant threat to life and property. Symptoms of heat exhaustion like headaches, heavy sweating, and dizziness can quickly escalate to heat stroke, which includes confusion, vomiting, and even seizures. Don't be that person rescuers have to carry off Camelback Mountain because you ignored every warning.
</AlertBox>

"Moderate" pollen isn't a dealbreaker on its own, but combined with the extreme heat and mediocre air quality, it's just another reason your respiratory system will hate you if you try to push it outdoors today.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### AQI is Moderate, Ozone is a Factor
The Air Quality Index is currently 55, dominated by PM2.5. The Arizona Department of Environmental Quality (ADEQ) also has an Ozone High Pollution Advisory in effect. This means sensitive groups – and honestly, everyone pushing their limits in this heat – might experience respiratory irritation.
</AlertBox>

If you absolutely, positively *must* move your body today, do it indoors. Your gym has AC. Your house has AC. The mall has AC. Run stairs in a high-rise (if you work in one and they allow it). Spin that bike in your living room. There are plenty of ways to stay active without risking a trip to St. Joe's. Hydrate. And then hydrate some more. Water. Not that sugary sports drink crap, unless you're actually exercising, and even then, mostly water.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Smart Choices for Phoenix Summers
*   **Move it indoors:** Seriously. Your life is more important than your Strava segment.
*   **Early bird gets the worm, but the heat gets the runner:** Even the early morning isn't safe with these overnight lows.
*   **Hydrate constantly:** Water, water, water. Before, during, and after.
*   **Wear light, loose clothing:** And definitely a hat if you step outside for anything.
</AlertBox>

Don't be a hero. Live to run another day when the temperatures aren't actively trying to turn you into jerky.
