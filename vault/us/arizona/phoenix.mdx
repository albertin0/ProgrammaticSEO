Look, I get it. We live in Phoenix. We're used to the heat. But "used to it" doesn't make 100 degrees any less dangerous, especially when you're pushing your limits. The city's Trail Heat Safety Program exists for a reason, specifically restricting access to popular spots like the Echo Canyon and Cholla trails on Camelback Mountain when things get this spicy. Just because an *official warning* timestamped yesterday evening might have lapsed doesn't mean the actual conditions have changed significantly enough for you to disregard caution. Your body doesn't care about a bureaucrat's deadline; it cares about core temperature.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Seriously, Avoid Midday Mountain Hikes
The Phoenix Trail Heat Safety Program specifically restricts trails like those on Camelback Mountain and Piestewa Peak between 8 AM and 5 PM when extreme heat warnings are active. Even without an *active* NWS warning for today, hitting these trails with temperatures still climbing into the triple digits is asking for trouble. Stick to flat, shaded paths, or better yet, indoor workouts during these hours. Your life, and the first responders who would have to rescue you, will thank you.
</AlertBox>

For those of you looking to get some miles in, the "Moderate" air quality is largely due to PM2.5. It's not great, particularly if you have asthma or other respiratory issues. Combine that with the moderate pollen count, and you've got a recipe for an uncomfortable, potentially unhealthy, lung workout. The ADEQ forecast indicates these conditions are sticking around for today.

And if you're driving anywhere to hit a trailhead or just move around the city, be aware that ADOT has a full slate of freeway closures and lane restrictions this weekend. Seriously, plan your routes. Loop 101, Loop 202, Loop 303, SR 51, SR 347—they all have issues through Monday morning. Don't add traffic stress to your heat stress.

Finally, for those of you participating or cheering on the "2026 Phoenix 9/11 Heroes Run 5K" over by Mountain America Stadium at ASU today, good on you for honoring the heroes. Just remember to stay hydrated, even if it's early. It's going to be a hot one.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Hydrate Like Your Life Depends On It (Because It Does)
With temperatures hitting 100°F, dehydration is a real and immediate threat. Drink water *before*, *during*, and *after* any outdoor activity, even if it's just a short walk. Don't wait until you're thirsty; by then, you're already behind. Electrolyte drinks can help too.
</AlertBox>
