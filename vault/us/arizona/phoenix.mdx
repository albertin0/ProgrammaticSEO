### The Heat is On (Seriously)

Look, I've run enough miles out here to know that 104°F isn't a suggestion; it's a threat. This isn't the day to try for a PR on Camelback Mountain. The City of Phoenix already has a Trail Heat Safety Program, and when the NWS *does* issue an Extreme Heat Warning, trails on places like Camelback are restricted. Even without an official warning today, those temperatures are dangerous.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**DANGER: Extreme Heat Risk is HIGH.**
Temperatures are projected to hit 104°F (40°C). Heat is the deadliest weather in Arizona. Headaches, heavy sweating, confusion, and even loss of consciousness are all on the menu if you push too hard. If you're out there between 10 AM and 4 PM, you're playing Russian roulette with heatstroke. Don't be that person.
</AlertBox>

### What's in the Air?

While the initial AQI for PM2.5 is low, ADEQ's forecast of moderate ozone (93 AQI) is concerning, particularly for sensitive groups. Ozone forms when sunlight reacts with vehicle and industrial emissions, and it's worse in the afternoon. If you've got asthma or other respiratory issues, this is your cue to reconsider that afternoon long run.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**WARNING: Moderate Ozone Levels Expected.**
The forecast indicates moderate ozone (O3) levels, which can irritate your throat, cause headaches, and aggravate respiratory conditions. Sensitive individuals, keep your outdoor time minimal during peak afternoon hours.
</AlertBox>

### Your Plan of Attack (or Retreat)

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip: Hydrate or Die-drate.**
It's Phoenix. You *always* need to hydrate. Drink water before, during, and after your workout. Seriously, chug it. Forget the fancy sports drinks unless you're out for an hour-plus and truly need electrolytes.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip: Early Bird Gets the Worm (and Avoids the Sunstroke).**
If you absolutely must be outside, go before the sun even thinks about cracking the horizon. Or wait until well after it's dipped below it. The forecast temps will be "near normal" in the mornings, but quickly rising.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Tip: Be Flexible. Or Get a Gym Membership.**
If your body starts telling you it's too much – dizziness, nausea, excessive sweating, or the dreaded *lack* of sweating – stop. Immediately. Find shade, get water, and cool down. And honestly, sometimes the smartest workout is the one you do indoors when it's this hot. There's no shame in the treadmill game when the alternative is heat exhaustion.

</AlertBox>
