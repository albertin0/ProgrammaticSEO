Forget that "get outside and seize the day" nonsense. Today in Phoenix, you're mostly just seizing a heat stroke.

<LungsJointsScore score={2} />

<BulletList>
*   **Extreme Heat Warning is Active, Folks:** We're talking dangerously hot, with afternoon temperatures expected to hit 106°F to 117°F today, July 15th. You know the drill, the city's popular trails on Camelback Mountain (Echo Canyon and Cholla Trails), Piestewa Peak Summit Trail, and sections of South Mountain Park and Preserve (like Holbert and Mormon Trails) are *closed* from 8 AM to 5 PM for a reason. Don't be "that guy" needing a rescue when first responders are already stretched thin.
*   **Monsoon Aftermath:** Even though the chance of rain today is practically nil after yesterday's activity, don't forget the Valley just got hit with some monsoon storms. There were flash flood warnings for areas like Papago Park and downtown Phoenix just yesterday. That means potentially slick surfaces, standing water in low-lying areas, and maybe even some trail erosion in the less-traveled spots. Watch your footing, especially near washes.
*   **Moderate AQI & Pollen:** Your lungs won't be thrilled either. We're sitting at an AQI of 54 (moderate) with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant, plus moderate pollen levels. It's not a choking hazard, but combine it with gasping for air in triple-digit heat and it's a recipe for a miserable time, not a good workout.

</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
**SERIOUS HEAT HAZARD:** An Extreme Heat Warning remains in effect through Thursday evening. Phoenix was forecasted to hit a brutal 119°F yesterday, and today isn't far behind. This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely life-threatening, with high risks of heat exhaustion and heat stroke. The pavement itself can cause contact burns in this kind of heat. Stay indoors during peak afternoon hours. Your PR can wait.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
**TRAIL CLOSURES ARE ENFORCED:** The City of Phoenix has closed key trails on Camelback Mountain, Piestewa Peak, and parts of South Mountain from 8 AM to 5 PM. Ignoring these closures not only puts you at risk but also burdens our emergency services. If you *must* be outside, stick to flat, shaded areas and go at dawn.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
**Hydration and Planning are Non-Negotiable:** If you decide to venture out, even for a short, early morning walk, hydrate aggressively *before*, *during*, and *after*. Wear light, loose-fitting clothing. Know the signs of heat stress (dizziness, weakness, headache) and get inside immediately if you feel them. Better yet, hit the gym with AC, or do some mobility work at home. Your body will thank you later. The Valleywise Health Chief Clinical Officer specifically warns against strenuous activity in these temperatures.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, save your outdoor miles for a day when it doesn't feel like you're running on the surface of the sun. There's always tomorrow (maybe).
