## The "Run Anyway" Mentality Will Land You in Trouble

Look, I get it. You've got your miles to hit. But today, May 16, 2026, Phoenix is reminding us why we sometimes hate this desert. The mercury is sitting at a balmy 38.7°C (that's 101.7°F for the non-metric crowd), under a mainly clear sky. This isn't just "warm," it's "call-the-fire-department-for-a-mountain-rescue" hot. The City of Phoenix has a standing Trail Heat Safety Program, and you can bet your last electrolyte tablet that popular trails like Echo Canyon and Cholla on Camelback Mountain are restricted from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Don't even *think* about Piestewa Peak Summit Trail or the restricted routes on South Mountain like Holbert Trail during those hours. They're closed for your own good – and for the sanity of our first responders who are already bracing for a long season. Seriously, Phoenix Fire Captain Rob McDade pointed out how early the heat kicked in this year, leading to trail closures in *March* and a jump in rescues. You think mid-May is going to be any kinder?

## What's in the Air?

Besides the scorching heat, our lovely Phoenix air quality is currently sitting at an AQI of 55, which is moderate, with PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant. Yesterday saw an Ozone High Pollution Advisory, and while ozone levels are expected to decrease today, we're still looking at moderate levels of PM10 (dust) and PM2.5 (smoke), especially with breezy southwest winds picking up in the afternoon. So, you'll be running in a hot, dusty, potentially smoky haze. Charming.

Fire and smoking restrictions are in effect across state trust lands in Maricopa County due to recent wildfire activity. While some fires like the Hazen Fire near Buckeye and the Jones Fire near Wickenburg are largely contained, their lingering effects, combined with the wind, contribute to that delightful PM2.5 in the air.
