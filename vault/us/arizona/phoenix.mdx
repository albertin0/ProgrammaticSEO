Look, Phoenix is preparing for a rough wildfire season, and PM2.5 is often a marker for smoke particulates. Even if there isn't a massive blaze directly next door, regional fires and gusty winds can kick up smoke and dust, adding to that delightful AQI. This isn't the day to push your limits if your respiratory system is already feeling iffy.

<AlertBox type="warning">
### AQI is a Concern for Sensitive Groups
With PM2.5 as the dominant pollutant and an AQI of 71, individuals with heart or lung disease, older adults, and children should seriously consider reducing prolonged or heavy exertion outdoors. Even if you're tough, your lungs are working harder. The Arizona Department of Environmental Quality (ADEQ) issues High Pollution Advisories when concentrations exceed federal health standards, and while one isn't active for today, the trend is heading that way.
</AlertBox>

Let's talk about the heat, because even at a seemingly mild 91°F (33°C), it's still Phoenix, and our trails are unforgiving. We've already seen record-breaking heat this year, forcing early trail closures on iconic spots like Camelback Mountain and Piestewa Peak in March. The city's Parks and Recreation department actively discourages hiking on trails like those in South Mountain Park and Preserve during midday heat. You might not face official closures today, but that doesn't mean the desert sun won't try to bake you.

<AlertBox type="danger">
### Heat: Not Extreme, But Still Dangerous
While the forecast isn't hitting "Extreme Heat Warning" levels today, a high of 91°F (33°C) means you need to be smart. Hydrate *before* you go, carry more water than you think you need (at least a liter per hour of activity, folks!), and plan to be done before 9 or 10 AM, or wait until after sundown. Midday is for indoor A/C, not for trying to set a new PR on Holbert Trail.
</AlertBox>

Finally, keep your head on a swivel. There's always *some* construction going on in the Valley, and ADOT has a full plate of freeway projects in 2026, including on Loop 303 and the I-10/Loop 101 interchange. While most of this won't block your favorite singletrack, it might mess with your commute to the trailhead or add to local dust and vehicle emissions. Factor in potential traffic or unexpected detours if you’re driving any distance to your workout spot.

<AlertBox type="info">
### Plan Your Route and Hydrate Smart
Check your route to your favorite running spot for any unexpected road closures or heavy construction zones. Most importantly, don't be a hero in this heat. Go early, hydrate relentlessly, and listen to your body. Phoenix summers start early, and you don't want to be the reason rescue crews are out enjoying the 90-degree sunshine.
</AlertBox>
