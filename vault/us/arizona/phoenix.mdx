Forget your delusions of a "refreshing" morning run, Phoenix. Today isn't the day to push it outdoors unless you're training for an ironman in hell.

<BulletList>
*   **Actual Surface-of-the-Sun Temperatures:** We're looking at a high of 108°F (42°C) today. That's not "warm," that's "your internal organs are cooking" hot. Phoenix Fire is still dealing with an upward trend in trail rescues, and they're not thrilled about it.
*   **"Moderate" Air Quality is a Lie for Your Lungs:** While the general AQI might look okay on paper at 47, we've got an Ozone High Pollution Advisory for the metro area, with Ozone levels hitting "Moderate" at 80 AQI. PM2.5 is also at a "Moderate" 52 AQI. If you've got lungs that actually function, active adults and anyone with respiratory issues should limit outdoor activity.
*   **Wildfire Whiff:** Dry conditions and rising temperatures mean wildfire risk is high across Arizona. The Kaibab National Forest is even implementing Stage 1 fire restrictions starting today. While no *major* active fires are choking the Valley *right now*, don't add to the problem by being careless.

<AlertBox type="danger">
#### Extreme Heat Warning
Seriously, folks. The National Weather Service is reiterating that high temperatures between 105°F and 115°F are expected. Phoenix has already seen its first heat-related death of 2026 back in April, and ER visits are surging. Stay inside, stay hydrated, and don't become another statistic. The city's Heat Relief Network has cooling centers if your AC decides to give up the ghost.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Trail Closures in Effect
Don't even *think* about hitting the high-intensity trails on South Mountain like Holbert, Mormon, or Hau'pal Loop between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. when an Extreme Heat Warning is in effect. Same goes for Camelback and Piestewa Peak Summit trails. These aren't suggestions; these are for your safety (and to save our overtaxed first responders from having to haul your heat-stroked self off the mountain). The Parks & Recreation Board voted on these closures for a reason.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Hydration is Not Optional
If you absolutely *must* be outside, drink water before, during, and after. Like, a lot of water. More than you think you need. Your body is losing fluids faster than you realize in this dry heat, even if you don't feel like you're sweating. Wear lightweight, light-colored clothing. Tell someone your route. And for the love of all that is holy, no kids or pets in parked cars. Ever.
</AlertBox>
