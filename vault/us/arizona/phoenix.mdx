Today is absolutely *not* the day to push it outside unless you fancy an express trip to the nearest emergency room.

<BulletList>
*   **Deadly Heat:** We're staring down 41.9°C (107°F) and the National Weather Service has an Extreme Heat Warning plastered all over the Valley through today, with overnight lows barely dipping below 90°F. That's not "warm," that's "your body's cooling system will fail."
*   **Toxic Air:** Forget that "Moderate" AQI. We've got *two* High Pollution Advisories running concurrently: one for Ozone and another for PM10 from smoke and dust, with levels expected to blow past federal health standards. So you'll be breathing in lung-irritating particulates and a chemical soup.
*   **Trail Closures & Rescue Risks:** Due to the extreme heat, specific trails at places like South Mountain Park and Preserve are closed from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Firefighters are already over it, dealing with an alarming number of heat-related hiker rescues this summer. Don't be another statistic for them to haul off a mountain.
</BulletList>

Look, I get it. We live in Phoenix, and we're tough. We also live in Phoenix, where August 28th means it's a hundred-and-damn-near-twelve degrees in the shade, if you can even find any. The overcast skies today might fool you into thinking it's "cooler," but 41.9°C is still 41.9°C. Your sweat won't evaporate effectively, your core temperature will climb, and you'll be a crispy critter before you know it. We're also seeing overnight lows clinging to the 90s, which means your body gets zero recovery. This isn't just about feeling uncomfortable; it's about life-threatening heat exhaustion and heat stroke.

Then there's the air. The Arizona Department of Environmental Quality (ADEQ) has issued *both* an Ozone High Pollution Advisory and a PM10 High Pollution Advisory for today, and the PM10 one extends into Saturday. PM10 means coarse particulate matter – think dust and fine smoke – that digs deep into your lungs, irritating everything and worsening conditions like asthma or heart disease. We're expecting blowing dust, especially this evening and Friday night, which will only make the PM10 problem worse. So, while you're trying to push that run, you'll be actively damaging your respiratory system. Good times.

Don't even think about hitting the major trails during peak hours. South Mountain's popular routes like Holbert and Mormon Trail are closed for a reason between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. Those closures aren't to annoy you; they're to keep you and our already overworked first responders safe. Phoenix firefighters have already handled nearly 75 mountain rescues this year. Let's not add to that tally, shall we?
