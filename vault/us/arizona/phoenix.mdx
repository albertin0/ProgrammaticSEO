Your lungs are probably fine today, all things considered. The AQI is a happy little 49, with Ozone as the dominant pollutant. ### Joints & Heat

This is where things get spicy, and not in a good way. While you might be looking at 22.1°C (around 72°F) right now, don't be fooled. The National Weather Service is predicting today's high around 91°F, and we're officially on track for the earliest and hottest triple-digit days ever recorded in March by midweek. An Extreme Heat Watch is blanketing southern Arizona from Wednesday through the weekend, with highs potentially hitting 103-106°F.

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Extreme Heat Warning incoming!** While today, March 17th, might not *officially* trigger the city's trail closures, the heat is escalating rapidly. Phoenix fire crews responded to at least three hiker rescues on Camelback Mountain just yesterday. Don't wait for a formal warning to take this seriously.
</AlertBox>

Seriously, if you're planning a hike on Camelback or exploring South Mountain trails, do it at dawn. By 8 AM, the sun's already got its full mean streak going, and by midday, you're just tempting fate. The city’s official trail restrictions, which close popular trails like Echo Canyon and Cholla on Camelback, and several at South Mountain Park, during Excessive Heat Warnings, are a response to a real problem: mountain rescues spiked in previous years. They start at 8 AM when an Extreme Heat Warning is issued, and we're looking at those warnings later this week.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Hydration is NOT optional.** Even if you're just walking the dog around Papago Park, carry more water than you think you need. Consider electrolyte drinks, not just plain water, especially if you're out for more than an hour.
</AlertBox>

The NWS specifically mentioned that this heat wave, driven by a high-pressure system, will likely break daily records by nearly 10 degrees from Wednesday through the weekend. That's not just "warm," that's "your-car-seatbelt-will-burn-you" hot.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Shift your schedule.** If you can, move your workouts indoors, or to the very early morning or late evening. This isn't the week for midday long runs, unless you enjoy risking heatstroke and inconveniencing our amazing fire and rescue teams.
</AlertBox>

Stay safe out there, or just stay inside. No one's judging.
