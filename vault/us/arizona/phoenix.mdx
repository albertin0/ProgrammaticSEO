Look, if you're even *thinking* about a serious outdoor workout in Phoenix today, July 25th, you need to seriously reconsider your life choices.

### Why today is absolutely NOT the day:

<BulletList>
  <li>**Extreme Heat Warning is Active, Folks.** We're talking "don't even look at your oven" kind of heat, with afternoon temperatures hitting anywhere from 105 to a staggering 120 degrees Fahrenheit (40.5 to 49°C). This isn't just uncomfortable; it's genuinely dangerous and could land you in the ER.</li>
  <li>**Trail Closures Are Real.** The city isn't messing around. Popular spots like Echo Canyon and Cholla trails on Camelback Mountain are *closed* from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. during these extreme heat warnings. This isn't for your convenience; it's to prevent you, and the first responders who'd have to rescue your sun-baked self, from becoming a statistic.</li>
  <li>**Air Quality and Pollen are Just Adding Insult to Injury.** While the general AQI is moderate at 57, there are ongoing concerns with ozone pollution and the potential for blowing dust throughout the weekend. Combine that with moderate pollen levels, and your lungs will thank you for staying inside.</li>
</BulletList>

<AlertBox type="danger">
  ## DANGER: Extreme Heat Warning in Full Effect
  The National Weather Service has issued an Extreme Heat Warning for the Phoenix metropolitan area, active until at least Monday, July 27th, at 8:00 PM MST. Daytime highs are expected to reach critical levels (105-120°F / 40.5-49°C). This heat can cause heat exhaustion and heat stroke extremely rapidly. If you MUST be outside, keep it to the absolute earliest morning hours, wear lightweight, loose-fitting clothing, and hydrate aggressively. But honestly, just don't. Your run can wait.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
  ## Air Quality & Allergies: Don't Push It
  While the PM2.5 AQI is technically "moderate," we've been dealing with Ozone High Pollution Advisories, and there's a forecast for potential blowing dust today, which can elevate PM10 levels. For anyone with respiratory issues or allergies, this combination of moderate pollen, ozone, and potential dust isn't ideal, especially when coupled with the extreme heat. Save your lungs the extra work.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
  ## Pro Tip: Embrace the Indoors (or the Very, Very Early Morning)
  Seriously, the only safe window for anything remotely strenuous outdoors is probably before the sun even thinks about showing up in full force, and even then, be hyper-aware. If you absolutely have to move, hit the treadmill, find an air-conditioned gym, or swim laps in a *shaded* pool. This isn't the day to try for a PR up Piestewa Peak. The City of Phoenix has even closed several trails, including those on South Mountain Park and Preserve, for your safety during these extreme heat warnings. Don't be that person needing a rescue. It ties up resources and puts others at risk.
</AlertBox>
