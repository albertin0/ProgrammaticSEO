## Weather Woes (or lack thereof)

It's 20.3°C and overcast out there right now, which is pretty comfortable for a run or walk. But don't be surprised if you feel a few drops. There's a 40% chance of showers throughout the day, mainly after 3 p.m., with similar chances tonight and into Tuesday. We're also looking at south winds around 11 mph, gusting up to 18 mph, so you might feel a bit of a push or pull on your way. These aren't hurricane-force winds, but they're enough to make you notice. No extreme heat or cold to worry about, thankfully, which is a nice change of pace for late September in Colorado.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pro Tip:** With rain in the forecast, remember that wet surfaces, especially on trails or near construction, can be slick. Adjust your pace and footwear accordingly. Nobody wants a twisted ankle, especially not with winter coming.
</AlertBox>

## Construction, Closures, & Other Annoyances

Ah, Aurora. Always building, always detouring. While the main segment of the East Colfax Avenue Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project between Broadway and Colorado Boulevard just finished up yesterday, don't get too excited. Construction on the Colfax BRT continues *east* of Colorado Boulevard toward I-225 in Aurora through the end of 2027. So, if you're jogging or cycling along Colfax, expect ongoing lane shifts and general chaos for the foreseeable future.

<AlertBox type="warning">
There's an ongoing partial closure of the **Westerly Creek Greenway** at Stanley Marketplace. You'll need to use the detour path along Beeler Street if that's part of your usual route. Don't just blindly follow your GPS; sometimes the quickest way is the well-marked detour.
</AlertBox>

On a less scenic note, if your workout involves navigating the southern parts of Aurora by car to get to a trail, be aware that the intersection of County Line and Monaghan was fully closed through today for new infrastructure installation. It *might* be reopening today, but check local traffic apps before you commit. Plus, Potomac Street north of Sixth Avenue has alternating lane closures for utility work, which just started today and runs through October 6.

<AlertBox type="danger">
While recent major flooding isn't on the books for the last 24 hours, remember that the Expo Park neighborhood saw significant flooding back in August. Given the rain chances today and later this week, keep an eye on rapidly pooling water, especially in underpasses or low-lying areas. It doesn't take much to turn a puddle into a problem.
</AlertBox>

Keep your head up, stay aware, and maybe bring a light jacket. It's a decent day to get outside, just don't expect perfect conditions.
