## The Air: A Mixed Bag You Need to Watch

Look, your phone might be flashing an AQI of 140 with PM2.5 as the star pollutant, and that's usually a cue to stay indoors. However, local forecasters from the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) were projecting "Good to Moderate" ozone and "Good to Moderate" particulate matter (PM2.5) for today, Saturday, June 6, 2026, with the highest recorded AQI for ozone at 67 (Moderate) and PM2.5 at 60 (Moderate) as of early morning.

Now, don't get complacent. "Moderate" still means "unusually sensitive individuals may experience respiratory symptoms." Especially with PM2.5 in the mix, those tiny particles can dig deep into your lungs. And let's not forget the "Moderate" pollen count hanging in the air. Double whammy for your airways.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**AQI Advisory: Don't Push It, Sensitive Folks**
Even with the local forecast leaning Moderate, if you're unusually sensitive to ozone or particulate matter (that's active kids, adults, and anyone with asthma or lung disease), seriously consider reducing prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion, especially from noon to 8 p.m. Your lungs will thank you.
</AlertBox>

## On the Ground: Obstacles and Detours Aplenty

The weather itself is playing nice – clear skies and a pleasant 17.2°C, warming up to a high of 33°C (91°F) later in the day. Great for getting outside, right? Not so fast.

If you're planning a long run or ride on the **Cherry Creek Trail**, prepare for a significant reroute. Sections of that trail are closed until "summer 2026," meaning you'll be detouring up to Monaco Parkway. The Sanderson Gulch Trail also has detours until June 2026, so expect the unexpected there too.

And if your workout takes you through downtown, be aware that various street construction projects are causing lane and sidewalk closures. This isn't just a car problem; pedestrians and cyclists need to stay alert for changed paths and equipment.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Construction Navigation: Stay Alert**
Always check local Denver Public Works alerts before you head out, especially for downtown areas or major trails. Detours can add unexpected mileage, and uneven surfaces are a twisted ankle waiting to happen.
</AlertBox>
