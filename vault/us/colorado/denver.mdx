## Today's Denver Workout Outlook

Don't let the good AQI fool you into thinking it's a perfect 10. We're still in that weird shoulder season where the weather can't make up its mind, and the city's construction crews are in full swing.

The good news is the air quality is sitting at a nice 33, with Ozone as the dominant pollutant, but at levels that shouldn't bother most folks. Just remember that the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE) sometimes flags "Moderate" ozone days for sensitive individuals, advising reduced exertion from noon to 10 pm. Today, we're better than moderate. So breathe easy, literally.

**Allergy Alert: Moderate Pollen's Out to Get You.**

### Weather & What to Wear

Clear skies are a go, which is always a win in my book. However, 10.8°C (51.4°F) isn't exactly shorts and tank top weather for everyone. If you're heading out for a morning loop around City Park or down the Platte River Greenway, consider a light jacket or long sleeves that you can shed. It’s May, but the mountains still whisper cold air down into the city.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Layer Up! It's Chilly, Not Tropical.**
Don't be that person shivering at the start line. Denver's spring mornings can bite. Layers are your friend. You can always tie that extra jacket around your waist, but you can't conjure one out of thin air.
</AlertBox>

### Local Hazards: Construction & Traffic

Now for the real fun: navigating Denver's ever-present urban sprawl and its accompanying construction. The Colfax Avenue Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project is still a beast, causing disruptions along that major artery. If your route takes you anywhere near Colfax, prepare for potential delays or unexpected detours. Mayor Johnston himself seems to acknowledge the headaches this project is causing.

For those of you who prefer the greener paths, the High Line Canal Trail has ongoing tree canopy care, and a vault replacement project at South Ash Street. While they say it won't require full trail closures, expect some heavy equipment or temporary reroutes, especially around that S. Ash Street area. There's also a detour at Yale Avenue for a pipe installation, though a temporary bike/pedestrian path should be in place. Always check the High Line Canal Conservancy's website before you head out, because "no closure" can sometimes mean "major annoyance."

<AlertBox type="danger">
**Construction Chaos on Colfax and High Line Canal!**
The Colfax BRT project is still a mess, and parts of the High Line Canal Trail are under construction. If you're running or biking either of these, be prepared for detours, uneven surfaces, and potential delays. Keep your head on a swivel!
</AlertBox>

It's also Memorial Day weekend, which means while CDOT is suspending construction on major state roads until Tuesday to ease traffic, local roads within Denver might still see project activity. Also, expect heavier traffic getting *to* your favorite mountain trailheads if you're venturing out, as low snowpack is making the high country more accessible than usual this year.
