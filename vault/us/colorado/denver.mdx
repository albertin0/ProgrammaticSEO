## Denver's Outdoor Workout Report: April 18, 2026

Alright, Denver, let's talk turkey about lacing up your shoes today. The temperature might look inviting at 12.2°C (54°F) and "Mainly Clear," but peel back that pretty facade, and things get a little grittier, as they always do.

First up, the elephant in the room: that Air Quality Index. A solid 140 for PM2.5 isn't just a number; it's a warning label. While some official forecasts might have optimistically predicted "Good" for particulate matter today, the actual reading is well into "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" territory. So, if you've got asthma, any other respiratory issues, or just a general preference for not feeling like you ran through a campfire, maybe dial it back or move that workout inside. Your lungs are not disposable.

Then there's the whiplash weather. Yesterday was apparently a pleasant 77 degrees, only to be followed by a "hard freeze" overnight, with temps plunging into the 18-24°F range. So, while the afternoon is warming up, don't be shocked if you find lingering ice on sidewalks, bridges, or shaded sections of trails, especially if there was any light snow yesterday. That's an express ticket to a sprained ankle, and I've got enough of those to deal with in my clinic.

#### AQI Alert: Breathe with Caution
With PM2.5 at 140, sensitive individuals (asthmatics, elderly, children, those with heart/lung conditions) should *seriously* consider rescheduling intense outdoor activity. Everyone else, cut down on prolonged exertion. This isn't just sniffles; it's about long-term lung health.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
#### Watch Your Step: Icy Leftovers
Despite the clear skies, that overnight hard freeze means potential black ice and slick spots, especially in the early hours or in shaded areas. Take it slow, particularly on less-traveled paths like sections of the **South Platte River Trail** where uneven surfaces might hold onto ice longer. Don't be a hero; walk if it looks sketchy.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
#### Trail Detours Are the New Normal
Speaking of the South Platte, a significant 1.4-mile stretch between the Broncos pedestrian bridge and the 19th Street pedestrian bridge remains closed until May 2026 for sewer pipe repairs. You'll be shunted to the east bank, so plan your routes accordingly. Same goes for parts of Cherry Creek Trail, which are also closed through summer. Always check Denver Parks & Rec for the latest trail updates before you head out, unless you enjoy impromptu detours through unfamiliar neighborhoods.
</AlertBox>
