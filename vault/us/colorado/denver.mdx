## The Rundown

It's clear out there, which is a blessing after some of the earlier spring nonsense we've had. Temperatures are hovering around 4.6°C (that's roughly 40°F for the Fahrenheit crowd), heading up to a pleasant 11°C later, so layer wisely. It's still a bit breezy, though, so don't expect perfectly still air to lull you into a false sense of warmth.

Your lungs should generally appreciate the good air quality, clocking in at an AQI of 37, which is low and lovely. However, it's prime season for allergies, and we're sitting at a "Moderate" pollen level. If you're susceptible, you know the drill: pre-run meds, sunglasses, maybe a bandana for that extra layer of defense against the invisible assault.

### Trail Talk & Concrete Chaos

Now, for the stuff that'll really test your patience and ankles: construction. It's Denver, so it's always construction season, apparently. Several of our beloved trails are still impacted by ongoing projects:

<AlertBox type="danger">
The **Cherry Creek Trail** continues to be a headache with a section closed for bridge replacement. You'll be detoured up to Monaco Parkway, then south to Florida Avenue, until at least Summer 2026. Don't be that person who ignores the signs; it's closed for a reason.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="warning">
Expect more detours on the **South Platte River Trail** between the Broncos pedestrian bridge and the 19th Street pedestrian bridge, with the north and west side closed. You'll be shunted to the east bank. Also, the **Weir Gulch Trail** is closed between the South Platte River Trail and Federal Blvd for flood risk reduction until late May. Keep your head up and check for updates before you commit to a long run through those areas.
</AlertBox>

<AlertBox type="info">
With the Rockies' Opening Day at Coors Field today, expect some extra foot traffic and general hubbub around the LoDo area. DOTI even painted a purple stripe on Blake Street, so you know it's a thing. If you're running downtown, just be aware of larger crowds and potential delays.
</AlertBox>

While Denver itself isn't under a Red Flag Warning today, the region was experiencing "moderate fire conditions" yesterday due to warmth and strong winds, especially south of us. It's a good reminder that while the mountains might be getting fresh snow, the plains can dry out fast this time of year. Keep an eye out for anything suspicious, because you never know what a stray gust of wind can do.

In short, it's a decent day to get outside if you plan your route carefully and dress for a brisk, breezy morning. Just don't assume your usual path is clear, and keep those eyes peeled.
