Alright, Indy runners, listen up.

Today's the kind of spring day where you think you've escaped winter's grip, but the city still has a few tricks up its sleeve to keep things interesting.

<BulletList>

*   The Air Quality Index is squarely in the "good" zone, so no smoky haze to choke on.
*   But don't get too comfortable: our old friend "construction" is still making a mess of things, and the pollen count is definitely going to remind you it's spring.
</BulletList>

The weather app probably told you it's 15.9°C and clear, which sounds fantastic on paper. And for your lungs, it mostly is. The AQI is a happy 50, with PM2.5 being the dominant pollutant, but at that level, you're not going to feel it. That means you can push the pace without feeling like you've been breathing through a coffee filter.

However, if your nose is anything like mine, you're already feeling that "Moderate" pollen level. It's not awful, but it's enough to trigger some seasonal sniffles and make you question if that burning sensation in your lungs is effort or allergies. Don't be a hero; take an antihistamine if you're prone to it.

Now, for the real joys of running in our fair city: construction. The IndyGo Blue Line project continues its relentless march, especially along **Washington Street**. You're looking at lane closures and pedestrian access headaches from the White River Bridge to Alabama Street, and specifically, Washington Street from Southeastern to State Avenues is closed to eastbound traffic for storm sewer work, which is slated to run into late 2027. If your route takes you anywhere near these areas, assume detours, uneven surfaces, and a higher-than-average chance of tripping over a rogue traffic cone or dodging impatient drivers. Pedestrian crosswalk availability is maintained but don't expect full accessibility in some parts until *tentatively* March 2026. Yeah, that's now.

<AlertBox type="warning">
**Construction Zone Chaos on Washington Street:** Expect significant delays, lane closures, and pedestrian re-routes due to the Blue Line project and ongoing storm sewer construction. This isn't just about traffic; it impacts sidewalks and crossing points. Be vigilant and don't trust your usual path.
</AlertBox>

Beyond the Blue Line, the Department of Public Works has various drainage and pavement rehabilitation projects ongoing across the city, including areas like Brookside Parkway and McCarty Street. While they've been working on potholes (claiming to have filled 480,000 in 2025), it's spring in Indiana, and fresh potholes are basically blooming faster than the daffodils. Keep an eye out, especially on less-traveled streets. Your ankles will thank you.

<AlertBox type="info">
**Pollen Prep:** Moderate pollen levels mean spring allergies are kicking in. If you're susceptible, take your medication *before* you head out, and consider an extra rinse for your running gear when you get back.
</AlertBox>

There aren't any massive race events or protests blocking the Monon Trail or other major running paths within city limits today, which is a small mercy. However, a large youth volleyball tournament at the Convention Center yesterday might still mean more downtown vehicle and foot traffic than usual. Just be aware if you're hitting the downtown canals or streets.

So, while the weather is playing nice, the city itself is still a bit of a minefield. Pick your route, watch your step, and maybe carry a tissue.
